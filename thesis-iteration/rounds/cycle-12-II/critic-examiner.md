# Critic (examiner lens, Maarten Schoukens), cycle 12, Section II

Score: 7 / 10

## Overall

Read once as an examiner, the section is one argument: a roadmap names II-A to II-D, and each subsection has
one job and runs problem, construction, consequence. The math shows what is built on the gantry: coordinates
and the P frames (eq:Ptransform), M(Y), C, K, the Y-free relation, the scheduling loop, G with its blocks, the
exact well-posedness condition, the ten combinations and the two compared models with input u_act and output
q_act. Where clauses give spaces. Citations match citation-log.md (Garcia Sec. 2.2, 2.3, Table I; Toth Def. 7.2;
Drenth thesis Sec. 2.1, 2.1.1; Drenth et al. Sec. 3.1, 3.2, Thm. 6; Ovchinnikov Def. 7). No em-dashes. No
identification problem or initialisation is owned here, so criterion B's display requirement is met by the
model displays. The open points below are what an examiner would still raise.

## Issues

1. MAJOR (A, coherence/consequence). II-B, opening paragraph and the last paragraph (lines 235 to 241, 327 to 331).
   The subsection never says why the thesis needs the LFR. Its problem sentence says M(Y)^{-1} is rational and an
   LPV-LFR "isolates such a dependence", which is a property, not a need of this thesis. Its closing paragraph says
   the code solves the loop in closed form and that the LFR "remains the form of the baseline block in Section III".
   Section III evaluates the same model, so this names no use. Its one result, well posed iff M(Y) is nonsingular,
   already follows from eq:eom. An examiner would ask what the LFR buys. Hoekstra's LFR framework, primary in the
   source map row II-B, is not cited in II-B. Neither is the research plan's Aspect 1 ("in what form the baseline
   is represented").
   Rule cause: skill "Order by kind of section" (Method): the "still for" rule accepts naming a section as the use
   and does not test whether that use needs the construction. The source map row II-B gives D-020 (derivation and
   structural analysis) as the use, but the draft does not state it.

2. MAJOR (D, choice not justified, and in tension with the contribution). II-B after eq:delta (line 281) and the
   opening (lines 102 to 104). "This six-channel realisation is not claimed to be minimal" gives no reason and no
   \todo. The header lists the full six-channel realisation under "Choices to justify". D-017 records minimality as
   open. M1 has rank 2 and M2 rank 1, so an LPV examiner sees at once that fewer channels suffice. The opening
   claims a "compact" realisation as the contribution, which the non-minimality sentence then undercuts.
   Rule cause: skill "Content", "The header": a non-minimal realisation that differs from the cited method "gets
   its reason or a \todo". The rule exists; the writer did not apply it, and no rule checks the contribution
   wording against the section's own limitation sentences.

3. MINOR (B/F, unstated assumption). II-B, eq:lfr_G and eq:wellposed (lines 287 to 322). D_zw uses M0^{-1}, and
   eq:wellposed divides by det M0, but the draft never says that M0 = M(0) is nonsingular. It holds at physical
   values, but the exact condition is only exact under that assumption.
   Rule cause: missing. No rule asks that a display state the invertibility (or other assumption) it uses
   (README "Math standard" item 1 covers spaces, not assumptions of a display).

4. MINOR (F, claim precision). II-C, line 361: "Exactly ten combinations of theta are therefore structurally
   identifiable". Under Ovchinnikov Def. 7, every function of the ten is identifiable, so the precise claim is that
   the identifiable functions are generated by ten independent combinations. A system-identification examiner
   would correct this wording.
   Rule cause: skill "Reasons and claims" ("state every claim as strongly as its evidence supports"). The rule
   exists; the cited definition was not applied to the sentence's quantifier.

5. MINOR (B, model not complete). II-D, eq:baseline_models (lines 399 to 417). M_LPV and M_LTI are named with
   input, output and fixed theta. The draft does not say what the models start from (initial state), how the
   continuous-time model is simulated against the sampled records (RK4 of Section III-A), or whether the
   comparison runs open loop on recorded forces or in the closed loop of Section III-C (the records are
   closed-loop). One clause with a pointer would settle this; otherwise a \todo, since THESIS-RESULTS step 1 does
   not fix the loop.
   Rule cause: skill "Order by kind of section" (Method: "Each model ends complete: what is trained, what is
   fixed, what it starts from"). The rule exists but assumes a trained model; for an untrained compared model it
   does not name the initial state and the simulation setting (open or closed loop) as required content.

6. MINOR (F, notation). eq:lfr_G uses C_z and C_y as blocks of G beside the damping matrix C and the damping
   coefficient c_y. Section III uses C^n and y for the output map and the recorded position. C_y (output block)
   next to c_y (Y-actuator damping) gives one letter two roles in adjacent displays.
   Rule cause: skill "Content", "Notation" (one meaning per symbol, including letters used as base and index).
   The rule exists but was not applied to the standard state-space block names taken from Drenth.
