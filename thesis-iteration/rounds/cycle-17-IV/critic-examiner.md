# Critic (examiner lens, Maarten Schoukens), cycle 17, Section IV

Score: 7.5 / 10

## Verdict
Read once, the section shows what is built and trained: the sensitivity (eq:obc_sens) in the training coordinates, the stacked coefficient (eq:obc_coef), the correction on the physical rows (eq:obc_corr), the identification problem with the changed line and the other constraints cited inside the display (eq:obc_problem), the true discrepancy (eq:obc_eps) and a short own Proposition with its proof. The roadmap names the three subsections. Each subsection has one job, and the math is specific to the gantry (the M(Y)^{-1}-weighted mass, damping and stiffness directions, the P frame, the free axes). Fixed and trained quantities are named: xi_lin is fixed per epoch and theta_aux is re-solved at every evaluation. Spaces are given in "where" clauses. I checked the algebra by hand: the proof residual, the Pythagoras split, the iff condition and the nonsingular Jacobian of eq:mdelta_map for m_Delta,0 != 0. All of them hold. Every Gyorok citation I checked matches citation-log rows 40 to 53, 71 to 82. The section loses points on readability of IV-A and on the order and reasoning of the scope part.

## Issues

1. MAJOR (A, coherence). Where: IV-A (sec:obc_map), all seven paragraphs. About 15 of roughly 45 sentences say what Gyorok et al. do (Ex. 1, Eq. 13/Remark 1, Sec. 3.2, Eq. 2, Sec. 4 Eq. 15, Eq. 18, Sec. 6, Sec. 3, Assumption 1, Eqs. 8/11/Lemma 3, Eq. 9, Eq. 13, Sec. 4, Remark 2). The construction reads as a back and forth of "they do X, we do Y" rather than one line of problem, construction and consequence. At one reading, the examiner has to rebuild which of the steps are the gantry construction. Each contrast is justified on its own, but a contrast whose difference is the display itself (Eqs. 9 and 13 before eq:obc_corr, Assumption 1 before eq:obc_coef) could be one "adapted from" clause. Rule cause: SKILL "Order", "Literature positioning sits at the component it contrasts, one sentence per cited method". The cap counts per method, so a subsection that adapts one method at every element passes with a contrast sentence at every step. No rule bounds the total share of literature sentences in a component, or merges contrasts whose difference the display already shows.

2. MINOR (A, structure after the formal statement). Where: IV-C, after the proof. Three paragraphs follow the proof: a one-sentence "Without the correction" paragraph, a 12-sentence scope paragraph, then (after a \todo) the excitation paragraph. The excitation condition is the most consequential limit, since the records are not designed for it, so no true recovery is guaranteed. It comes last. Rule cause: SKILL "Order", Method ("Scope items ... form one scope paragraph after the proof ... the most consequential first"). The rule exists, but "4. Review loop" has no check for the count or order of the scope paragraph, so it did not prevent this.

3. MINOR (B/A, what the Proposition buys). Where: Proposition 1 statement. "For every theta_aug ... has the unique minimiser over xi" also holds without the correction: for fixed theta_aug, the minimiser over xi is then xi* + Phi^+(eps - f_aug), also unique. What OBC adds is that the minimiser does not depend on theta_aug, so all joint minimisers share one xi. The statement leaves this implicit, and only the sentence after the proof makes the contrast. An examiner can object that the roadmap's "unique" is not what the statement distinguishes. Rule cause: missing. "Reasons and claims" governs strength, not whether a formal statement states the property that separates the construction from the method it replaces.

4. MINOR (D, offset reason). Where: IV-C scope paragraph, "The offset can still be omitted, because the minimiser of Proposition 1 depends on eps only through Pi eps". The caveat just before it concerns a NETWORK term along f_base. The fact that answers it is that the xi minimiser does not depend on f_aug at all. The dependence on eps through Pi eps is about the true discrepancy, so the stated reason does not answer the caveat it follows, and it is not traced to D-186. Rule cause: SKILL "Reasons and claims" ("When the source of a choice also records a caveat ... the scope states it with the reason the choice still holds here"). The rule does not require that reason to address the caveat's own object, nor to trace to the source or be marked own.

5. MINOR (D, incomplete justification). Where: IV-A, tuple paragraph, "An open-loop simulation would drift on the free axes". The thesis itself uses a closed-loop simulation (III-C) as the other way to get unmeasured states. The reason rejects only the open-loop alternative. Rule cause: SKILL "Rules that produce this style", "Justify only what an examiner would question" (the reason "must hold against every alternative the thesis itself uses elsewhere (a closed-loop simulation in another section is such an alternative)"). The rule names exactly this case, but "4. Review loop" has no item that checks it, so it failed to prevent this.

6. MINOR (E, restatement). Where: (a) lead-in of eq:obc_coef, "which makes the corrected stack orthogonal to it", repeated by the display's fourth line; (b) IV-B, "Before each validation, theta_aux is re-solved from the current network", which repeats theta_aux(theta_aug) and the "where" clause of eq:obc_problem ("solved ... at every evaluation of V"). Only "with the basis of the last epoch" is new. Rule cause: SKILL "Rules that produce this style", "Say each fact once". README "Prose around the math" allows the orthogonality as a display line or a sentence, but its "stated once" is not applied to the lead-in of the same display.

7. MINOR (F, known before new). Where: IV-B naming paragraph, "from the correct and from a detuned start". "Detuned start" is defined in no earlier section; Section III has it only inside a \todo, and Section V defines it. Rule cause: SKILL "Order", "Known before new". The forward term is not carried by the section's one pointer to Experiment design. The rule exists, and the review-loop item "Every argument starts from something already established" did not catch a term in a purpose sentence.
