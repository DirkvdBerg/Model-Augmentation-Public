## Verdict in one paragraph
On the math, the draft does what Maarten asked for. The augmented transition, network, zero start, encoder with its W^b map, both controller loops and the residual form, the m_Delta coordinates, a single identification-problem display with its constraints (Hoekstra Eq. 22 / Drenth Eq. 19 pattern), the selection score, and the S1/S2 objectives of PS2 are all displayed. An examiner can see what is built and what is trained against what. Attribution is careful and the cited passages match the log. The weak points are coherence and the prose around the math. III-C does four jobs. Several displays are followed by four to eight consequence sentences, so parts read as formula-and-reason blocks. The normalisation display reuses $\tilde x$ for a different quantity and does not show how $f_{\mathrm{base}}$ acts in normalised coordinates. The section is about twice its page budget.

## Issues

1. MAJOR, A (one job per subsection). III-C covers four jobs: the closed-loop rollout (eqs. aug_direct_loop, aug_residual_loop), the training coordinates of theta_base (eq. mdelta_map), the identification criterion (eq. aug_loss), and the optimiser, checkpoint selection (eq. aug_val) and model naming. The m_Delta paragraph sits between the residual loop and the criterion that uses both, which breaks the problem, construction, consequence line. Hoekstra 2026 Sec. 5 gives criterion, regularisation, normalisation and initialisation each their own subsection. Fix: split into "closed-loop model" and "identification problem" (coordinates, criterion, optimiser, selection), or move the coordinates to III-A next to f_base. Rule cause: README "Paper structure and ownership" (row III) assigns the optimiser, checkpoint selection and the moved theta_base coordinates to III. Neither the README nor the skill says where these go in the argument, and the "one job per subsection" rule in README "Prose around the math" and SKILL "Order" does not reconcile with that ownership list.

2. MAJOR, D (at most one consequence after a display). The section never shows how $f_{\mathrm{base}}$ (RK4 of eq. eom in physical units) acts on the normalised state, which Hoekstra 2026 Eq. (28a,b) displays and the rubric names. It also leaves open whether $u$ or $u_{\mathrm{act}}$ is normalised and what $h_{\mathrm{base}}=P^\top q$ is in normalised coordinates. Fix: use a separate symbol for the normalised state and display $\bar f_{\mathrm{base}}=T_x\bigl(f_{\mathrm{base}}(T_x^{-1}\tilde x+\mu_x,\cdot)-\mu_x\bigr)$ in one line. Rule cause: README Math standard item 4 requires normalisation to be "displayed" but not the normalised maps. The SKILL "Notation" rule only covers renaming across sections, not reuse of one symbol for two quantities within a section.
