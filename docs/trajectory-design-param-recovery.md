# Trajectory Design for Parameter Recovery

**Date**: 2026-04-29
**Status**: Design decisions — pending implementation in `export_lpv_multi_traj.m` and `train_param_recovery.py`

---

## 1. Context

The parameter recovery training (`train_param_recovery.py`) fits 13 physical parameters of the LPV-LFR gantry model to simulated trajectory data using gradient-based BPTT. The trajectory set must be designed such that every trainable parameter produces a distinguishable, non-collinear effect on the position output. This document records all design decisions made for the trajectory set, including the rationale, the García reference, and planned extensions.

**Trainable parameters (13):**

| Symbol | Description | Units |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| kb1, kb2 | Stiffness of flexible joints 1 and 2 | Nm/rad |
| cg1, cg2 | Viscous friction of actuators X1 and X2 | N/(m/s) |
| cy | Viscous friction of Y-axis | N/(m/s) |
| cb1, cb2 | Viscous friction of flexible joints 1 and 2 | Nm/(rad/s) |
| mh | Mass of payload (Y-axis) — sole LPV scheduling parameter | kg |
| m1, m2 | Mass of actuators X1 and X2 | kg |
| mb | Mass of cross-arm | kg |
| Jb, Jh | Rotary inertia of cross-arm and payload | kg·m² |

**Fixed buffers — not trained:**

| Symbol | Value | Why fixed |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Lb | 0.725 m | Defines the coordinate transformation Θ = (X1−X2)/Lb — making it trainable contaminates the coordinate definition itself and makes it architecturally invasive throughout the data pipeline. Also precisely known from hardware geometry. |
| d | 0.1 m | CoM offset of payload from cross-arm centre. Currently fixed; **candidate for adding as trainable parameter** — see Section 5. |

**Not modelled (architectural decision):**

Coulomb friction (cc1, cc2, ccy) is absent from the LFR state-space structure — no sign(ẋ) terms. These three parameters are not identified and no trajectory design targets them.

## 2. Reference: García's Identification Experiments

García et al. (2013), Section 2.4, identifies 14 physical parameters via 7 sequential experiments on the physical hardware. Their approach is algebraic and sequential — each experiment zeros out terms so parameters can be read from closed-form expressions. Our gradient-based approach does not need this isolation, but the motion modes García chose are grounded in the natural decoupled coordinates of the system (Y, X, Θ) and carry over directly.

### What is NOT useful from García for our approach

| García element | Reason not applicable |
|---|---|
| Coulomb friction experiments (Exp 1, 3) | Not modelled in LFR |
| Constant-velocity regime design (Exp 1, 3) | Designed to separate viscous from Coulomb algebraically — not needed without Coulomb |
| Constant-acceleration regime design (Exp 2, 4) | Designed for closed-form algebraic recovery — gradient approach handles smooth profiles |
| d identification via F1−F2 asymmetry (Exp 2) | Requires force measurement; currently position output only |
| Static angle hold design (Exp 5) | Zeros out damping and inertia to read stiffness algebraically — not needed for gradient |
| Constant rotational speed design (Exp 6) | Zeros out inertia to read damping algebraically — not needed |
| Bar inertia assumption Eq.(21): Jb=mbLb²/12 | We train Jb and Jh as free parameters — no assumption needed |

### What IS useful from García

The motion modes themselves: Y-axis motion, synchronized X (X1=X2), anti-symmetric X (X1=−X2), and varying Y position for LPV coupling. The specific regime execution (constant velocity, static hold) does not carry over; the mode decomposition does.

## 3. Current Trajectory Set: T1–T6

Generated by `Matlab-scripts/export_lpv_multi_traj.m`. All trajectories use smooth third-order (jerk-limited) set-point profiles. Sampling rate: 20 kHz.

### T1 — Y sweep, conservative

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | Y: 0.3 → −0.3 m |
| vmax_Y | 1.0 m/s |
| amax_Y | 10 m/s² |
| X | stationary |

**Parameters targeted**: cy, mh

**Justification**: At these dynamics, cy·v/(mh·a) = 10×1.0/(10.1×10) = **0.099**. The friction force is ~10% of the inertial force — both cy and mh contribute meaningfully to the position output. This is the friction-dominant Y regime.

**García analog**: Exp 1 (Y constant velocity) + Exp 2 (Y constant acceleration) — combined into one smooth trajectory.

---

### T6 — Y sweep, aggressive

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | Y: 0.3 → −0.3 m |
| vmax_Y | 2.0 m/s (hardware max) |
| amax_Y | 50 m/s² (hardware max) |
| X | stationary |

**Parameters targeted**: mh (primary), cy (secondary)

**Justification**: At hardware-maximum dynamics, cy·v/(mh·a) = 10×2.0/(10.1×50) = **0.040**. The friction force is only 4% of the inertial force — mh dominates. T1 and T6 together provide a 2.5× contrast in the cy/mh ratio. A model with cy too high and mh too low can fit T1 reasonably but will fail on T6, and vice versa. Both trajectories are required to break the cy/mh collinearity. Neither alone is sufficient.

**García analog**: Same as T1.

---

### T2 — X symmetric at Y = 0.3 m

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | X1=X2: 0 → +150 mm |
| vmax_X | 1.5 m/s |
| amax_X | 20 m/s² |
| Y | held at 0.3 m |

**Parameters targeted**: cg1+cg2, m1+m2+mb+mh, mh (via LPV coupling)

**Justification**: Synchronized X motion (X1=X2, Θ≈0) excites the translational X dynamics. At Y=0.3 m, the LPV coupling term M_s[0,1] = (m1−m2)Lb/2 − mh·Y = (m1−m2)·0.3625 − 10.1·0.3 = **−3.03 kg·m** (at nominal, with m1≈m2). This is the maximum coupling in the operating range — mh contributes strongly to the cross-inertia term. The X motion at this Y position makes mh visible in the X equation.

**García analog**: Exp 3 (X synchronized constant velocity) + Exp 4 (X synchronized constant acceleration).

---

### T3 — X symmetric at Y = 0.0 m

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | X1=X2: 0 → +150 mm (identical to T2) |
| vmax_X | 1.5 m/s |
| amax_X | 20 m/s² |
| Y | held at 0.0 m (settle move prepended automatically) |

**Parameters targeted**: cg1+cg2, m1+m2+mb

**Justification**: Identical motion to T2 but at Y=0. The LPV coupling term M_s[0,1] = −mh·0 = **0** — mh does not appear in the X translational equation. The contrast between T2 (mh coupling active) and T3 (mh coupling zero) is the mechanism that breaks the collinearity between mh and (m1+m2+mb). Without T3, mh and the other masses appear only as a sum in the X equations and cannot be separated.

**García analog**: Same as T2, at a different Y position.

---

### T4 — X anti-symmetric at Y = 0.2 m

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | X1: 0 → +35 mm, X2: 0 → −35 mm (differential = 70 mm) |
| vmax_X | 0.5 m/s |
| amax_X | 8 m/s² |
| Y | held at 0.2 m |

**Parameters targeted**: kb1+kb2, cb1+cb2, Jb+Jh

**Justification**: The only trajectory in the set that excites the rotational mode (X1=−X2 → Θ≠0). kb, cb, Jb, Jh appear exclusively in the Θ equation and have zero gradient in every other trajectory regardless of dynamics or multisine excitation. This trajectory is structurally irreplaceable. The 70 mm differential gives Θ = 70/725 = **0.097 rad**, just within the ±0.1 rad mechanical limit. Low dynamics (amax=8 m/s²) chosen to keep Θ within limits given the large restoring torque: kb_sum·Θ = 3975·0.097 = 386 Nm.

**Note**: The current smooth profile barely excites the rotational resonance (~5 Hz, ~32 rad/s). A resonance-targeted multisine on the (F1−F2) channel would directly improve kb/J separation — see Section 6.

**García analog**: Exp 5 (static stiffness) + Exp 6 (constant rotational speed) + Exp 7 (constant rotational acceleration) — all three collapsed into one dynamic trajectory.

---

### T5 — X symmetric + Y sweep simultaneously

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | X1=X2: 0 → +100 mm, Y: 0.2 → −0.2 m (simultaneous) |
| vmax_X | 1.0 m/s |
| amax_X | 15 m/s² |
| vmax_Y | 1.0 m/s |
| amax_Y | 20 m/s² |

**Parameters targeted**: mh over continuous Y range, M1(Y) = −mh·Y schedule

**Justification**: T2 and T3 identify mh at two discrete Y positions (0.3 and 0.0). T5 traces the full LPV coupling schedule by varying Y continuously while X accelerates. This provides gradient signal for mh across the entire scheduling variable range rather than at two points only. This trajectory has no García analog — García's model has no scheduling variable.

## 4. Proposed New Trajectories: T7 and T8

The training loop uses `TRAIN_SEGMENTS_PER_EPOCH = 8`. With 6 trajectories this leaves segments unevenly distributed. Adding 2 trajectories to reach 8 total, with one segment sampled from each trajectory per epoch (see Section 7), gives balanced coverage. The 2 new trajectories are designed to maximise simultaneous parameter excitation — filling the gap that no existing trajectory has all parameters active in the same window.

**Gap in T1–T6**: No trajectory combines anti-symmetric X motion (Θ excitation) with Y motion simultaneously. This means kb, cb, Jb, Jh (only active in T4) and cy, mh, d (only active in Y trajectories) never produce gradient in the same training window.

---

### T7 — X anti-symmetric + Y sweep simultaneously

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | X1: 0 → +35 mm, X2: 0 → −35 mm, Y: 0.3 → −0.3 m (simultaneous) |
| vmax_X | 0.5 m/s |
| amax_X | 8 m/s² |
| vmax_Y | 1.5 m/s |
| amax_Y | 20 m/s² |
| Y_initial | 0.3 m |

**Parameters with non-zero gradient**: all 13 trainable parameters + d

**Justification**: Combines T4's rotational excitation with a full Y sweep. All three DOFs (X translational, Θ rotational, Y payload) are active simultaneously. The critical new capability: Y acceleration while Θ moves creates the mh·d·Ÿ coupling torque — the first trajectory where d is observable from position output (via the resulting Θ perturbation). This is the highest information-density trajectory in the set.

**Physical constraint**: Θ = 70/725 = 0.097 rad — same as T4, within ±0.1 rad limit. The simultaneous Y motion does not increase differential X displacement, so the rotation limit is not additionally stressed.

---

### T8 — X symmetric + X anti-symmetric perturbation + Y sweep

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Motion | X1=X2: 0 → +100 mm (symmetric), X anti perturbation: ±20 mm, Y: 0.2 → −0.2 m |
| vmax_X | 1.0 m/s |
| amax_X | 12 m/s² |
| vmax_Y | 1.5 m/s |
| amax_Y | 25 m/s² |
| Y_initial | 0.2 m |

**Parameters with non-zero gradient**: all 13 trainable parameters + d

**Justification**: Provides a second all-parameters trajectory at a different operating point from T7 — different Y range (0.2→−0.2 vs 0.3→−0.3), different X symmetric/anti-symmetric amplitude ratio, different dynamic levels. Two different all-parameters operating points break remaining collinearities that a single T7 cannot resolve. The X_anti perturbation is kept small (20 mm → Θ = 40/725 = 0.055 rad) to stay well within the rotation limit while still exciting the rotational mode.

## 5. Adding d as a Trainable Parameter

**What d is**: The distance between the centre of mass of the payload and the centre of the cross-arm. Currently fixed at 0.1 m in `lfr_param_block.py` as `_d`.

**Where d appears in the model**:
- Rotary inertia: mh·d² in M_s[1,1]
- Y-to-Θ coupling force: mh·d·Ÿ in the rotational force vector

**Why d is identifiable from position output**: mh appears independently in the Y equation (Eq. 17): mh·Ÿ = Fy − cy·Ẏ. Once mh is identified from Y dynamics, the product mh·d can be separated from the Θ response to Y acceleration. d = (mh·d) / mh. This separation requires a trajectory where Y accelerates while Θ is observed — which T7 provides.

**Why Lb is NOT added for comparison**: Lb defines the coordinate transformation Θ = (X1−X2)/Lb. Making Lb trainable means the coordinate mapping itself becomes a function of a trainable parameter, requiring the full data pipeline to recompute Θ at every gradient step. Additionally, Lb is a precisely known geometric constant measurable with a ruler. d, by contrast, depends on payload mounting and mass distribution and is genuinely uncertain between configurations.

**Implementation**: Add `d` to `_PARAM_NAMES` in `lfr_param_block.py`, initialise with a detuned value, and include it in `_build_matrices` wherever `_d` currently appears as a fixed buffer.

## 6. Multisine Excitation

### Injection point

Multisines are injected as **force perturbations on the feedforward channel** (F1, F2, Fy) — not on the position reference. This bypasses the position control loop filtering, directly exciting the mechanical dynamics. The position references remain smooth third-order profiles, so hardware safety constraints on position are not affected by the multisine.

### Strategy: add to all 8 trajectories, filter by physical constraints

Apply multisines to all 8 trajectories and filter out or reduce amplitude where hardware constraints are violated.

**Per-channel design:**

| Channel | Target trajectories | Frequency content | Rationale |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|-----------|
| Fy | T1, T5, T6, T7, T8 | Broadband 0.5–20 Hz | Separates cy (velocity-dependent, lower frequencies) from mh (acceleration-dependent, higher frequencies) in frequency domain |
| (F1+F2) | T2, T3, T5, T8 | Broadband 0.5–20 Hz | Separates cg1+cg2 from translational mass sum in frequency domain |
| (F1−F2) | T4, T7, T8 | Resonance-targeted ~3–10 Hz | Directly excites the rotational resonance (~5 Hz, ω≈32 rad/s). Stiffness kb_sum dominates below resonance, inertia Jb+Jh dominates above — resonance is the most sensitive point for their separation |

**Physical constraint — rotation limit on (F1−F2) channel:**

T4 base trajectory already uses Θ = 0.097 rad, within 3% of the ±0.1 rad mechanical limit. A (F1−F2) multisine on T4 must have a very small amplitude to avoid exceeding this limit. Options:
1. Reduce T4 base amplitude (e.g. X_anti = 25 mm → Θ = 50/725 = 0.069 rad) to create headroom for the multisine.
2. Accept that T7 carries the resonance-targeted anti-symmetric excitation and T4 remains a clean base trajectory.

**For all other channels**: compute expected peak position deviation as approximately F_amplitude / (m_effective · ω²) and verify against travel limits before finalising amplitudes.

**Note on off-axis multisines**: Adding Fy multisine to X-only trajectories (T2, T3) or (F1+F2) multisine to Y-only trajectories (T1, T6) creates small off-axis motion that the position controller partially rejects. This is acceptable and adds additional cross-channel gradient signal for parameters that appear in coupling terms.

### Multisine design parameters (to be determined from system identification lecture review)

A dedicated session has been prepared to extract the following from system identification literature:
- Frequency grid design (harmonic vs non-harmonic, frequency resolution)
- Amplitude spectrum shaping
- Phase design (Schroeder phase for crest factor minimisation)
- Number of periods and transient handling
- MIMO excitation strategy for simultaneous multi-channel injection

## 7. Sampling Strategy Change

**Current**: `TRAIN_SEGMENTS_PER_EPOCH = 8`, segments sampled randomly across the 6 trajectories. Some trajectories (especially T4) may not be sampled in a given epoch.

**Problem**: Random sampling means rotational parameters (kb, cb, Jb, Jh) receive gradient only when T4 is sampled. With 6 trajectories and 8 segments per epoch, T4 has expected coverage of 8/6 ≈ 1.3 segments/epoch but with high variance — it can be missed entirely in some epochs.

**Change**: With 8 trajectories and 8 segments per epoch, sample **exactly one segment from each trajectory per epoch**. This guarantees:
- Every parameter receives non-zero gradient every epoch
- T4, T7, T8 (rotational and all-parameters trajectories) contribute every epoch
- The 8 segments give balanced Fisher information coverage per epoch rather than in expectation only

**Implementation**: Replace the random segment sampler in `train_param_recovery.py` with a round-robin or fixed-one-per-trajectory sampler.

## 8. Force Logging (Future Consideration)

The LFR model is a plant model: it takes forces (F1, F2, Fy) as inputs and produces positions (X1, X2, Y) as outputs. Currently, the forces used as model inputs are the commanded/simulated forces from the Simulink controller output.

In the real hardware system, actual forces can be estimated from motor currents via the motor force constant. Logging actual forces and using them directly as model inputs (rather than commanded forces) would improve identification because:
- Actual forces may differ from commanded forces due to amplifier dynamics, saturation, and current control bandwidth
- Force measurements provide additional output channels: fitting F1+F2 and F1−F2 as outputs alongside positions dramatically improves the Fisher information condition number
- F1−F2 asymmetry during Y acceleration directly gives mh·d·Ÿ — enabling d identification by García's algebraic method in addition to the gradient-based approach

This is not required for the simulation-based parameter recovery but should be considered for real hardware identification.

## 9. Future: Jacobian-Based Trajectory Validation (Optimal Experiment Design)

Before committing to hardware experiments, the trajectory set should be validated using the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM):

$$F = \sum_{i=1}^{8} J_i^\top J_i, \quad J_i = \frac{\partial \hat{y}_i}{\partial \theta}$$

where $\hat{y}_i$ is the simulated position output for trajectory $i$ and $\theta$ is the full parameter vector.

**What this tells you:**
- **Rank of F**: must equal the number of trainable parameters (13 or 14 with d). Rank deficiency means at least one parameter is structurally unidentifiable from the trajectory set.
- **Eigenvalues of F**: small eigenvalues indicate poorly identified parameter directions (collinear gradients). The eigenvectors identify which parameter combinations are confounded.
- **Condition number**: large condition number means some parameters are identified orders of magnitude worse than others — a poorly designed trajectory set.

**Criterion**: E-optimality — maximise the minimum eigenvalue λ_min(F). This ensures the worst-identified parameter direction is as informative as possible, which is the right criterion when all 13/14 parameters must be recovered.

**Practical implementation**: PyTorch autograd already computes the Jacobian through the BPTT computation graph. Use `torch.autograd.functional.jacobian` on the simulate function evaluated at the true parameter values. Build F and check its spectrum. If λ_min is near zero, identify the corresponding eigenvector to find the confounded parameter pair, then design a trajectory that breaks that specific collinearity.

**Greedy trajectory selection**: Generate a large candidate pool of trajectories (varying Y positions, amplitudes, mode combinations). Select trajectories one at a time by choosing the candidate that maximally increases λ_min(F) at each step. Stop when the condition number is acceptable or improvement saturates.
