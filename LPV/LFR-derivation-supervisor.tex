\section{Purpose and Scope}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This note derives an exact continuous-time LPV-LFR realization of the
dual-gantry baseline model. The generic LPV-LFR framework is taken from
Drenth's thesis \cite{drenth2025thesis}. The particular latent-variable
construction, the constant interconnection matrices, and the reduction of the
well-posedness problem to invertibility of the gantry inertia matrix are
plant-specific steps for the present dual-gantry model.

The derivation is organized around one exactness requirement: after eliminating
the latent loop, the LPV-LFR must collapse back to the original MATLAB-derived
continuous-time state-space model.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Starting Point: Dual-Gantry CT Model}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In logical coordinates
\[
q = [X,\Theta,Y]^\top \in \R^3,
\]
the dual-gantry equations of motion are
\begin{equation}
    M(Y)\,\ddot{q}(t) + C\,\dot{q}(t) + K\,q(t) = f_\ell(t),
    \label{eqs:eom}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    M(Y) =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        m_1 + m_2 + m_b + m_h &
        \dfrac{(m_1-m_2)L_b}{2} - m_h Y &
        0 \\
        \dfrac{(m_1-m_2)L_b}{2} - m_h Y &
        J_b + J_h + \dfrac{(m_1+m_2)L_b^2}{4} + m_h d^2 + m_h Y^2 &
        -m_h d \\
        0 & -m_h d & m_h
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:mass}
\end{equation}
Here $C \in \R^{3\times3}$ and $K \in \R^{3\times3}$ denote the constant
damping and stiffness matrices of the baseline gantry model.

This model is second order in the configuration coordinates $q$. The standard
first-order state choice is therefore
\begin{equation}
    x = \begin{bmatrix} q \\ \dot{q} \end{bmatrix} \in \R^6,
    \label{eqs:state}
\end{equation}
which stores the positions and velocities needed to evolve the second-order
mechanics.

Let
\begin{equation}
    u := f_\ell
    \label{eqs:input}
\end{equation}
denote the generalized logical input. The MATLAB-derived continuous-time
state-space model is then
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x}(t) = A_c(Y)\,x(t) + B_c(Y)\,u(t),
    \qquad
    y(t) = C_c\,x(t),
    \label{eqs:ctss}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    A_c(Y) =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & I_3 \\
        -M(Y)^{-1}K & -M(Y)^{-1}C
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    B_c(Y) =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 \\
        M(Y)^{-1}
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    C_c = \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:ctmats}
\end{equation}
This is the target continuous-time model that the LPV-LFR realization must
recover after elimination of the latent loop.

The output choice is
\begin{equation}
    y = q.
    \label{eqs:yq}
\end{equation}
This matches the MATLAB output map, because
\begin{equation}
    C_c x
    =
    \begin{bmatrix} I_3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} q \\ \dot{q} \end{bmatrix}
    = q.
    \label{eqs:ccx}
\end{equation}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Why the Model Is Rational and Why an LPV-LFR Is Needed}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the present single-scheduler setting, an affine dependence on $Y$ means that
each matrix entry depends linearly on $Y$, while a rational dependence on $Y$
means that matrix entries are ratios of polynomials in $Y$
\cite{drenth2025thesis}.

The matrices in \eqref{eqs:ctmats} contain $M(Y)^{-1}$. Since $M(Y)$ is
polynomial in $Y$, its inverse satisfies
\begin{equation}
    M(Y)^{-1} = \frac{\adj(M(Y))}{\det(M(Y))},
    \label{eqs:adjdet}
\end{equation}
where $\adj(M(Y))$ and $\det(M(Y))$ are polynomial in $Y$. Therefore the
entries of $M(Y)^{-1}$ are rational functions of $Y$ wherever $M(Y)$ is
invertible. It follows that the exact continuous-time dual-gantry model is
rational in $Y$, not affine in $Y$.

This is the reason to seek a rational LPV-LFR realization: the exact
$Y$-dependence can then be represented through a constant interconnection and a
scheduling block, rather than by introducing an unnecessary affine
overbounding.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Drenth's CT LPV-LFR Target Form}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Drenth defines a continuous-time LPV-LFR as the interconnection between a
constant matrix $G$ and a scheduling block $\Delta(p)$
\cite{drenth2025thesis}. The equations are
\begin{align}
    \dot{x} &= A x + B_w w + B_u u,
    \label{eqs:lfrstate} \\
    z &= C_z x + D_{zw} w + D_{zu} u,
    \label{eqs:lfrz} \\
    y &= C_y x + D_{yw} w + D_{yu} u,
    \label{eqs:lfry} \\
    w &= \Delta(p) z,
    \label{eqs:lfrw}
\end{align}
with constant interconnection matrix
\begin{equation}
    G =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        A & B_w & B_u \\
        C_z & D_{zw} & D_{zu} \\
        C_y & D_{yw} & D_{yu}
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:G}
\end{equation}
All scheduling dependence is carried by $\Delta(p)$; the blocks of $G$ are
constant.

Drenth also allows repeated block-diagonal scheduling structures of the form
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(p) =
    \diag\!\bigl(p_1 I_{\eta_1}, \dots, p_{n_p} I_{\eta_{n_p}}\bigr),
    \label{eqs:deltageneric}
\end{equation}
which is the structural template used below.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Scheduling Choice and Repeated-Delta Structure}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The physical scheduling quantity in the dual-gantry model is the single
coordinate $Y$. Although $Y^2$ appears in \eqref{eqs:mass}, it is not
introduced as a second independent scheduling variable. This follows the
motivation in Drenth's discussion of rational versus affine embeddings
\cite{drenth2025thesis}: repeated use of one physical scheduler preserves the
coupling between $Y$ and $Y^2$, whereas treating them as independent scheduling
variables would overbound that coupling.

Accordingly, the plant-specific realization below uses repeated action of the
same scalar scheduler $Y$ inside $\Delta(Y)$, rather than introducing multiple
independent scheduling coordinates. In the notation of
\eqref{eqs:deltageneric}, the chosen realization has one independent scheduler,
$p_1 = Y$, with repetition count fixed later by the chosen loop signals.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Plant-Specific Latent Variables and Loop Signals}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Define the generalized force
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{gen}} = [-K,-C]\,x + u.
    \label{eqs:fgen}
\end{equation}
Here $[-K,-C]$ denotes the horizontal concatenation of $-K$ and $-C$.
Now define the plant-specific latent vectors
\begin{equation}
    v = M(Y)^{-1} f_{\mathrm{gen}},
    \qquad
    v_1 = Y v,
    \qquad
    v_2 = Y^2 v = Y v_1.
    \label{eqs:vv1v2}
\end{equation}
Here $v$, $v_1$, and $v_2$ are whole vectors in $\R^3$. In logical coordinates,
\begin{equation}
    v = \ddot{q} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \ddot{X} \\
        \ddot{\Theta} \\
        \ddot{Y}
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:vlogic}
\end{equation}
The vectors $v_1$ and $v_2$ are derived latent vectors, not physical states and
not components of $v$. They are internal helper variables introduced for this
particular dual-gantry realization; Drenth's framework supplies the generic
roles of $z$ and $w$, but not this plant-specific choice of $v$, $v_1$, and
$v_2$.

Decompose the inertia matrix as
\begin{equation}
    M(Y) = M_0 + Y M_1 + Y^2 M_2,
    \label{eqs:decomp}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
    M_0 &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        m_1 + m_2 + m_b + m_h &
        \dfrac{(m_1-m_2)L_b}{2} &
        0 \\
        \dfrac{(m_1-m_2)L_b}{2} &
        J_b + J_h + \dfrac{(m_1+m_2)L_b^2}{4} + m_h d^2 &
        -m_h d \\
        0 & -m_h d & m_h
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eqs:M0} \\
    M_1 &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & -m_h & 0 \\
        -m_h & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eqs:M1} \\
    M_2 &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & m_h & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:M2}
\end{align}

Choose the loop signals
\begin{equation}
    z =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v \\
        v_1
    \end{bmatrix}
    \in \R^6,
    \qquad
    w =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v_1 \\
        v_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    \in \R^6.
    \label{eqs:zw}
\end{equation}
Because $v \in \R^3$ and $v_1 \in \R^3$, both $z$ and $w$ lie in $\R^6$. This
fixes the repeated block dimension in the chosen realization. With
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(Y) =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        Y I_3 & 0 \\
        0 & Y I_3
    \end{bmatrix}
    = Y I_6,
    \label{eqs:Delta}
\end{equation}
one obtains
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(Y) z
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        Yv \\
        Yv_1
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v_1 \\
        v_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    = w.
    \label{eqs:Deltaz}
\end{equation}
Thus the chosen repeated block is one independent scheduler $Y$ repeated over
six latent channels. No minimality claim is made for this choice.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Derivation of the Constant LPV-LFR Interconnection}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Drenth's framework fixes the block structure \eqref{eqs:lfrstate}--\eqref{eqs:G}.
The explicit coefficients below are obtained by plant-specific coefficient
matching once the dual-gantry equations have been rewritten in terms of $x$,
$w$, and $u$.

\subsection{Rewrite the Acceleration Equation}

From \eqref{eqs:vv1v2},
\[
M(Y)\,v = f_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]
Using \eqref{eqs:decomp},
\[
(M_0 + Y M_1 + Y^2 M_2)\,v = f_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]
Using $v_1 = Yv$ and $v_2 = Y^2 v$, this becomes
\begin{equation}
    M_0 v = f_{\mathrm{gen}} - M_1 v_1 - M_2 v_2.
    \label{eqs:M0v}
\end{equation}
Substitute \eqref{eqs:fgen}:
\begin{equation}
    M_0 v = [-K,-C]\,x + u - M_1 v_1 - M_2 v_2.
    \label{eqs:M0vsub}
\end{equation}
Multiply by $M_0^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
    v = M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x - M_0^{-1}M_1 v_1 - M_0^{-1}M_2 v_2 + M_0^{-1}u.
    \label{eqs:vexpanded}
\end{equation}
Since
\[
w =
\begin{bmatrix}
    v_1 \\
    v_2
\end{bmatrix},
\]
the terms involving $v_1$ and $v_2$ can be written as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    w
    =
    -M_0^{-1}M_1 v_1 - M_0^{-1}M_2 v_2.
    \label{eqs:wrewrite}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
    v =
    M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    w
    +
    M_0^{-1}u.
    \label{eqs:vxwu}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derive the State Equation}

From the state definition \eqref{eqs:state},
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \dot{q} \\
        \ddot{q}
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:xdotdef}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eqs:vlogic}, $\ddot{q} = v$, so
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \dot{q} \\
        v
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:xdotv}
\end{equation}
The upper block is
\begin{equation}
    \dot{q}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & I_3
    \end{bmatrix}
    x,
    \label{eqs:qdotselector}
\end{equation}
because
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
    0 & I_3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
    q \\
    \dot{q}
\end{bmatrix}
= \dot{q}.
\]

For the lower block, rewrite the first term in \eqref{eqs:vxwu} as
\begin{equation}
    M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}K & -M_0^{-1}C
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:blocksplit}
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eqs:vxwu} becomes
\begin{equation}
    v
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}K & -M_0^{-1}C
    \end{bmatrix}
    x
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    w
    +
    M_0^{-1}u.
    \label{eqs:vfinalblock}
\end{equation}
Stacking \eqref{eqs:qdotselector} and \eqref{eqs:vfinalblock} gives
\begin{equation}
    \dot{x}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & I_3 \\
        -M_0^{-1}K & -M_0^{-1}C
    \end{bmatrix}
    x
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0 \\
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    w
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 \\
        M_0^{-1}
    \end{bmatrix}
    u.
    \label{eqs:stateblocks}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
    A =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & I_3 \\
        -M_0^{-1}K & -M_0^{-1}C
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    B_w =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0 \\
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    B_u =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 \\
        M_0^{-1}
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:ABmats}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derive the Loop-Output Equation}

By definition,
\begin{equation}
    z =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v \\
        v_1
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:zdef}
\end{equation}
The first block is already given by \eqref{eqs:vxwu}. For the second block,
because
\[
w =
\begin{bmatrix}
    v_1 \\
    v_2
\end{bmatrix},
\]
the first half of $w$ is exactly $v_1$, so
\begin{equation}
    v_1 =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}
    w.
    \label{eqs:v1selector}
\end{equation}
There is no direct dependence of $v_1$ on $x$ or $u$. Hence
\begin{equation}
    z
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        M_0^{-1}[-K,-C] \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix}
    x
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2 \\
        I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}
    w
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        M_0^{-1} \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix}
    u.
    \label{eqs:loopblocks}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
    C_z =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        M_0^{-1}[-K,-C] \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    D_{zw} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -M_0^{-1}M_1 & -M_0^{-1}M_2 \\
        I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    D_{zu} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        M_0^{-1} \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:CDloop}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derive the Output Equation}

The chosen output is $y=q$. Since
\[
x =
\begin{bmatrix}
    q \\
    \dot{q}
\end{bmatrix},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
    y =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}
    x.
    \label{eqs:yfromx}
\end{equation}
There is no direct dependence on $w$ or $u$, so
\begin{equation}
    C_y =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    D_{yw} = 0,
    \qquad
    D_{yu} = 0.
    \label{eqs:Cy}
\end{equation}

The matrices \eqref{eqs:ABmats}, \eqref{eqs:CDloop}, and \eqref{eqs:Cy} define
the constant interconnection matrix $G$ in \eqref{eqs:G}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Exact Recovery of the Original CT Model}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To verify exactness, substitute the loop relation
\[
w = \Delta(Y) z = Y z
\]
into the LPV-LFR. Since
\[
z =
\begin{bmatrix}
    v \\
    v_1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
w =
\begin{bmatrix}
    w_1 \\
    w_2
\end{bmatrix},
\]
one obtains
\begin{equation}
    w_1 = Yv,
    \qquad
    w_2 = Yv_1 = Y^2 v.
    \label{eqs:wcollapse}
\end{equation}

Substitute \eqref{eqs:wcollapse} into the first block of
\eqref{eqs:loopblocks}:
\begin{equation}
    v
    =
    M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x
    - M_0^{-1}M_1\,(Yv)
    - M_0^{-1}M_2\,(Y^2 v)
    + M_0^{-1}u.
    \label{eqs:collapsev}
\end{equation}
Multiply by $M_0$:
\begin{equation}
    M_0 v
    =
    [-K,-C]\,x + u - Y M_1 v - Y^2 M_2 v.
    \label{eqs:collapseM0}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eqs:fgen} and collecting terms on the left gives
\begin{equation}
    M_0 v + Y M_1 v + Y^2 M_2 v = f_{\mathrm{gen}}.
    \label{eqs:collapsecollect}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eqs:decomp}, this is
\begin{equation}
    M(Y)\,v = f_{\mathrm{gen}}.
    \label{eqs:collapseMY}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
    v = M(Y)^{-1} f_{\mathrm{gen}}.
    \label{eqs:collapsevinv}
\end{equation}

Now return to the state equation. From \eqref{eqs:xdotv},
\[
\dot{x} =
\begin{bmatrix}
    \dot{q} \\
    v
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Using \eqref{eqs:collapsevinv} and \eqref{eqs:fgen},
\begin{align}
    \dot{x}
    &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \dot{q} \\
        M(Y)^{-1}\bigl([-K,-C]\,x + u\bigr)
    \end{bmatrix}
    \notag\\
    &=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & I_3 \\
        -M(Y)^{-1}K & -M(Y)^{-1}C
    \end{bmatrix}
    x
    +
    \begin{bmatrix}
        0 \\
        M(Y)^{-1}
    \end{bmatrix}
    u
    \notag\\
    &= A_c(Y)\,x + B_c(Y)\,u.
    \label{eqs:recoverstate}
\end{align}
The output equation is unchanged:
\begin{equation}
    y =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix}
    x
    = C_c x
    = q.
    \label{eqs:recoveroutput}
\end{equation}
Therefore the LPV-LFR collapses exactly to the original MATLAB-derived
continuous-time state-space model \eqref{eqs:ctss}--\eqref{eqs:ctmats}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Well-Posedness}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Drenth's Exact Criterion}

For a continuous-time LPV-LFR, Drenth's exact well-posedness criterion is that
\begin{equation}
    I - D_{zw}\Delta(p)
    \quad \text{is nonsingular for all admissible } p.
    \label{eqs:drenthcriterion}
\end{equation}
For the present realization, this becomes
\begin{equation}
    I - D_{zw}\Delta(Y)
    \quad \text{is nonsingular for all admissible } Y.
    \label{eqs:presentcriterion}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, substituting $w = \Delta(Y) z$ into \eqref{eqs:lfrz} gives the
generic loop equation
\begin{equation}
    (I - D_{zw}\Delta(Y))\,z = C_z x + D_{zu}u.
    \label{eqs:genericloop}
\end{equation}
For fixed $x$, $u$, and $Y$, nonsingularity of $I-D_{zw}\Delta(Y)$ is
equivalent to unique solvability of \eqref{eqs:genericloop}, and hence to
unique solvability of the internal loop variables $z$ and
$w=\Delta(Y)z$.

\subsection{Plant-Specific Reduction}

Using \eqref{eqs:CDloop} and \eqref{eqs:Delta},
\begin{equation}
    D_{zw}\Delta(Y)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        -Y M_0^{-1}M_1 & -Y M_0^{-1}M_2 \\
        Y I_3 & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eqs:DzwDelta}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
    I - D_{zw}\Delta(Y)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I_3 + Y M_0^{-1}M_1 & Y M_0^{-1}M_2 \\
        -Y I_3 & I_3
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:IDzwDelta}
\end{equation}
Also,
\begin{equation}
    C_z x + D_{zu}u
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x + M_0^{-1}u \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:rhsloop}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eqs:zw}, \eqref{eqs:IDzwDelta}, and \eqref{eqs:rhsloop}
into \eqref{eqs:genericloop} yields
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        I_3 + Y M_0^{-1}M_1 & Y M_0^{-1}M_2 \\
        -Y I_3 & I_3
    \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v \\
        v_1
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x + M_0^{-1}u \\
        0
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eqs:blockloop}
\end{equation}
The two block equations are
\begin{align}
    v + Y M_0^{-1}M_1 v + Y M_0^{-1}M_2 v_1
    &= M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x + M_0^{-1}u,
    \label{eqs:block1} \\
    -Yv + v_1 &= 0.
    \label{eqs:block2}
\end{align}
From \eqref{eqs:block2},
\begin{equation}
    v_1 = Yv.
    \label{eqs:v1Yv}
\end{equation}
Substitute \eqref{eqs:v1Yv} into \eqref{eqs:block1}:
\begin{equation}
    v + Y M_0^{-1}M_1 v + Y^2 M_0^{-1}M_2 v
    = M_0^{-1}[-K,-C]\,x + M_0^{-1}u.
    \label{eqs:vreduced1}
\end{equation}
Multiply by $M_0$:
\begin{equation}
    M_0 v + Y M_1 v + Y^2 M_2 v = [-K,-C]\,x + u = f_{\mathrm{gen}}.
    \label{eqs:vreduced2}
\end{equation}
Thus the generic loop equation reduces to
\begin{equation}
    M(Y)\,v = f_{\mathrm{gen}},
    \qquad
    v_1 = Yv.
    \label{eqs:finalreduction}
\end{equation}
This reduction is specific to the chosen dual-gantry realization. It is not a
generic LPV-LFR fact in isolation.

\subsection{Unique Solvability and Well-Posedness}

Fix admissible $x$, $u$, and $Y$. After the reduction
\eqref{eqs:finalreduction}, the internal loop has a unique solution if and only
if the linear system
\[
M(Y)\,v = f_{\mathrm{gen}}
\]
has a unique solution. Since this is a square linear system in $v$, that occurs
if and only if $M(Y)$ is invertible.

When $M(Y)$ is invertible,
\begin{equation}
    v = M(Y)^{-1} f_{\mathrm{gen}}
    \label{eqs:wellposedv}
\end{equation}
is unique. Then
\begin{equation}
    v_1 = Yv,
    \qquad
    v_2 = Y^2 v
    \label{eqs:wellposedv1v2}
\end{equation}
are unique, and therefore
\begin{equation}
    z =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v \\
        v_1
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    w =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        v_1 \\
        v_2
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eqs:wellposedzw}
\end{equation}
are unique. This is exactly well-posedness for the chosen realization: for each
fixed admissible $x$, $u$, and $Y$, the internal loop variables have a unique
solution.

Hence, for the chosen dual-gantry realization,
\begin{equation}
    \text{the algebraic loop is well posed}
    \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
    M(Y) \text{ is invertible}.
    \label{eqs:equivwellposed}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Global Well-Posedness and Relation to Drenth's Section 2.2}

The companion note \texttt{LPV/M-invertibility.tex} proves, using Sylvester's
criterion, that under positive masses, inertias, and geometric parameters,
\begin{equation}
    M(Y) \succ 0
    \qquad
    \forall\, Y \in \R.
    \label{eqs:MYpd}
\end{equation}
Therefore $M(Y)$ is invertible for all real $Y$, and the chosen LPV-LFR
realization is globally well posed in $Y$.

Drenth's Section~2.2 provides a generic sufficient well-posedness theorem based
on diagonal $\Delta(p)$, bounded scheduling, and a spectral-radius condition on
$D_{zw}$ \cite{drenth2025thesis}. That theorem is not the proof route used
here. The present argument uses the plant-specific exact reduction
\eqref{eqs:finalreduction} together with the companion proof of invertibility of
$M(Y)$.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Final Remarks and Scope of Claims}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The derivation above establishes an exact continuous-time LPV-LFR realization of
the dual-gantry baseline model. The realization uses one physical scheduling
variable, namely $Y$, represented through the repeated block
\[
\Delta(Y) = Y I_6.
\]
After collapse, the realization reproduces the original MATLAB-derived
continuous-time state-space model and preserves the MATLAB output map
$y=q=C_cx$.

The generic LPV-LFR framework, the role of the constant interconnection matrix
$G$, and the repeated block-diagonal structure of $\Delta(p)$ come from
Drenth's thesis \cite{drenth2025thesis}. The particular latent-variable
construction, the explicit block matrices, and the reduction of the
well-posedness problem to invertibility of $M(Y)$ are plant-specific steps for
the present dual-gantry model. No claim is made that the chosen realization is
minimal.
