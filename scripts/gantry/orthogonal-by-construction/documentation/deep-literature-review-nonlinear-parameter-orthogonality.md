# Orthogonal-by-Construction Augmentation for a Nonlinear-in-Parameters LPV State-Space Gantry Model

## 1. Executive verdict

**Recommendation: option 6, a staged hybrid construction.** Use a separately identified physical model to define a compact, physically credible parameter region; protect a **fixed, rank-revealed multi-point affine tangent space** of the **discretized state transition**, and add finite secant directions only when a held-out curvature test shows that the tangent union does not represent the sampled physics manifold accurately enough. Warm up the augmentation to obtain nonzero latent-state trajectories, refresh the reference tuples only between a small number of warm-up stages, and then freeze both the reference tuples and protected basis for the final optimization and uncertainty analysis.

This verdict is deliberately negative about a single updated tangent and about a full nonlinear model-manifold projection:

- A single tangent is only local. It cannot imply global non-overlap with the rational family generated by \(M_\theta(Y)^{-1}\). **[D: derived from model-manifold geometry and first-order optimality]**
- Recomputing a tangent every optimizer step makes the projector parameter dependent. The extra derivative of the projector destroys the simple block-orthogonality argument used by Györök et al.; detaching it instead creates an alternating, time-varying objective for which neither cited Györök paper proves convergence. **[D]**
- A nonlinear closest-point projection onto the whole physics manifold is conceptually faithful, but it is an inner nonlinear least-squares problem coupled to every neural forward pass and rollout. The calibration literature supplies useful geometry and estimators, not a causal, computationally credible by-construction state-update formula for this setting. **[E/D]**
- A frozen multi-point basis retains the algebraic strength of orthogonal-by-construction subtraction on a declared finite reference set, while covering curvature better than one Taylor point and remaining compatible with automatic differentiation. **[P: proposed engineering choice]**

The protected columns should be constructed in the ten structurally identifiable physical combinations, not the fourteen raw parameters. At each representative parameter \(\alpha^{(\ell)}\), stack the transition sensitivities and an affine/nominal direction. A convenient equivalent form is

\[
\mathcal B_\ell=\operatorname{col}_{i}
\left[\frac{\partial F_\alpha(x_{p,i},u_i)}{\partial \alpha}
\bigg|_{\alpha^{(\ell)}},\quad F_{\alpha^{(\ell)}}(x_{p,i},u_i)\right].
\]

For one tangent point, \(\operatorname{range}[J,F_{\bar\alpha}]\) equals \(\operatorname{range}[J,c]\), because \(c=F_{\bar\alpha}-J\bar\alpha\). Thus this form includes the offset required by the supervisor's stronger “orthogonal to the physics output” interpretation. Protecting \(J\) alone is sufficient only for the narrower goal of preventing *infinitesimal parameter changes* from being absorbed by the ANN. **[D]**

Let \(B=[\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_L,S]\), where \(S\) contains any retained secants \(\operatorname{col}_i(F_{\alpha^{(\ell)}}-F_{\alpha^{(0)}})\). With a positive-definite or regularized positive-semidefinite metric \(W\), compute a whitened truncated SVD

\[
W^{1/2}B=Q_r\Sigma_rV_r^\top,
\qquad
P_{B,W}=W^{-1/2}Q_rQ_r^\top W^{1/2}.
\]

For the physical neural writes \(G_\eta=\operatorname{col}_i R_pg_\eta(x_{p,i},x_{a,i},u_i)\), use

\[
a_\eta=(B^\top WB)^\dagger B^\top WG_\eta,
\qquad
\widetilde G_\eta=G_\eta-Ba_\eta=(I-P_{B,W})G_\eta.
\]

The linear solve and \(G_\eta\) must remain in the differentiation graph; the frozen \(B,W\), parameter samples, and reference tuples must not. This gives the exact finite-set identity

\[
B^\top W\widetilde G_\eta=0
\]

and, between basis refreshes,

\[
B^\top W\frac{\partial \widetilde G_\eta}{\partial\eta}=0.
\]

It does **not** give global orthogonality to the nonlinear physics family, orthogonality at arbitrary rollout states, multi-step output orthogonality, recovery of fourteen individual physical parameters, consistency under the 2026 theorem, zero asymptotic cross-covariance, or stability. **[E/D]**

### Claim-status notation

- **[E] Established directly:** stated or proved by the cited source under its assumptions.
- **[D] Derived:** a mathematical consequence of cited results plus the declared gantry formulation.
- **[P] Proposed:** a defensible engineering choice requiring validation.
- **[O] Open:** not established by the located literature.

## 2. Exact mathematical problem

The continuous-time physical model is

\[
M_\theta(Y)\ddot q+C_\theta\dot q+K_\theta q=Pu,
\qquad M_\theta(Y)=M_0(\theta)+M_1(\theta)Y+M_2(\theta)Y^2,
\qquad Y=q_3,
\]

with \(x_p=\operatorname{col}(q,\dot q)\in\mathbb R^6\). Because the acceleration contains \(M_\theta(Y)^{-1}\), the vector field and its numerical flow map are generally rational, not linear, in the physical parameters. State/input linearization around \((\bar x,\bar u)\) is a different operation and is not needed to define the parameter tangent.

After discretization, the augmented state-space model is

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{p,k+1}&=F_\theta(x_{p,k},u_k)+R_pg_\eta(x_{p,k},x_{a,k},u_k),\\
x_{a,k+1}&=R_ag_\eta(x_{p,k},x_{a,k},u_k),\\
y_k&=C_px_{p,k}+Du_k,
\end{aligned}
\]

with initial states supplied by an encoder and parameters estimated using free-running multiple-shooting loss. The baseline has no latent-state equation. Consequently, an orthogonality condition against baseline parameter directions constrains only \(R_pg_\eta\); the \(R_ag_\eta\) block is orthogonal to a zero baseline block vacuously. The local project verification establishes this structural fact and also verifies exact IO/delay-state equivalence for the separate NARX realization.[^1]

For a reference set \(Z=\{(x_{p,i},x_{a,i},u_i)\}_{i=1}^N\), define the sampled physics-model map

\[
\mathcal F(\alpha)=\operatorname{col}_{i=1}^N F_\alpha(x_{p,i},u_i)\in\mathbb R^{6N}.
\]

The set \(\mathcal M_Z=\{\mathcal F(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathcal A\}\) is a nonlinear model manifold in sampled-transition space. Its tangent at (\bar\alpha) is \(T_{\bar\alpha}\mathcal M_Z=\operatorname{range}J_Z(\bar\alpha)\), where (J_Z=\partial\mathcal F/\partial\alpha). Nonlinear least squares is geometrically the problem of finding a point on such a prediction manifold closest to data; changing the parameter point changes the tangent whenever the manifold is curved.[^2] **[E]**

The design question is therefore not whether a unique global feature matrix exists—it does not—but which *linear surrogate of \(\mathcal M_Z\)* should be declared protected, under which sample measure and metric, and for how long it should remain fixed.

## 3. What Györök et al. (2026) covers—and does not

### 3.1 Exact scope

Györök et al. allow a baseline that is nonlinear in the measured-history regressor \(x_k\), because \(\phi(x_k)\) may be nonlinear. They explicitly require the baseline output to be linear in the physical parameters:

\[
\hat y_k=\phi(x_k)\theta_b
\]

(2026 paper, Eq. (2), PDF p. 2).[^3] The ANN is static and the model is a discrete-time NARX input-output predictor using lagged measured inputs and outputs (Eq. (1), p. 2). There is no state/input operating-point linearization and no parameter linearization. **[E]**

On the stacked estimation set, Eqs. (8)–(10), PDF p. 3, define

\[
\theta_{\rm aux}=(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}\Phi^\top F_{\rm ANN},
\qquad
\widetilde F_{\rm ANN}=\left[I-\Phi(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}\Phi^\top\right]F_{\rm ANN}.
\]

Lemma 3, Eq. (11), p. 3, proves \(\Phi^\top\widetilde F_{\rm ANN}=0\). This is an algebraic identity for fixed, full-column-rank \(\Phi\). The paragraph after Lemma 3 permits a synthetic auxiliary evaluation set or subset of the estimation data. Equation (13), p. 3, freezes the learned auxiliary coefficient for prediction on new points. **[E]**

The Moore–Penrose version is more general than the paper's displayed inverse:

\[
\widetilde F=(I-\Phi\Phi^\dagger)F,
\qquad \Phi^\top\widetilde F=0.
\]

This identity holds at any rank because (\Phi\Phi^\dagger) is the unique Euclidean orthogonal projector onto \(\operatorname{range}\Phi\). The coefficient (\Phi^\dagger F) may be only one of many least-squares coefficients, but the fitted vector (\Phi\Phi^\dagger F) and projected residual are unique. **[D]**

## 4. The 2025 nonlinear-parameter extension

Györök et al. (2025) treat a nonlinear state-space baseline and a multi-step SUBNET-style identification loss (Eqs. (1)–(4), PDF pp. 3–4).[^4] Section 4 takes a Taylor expansion with respect to physical parameters, not state or input:

\[
f_\theta(\hat x_k,u_k)\approx f_{\bar\theta}(\hat x_k,u_k)
+\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\hat x_k,u_k)(\theta-\bar\theta)
\tag{15, p. 7}
\]

and rewrites it as

\[
f_\theta\approx \Phi_{\bar\theta}\theta+
\underbrace{f_{\bar\theta}-\Phi_{\bar\theta}\bar\theta}_{\Gamma_{\bar\theta}}
\tag{16, p. 7}.
\]

Equations (17)–(18), p. 7, append (\Gamma_{\bar\theta}) as a column and augment the parameter vector with a fixed scalar one. The stated reason is “to consider all components of the baseline model” and to allow adjustment of the linearization point. Thus the construction protects both the local parameter-variation directions and the affine nominal model response—not merely (J\delta\theta). **[E]**

The method then penalizes the projection of the ANN output in Eq. (19), p. 7. It is regularization, not by-construction subtraction, and has a tunable (\beta). The paragraph on p. 8 states that (\bar\theta) may be updated at each iteration/current estimate, but that doing so requires recomputing the SVD whenever the cost is evaluated; nominal (\theta_0) is offered as a cheaper fixed approximation. Remark 2, p. 7, separately suggests updating state estimates and the projector at the start of each epoch when full-state measurements are unavailable. **[E]**

Important limits are explicit in the source:

- The reported F1Tenth experiment used the nominal linearization/basis. The conclusion, p. 10, says online recalculation of the projection matrix “has not been evaluated” and is future work. **[E]**
- No convergence theorem is provided for a changing projector. **[E]**
- The paper does not specify a stop-gradient/differentiation policy through an updated SVD, nor prove a covariance result. **[E]**
- The nonlinear Taylor construction is proposed as a regularizer. Combining its affine basis with the 2026 exact residual subtraction is a new derivation, not a theorem from either paper. **[D]**

The legitimate combination is narrow but useful: freeze (B) over a differentiable forward/backward pass, define (a_\eta=B_W^\dagger G_\eta), and subtract (Ba_\eta). Then the same projector algebra as Lemma 3 applies to the chosen affine surrogate. It does not upgrade that surrogate into the exact nonlinear family. **[D]**

## 5. Literature map of alternatives

### 5.1 Nonlinear model manifolds and calibration

Transtrum, Machta, and Sethna view all predictions of a nonlinear model as an embedded manifold in data space. The Jacobian spans the local tangent; nonlinear least-squares pathologies arise from manifold width, boundaries, and curvature.[^2] This directly supports using multiple tangents or secants when a single tangent is a poor global approximation, but it does not prescribe orthogonal residual learning. **[E]**

In computer-model calibration, Tuo and Wu define the physical parameter as the minimizer of an (L_2) discrepancy over an operating domain (Eq. (2.2), p. 4). Their Theorem 1 (Eq. (3.7), p. 9) establishes asymptotic normality and semiparametric efficiency under A1–A5, B1–B4, and C1–C2.[^5] The first-order condition at an interior optimum makes the discrepancy orthogonal to the simulator's parameter derivatives. **[E]**

Plumlee's Theorem 1 (JASA 2017, pp. 1278–1279, Eq. (8)) states the corresponding (L_2) orthogonality of model bias to the computer-model gradient when the target parameter is an interior loss minimizer; Theorem 2 extends it to a Sobolev metric.[^6] This is strong evidence that the *tangent* is the canonical object when the objective is definition and estimation of a best physical parameter. It is also evidence against declaring the nominal-output direction mathematically necessary for parameter identifiability: the first-order optimality condition does not include it. **[E/D]**

Tuo's projected-kernel construction defines (G_\theta=\operatorname{span}\{\partial y^s/\partial\theta_j\}) in Eq. (12) and requires discrepancy orthogonality to (G_{\hat\theta}) in Eq. (13), PDF p. 8.[^7] The protected subspace changes with (\theta). Under the paper's independent-design, smoothness, RKHS, and asymptotic assumptions, the estimator is semiparametrically efficient; those assumptions are far from the recursive, closed-loop gantry estimator. **[E]**

These calibration methods are the closest principled nonlinear-manifold alternatives, but they solve a batch parameter-estimation problem. They do not yield a causal neural state update, do not cover learned latent dynamics, and do not establish convergence of epochwise projector refreshes. **[D]**

### 5.2 Variable projection

Golub and Pereyra's variable-projection method eliminates parameters that enter linearly from separable nonlinear least squares and differentiates the resulting pseudoinverse/projector.[^8] Modern reviews emphasize reduced dimension and often better conditioning.[^9] This is relevant computationally to (a_\eta), which is a linear least-squares nuisance coefficient conditional on a frozen basis. It does not solve the nonlinear overlap problem because the physical parameters do not form the eliminable linear block, and it does not make a moving (B(\theta)) harmless: the derivative of the projector must then be retained. **[E/D]**

### 5.3 Sensitivity, Fisher information, and active subspaces

Local sensitivity identifiability methods analyze the singular values and collinearity of scaled output-sensitivity matrices. Brun, Reichert, and Künsch explicitly note dependence on parameter scaling, the experimental layout, and the nominal point (Sec. 4.2, especially p. 1020).[^10] Brouwer and Eisenberg show that sensitivity Fisher information connects local identifiability, sloppiness, and active subspaces, while stressing the distinction between local/global and linear/nonlinear conclusions.[^11] **[E]**

These methods justify three design choices: build the basis in identifiable combinations; whiten/scale before deciding numerical rank; and sample several physically representative parameters and trajectories. They diagnose information directions; they do not themselves impose complementarity between physics and a neural residual. **[D]**

### 5.4 Secants and tangent unions

A secant

\[
s_{\ell}=\mathcal F(\alpha^{(\ell)})-\mathcal F(\alpha^{(0)})
\]

protects an actual finite physical variation. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

\[
s_\ell=\int_0^1 J_Z(\alpha^{(0)}+t\Delta\alpha_\ell)\,\Delta\alpha_\ell\,dt.
\]

Thus a dense union of tangents along the connecting path represents every secant; a finite tangent union need not. Conversely, a few secants protect only sampled chords and can miss orthogonal local directions. **[D]**

No located primary source proves that either a finite tangent union or a finite secant set gives global complementarity for a neural state-space residual. Their use here is a reduced-order approximation of the nonlinear prediction manifold. **[O]**

### 5.5 Grey-box neural residuals and stability

Györök et al. (2025) demonstrate nonlinear state-space augmentation with multi-step training, but only regularized local affine protection.[^4] Donati et al. study multi-step physics-based identification with off-white and sparse black components and derive prediction/gradient bounds under their own assumptions; they do not impose the required orthogonal-by-construction split.[^12] Bolderman et al. provide ISS conditions for a feedforward PGNN architecture via neural Lipschitz bounds.[^13] Bonassi et al. and Seel et al. supply NARX delay-state realizations and stability conditions for their specific neural predictors/controllers.[^14][^15] None of these theorems applies automatically to the gantry's marginal physical modes, routed correction, encoder, and learned additional states. **[E/D]**

### 5.6 Semiparametric nuisance tangents

Semiparametric orthogonal scores project target scores away from nuisance tangent spaces to reduce first-order sensitivity to nuisance estimation. That is the reverse orientation of the present construction: here the learned nuisance contribution is projected away from physical tangent directions. The analogy supports Jacobian/block-information reasoning, but standard orthogonal-score theory assumes a fixed statistical estimand and regularity/cross-fitting conditions absent here. It is useful language, not a transferred theorem. **[D]**

## 7. Fixed, updated, multi-point, secant, and manifold approaches

### 7.1 Which parameter reference?

1. **Initial 10%-detuned parameters.** They are useful as a stress-test point but are not the preferred center; protecting only their tangent anchors the decomposition to a deliberately wrong model. **[P]**
2. **Separately identified nominal parameter vector.** This is the preferred center. Fit the ten identifiable combinations with the ANN disabled, using the same discretization, multiple-shooting layout, and output weighting. It defines what “physical parameter” means before the flexible nuisance is introduced. **[P]**
3. **Several fixed points.** Draw them from a constrained confidence ellipsoid, bootstrap cloud, or engineering tolerance box around the separate estimate, including the 10%-detuned point and boundary points for (Y). Use space-filling selection in the scaled identifiable-combination coordinates. **[P]**
4. **Epochwise update.** Permissible only as a warm-up heuristic. Freeze the basis and reference tuples within the epoch; do not backpropagate into their generation. Recompute, compare principal angles/rank, and accept a refresh only if validation improves without a large subspace jump. Freeze for the final stage. **[P]**
5. **Every-step update.** Reject. With (P=P(\theta)),

   \[
   \frac{\partial[(I-P(\theta))G_\eta]}{\partial\theta}
   =-\frac{\partial P}{\partial\theta}G_\eta+(I-P)\frac{\partial G_\eta}{\partial\theta}.
   \]

   The first term is absent from Györök's block argument. Detaching (P) removes that derivative numerically but changes the method to alternating optimization. **[D]**

Within a frozen epoch the orthogonality and neural-Jacobian identities are exact at the reference tuples. Across refreshes there is no common fixed subspace, no single stationary criterion, and no cited convergence or asymptotic covariance result. **[D/O]**

### 7.2 Differentiation policy

The right rule is: **detach the basis; differentiate through the auxiliary coefficient.** For fixed (B,W),

\[
\partial_\eta\widetilde G=(I-P_{B,W})\partial_\eta G,
\]

so (B^\top W\partial_\eta\widetilde G=0). If (a_\eta) is detached, the forward residual remains orthogonal at the instant it was computed, but the optimizer sees (\partial_\eta\widetilde G=\partial_\eta G), which generally has components in \(\operatorname{range}B\). This forfeits the block-Jacobian property. **[D]**

### 7.3 Why not full nonlinear projection?

One could define a correction by solving

\[
\alpha^*(G)=\arg\min_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}
\|G-\mathcal F(\alpha)\|_W^2
\]

and subtracting a point or displacement on the manifold. At an interior unique optimum the residual is tangent-orthogonal, matching calibration first-order conditions.[^5][^6] But a point on a nonlinear manifold is not a linear “component”: subtracting it introduces an origin choice, and the closest point can be nonunique. Differentiating through the argmin requires implicit differentiation and a nonsingular local Hessian; doing it on recursive trajectories couples the inner solve to the network and encoder. **[D]**

This is suitable as an offline diagnostic: calculate distances and principal angles between trained ANN writes and the sampled manifold. It is not the recommended production parameterization. **[P]**

## 8. Tangent versus affine protection

### 8.1 Tangent-only goal

If the scientific estimand is the locally best physical parameter under a declared weighted (L_2) loss, first-order optimality requires the discrepancy to be orthogonal to (J(\theta^*)). The calibration literature supports precisely this condition.[^5][^6][^7] It prevents the ANN from producing a first-order direction that an infinitesimal change in physical parameters can reproduce. **[E/D]**

Tangent protection does not stop cancellation of the nominal physics response. A vector parallel to (F_{\bar\theta}) can be orthogonal to (J_{\bar\theta}) when the model is nonlinear in parameters. This can leave physically implausible decompositions even if local parameter variations are distinguishable. **[D]**

### 8.2 Affine/nominal-output goal

If the supervisor's requirement is literally that the learned physical write be orthogonal to the sampled output of the physics function, the nominal direction must be included. At one point,

\[
\operatorname{range}[J,c]=\operatorname{range}[J,F_{\bar\theta}],
\quad c=F_{\bar\theta}-J\bar\theta.
\]

This prevents the ANN from globally scaling or cancelling the nominal sampled transition direction. It reduces available ANN function space by at most one additional independent direction per affine point, although a multi-point construction can add more nominal directions. **[D]**

Including the offset is therefore a **modeling choice required by the stronger decomposition convention**, not a universal mathematical requirement for local parameter identifiability. It may be too restrictive if the physical baseline has a systematic amplitude bias that the physical parameters cannot express and the ANN is expected to correct it. That trade-off must be tested by an offset-included versus tangent-only ablation. **[D/P]**

### 8.3 Reduction to Györök 2026

If (F_\theta=\Phi\theta), then (J=\Phi) and (c=0); tangent and affine protection coincide. With no learned states, all physical rows routed, measured one-step IO regressors, Euclidean weighting, and the estimation set as reference set, the proposed subtraction reduces to Györök's Eqs. (8)–(11) and prediction Eq. (13), using a pseudoinverse if necessary. **[D]**

## 9. Rank deficiency and identifiable combinations

The project has established rank ten for fourteen raw sensitivity columns and rank eleven after the offset. Four structural null directions mean that no amount of excitation or numerical regularization can recover fourteen individual values from this model/output structure. A pseudoinverse cannot create information. **[D]**

The consequences separate cleanly:

- (B^\top W\widetilde G=0) remains well defined at deficient rank. **[D]**
- The auxiliary coefficient is nonunique before the Moore–Penrose convention, but (Ba_\eta=P_BG_\eta) and the projected correction are unique. **[D]**
- The raw physical parameter estimate is not unique; covariance in the null directions is undefined/infinite in the usual local approximation. **[D]**
- Györök's Assumption 1, Theorem 7, Eq. (21), and the full-rank block inversions in Theorem 18 fail in the raw coordinates. **[E/D]**

**Recommendation:** structurally reparameterize the physical model as (\theta=T\alpha+\theta_{\rm fixed}), (\alpha\in\mathbb R^{10}), using the already derived identifiable combinations. Report those combinations—not recovered members of a nonidentifiable split—as the physical estimates. This makes optimization and uncertainty reporting honest and removes arbitrary null-space scaling before the protected basis is constructed. **[P]**

For numerical rank, first whiten rows by (W^{1/2}) and scale columns using physically meaningful parameter scales (engineering tolerances or the separate-fit standard errors). Then use an SVD and select directions that satisfy both:

1. a clear singular-value gap stable under bootstrap resampling of reference trajectories; and
2. a noise/solver threshold established by perturbing states, parameters, and integration tolerances.

A machine-precision rule such as (\tau=\max(m,n)\epsilon\sigma_1) is a computational rank test, not a practical-identifiability test. Truncated SVD is preferable to ridge inversion for defining a genuinely orthogonal protected subspace: ridge produces shrinkage, so (B^\top W(G-Ba)\neq0) in general. Ridge may stabilize a diagnostic solve but weakens exact orthogonality. **[D/P]**

## 10. Continuous-time versus discrete-time construction

For the vector field (\dot x=f_\theta(x,u)), the sensitivity (S(t)=\partial x(t)/\partial\theta) obeys

\[
\dot S=f_xS+f_\theta,\qquad S(0)=\partial x_0/\partial\theta.
\]

For a zero-order-held input and exact flow (F_{\theta,h}),

\[
\frac{\partial F_{\theta,h}}{\partial\theta}
=\Phi_x(h,0)S(0)+\int_0^h\Phi_x(h,\tau)f_\theta(x(\tau),u)\,d\tau.
\]

Thus flow-map sensitivities are propagated and integrated vector-field sensitivities. Their sampled column spaces need not coincide except in special cases (for example sufficiently small (h) to first order, or invariant sensitivity subspaces). A numerical integrator adds its own discretization map and stage evaluations. **[D]**

Orthogonality of continuous vector-field samples is not generally preserved after integration because the variational dynamics weight directions differently. Conversely, protecting the discrete transition depends on sample time and integration method, but that dependence is appropriate when the training loss uses that transition. **[D]**

**Recommendation:** construct (B) by automatic differentiation through exactly the same discrete integrator, sample time, normalization transforms, and routing used in training. Apply the subtraction to the discrete physical transition/write. Maintain a continuous-vector-field version only as an ablation and for physical diagnostics. **[P]**

## 11. Reference set, closed-loop data, latent states, and metric

### 11.1 Reference tuples

Closed-loop data are not equivalent to exogenous open-loop samples. Classical closed-loop identification shows that feedback correlates inputs and disturbances and that an adequate noise model/external excitation is essential for unbiased process identification.[^16] Györök's Theorem 12 assumes a quasi-stationary input independent of the white noise; that assumption cannot simply be asserted for the gantry data. **[E/D]**

Use a stratified mixture of reference tuples:

- multiple-shooting physical-state estimates from measured closed-loop trajectories, with folds separated by experiment; **[P]**
- augmented rollouts from the preceding warm-up stage, detached before basis construction, to activate learned latent pathways; **[P]**
- encoder-produced initial latent states plus designed latent perturbations within an empirically bounded ellipsoid; **[P]**
- held-out and synthetic coverage of scheduling position (Y=q_3), velocity, and input extremes; **[P]**
- a smaller baseline-only set as a declared (x_a=0) anchor, not as the only reference set. **[P]**

A baseline-only rollout has (x_a=0) and is blind to parameters whose physical effect appears only after latent-to-physical recursion. The local symbolic counterexample establishes that blindness for the implemented structure.[^1] Previous-epoch augmented rollouts repair coverage within the next frozen stage but create an unproved fixed-point iteration across stages. Designed latent samples prevent the zero-initialized network from remaining trapped on the zero-latent slice. **[D/P/O]**

Use cross-fitting: generate reference tuples from trajectories/folds not used to evaluate the same minibatch loss, and maintain a held-out reference set for leakage diagnostics. This does not import a semiparametric cross-fitting theorem, but reduces the chance that the projector merely fits a particular rollout. **[P]**

### 11.2 Metric and coordinate invariance

Euclidean orthogonality is not invariant to a state rescaling (z=Tx). A coordinate-invariant statement requires the metric to transform as (W_z=T^{-\top}W_xT^{-1}). Therefore “SI Euclidean” and “normalized Euclidean” answer different questions. **[D]**

The preferred primary metric is a **loss-induced, uncertainty-whitened metric expressed consistently in SI coordinates**:

\[
W_i=H_i^\top R_y^{-1}H_i+\lambda_s\Sigma_{x,i}^{-1},
\]

where (H_i) is a frozen finite-horizon linear map from a one-step physical-state write to measured outputs along the reference trajectory, (R_y) is output-noise covariance, and (\Sigma_{x,i}) represents encoder/process uncertainty. A small (\lambda_s>0) or eigenvalue floor makes the metric positive definite on weakly observed state directions. Implement the equivalent transformed metric in normalized coordinates. **[P]**

This choice has three advantages: it respects measurement reliability, reflects which state directions affect the actual output loss, and remains invariant when the normalization is applied correctly. Pure SI Euclidean weighting mixes positions and velocities without a statistical scale; normalized Euclidean weighting silently makes empirical standard deviations the scientific metric; pure Fisher weighting can be singular and parameter-dependent; a mechanical energy metric is attractive for stability analysis but does not align directly with measured-output identification. **[D/P]**

Report sensitivity to three ablations: normalized Euclidean, block-diagonal inverse state uncertainty, and the proposed observability/loss-induced metric. **[P]**

## 12. One-step versus horizon behavior

The exact projector controls stacked one-step writes only. For a frozen reference rollout, a perturbation injected at step (j) affects output at step (k>j) through products of state Jacobians:

\[
\delta y_k=C_pA_{k-1}A_{k-2}\cdots A_{j+1}\,\delta x_{p,j+1}.
\]

These propagation operators are time dependent, can rotate/scale directions, and include latent-to-physical coupling. Hence cancellation in the one-step inner product need not survive a horizon lift. The project's symbolic counterexample already establishes nonzero multi-step overlap despite exact one-step orthogonality.[^1] **[D]**

A tractable horizon diagnostic is available. For each frozen shooting window, compute

\[
S_{\theta,H}=\frac{\partial\operatorname{col}(\hat y_{1:H})}{\partial\alpha},
\qquad
S_{\eta,H}=\frac{\partial\operatorname{col}(\hat y_{1:H})}{\partial\eta}
\]

or, more economically, Jacobians with respect to the sequence of learned physical writes. Measure principal angles, canonical correlations, and

\[
\rho_H=
\frac{\|S_{\theta,H}^\top W_HS_{\eta,H}\|_F}
{\|W_H^{1/2}S_{\theta,H}\|_F\|W_H^{1/2}S_{\eta,H}\|_F}.
\]

Automatic differentiation/adjoints make Jacobian-vector products feasible without forming every dense matrix. **[P]**

An approximate lifted tangent could be regularized during training, but an exact *causal by-construction* subtraction at the state-write level is obstructed: the horizon map depends nonlinearly on the same correction and on future latent states. Solving the coefficient from the current rollout is implicit and, if it uses future points to correct an earlier step, noncausal as an online formula. Therefore horizon protection should **supplement**, not replace, the exact one-step construction. **[D/O]**

## 13. Marginal stability and boundedness

Györök's Condition 10 requires exponential decay (\lambda^{k-k_0}) with (\lambda<1); pure integrators do not forget initial conditions in that sense. Condition 11 imposes analogous predictor stability. Thus Theorems 12 and 16 cannot support asymptotic claims for the marginally stable gantry. **[E/D]**

Finite-horizon computations remain meaningful whenever the transition, sensitivities, and encoder outputs remain bounded on the compact evaluated region. No asymptotic forgetting is required for the algebraic projector identity or for finite-window sensitivity propagation. **[D]**

The subtraction term (-B(x_k,u_k)a_\eta) can destabilize because orthogonality is an average geometric condition, not a gain bound. The latent map can also contain an unstable autonomous mode invisible in short output records. ISS results in Bolderman et al., Bonassi et al., and Seel et al. depend on their architectures and contraction/Lipschitz inequalities and cannot be reused without proving those inequalities for the routed augmented transition.[^13][^14][^15] **[E/D]**

Monitor, per shooting window and on long held-out rollouts:

- maximum state and latent norms and growth ratios;
- spectral radius/singular value of the local augmented Jacobian (A_k=\partial F_{\rm aug}/\partial x);
- finite-time products (\|A_{k:j}\|_2) or Lyapunov-exponent estimates;
- norms of raw write, subtraction, and corrected write separately;
- (\|a_\eta\|), condition number and retained singular values of (W^{1/2}B);
- energy/work injection (\Delta V_k) under a mechanical storage candidate;
- one-step and lifted-horizon overlap metrics on training and held-out sets.

Hard rollout termination, gradient clipping, spectral/Lipschitz control of latent recurrence, and a trust-region bound on (\|Ba_\eta\|) are sensible safeguards, but each changes the estimator and none is a proof of stability. **[P/O]**

## 14. Recommended gantry formulation

### 14.1 Offline physical stage

Reparameterize to (\alpha\in\mathbb R^{10}). Estimate (\hat\alpha_0) with the ANN disabled using the same discrete transition and shooting/output loss. Bootstrap by experiment or obtain a Gauss–Newton covariance on the identifiable space. Define a compact physical region \(\mathcal A\) intersecting engineering bounds with an uncertainty ellipsoid. **[P]**

Choose (L\) representative points (\alpha^{(\ell)}\): the center, the 10%-detuned point if admissible, principal-axis boundary points, and several maximin/Latin-hypercube points. **[P]**

### 14.2 Frozen sampled physics space

For fixed (Z_{\rm ref}), form

\[
J_\ell=\frac{\partial\mathcal F(\alpha)}{\partial\alpha}
\bigg|_{\alpha^{(\ell)}},
\quad b_\ell=\mathcal F(\alpha^{(\ell)}),
\quad s_\ell=\mathcal F(\alpha^{(\ell)})-\mathcal F(\hat\alpha_0).
\]

Start with

\[
B_0=[J_1,b_1,\ldots,J_L,b_L].
\]

For each sampled parameter point, evaluate the relative weighted projection error

\[
e_{\rm man}(\alpha)=
\frac{\|(I-P_{B_0,W})[\mathcal F(\alpha)-\mathcal F(\hat\alpha_0)]\|_W}
{\|\mathcal F(\alpha)-\mathcal F(\hat\alpha_0)\|_W+\varepsilon}.
\]

If the held-out maximum exceeds a declared tolerance, append the worst residualized secants and recompute the rank-revealing SVD until validation plateaus or a dimension budget is reached. This makes secant inclusion evidence driven. **[P]**

### 14.3 Applied transition

Let (Q_r) be the retained left singular vectors of (W^{1/2}B), and define

\[
a_\eta=V_r\Sigma_r^{-1}Q_r^\top W^{1/2}G_\eta,
\qquad
d_\eta(z)=R_pg_\eta(z)-B(z)a_\eta.
\]

Here (B(z)) evaluates the same retained linear combinations of pointwise Jacobian, nominal, and secant columns at the current ((x_p,u)). The coefficient is fitted once per reference batch/pass but applied causally at each current point, as in Györök's prediction Eq. (13). The augmented transition is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
x_{p,k+1}&=F_\alpha(x_{p,k},u_k)+d_\eta(x_{p,k},x_{a,k},u_k),\\
x_{a,k+1}&=R_ag_\eta(x_{p,k},x_{a,k},u_k),\\
y_k&=C_px_{p,k}+Du_k.
\end{aligned}}
\]

The subtraction writes only into physical rows. Gradients pass through \(G_\eta\), \(a_\eta\), and the current evaluation \(B(z_k)a_\eta\), but not through construction of the frozen reference \(B,W,Z_{\rm ref}\). If the current pointwise basis depends on the trainable current \(\alpha\), evaluate it at the frozen representative points instead; otherwise the simple derivative guarantee changes. **[P/D]**

### 14.4 Exact and inexact claims

Exactly guaranteed:

\[
B^\top W(G_\eta-Ba_\eta)=0
\]

on the frozen reference tuples, up to the declared SVD truncation and floating-point tolerance; and the analogous neural-Jacobian identity when differentiating through (a_\eta). **[D]**

Not guaranteed: pointwise orthogonality, orthogonality on new trajectories, global orthogonality to \(\mathcal M_Z\), horizon-output orthogonality, parameter truth, cross-covariance, or stability. **[D]**

## 15. Training algorithm

```text
Input: experiments D, discrete integrator F, engineering parameter bounds,
       identifiable map theta = T alpha + theta_fixed.

1. Physics stage
   Fit alpha_0 with the ANN disabled using the final shooting/output loss.
   Estimate a bootstrap/Gauss-Newton uncertainty region A.

2. Parameter design
   Select fixed alpha^(1:L) in A, including alpha_0 and stress-test points.

3. Warm-up without final projector
   Initialize ANN so physical write is small, but seed latent dynamics/encoder
   so reference latent states are not identically zero.
   Train briefly with bounded rollouts.

4. Reference design
   Build Z_ref from detached measured-state estimates, previous-stage augmented
   rollouts, designed latent perturbations, and held-out Y/input coverage.
   Build W in SI coordinates and transform it consistently to training coordinates.

5. Physics-space construction
   Differentiate the exact discrete integrator at alpha^(1:L).
   Build multi-point [J_l, F_l] columns.
   Whiten rows, scale parameter columns, and compute SVD.
   Determine stable numerical rank by bootstrap/noise/solver perturbations.
   Add residualized secants only if held-out manifold projection error requires them.
   Freeze B, W, Q_r, Sigma_r, V_r, and Z_ref.

6. Final joint training
   On each projection refresh/batch, evaluate G_eta on Z_ref.
   Solve a_eta = V_r Sigma_r^{-1} Q_r' W^(1/2) G_eta.
   Keep this solve in autograd.
   Roll out multiple-shooting windows with physical write
       R_p g_eta(z_k) - B(z_k) a_eta.
   Optimize alpha, eta, and encoder parameters subject to physical/stability guards.

7. Optional warm-up refresh (at most a few times)
   Generate detached augmented reference trajectories.
   Rebuild the basis only between stages; log subspace principal angles and rank.
   Accept only if held-out projection/rollout metrics improve.
   Return to step 6 and finally freeze permanently.

8. Validation
   Report one-step exact residual, off-reference residual, manifold error,
   horizon overlap, parameter uncertainty in alpha, long-rollout boundedness,
   and every requested ablation.
```

This is an alternating continuation heuristic during warm-up and a fixed-objective estimator only after the final freeze. No convergence claim should be made for the warm-up refresh. **[P/O]**

### 16.2 Reference and metric ablations

Cross baseline-only, measured/multiple-shooting, preceding augmented rollout, and designed latent reference sets with normalized Euclidean, SI Euclidean, inverse-uncertainty, and loss/observability-induced metrics. ### 16.3 Outcomes

Report:

- prediction NRMS/RMSE at one step and all trained/untrained horizons;
- identifiable-combination error and bootstrap intervals;
- physical-only model performance after joint training;
- exact reference-set (\|B^\top W\widetilde G\|) and off-reference counterpart;
- smallest retained/discarded singular values and rank stability;
- principal angles between tangent spaces at sampled parameters;
- secant residual (e_{\rm man});
- horizon canonical correlation (\rho_H);
- latent-state activation and contribution by pathway;
- long-rollout maximum norms, Jacobian-product growth, and intervention count;
- computation and memory cost.

Use paired seeds and experiment-level bootstrap confidence intervals. A successful result requires not just equal fit, but stable identifiable-combination estimates, preserved physical-only performance, low off-reference/horizon overlap, and bounded rollouts. **[P]**

## 17. Risks, limitations, and unresolved questions

1. **Finite subspace approximation.** A finite tangent/secant basis cannot certify global separation from a curved nonlinear manifold. **[O]**
2. **Reference distribution dependence.** The decomposition is defined by (Z_{\rm ref}) and (W); changing either changes the meaning of the physical parameter. **[D]**
3. **Latent-state gauge freedom.** Orthogonalizing physical writes does not identify latent coordinates or prevent invertible transformations of latent state. **[D]**
4. **Encoder confounding.** Encoder parameters can change initial physical states and absorb physical mismatch without appearing in the one-step write condition. **[D/O]**
5. **Changing references.** Convergence of preceding-epoch rollout refresh, especially from zero initialization, is not established. Designed latent excitation is a heuristic repair. **[O]**
6. **Horizon gap.** Exact one-step orthogonality gives no general multi-step output guarantee. A computationally efficient causal by-construction horizon method remains open. **[O]**
7. **Marginal stability.** No cited consistency/stability theorem covers the complete gantry estimator. Only finite-horizon monitoring and separately proved structural constraints are defensible. **[O]**
8. **Metric choice.** The proposed loss/observability metric is principled but still a modeling decision; results must be sensitivity tested. **[P]**
9. **Offset restriction.** Nominal-output protection implements the supervisor's decomposition convention but can deny the ANN a useful systematic correction. **[D]**
10. **Uncertainty after selection.** SVD rank, secant selection, early stopping, and basis refresh are data-dependent. Naive Gauss–Newton intervals after these selections understate uncertainty. **[D/O]**
