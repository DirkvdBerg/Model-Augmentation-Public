## 0. Context in one paragraph (read this first)

The thesis augments a physics-based gantry model (FP baseline, Garcia-Herreros dual gantry,
LPV in Y) with a learned dynamic parallel component (Jan Hoekstra's framework, SUBNET encoder).
The truth system in this phase is a noiseless Simulink simulation: the FP baseline PLUS a hidden
mass-spring-damper absorber (150 Hz, mass fraction 0.10) on the Y drive. The ANN's job is to learn
the absorber's effect: at every 4 kHz sample the augmented model computes
`x[k+1] = baseline_step(x[k], u[k]) + w[k]`, with `w[k]` the ANN's 8-row per-step state correction.
Training minimizes a windowed simulation loss (nf = 400 samples = 0.1 s windows, encoder-initialized).
**The problem**: the trained ANN's correction carries a small CONSTANT (non-zero time-mean, "DC").
The gantry's X and Y axes have no stiffness to ground (K=0, free integrators: only damping,
tau_X = 21.05 s, tau_Y = 0.049 s), so any constant integrates into unbounded position drift:
12 s free-run gives X +7 mm, Y +25 mm against a ~22 um absorber signal, while the sprung yaw
(Theta) only parks a bounded offset. Training cannot see this: the window loss descends
monotonically while deployment degrades ~100x. Established framing: the K=0 axes are the
AMPLIFIER; the cause is the identification setup (no DC in the excitation, window blind to the
drift). Jan's meeting (section 6) asks whether part of the cause is instead in the system or the
estimator's zero-mean design assumptions.

## 3. Glossary (project idiom used below)

- **DC / the constant**: the time-mean of a signal = its 0 Hz Fourier component.
- **|mean|/rms**: fraction of a signal's magnitude that is constant (0 = zero-mean, 1 = pure
  constant; squared = fraction of power at DC).
- **K=0 axes**: X and Y, no stiffness to ground (free integrators with damping). Theta is sprung.
- **Logical vs stage frame**: logical = [X, Theta, Y]; stage = sensor coordinates [X1, X2, Y];
  stage positions = P^T @ logical. One transform, defined once in `drift-visual/config.py`.
- **Required correction (target)**: `w_perfect[k] = x_true[k+1] - baseline_step(x_true[k], u[k])`:
  the correction a perfect parallel block would output; checkpoint-independent; zero-mean here.
- **Encoder-IC transient**: the free-run starts from the encoder's state estimate; its velocity
  components (reconstructed from 17 position samples carrying 150 Hz ripple) are the weakest
  part (d17 mechanism). Explains the t = 0 spike in f08 and the parked offsets in f03.
- **nf window**: the BPTT/simulation-loss horizon in samples (400 = 0.1 s at 4 kHz).

## 6. Meeting notes, structured (Jan, 2026-07-15)

### Theme A: is the system itself zero-mean? (Jan's primary hypothesis)
- Run the signals before and after adding the extra mass (MSD): are they all still zero-mean?
  Can be checked directly in MATLAB/Simulink.
- The MSD may have an offset "just from the formulas": its mass hangs to one side of the Y beam.
  If the true system is not zero-mean, the ANN is CORRECTLY learning a non-zero-mean signal,
  which would explain the problem.
- Observed in this direction: running the baseline on data, the error rises to a new "stable"
  point and oscillates there (not zero-mean). One could correct that with a constant
  force/offset. The difference between baseline and true system: should it be zero-mean?
- A 50/50 split of the MSD mass might restore symmetry (but the offset is to one side of the
  beam, so possibly still not zero-mean).
- Physical expectation (Jan): driven by a zero-mean signal, the gantry should move back and
  forth around a fixed point (bounded oscillation). A system that instead walks away to one
  side is the signature of a non-zero-mean component somewhere in the chain. This is the
  system-level pass/fail intuition behind this theme; it feeds V1.

### Theme B: the estimator is designed for a zero-mean world
- Xavier initialization expects zero-mean, std-1 inputs and outputs.
- Our normalization, and the cost function, are likewise built around zero-mean (Gaussian)
  values.
- To work out: what is the impact of the system carrying a constant component that pulls it to
  one side?

### Theme C: where/when is the constant born during training?
- No clear reason why the ANN pushes into non-zero-mean behavior; find the reason.
- Look at the FIRST EPOCH, per parameter-update step: the ANN output is zero-mean before the
  first update (zero-init), so if a mean appears at the first steps there is a cause to find.
- Use a small enough lr that nothing explodes in the first steps.
- Do not fix the seed. Monte Carlo over seeds / Xavier draws (Jan's paper's approach; 500 models
  is a diagnosis, not a solution). Jan sends a parallel-batch script for this (runs are
  identified by a batch id).
- Jan also named the failure a TRAIN/TEST MISMATCH: the windowed training objective and the
  free-run deployment disagree (window loss improves while the free-run explodes), and the cost
  landscape is badly initialized relative to the zero-mean/std-1 world Xavier assumes (links
  Theme B to this theme). Substance already established in section 0 and f09; recorded here
  because Jan raised it explicitly in the meeting.

### Theme D: the window has no fading memory
- The K=0 position states have no restoring force, so within an nf window the error ACCUMULATES;
  the first steps carry the clean information.
- Look at what happens in the first ~100 steps of the window; does it explode after them?
- Consider lowering nf.

### The open question raised against the deck
f04b/f04c show the required correction is zero-mean, yet Theme A says an MSD offset would explain
the constant. Both cannot drive the same conclusion. Resolved in step V6 below.

## 7. Goal

Determine where the non-zero mean originates, among three distinguishable sources; each step
gates the next:

| id | source | consequence if true |
|----|--------|---------------------|
| G-A | system/data: the with-MSD system genuinely demands a non-zero-mean correction | the ANN is right; fix is model-side (baseline offset) |
| G-B | estimator assumptions: constant signal components violate the zero-mean design of normalization / init / cost | the constant is GENERATED by the estimator's operating point |
| G-C | training dynamics: the constant is born or accumulated during optimization in a direction the loss does not oppose | systematic early gradient vs. unconstrained wander; fix is the Layer-2 pin |

G-A first: its answer decides how to read everything else.

## 8. Verification plan

### V1: signal-level zero-mean audit (settles G-A)
- **Method**: per-channel time-mean vs std of `u`, `y`, `delta_a` for matched records with and
  without the MSD (with-MSD root and `baseline/` subfolder of
  `data/gantry/matlab/trajectory/augmentation/`); plus the mean of the required correction.
  Optionally repeat at 20 kHz native in Simulink (Jan's ask).
- **Pass/fail**: force-level and correction-level means near zero refutes G-A. Large means in
  POSITION channels are operating-point constants and feed V2, not G-A. System-level version of
  the same test (Jan's expectation, Theme A): under the zero-mean excitation the gantry should
  move back and forth around its operating point; a one-sided walk-away in the raw signals
  (about the operating point, not the operating-point constant itself) would be the G-A
  signature.
- **Status: measured 2026-07-15 (Python-side; Simulink confirmation open).**
  - `delta_a` mean 3e-9 vs std 2.2e-5 (|mean|/std ~ 1e-4), T1 and V1: zero-mean.
  - input forces `u`: |mean|/std <= 3e-4 all channels, with and without MSD: zero-mean.
  - required correction (f04b): |mean|/rms = 0.000 on every row; required dY mean (1.1e-8) is
    ~50x SMALLER than the ANN's parked DC (5.8e-7). **G-A refuted at first order.**
  - BUT position signal Y: mean -0.30 m (T1) / +0.10 m (V1) vs ripple std ~4e-6
    (|mean|/std 2e4..7e4). The raw signals are constants with microscopic ripple: input to V2.
  - **with/without-MSD mean comparison on the MEASURED signals (v1b, 2026-07-15;
    definition-independent, does not rely on how delta_a is defined)**: script
    `v1b_q_mean_with_without_msd.py`, figures `figures/v1b_dmean_{positions,velocities,forces}.png`,
    data `data/v1b_dmean.npz`. On all 12 matched T-records (native 20 kHz, paired 1 s segments),
    `dmean = mean(with) - mean(without)` is consistent with ZERO on every channel: positions
    (stage X1/X2/Y and logical X/Theta/Y), velocities (dX/dTheta/dY), and forces (F_X1/F_X2/F_Y);
    |dmean|/(2*SE) <= 0.31 everywhere (1.0 = 95% significance). Scales: position mean shifts
    1e-9..5e-8 m (vs ripple std ~4e-6 m and Y operating points 0.1..0.3 m); force mean shifts
    1e-4..8e-3 N (vs 36-45 N in-band RMS). Closed-loop logic: a static pull from the offset mass
    would have to appear as a mean shift in q and/or in u (the loop splits a constant disturbance
    between position offset and holding force); it appears in NEITHER. delta_a per record:
    |mean|/std 1.5e-5..1.6e-4 (the second-order M-rectification level). **G-A closed,
    definition-independent.** Caveat: the two datasets use different multisine bands by design
    (with-MSD 130-180 Hz, no-MSD 1-7 Hz); both zero-mean, so the means are comparable and the
    segment spread absorbs the ripple difference into the SE.
- **Formula-level check (why zero-mean is EXPECTED despite the L0 offset)**: the truth EOM is
  `Matlab-scripts/Augmentation/gantrySystemExtended.m` (the exact 8-state ODE behind the
  Simulink model `gantry_additional_state_2025a`; symbolic derivation in
  `Matlab-scripts/Augmentation/additional_state_lagrangian.m`; parameter block in
  `Matlab-scripts/Augmentation/data/gtd_config.m`, L0 = 0.10 m, fa = 150 Hz). In those
  formulas: `delta_a` is DEFINED as the deviation from the offset equilibrium (the absorber's
  absolute position along the beam is `Y + L0 + delta_a`); the only forces on `delta_a` are
  the restoring `ka*delta_a` and damping `ca*vdelta_a`, with NO constant term (no gravity
  along Y, spring rest position = the offset itself, so the spring is NOT preloaded); L0
  appears ONLY inside the mass matrix M (the Theta coupling `-ma*L0` and the inertia
  `ma*(Y+L0+delta_a)^2`), where it multiplies ACCELERATIONS. So the one-sided offset is
  geometric/inertial, not a force: `delta_a = 0` is an exact equilibrium, and zero-mean
  forcing gives zero-mean responses about the operating point. The only DC the formulas
  permit is second-order rectification of zero-mean oscillation through the nonlinear
  M(Y+L0+delta_a), which is what the audit measures (|mean|/std ~ 1e-4). A static offset in
  the OUTPUTS would require a constant force term the formulas do not contain. (Contrast: a
  VERTICAL absorber under gravity would preload the spring and genuinely shift the mean;
  this one is horizontal.)

  definitive no-persistent-force-gap test on a K=0 axis); f10 (input spectrum, zero at DC).

### V1f: open-loop DC + AC excitation, plant with vs without the MSD (MATLAB; closes G-A on DC-exciting data)
- **Script**: `v1f_dc_excitation_openloop.m` in THIS folder; figures `figures/v1f_<axis>_dcac_response.png`,

- **Why this exists (beyond v1d)**: v1d replayed the recorded multisine (U0 = 0), which is
  structurally blind to a STATIC DC (identifiability, methodology doc §5). A pure-DC test in turn is
  blind to the AC-driven second-order rectification DC (methodology doc §6). The DC question splits
  into TWO mechanisms, and one waveform must excite both: (A) STATIC-GAIN DC (needs a sustained
  offset); (B) RECTIFICATION DC = the truth-only `ma*(Y+L0+delta_a)^2` term rectifying oscillation
  into a nonzero-mean output (needs an AC tone at the MSD resonance fa = 150 Hz). B is the mechanism
  that bears on the ANN, which trains on the 130-180 Hz multisine.
- **Method**: drive ONE logical axis at a time, open-loop, SAME input to both plants, with a
  sustained OFFSET plus a sustained 150 Hz TONE (raised-cosine onset), from four initial Y
  (-0.30/0/+0.10/+0.30 m). Read the steady DC by HARMONIC LEAST SQUARES (constant + first two
  harmonics of fa) over the tail, on ALL THREE logical channels (dX, Theta, dY) for every drive, not
  just the driven one, plus `delta_a` tail RMS (excitation proof). Rationale for all-channel: B lands
  in Theta (the rectifying quadratic sits in the Theta inertia M(2,2)), so reading only the driven
  axis is blind to it; the decisive combination is drive-Y (max `delta_a`), read-Theta.
- **Status: RUN 2026-07-17 (user, MATLAB), read-out extended and re-run same day. Verdict: G-A
  refuted on DC-exciting data covering BOTH mechanisms.**
  - **Mechanism A (static-gain DC) = 0**: `baseDC` and `truthDC` on the driven axis are identical to
    ~1e-7 relative on every axis and every Y0. (Both sit below the analytic 3e-2/1e-3 only by
    incomplete K=0 settling at 5 s, identical for both plants, not a finding.) Matches the algebra:
    at steady state qddot = 0, M drops out of C*qdot+K*q=F and the MSD spring is unpreloaded.
  - **Mechanism B (delta_a^2 rectification) measured and negligible**: only the Y drive excites the
    MSD (`delta_a` RMS 1.67e-5; X/Theta drives 3e-11 / 2e-9). On that run, read on Theta (B's home
    channel), `B[Theta] = 3.1e-10 rad`. This IS the second-order rectification, confirmed by SCALING:
    `<delta_a^2> = (1.67e-5)^2 = 2.8e-10`, matching the measured 3.1e-10 to ~10% (amplitude-squared,
    exactly the §6 Volterra prediction).
  - **Largest DC anywhere = the static mass-matrix asymmetry, also negligible**: the biggest
    truth-minus-baseline DC in the whole table is Theta-drive `B[dX] = -1.1e-7 m/s` and X-drive

  - **Conclusion**: across every drive x every channel, including the genuinely-MSD-excited case read
    on its home channel, the largest DC the truth carries that the baseline lacks is ~1e-7 (rel
    ~1e-5), 5+ orders below anything the ANN could learn as a meaningful correction. The physics does
    NOT justify the ANN's DC; it originates in the estimator/training (G-B / G-C). This is the
    on-target measurement of the §4/§6 formula-level expectation.
