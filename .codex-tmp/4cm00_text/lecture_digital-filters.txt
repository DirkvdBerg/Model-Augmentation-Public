  It is easy to derive the impulse response from the z-transform:
      I   write H (z) in the form H (z) = c0 + c1 z −1 + c2 z −2 + c3 z −3 + . . .
      I   i.e. eliminate denominator term

  Difference equation then becomes:

            y(k ) = c0 u(k ) + c1 u(k −1) + c2 u(k −2) + c3 u(k −3) + . . .

  Impulse input: u(k ) = 1 for k = 0, and u(k ) = 0 for k 6= 0.
      I   At time step k = i only u(k − i ) contributes to output
      I   So coefficient ci determines output: y(i ) = ci

  It is easy to derive the impulse response from the z-transform:
      I   write H (z) in the form H (z) = c0 + c1 z −1 + c2 z −2 + c3 z −3 + . . .

  Anti-causal systems                                                      25/39


  Ordinary causal systems:
      I   output only depends on present and past input values:
          y(k ) = f (u(k ), u(k −1), u(k −2), . . .)
  Anti-causal systems:
      I   output also depends on future input values
      I   ‘predicts’ future
      I   do not occur in reality

  How to recognize recognize anti-causal systems?
      I   Impulse response of H (z):
          contains positive powers of shift operator, i.e. z, z 2 , etc.
      I   Transfer function H (z):
          numerator has higher degree than denominator,

  Tustin with prewarping                                                   33/39


  Extension of the Tustin discretization:
      I   Prewarping matches frequency responses of C (z) and C (s) at a
          specified frequency ωp
      I   Combination of time and frequency domain techniques
  Tustin substitution changes into:
                                                   ωp        z −1
                                          s→               ·
                                               tan(ωp T /2) z + 1

  As a consequence:
                                                                     
                                       C (s = j ωp ) = C z = e j ωp T

      I   Completely frequency based

      I   Determine poles pi and zeros qi of C (s)
