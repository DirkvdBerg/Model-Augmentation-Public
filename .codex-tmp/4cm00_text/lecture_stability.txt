  Simplifying Nyquist                                                                   26/36
      ▶    A necessary requirement for stable closed loop:
          Nyquist: (-1,0) is on the left side of L (j ω) = H (j ω)C (j ω),
                   where 0 < ω < ∞ (only positive frequencies)
                                  ⇕
          Bode: slope at first crossover frequency ωco is approx -1,
                 i.e. ∠L (j ω) at ωco is between −180◦ and 0◦
      ▶ When there are no more crossovers and Bode gain-phase holds,

        then this is also sufficient, i.e. when L (s):
               •   is stable and minimum phase (all poles and zeros in LHP),
               •   and |L (j ω)| < 1, ∀ω > ωco
          then all ‘rotations’ have been compensated by the time L (j ω) hits
          the unit disc, and L (j ω) stays inside this disc for ω > ωco .
      ▶   If |L (j ω)| = 1 at multiple frequencies, then also count the possible
          encirclements between subsequent crossovers.
  Crossover ωco : Frequency where |L (j ω)| = 1, i.e. where L (j ω) crosses 0dB line.

  Robustness                                                                    29/36


  Robustness: the quality of being able to withstand changes or
       variations in circumstances or dynamics
      ▶   A robust closed loop stays stable after small variations in dynamics
      ▶   A Nyquist curve far from -1 is more robust than a curve close to -1

  Robustness margins:
  Phase margin (PM): Phase lead above −180◦ at the frequency where
        |L (j ω)| = 0dB
  Gain margin (GM): The inverse of GM is the gain at the frequency where
        ∠L (j ω) = −180◦
  Modulus margin (MM): The inverse of the smallest distance of L (j ω)
       over all ω to the point -1

      ▶   choose desired crossover frequency ωco
      ▶   tune L (j ω) for low frequencies ω ≤ ωco
               • Bode: change slope of L (j ω) at ωco to approx -1,
                        i.e. create phase between −180◦ and 0◦
                 Nyquist: leave (-1,0) on the left of L (j ω)
               • if RHP zeros or poles in open loop:

                        check encirclements in Nyquist
                                ⇓
                        if unstable, try different ωco
      ▶   tune L (j ω) for higher frequencies ω > ωco
               •   check phase at other 0dB crossings and correct if necessary
      ▶   make adjustments to meet desired margins

  For stable minimum-phase open loops:
      ▶   Use simplified Nyquist criterion
    ⇒ Leave -1 to the left (i.e. phase lead above −180◦ at cross-over)
  For unstable open loops (P RHP poles):
      ▶   Use full Nyquist criterion
      ▶   Create P counterclockwise encirclements (i.e. extra phase)
      ▶   Use phase lead of RHP pole to create extra phase outside unit disc
    ⇒ Choose crossover ωco above unstable poles
  For non-minimum phase open loops:
      ▶   Use full and/or simplified Nyquist criterion
      ▶   RHP zero creates undesired phase lag
      ▶   Allow this phase lag only inside the unit disc
    ⇒ Choose crossover ωco below RHP zeros
