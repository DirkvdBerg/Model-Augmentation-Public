Iterate Averaging Paradigms: Polyak-Ruppert and BeyondWhen stochastic gradient descent and its momentum variants approach the global minimum of a strictly convex basin, the inherent variance of the mini-batching prevents the optimizer from settling precisely on the minimum. Instead, the parameter iterates form a stationary distribution, oscillating randomly around the true optimal parameter vector. Iterate averaging methods capitalize on this behavior by computing the mathematical average of the parameter vectors over time, effectively filtering out the zero-mean sampling noise.4.1 Mathematical Foundations of Polyak-Ruppert AveragingThe classic Polyak-Ruppert (PR) averaging scheme computes a uniform running average of all iterates generated during the optimization process. If the parameter vector at epoch $t$ is denoted as $w_t$, the uniform PR average at epoch $n$ is defined as:
$$\bar{w}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n w_t$$
In the context of the 13-parameter system identification task, theoretical analyses demonstrate that this averaging scheme exerts a profound regularizing effect. For linear least-squares formulations and locally convex physical parameter spaces, PR averaging is asymptotically equivalent to Ridge Regression (Tikhonov regularization).However, uniform averaging incorporates all early iterates from the beginning of the training run, when the parameters were highly inaccurate and rapidly traversing the initial steep gradients. To address this, modern implementations utilize Tail Averaging, which only begins computing the average after the optimizer has reached the asymptotic phase. Given the user's setup, this corresponds to the "plateau phase," specifically after the ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler has dropped the learning rate toward the minimum boundary of 1e-5.Advanced variants from the recent literature propose geometric Polyak-Ruppert averaging, a weighted scheme where the weights decay geometrically, placing different emphasis on different stages of the plateau phase. The geometrically weighted average is defined as:
$$\bar{w}_n = \frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^n (1 - \eta \lambda)^k} \sum_{t=0}^n (1 - \eta \lambda)^t w_t$$
where $\lambda$ acts as a specific tuning parameter that dictates the decay rate. This method yields finite-sample bounds that match the optimal known results for regularized stochastic gradient methods, accelerating convergence without requiring explicit regularization penalties.4.2 Comparative Efficacy: Polyak-Ruppert vs. Best-Epoch TrackingThe user specifically queries whether Polyak-Ruppert iterate averaging over the plateau phase is considered superior to best-epoch tracking on a fixed held-out set, and under what conditions each approach prevails.For the specific 13-parameter, physics-constrained validation study outlined in the prompt, Polyak-Ruppert tail averaging is unequivocally superior to best-epoch tracking.The superiority of PR averaging in this context relies on three interacting factors:Low Dimensionality and Convexity: The mathematical guarantees of PR averaging are strongest when optimizing low-dimensional, locally convex parameter spaces. In massive deep neural networks with millions of parameters, averaging weights across different epochs can destructively bridge distinct, incompatible local minima, destroying the model. In a 13-parameter model, once the learning rate decays to the plateau phase, all iterates are guaranteed to reside within the single, unified basin of attraction corresponding to the physical ground truth. Averaging these iterates perfectly cancels out the noise generated by the multiple shooting batches.Computational Efficiency: PR averaging requires virtually zero additional computational overhead. Specialized Checkpoint Architectures for Multiple Shooting Parameter RecoveryThe multiple shooting method intrinsically redefines the structure of the gradient-based optimization problem. In standard parameter estimation, the objective function depends solely on the global physical parameters. In a multiple shooting framework, the optimizer must simultaneously estimate the 13 global physical parameters and the latent initial states of every short segment included in the batch.This creates a dual optimization space where the optimizer is attempting to minimize the data fidelity loss (the distance between the model output and the measurements within each segment) while concurrently satisfying a continuity constraint. The continuity constraint demands that the terminal state of segment $i$ exactly matches the estimated initial state of segment $i+1$. The discrepancy between these two points is defined as the consistency error or defect.5.1 Consistency-Error Thresholding as a Checkpoint CriterionThe literature specifically addressing PyTorch-based differentiable multiple shooting emphasizes that traditional metrics like training MSE are blind to the underlying physics of the integration. An epoch may display an incredibly low training MSE simply because the optimizer has allowed the short segments to become entirely disconnected from one another, fitting the data locally while utterly failing to produce a continuous global trajectory.Therefore, the most robust, physics-informed criterion for checkpoint selection in multiple shooting architectures is Consistency-Error Thresholding. Under this paradigm, the checkpoint selection algorithm evaluates two distinct metrics at the end of an epoch: the normalized data fidelity MSE and the global consistency error.A parameter vector is only considered a candidate for selection if the consistency error falls below a strict numerical tolerance $\tau$ (e.g., $10^{-4}$). This threshold guarantees that the segments can be seamlessly stitched together into a physically valid, continuous trajectory. Among the epochs that satisfy this physical continuity constraint, the algorithm then selects the epoch with the lowest validated data fidelity error, or begins applying Polyak-Ruppert averaging exclusively on the iterates that meet the consistency requirement.5.2 Homotopy Optimization and Sequential CheckpointingState-of-the-art frameworks from 2022 to 2026 frequently employ Homotopy Optimization (or continuation methods) to manage the dual optimization space of multiple shooting. In a homotopy framework, the objective function is defined as a weighted sum of the data fidelity loss and the consistency error, formulated as:
$$\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + k \cdot \mathcal{L}_{consistency}$$
The coupling strength $k$ acts as the homotopy parameter. The training run is divided into distinct phases. In the early epochs, $k$ is kept exceptionally low, allowing the optimizer to freely adjust the initial segment states to fit the data without worrying about continuity. As the 2000-epoch run progresses, the parameter $k$ is exponentially scaled, forcing the segments to connect and demanding that the 13 global parameters assume the burden of accurately modeling the full trajectory.In a homotopy-based multiple shooting setup, checkpoint selection strategies are rigidly phased. Any parameter vector generated during the early or middle phases of the homotopy schedule is automatically disqualified from selection, as the physics model is operating in a disjointed, non-continuous state. Checkpoint tracking or iterate averaging is only initiated during the final phase of the schedule, when the consistency constraint is fully enforced, and the physical integrity of the LPV-LFR model is guaranteed.6. Due to the phenomenon of "clean priority learning," the optimization trajectory rapidly converges toward the true physical parameters, ignoring the high-frequency measurement noise. If the training were halted at this point, the parameter recovery would be highly accurate.However, if the optimization is permitted to continue for 2000 epochs into the deep asymptotic plateau, the optimizer will eventually exhaust the clean signal and begin aggressively minimizing the residual data mismatch caused by the measurement noise. To minimize this noise-induced loss, the gradient descent algorithm will contort the 13 physical parameters, dragging them away from the true physical ground truth and into statistically distorted configurations. The loss continues to decrease, but the physical accuracy of the parameters degrades significantly.6.2 The Catastrophe of Iterate Averaging Under NoiseBecause of semi-convergence, the strategies that proved optimal for clean data become catastrophic when measurement noise is present.
If the system uses ReduceLROnPlateau and Polyak-Ruppert tail averaging over the final epochs, it will perfectly average a set of parameter iterates that have already been thoroughly corrupted by the noise. The entire plateau phase represents a regime where the optimizer is attempting to fit the stochastic measurement error, rendering the final parameter vector physically invalid.When measurement noise is present, the optimal checkpoint is never the last epoch, nor is it the average of the late-stage epochs. The optimal parameter vector is achieved mid-training, precisely before the onset of semi-convergence. To capture this optimal state, the training process must utilize rigorous early stopping methodologies specifically designed for inverse problems.6.3 The Morozov Discrepancy PrincipleThe gold-standard early stopping methodology for gradient descent in noisy inverse problems is the Morozov Discrepancy Principle.The Discrepancy Principle asserts a fundamental logical constraint: an optimizer should never attempt to fit the training data more accurately than the inherent uncertainty of the measurements themselves. If the variance of the measurement noise $\delta^2$ is known or can be estimated prior to training, the Discrepancy Principle dictates that the gradient descent optimization must be halted the exact moment the training residual (the data fidelity loss) falls to the level of the noise floor.Mathematically, if the noisy observations are denoted as $Y^\delta$ and the physics model output is $A(w_t)$, the stopping criterion is defined as the first epoch $t^*$ where the squared residual norm satisfies:$$\| A(w_{t^*}) - Y^\delta \|^2 \approx \tau \delta^2$$
where $\tau > 1$ is a safety constant.Implementing this in the PyTorch multiple shooting loop involves tracking a moving average of the per-epoch stochastic loss. When this smoothed loss curve intersects the predetermined noise threshold $\tau \delta^2$, the current epoch's parameter vector is checkpointed, and the 2000-epoch training run is immediately aborted. Theoretical guarantees established between 2022 and 2025 demonstrate that early stopping via the classical discrepancy principle yields minimax-optimal convergence rates for parameter recovery in stochastic gradient descent architectures.6.4 The L-Curve Method for Unknown Noise VarianceIf the variance of the measurement noise is unknown, the Discrepancy Principle cannot be directly implemented, as there is no numerical floor to trigger the halting condition. State-of-the-Art Optimization Alternatives (2018–2026)While Polyak-Ruppert averaging and Discrepancy Principle early stopping represent the gold standards for their respective noise environments, the literature from 2022 to 2026 has introduced advanced optimization algorithms that algorithmically bypass the complexities of epoch selection entirely. While PR averaging passively records iterates as the learning rate decays on a plateau, SWA actively manipulates the optimization landscape to discover wider, more generalizable optima.The user’s current architecture relies heavily on ReduceLROnPlateau. In highly stiff continuous-time physical simulations, an exponentially decaying learning rate can easily trap the parameter vector in a sharp, fragile local minimum, or maroon the parameters in a "flat valley" where the gradients vanish prematurely.SWA remedies this by replacing the decaying plateau with a Cyclical or High Constant (CHC) learning rate schedule.The standard Adam optimizer runs until it reaches the general vicinity of the minimum.Instead of decaying the learning rate, SWA elevates the learning rate to a constant, elevated value or initiates a cyclical schedule. This sustained energy forces the parameter vector to aggressively explore the outer boundaries of the loss basin, preventing it from settling into localized topological anomalies caused by the multiple shooting batch variance.The parameters generated at the end of each exploration cycle are mathematically averaged.For a 13-parameter model, the averaging of these boundary exploration points invariably secures a final parameter vector that is perfectly centered in the widest, most robust physical basin. Recent adaptations, such as Periodic SWA (PSWA), initialize the averaging sequences much earlier in the training run, capturing multiple high-quality local optima and dramatically outperforming standard Adam in generalizing physical parameters.7.2 Schedule-Free Optimization (Defazio et al., 2024)The most paradigm-shifting advancement in gradient-based parameter recovery is the Schedule-Free Optimizer, introduced by Defazio et al. in 2024. This methodology elegantly resolves the tension between early stopping, iterate averaging, and hyperparameter tuning by rendering learning rate schedules obsolete.The fragility of the user's ReduceLROnPlateau configuration (patience=125, factor=0.5) lies in its susceptibility to the stochastic noise of the 8-segment multiple shooting batches. A spuriously bad sequence of batches can trigger a premature learning rate collapse, while a spuriously good sequence can delay necessary decays. Furthermore, traditional Polyak-Ruppert averaging is flawed because near the conclusion of a 2000-epoch run, the uniform or geometric average is still heavily polluted by the most recent, highly noisy gradient steps.Schedule-Free optimization entirely unifies momentum dynamics and iterate averaging through a novel interpolation technique. It eschews the explicit stopping time $T$ and the decaying learning rate. Instead, the algorithm simultaneously maintains two distinct parameter sequences: a base sequence $z_t$ that actively explores the loss landscape, and an evaluation sequence $y_t$ defined as a momentum-interpolated average:
$$y_t = (1 - \beta) z_t + \beta y_{t-1}$$
where $\beta$ is a momentum hyperparameter (typically bounded near 0.9).Transformative Impact on Physical Parameter Recovery:Eradication of Epoch Selection: The primary crisis of selecting the "best epoch" vanishes. Because the evaluation sequence $y_t$ continuously updates and naturally tracks the Pareto optimal frontier of loss versus time, the algorithm ensures that the latest iterate of $y_t$ is mathematically and empirically the optimal parameter vector.Immunity to Multiple Shooting Variance: By modestly boosting the contribution of recent gradients through the $\beta$ parameter interpolation, the Schedule-Free optimizer circumvents the sluggish convergence typical of primal averaging while completely neutralizing the late-stage instability that plagues traditional Polyak-Ruppert tracking.Operational Stability: It removes all hyperparameter tuning associated with step decays or patience thresholds, allowing the 13 parameters to converge smoothly regardless of the inherent stochasticity of the segment sampling.7.3 Lookahead OptimizersFinally, the Lookahead optimizer represents a highly effective meta-learning wrapper designed to stabilize physics-informed parameter identification in chaotic or noisy environments. Lookahead mitigates the erratic behavior of stochastic gradient descent by maintaining two distinct sets of weights: "fast weights" and "slow weights."Within the context of the LPV-LFR model, the fast weights (driven by the standard Adam optimizer) explore the highly variable, noisy loss landscape generated by the random batching of the multiple shooting segments. After $k$ exploratory steps, the slow weights are linearly interpolated toward the final position of the fast weights. The fast weights absorb the high-frequency variance of the stochastic batches, while the slow weights track a profoundly smoothed, highly stable trajectory toward the physical ground truth. In this architecture, checkpoint selection is trivialized: the researcher simply extracts the final state of the slow weights at the conclusion of the 2000-epoch run, completely disregarding the erratic trajectory of the fast weights.8.
