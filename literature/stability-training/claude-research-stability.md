# Stability-Constrained Neural Augmentation of a Free-Integrator Physics Model: Method Survey and the Marginal-Mode Gap

**Bottom line:** No published method satisfies all four of your requirements simultaneously (knowledge-free, friction-permitting, marginal-preserving, non-drifting) — this is a genuine gap, and the correct solution must be assembled from existing building blocks around a dissipativity/negative-imaginary supply rate applied to a *positive-semidefinite* (marginal-allowing) storage function, which no released neural construction currently provides.

## TL;DR
- **The four-requirement method does not exist off-the-shelf.** The only classical framework that natively handles a pole at the origin in collocated force-to-position mechanical systems — nonlinear Negative-Imaginary (NI)/counterclockwise dynamics with the free-body-dynamics extension (Mabrok–Kallapur–Petersen–Lanzon, IEEE TAC 2014; Ghallab–Petersen; Shi–Vladimirov–Petersen) — is the right theory, but its two neural instantiations (Shi–Wang–Manchester "NINODE," arXiv:2504.19497; Cheng–Wang–Manchester, arXiv:2404.12554) both force a *strictly positive-definite* storage function (a PLNet Hamiltonian "lower- and upper-bounded by quadratic functions"), which structurally precludes a preserved pole at the origin — and NINODE constrains a *controller*, not a forward model.
- **The correct forward-model stability condition** is a dissipativity/NI supply rate on the learned block routed to the integrator rows: require the learned free-axis force to be dissipative with respect to velocity (V̇ ≤ F·q̇, with V *positive semidefinite* and flat along the rigid-body coordinate so the pole stays exactly at the origin). This forbids the energy-injecting DC drift while permitting Coulomb/velocity-dependent friction (F·q̇ ≤ 0 including F(0)≠0 as a set-valued map) — knowledge-free, because it is a property of the block's structure, not of the data.
- **Most pragmatic route now:** (i) factor the integrator out structurally (learn the residual as force/acceleration on the velocity row, integrate position exactly — Tustin-net/SUBNET style); (ii) split the learned free-axis force into a *dissipative friction channel* (maximal-monotone/set-valued, nonzero at rest) and a *bounded-integral channel* (residual written as an exact time-derivative of a bounded function, so its integral — hence the position ramp — is bounded regardless of horizon). This is knowledge-free, friction-permitting, marginal-preserving, and non-drifting, but it is a synthesis of building blocks, not a single citable method.

## Key Findings

**1. Contraction-based methods (RENs, stable-by-design LPV/subspace, the Györök/Hoekstra LFR augmentation) all fail requirement 3.** Your cited paper is real: the LFR-augmentation line is Hoekstra, Györök, Tóth & Schoukens, "Learning-based augmentation of first-principle models: A linear fractional representation-based approach," arXiv:2602.17297 (submitted to *Automatica*), with the ECC-2025 predecessor arXiv:2404.01901 (Hoekstra, Verhoek, Tóth, Schoukens). The follow-up arXiv:2604.11421 is authored by **Bendegúz Györök, Roel Drenth, Chris Verhoek, Tamás Péni, Maarten Schoukens & Roland Tóth** (HUN-REN Budapest / TU Eindhoven / UPenn); its §4 "Stable-by-construction model augmentation," §4.1 "Contracting model property," confirms the guarantee is via **contraction**. Contraction ⇒ incremental exponential stability ⇒ every mode strictly inside the LHP, which actively damps your zero-stiffness axis into a stable one and corrupts the physics. Recurrent Equilibrium Networks (Revay, Wang & Manchester, arXiv:2104.05942, IEEE TAC 69(5), 2024) state it plainly: "All models in the proposed class are contracting — a strong form of nonlinear stability — and models can satisfy prescribed incremental integral quadratic constraints (IQC), including Lipschitz bounds and incremental passivity." The passivity is available, but *only bundled with contraction* (metric P≻0). Fails req. 3.

**2. The NI / counterclockwise framework is the right classical theory** because it was built for force-to-position (collocated) mechanical systems whose transfer functions have relative degree two and poles at the origin (free-body motion). Mabrok, Kallapur, Petersen & Lanzon, "Generalizing Negative Imaginary Systems Theory to Include Free Body Dynamics," IEEE TAC 59(10):2692–2707, 2014 (arXiv:1305.1079): "A new NI system definition is given, which allows for flexible structure systems with colocated force actuators and position sensors, and with free body motion… necessary and sufficient conditions are provided for the stability of positive feedback control systems where the plant is NI according to the new definition and the controller is strictly negative imaginary." It admits **up to two poles at the origin** — exactly your rigid-body mode.

**3. The nonlinear NI supply rate is friction-compatible and knowledge-free.** Ghallab, Mabrok & Petersen (CDC 2018; *Automatica* 2025, arXiv:2201.00144) and Shi, Vladimirov & Petersen (2021) define a nonlinear system as NI if there is a **positive-semidefinite** C¹ storage function V with V̇(x) ≤ u(t)ᵀẏ(t). For force input u=F and position output y=q, ẏ=q̇, so the supply rate F·q̇ *is* mechanical power. A dissipative learned force (F·q̇ ≤ V̇ ≤ 0) forbids the energy-injecting drift but permits Coulomb friction (F·q̇ ≤ 0 with F(0)≠0). This is the single existing definition whose storage function is only *semidefinite* — the one formally compatible with a pole at the origin (req. 1 + req. 3 in principle).

**4. The two existing neural NI/passive constructions both force strict positive-definiteness and hence exclude the marginal mode** (verified against primary sources):
- *NINODE* (Shi, Wang, Manchester, arXiv:2504.19497) is a **controller**: "We propose a neural control method to provide guaranteed stabilization for mechanical systems using a novel negative imaginary neural ordinary differential equation (NINODE) **controller**." Its Hamiltonian is a PLNet H₂(x₂)=½|𝒢(x₂)|² with 𝒢 bi-Lipschitz ⇒ H₂(x₂) ≥ ½γ²|x₂|² (strictly PD). Theorem 2 permits the *given plant* to be lossless (R₁ ≥ 0, semidefinite) but forces the *learned controller* to be strictly dissipative (R₂ > 0), and its Assumption 3 explicitly "rules out the case that the cascade… has DC gain 1" — i.e. it re-imposes the strict condition and abandons the marginal case. Fails req. 3 for the learned block; wrong object (controller).
- *Learning Stable and Passive Neural Differential Equations* (Cheng, Wang, Manchester, arXiv:2404.12554) is a **forward model** ẋ=f(x,u), y=h(x) — the right object — but "the Hamiltonian is lower- and upper-bounded by quadratic functions… also positive definite w.r.t. either a known or learnable equilibrium," i.e. 0.5µ²|x−x⋆|² ≤ H(x), µ>0. Strictly PD ⇒ isolated asymptotically/exponentially stable equilibrium, no free integrator. Fails req. 3.

**5. Port-Hamiltonian / Lagrangian neural nets with dissipation fail requirement 2 at rest.** D-HNN (arXiv:2201.10085), dissipative SymODEN, and port-Hamiltonian neural ODEs on Lie groups (Duong et al., arXiv:2401.09520; Desai et al., arXiv:2107.08024) all model dissipation via a Rayleigh function or R(x)⪰0 multiplying velocity, giving F = −R(x)q̇ → 0 as q̇→0. This kills Coulomb/static friction. Fails req. 2.

**6. ISS/Lipschitz feedforward (Bolderman, Butler, Lazar, *Control Engineering Practice* 2024, S096706612400011X; arXiv:2301.08568) is a feedforward *controller* result that does not forbid a DC offset.** A constant force is perfectly Lipschitz, so bounding gain/slope does not stop the ramp; and the ISS analysis assumes the unforced system is asymptotically stable, which a free integrator is not. Fails req. 4 for your case; wrong object.

**7. Coordinate factorization is established practice and fixes the encoder pathology, but by itself does not forbid a DC force.** Tustin-nets (Forgione & Piga, arXiv:1911.01310; rotary-pendulum account arXiv:2408.12266) fix the position update to the *exact* trapezoidal integral of velocity and let the net learn only the velocity increment (acceleration). This makes the integrator structural and removes the reconstructability problem — but a persistent DC acceleration still ramps velocity and doubly-ramps position. Factorization must be *combined* with a DC-behavior constraint.

## Details

### The problem restated in supply-rate terms
Your failure mode is a nonzero time-mean (DC) component in the learned force on the integrator row. On m·q̈ + c·q̇ = F, a constant residual F̄ injects power F̄·q̇ of indefinite sign and drives a ramp ∝ F̄·t². The 0.1 s window makes the ½(F̄/m)t² term ~10⁴× smaller than over a 10 s rollout, so the short-window simulation loss is blind to it. Precisely what you need: the learned block, seen as a map on the free-axis velocity (or collocated force), must be **dissipative/passive/negative-imaginary with a storage function that is flat along the rigid-body position coordinate** — positive *semi*definite, with a zero eigenvalue exactly on the integrator mode. Strict positive-definiteness (what every existing neural construction imposes) is exactly what must be avoided.

### The free-body NI interconnection condition (the marginal-case stability theorem)
When the plant has poles at the origin, the classical finite DC-gain test λ_max(G(0)Ḡ(0)) < 1 is undefined (G(0) infinite). Mabrok et al. 2014 replace it via a Laurent expansion of G(s) about s=0: residues G₂ = lim_{s→0} s²G(s) (double-pole/rigid-body residue, required Hermitian PSD, factored G₂ = JJᵀ), G₁ = lim_{s→0} s(G(s) − G₂/s²) (single-pole/frictional free-body term), and G₀ (finite DC part). The positive-feedback interconnection of an NI plant G with a strictly-NI controller Ḡ is stable iff (clean special case, their Corollary 2) **Ḡ(0) < 0**; more generally, an *instantaneous/DC-gain sign condition on the controller projected onto the free-body subspace* (JᵀḠ(0)J < 0, or F₁ᵀḠ(0)F₁ < 0), **plus** a *finite-part gain condition* generalizing λ_max(Ḡ(0)G₀) < 1 to I − N^{½}G₀N^{½} > 0. Crucially, **the map norm is allowed to equal 1 at DC on the free-body subspace (not strictly < 1)** — exactly the marginal-preserving property you need. This exists only in the linear theory; the nonlinear NINODE paper's Assumption 3 re-imposes strict DC gain ≠ 1 and thus abandons it.

### Dissipativity/passivity supply-rate machinery you can attach to a net
- **General (Q,S,R) supply rates in learned models.** RENs (arXiv:2104.05942) admit prescribed incremental IQCs including passivity via an *unconstrained direct parameterization*; NodeRENs (Martinelli, Galimberti, Manchester, Furieri, Ferrari-Trecate, arXiv:2304.02976) give an "unconstrained parametrization of dissipative and contracting neural ODEs" with a specified (Q,S,R) supply rate. **In all of these the storage/metric P is P≻0 and the model is contracting**, so as published they cannot host a marginal mode. What you need is the passivity supply rate *decoupled* from contraction (a passive-but-not-contracting REN with semidefinite storage). Passivity is a (Q,S,R) IQC that does not require Q<0, so the REN framework can in principle express it, but no released parameterization drops the P≻0 contraction metric to P⪰0 — a concrete, bounded extension.
- **Nonlinear NI storage-function definition** (Ghallab–Petersen; Shi–Vladimirov–Petersen): V̇ ≤ uᵀẏ with V only *positive semidefinite* — the sole existing definition allowing a flat storage direction (pole at origin). The neural instantiations regressed this to strict PD (PLNet) for optimizability. Re-deriving a PLNet-like *semidefinite* Hamiltonian (bi-Lipschitz in the damped coordinates, affine/flat in the rigid-body coordinate) is the missing construction.

### Friction at rest: keeping Coulomb without injecting drift
- **The naive dissipative factorization F = −φ(x,q̇)·q̇ with φ≥0 fails req. 2** because it →0 at q̇=0. Static/Coulomb friction is nonzero at rest.
- **The correct object is a maximal-monotone/set-valued map.** Coulomb friction is F ∈ −F_c·Sign(q̇) (Sign(0)=[−1,1]), a maximal monotone operator, dissipative (F·q̇ ≤ 0) *including* at rest. Mature learning literature exists: maximal-monotone operator networks built from nonexpansive layers via the resolvent (Combettes–Pesquet-style, arXiv:2012.13247), constrained-monotonic nets (arXiv:2205.11775; the Duke ACC'22 monotone-dynamics net), and set-valued Lur'e / dissipative-differential-inclusion theory (Brogliato; Haddad's dissipative differential inclusions) providing passivity + maximal-monotone-feedback well-posedness. A smooth surrogate F = −φ(x,q̇)·tanh(q̇/ε) is nonzero-at-rest only as ε→0; the set-valued route is the principled fix and stays dissipative by construction. A dedicated recent construction — "Variational Neural Networks for Observable Thermodynamics" (arXiv:2509.09899) — derives "Dissipative neural networks" approximating *any* dissipative force with F·v ≤ 0 by construction (with a small-velocity regularity assumption); PINN-based LuGre/Coulomb identifiers (arXiv:2504.12441) learn the nonzero-at-rest structure but without a hard dissipativity guarantee.

### The zero-DC-gain / bounded-integral trick (the most direct drift fix)
The cleanest *knowledge-free* way to forbid the ramp without forbidding instantaneous force: make the integrator-row residual an **exact time-derivative of a bounded function**, g_row = d/dt[ψ(x)] with ψ bounded (e.g. ψ = tanh∘net). Then ∫₀ᵀ g_row dt = ψ(x(T)) − ψ(x(0)) is bounded for all T, so its contribution to the position ramp is bounded regardless of horizon — the DC gain from net to integrated state is exactly zero, yet instantaneous force is unconstrained and can be nonzero at rest. Equivalently, parameterize the free-axis learned force as **F = −∂V/∂q with V a bounded potential** (gradient-of-bounded-potential), bounding accumulated impulse. This is the structural analogue of "penalize the mean force" but is knowledge-free (a property of the parameterization, not the residual), so it does not suppress real friction — friction enters through a *separate* velocity-channel dissipative term. This construction does not appear in the mechatronic-augmentation literature and is, in my assessment, the highest-leverage novel building block for your problem. It parallels conserved-quantity/flux-form constraints used in learned PDE/weather emulators to bound long-horizon drift by construction (constraint-projected neural PDE solvers, arXiv:2511.03578; flux-form conservative surrogates, arXiv:2602.01941; ECO energy-constrained operator learning, arXiv:2512.01984).

### Encoder/identification note for integrating systems
The SUBNET/deepSI lineage (Beintema, Schoukens, Tóth, *Automatica* 2023, arXiv:2210.14816; CT version arXiv:2204.09405) assumes the baseline is incrementally exponentially stable so the encoder has a finite reconstructability map — a pure integrator violates this (absolute position is unobservable from a finite past I/O window). Established practice for integrating systems is to **work in incremental/differenced coordinates** (learn velocity/acceleration, integrate position exactly) — exactly the Tustin-net factorization — which fixes both the encoder issue and localizes drift to the velocity channel where a dissipativity constraint applies cleanly.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Make the integrator exact and move the residual to force/acceleration coordinates (do first).** Route the network onto the *velocity (acceleration) row* of each zero-stiffness axis and fix the position row to the exact integral of velocity (Tustin/trapezoidal), à la Forgione–Piga. This removes the encoder-reconstructability pathology and localizes drift to the DC component of the learned acceleration. *Benchmark:* free-run RMSE over ≥10 s rollouts should improve and the position-ramp diagnostic should drop; a residual slow ramp remains if learned acceleration has nonzero mean → proceed to Stage 2.

**Stage 2 — Split the learned free-axis force into two structurally-typed channels.**
- *Dissipative friction channel:* F_fric = −Φ(x,q̇) through a maximal-monotone/set-valued sign structure so F_fric·q̇ ≤ 0 by construction and F_fric(0)≠0 is allowed (Coulomb/static). Build Φ from a nonnegative/monotone net (maximal-monotone operator nets, arXiv:2012.13247; constrained-monotonic nets, arXiv:2205.11775) or the dissipative-neural-network construction of arXiv:2509.09899. Gives req. 1+2.
- *Conservative/coupling channel:* the remaining force as an **exact time-derivative of a bounded function** (or F = −∂V/∂q, V bounded), so its time-integral is bounded → zero DC gain to integrated position → no ramp, regardless of horizon. Gives req. 4 without a data-dependent mean penalty. Because you add no stiffness and no damping to the position row, the pole stays exactly at the origin → req. 3 by construction.

*Benchmark/thresholds:* verify that removing the network's time-mean output on the integrator rows changes long-horizon RMSE by **<2×** (your current diagnostic shows ~100×); verify eigenvalue analysis of the linearized augmented model shows an eigenvalue at the origin to numerical tolerance (not shifted into the LHP).

**Stage 3 — If you need a single certified block instead of the split, extend a passive REN/NodeREN to semidefinite storage.** Take the (Q,S,R)/incremental-passivity REN parameterization (Revay–Wang–Manchester; NodeREN arXiv:2304.02976) and relax the contraction metric P≻0 to P⪰0 with the zero eigenvalue aligned to the rigid-body coordinate, using the *nonlinear-NI supply rate* uᵀẏ (Ghallab–Petersen) rather than standard passivity uᵀy (NI is the relative-degree-2 force-to-position case you have). Research-grade extension, not off-the-shelf; pursue for a formal certificate. Switch to it if Stage 2's split cannot represent the observed coupling.

**Stage 4 — Validation regardless of route.** (a) Long-horizon free-run on held-out trajectories ≥50× the training window; (b) constant-force probe: inject a small constant force on each free axis, confirm bounded position response from the *learned* block; (c) at-rest test: confirm nonzero breakaway force is represented; (d) energy audit: integrate learned-force·velocity over a rollout, confirm ≤ 0 on the free axes.

## Caveats
- **The four-requirement-satisfying method is not a citable published result; the recommendation is a synthesis of building blocks.** I judge this a genuine gap, not a retrieval failure: NI free-body theory (marginal-preserving) is linear-only; neural NI/passive constructions (Shi/Cheng/Wang/Manchester) buy optimizability by forcing strict PD storage and discard the marginal case; port-Hamiltonian/Rayleigh nets kill at-rest friction; the mechatronic-augmentation lineage (Tóth/Schoukens/Bolderman) is either contraction-based or controller-side.
- **arXiv:2604.11421 (Györök, Drenth, Verhoek, Péni, Schoukens, Tóth) is real and recent** — a follow-up to the LFR-augmentation line (arXiv:2602.17297); confirmed it guarantees stability via contraction (its §4.1 "Contracting model property"), so it fails req. 3 as you suspected. Watch the same Eindhoven TU/e group (Tóth, Schoukens, Beintema, Hoekstra, Györök) for a semidefinite/marginal extension; none exists as of this writing.
- **The bounded-integral/exact-derivative parameterization forbids a *sustained* DC force on the integrator row.** If your true system genuinely has a sustained non-dissipative DC force on a free axis (e.g. constant preload/gravity not in f_base), that belongs in f_base, not the residual; the constraint will correctly refuse to represent it as drift. Confirm your baseline captures known static loads.
- **Set-valued/maximal-monotone friction introduces nonsmoothness** (differential inclusions), complicating the free-run ODE solve and gradients; smooth surrogates (tanh(q̇/ε)) restore differentiability but are nonzero-at-rest only as ε→0 — a fidelity/trainability compromise.
- **Several NI results quoted are frequency-domain/LTI** (Lanzon–Petersen; Xiong–Petersen–Lanzon; Mabrok et al.); their transfer to a nonlinear learned block relies on the nonlinear NI/counterclockwise storage-function definition (Angeli 2006; Ghallab–Petersen), which is established but whose *free-body/semidefinite-storage* subcase has not been worked out in the nonlinear setting — an additional theoretical gap that your work would be first to fill.
