# A Citation-Backed Catalog of Diagnostics for a Model-Augmentation Pathology: an Unidentifiable Learned DC Force on a Zero-Stiffness Integrator

## TL;DR
- The user already owns a strong **attribution** toolkit (mean/RMS ratios, DC-removal counterfactuals, error-vs-horizon curves, per-window paired statistics, encoder-bias decompositions). The biggest **missing, citable** items are (1) a formal **identifiability argument** — a Fisher-information/regressor-Gram-matrix null-space analysis plus a persistence-of-excitation (PE) order/spectral argument tying the failure to the unexcited DC direction — and (2) a committee-facing **before/after mechanism-demonstration set**: pole-zero (z-plane/unit-circle) maps, an FRF/Bode overlay of the learned block exposing its DC gain, an energy/power audit of the learned force, and a phase portrait of the drifting mode.
- Almost every diagnostic the user needs has a canonical published home: PE and informativity (Ljung; Söderström & Stoica), Fisher/CRLB and input design (Pintelon & Schoukens; De Cock/Gevers/Schoukens D-optimality), sloppiness eigenspectra (Transtrum/Sethna; Gutenkunst), profile-likelihood practical identifiability (Raue et al. 2009), structural identifiability (Villaverde/STRIKE-GOLDD), loss-blindness/rollout curves (Beintema/Tóth/Schoukens SUBNET; Ribeiro et al. smoothness; Brandstetter pushforward; Sanchez-Gonzalez GNS; List/Chen/Thuerey), drift-vs-horizon conventions (KITTI/Zhang & Scaramuzza RPE; Allan variance/IEEE Std 952-1997), and paired tests (Demšar 2006).
- **Critical caveat for the user's setting:** the data are *deterministic and noiseless*, so the statistical machinery of Fisher information / Cramér–Rao **degenerates** — with zero noise the CRLB is zero and the FIM is the noise-free Gram/Hessian (Gauss–Newton) matrix. The defensible framing is *deterministic informativity / regressor rank & conditioning* (a structural/geometric null-space statement), NOT a variance/CRLB claim. Use the FIM's **rank and near-null eigenvector** (which points along the DC/velocity-row direction) as the identifiability object, and reserve "Cramér–Rao" language for an explicitly-flagged hypothetical noisy case.

---

## Key Findings

**The pathology in one sentence.** A short windowed simulation loss cannot price the free-run consequence of a constant force on a marginally stable (z=1) integrator, and narrowband excitation carries no DC information, so the DC/velocity-row direction of the learned residual is unidentifiable and gets parked at a non-zero mean; free-run integration turns that into unbounded position drift. This is simultaneously an **identifiability** failure (the data do not constrain the direction) and a **loss-blindness** failure (the objective does not penalize it). The marginal integrator is the *amplifier*, not the *cause*.

**Biggest gaps relative to the diagnostics already held.** The user's existing eight diagnostics are essentially *attribution* and *mechanism-symptom* tools. Missing and citable:
1. A **regressor/FIM conditioning + null-space** figure that localizes the unidentifiable direction (Category 1).
2. A **PE-order / input-spectrum** argument stated formally with the canonical order condition and the "no power at DC ⇒ DC gain unconstrained" spectral statement (Category 1).
3. A **profile-likelihood** (its deterministic analogue: a "loss profile along the mean-force direction") flat-ridge plot (Category 1).
4. A **loss-landscape slice along the mean-force weight direction** showing flatness/slight favorability (Category 2).
5. A **pole-zero / unit-circle map** and an **FRF/Bode overlay of the learned block** exposing DC gain (Category 4).
6. An **energy/power audit (F·v sign) and/or energy-vs-time plot** of the learned force (Category 4).
7. A **phase portrait** of the drifting mode — velocity bounded, position ramping (Category 4).

## Details

### CATEGORY 1 — IDENTIFIABILITY GAP: showing the learned direction is unconstrained by data

**1.1 Persistence-of-excitation (PE) order condition + input-spectrum ("no power at DC") argument — CANONICAL; MISSING; applies with care.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* State the PE order condition: for consistent identification the input must be persistently exciting of sufficient order. The frequency-domain interpretation is canonical and verbatim: *"The frequency domain interpretation of persistent excitation of order n is that the spectrum of the signal is nonzero in at least n frequencies in the interval (–π, π) (Ljung, 1987; Söderström and Stoica, 1989)."* Construct the figure by overlaying your multisine input power spectrum (which you already have) and annotating that it has **exactly zero power at ω=0 (DC)** and support only in 130–180 Hz; then argue the DC gain / constant-force direction is unconstrained because no input energy probes it. Axes: magnitude (log) vs frequency, with an explicit marker at DC.
- *Canonical sources.* L. Ljung, *System Identification: Theory for the User*, 2nd ed., Prentice Hall, 1999 (informativity of data, PE, experiment design, Ch. 13–14); T. Söderström & P. Stoica, *System Identification*, Prentice Hall, 1989 (PE order and its frequency-domain characterization).
- *Canonical / standard?* Fully canonical in system identification.
- *Applies to user's setting?* Yes, and this is arguably the **single most defensible** identifiability statement here because it is a deterministic, spectral argument that does not require a noise model. **Caveat (verbatim from Söderström & Stoica):** *"The condition of persistent excitation (pe) is important only in noisy systems. For noise-free systems, it is not necessary that the input be pe … noise-free systems can be identified from a finite number of data points (N<∞) whereas pe concerns the input properties for N→∞."* Therefore frame the claim precisely as: the *constant-force direction is unexcited and hence unconstrained*, independent of any consistency/variance argument.

**1.2 Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) rank / conditioning + null-space eigenvector — CANONICAL (in sloppiness / experiment-design form); MISSING; degenerate under noiseless data, so reinterpret.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Build the FIM — or, in the noiseless case, the Gauss–Newton Gram/Hessian J⊤J of the simulation-loss residuals with respect to the parameters (or the per-row force components). Plot its **eigenvalue spectrum on a log axis** and inspect the **eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue**; the claim is that this near-null eigenvector aligns with the DC/velocity-row (constant-force) direction, i.e., that direction is "sloppy"/unconstrained. Report the **condition number** and the singular-value spectrum of the regressor.
- *Canonical source.* M. K. Transtrum, B. B. Machta, K. S. Brown, B. C. Daniels, C. R. Myers, J. P. Sethna, "Perspective: Sloppiness and emergent theories in physics, biology, and beyond," *J. Chem. Phys.* 143, 010901 (2015) — **Fig. 1**, whose caption reads verbatim: *"Sloppy eigenvalue spectra of multiparameter models from various fields. Eigenvalues of the FIM, indicating sensitivity to perturbations along orthogonal directions in parameter space, are roughly evenly spaced in log-space, extending over many orders of magnitude. Reprinted with permission from Machta et al., Science 342, 604-607 (2013)."* Foundational: R. N. Gutenkunst et al., "Universally sloppy parameter sensitivities in systems biology models," *PLoS Comput. Biol.* 3(10):e189 (2007).
- *Canonical / standard?* The log-eigenvalue-spectrum plot is canonical in the sloppiness literature; FIM/CRLB machinery is canonical in statistical system ID.
- *Applies to user's setting?* **Only with explicit reinterpretation.** With deterministic noiseless data the statistical FIM (= (1/σ²)·J⊤J) and the CRLB **degenerate** (σ²→0 drives the CRLB variance to zero and the information formally to infinity). The defensible object is the **noise-free Gram/Hessian J⊤J**: its **rank deficiency / near-null eigenvector** is a structural statement about which parameter combinations the loss cannot see. State explicitly: "we use the FIM as the loss Hessian / regressor Gram matrix, a deterministic sensitivity object; we do NOT claim a Cramér–Rao variance bound because the data are noiseless." This matches the sloppiness-community usage (sensitivity-matrix eigendecomposition) exactly, which is the right citation to lean on.

**1.3 Parameter sloppiness / null-space analysis — CANONICAL; MISSING; applies (as sensitivity, not variance).**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Eigendecompose the sensitivity/Hessian matrix; classify "stiff" vs "sloppy" eigen-parameter combinations; show the learned mean-force direction is a sloppy (flat) combination. Normalize eigenvalues by the largest (λ_j/λ_1).
- *Sources.* Transtrum et al. 2015 (above); Gutenkunst et al. 2007; for an applied recipe, "Strategic model reduction by analysing model sloppiness" (arXiv:2204.05602) spells out the sensitivity-matrix eigendecomposition and λ_j/λ_1 scaling.
- *Applies?* Yes — this is the most natural formal home for the user's already-observed "loss is neutral-to-favorable along the DC direction": that is precisely a sloppy/flat eigendirection.

**1.4 Profile-likelihood practical identifiability — CANONICAL; MISSING; needs deterministic reinterpretation.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Fix one parameter (here: the mean of the learned force on a zero-stiffness velocity row) at a grid of values, re-optimize all others, and plot the resulting minimal loss vs the fixed value. Verbatim from the source: *"Structural non-identifiable parameters are characterized by a flat profile likelihood. The profile likelihood of a practically non-identifiable parameter has a minimum, but is not exceeding a threshold Δα for increasing and/or decreasing values of θᵢ."* Thus a flat profile ⇒ structural non-identifiability; a one-sided/open profile that never crosses the confidence threshold ⇒ practical non-identifiability.
- *Canonical source.* A. Raue, C. Kreutz, T. Maiwald, J. Bachmann, M. Schilling, U. Klingmüller, J. Timmer, "Structural and practical identifiability analysis of partially observed dynamical models by exploiting the profile likelihood," *Bioinformatics* 25(15):1923–1929, 2009 (doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358).
- *Applies?* **Yes, with relabeling.** In a noiseless deterministic setting there is no likelihood, but the *loss profile* (profile of the windowed simulation loss along the mean-force direction) is the exact deterministic analogue and will be **flat-to-slightly-decreasing** — the strongest single figure showing the loss does not constrain (and indeed slightly rewards) the DC. Present it as a "loss profile along the constant-force direction," cite Raue et al. for the method, and note the flat ridge as practical non-identifiability.

**1.5 Structural identifiability test — CANONICAL; PARTIALLY RELEVANT (use to contrast).**
- *What it shows.* A symbolic/differential-geometric test (observability rank condition, Lie derivatives) of whether parameters are *even in principle* recoverable from the chosen outputs, independent of data richness.
- *Sources.* A. F. Villaverde, A. Barreiro, A. Papachristodoulou, "Structural identifiability of dynamic systems biology models," *PLoS Comput. Biol.* 12(10):e1005153 (2016); STRIKE-GOLDD toolbox — S. Díaz-Seoane, X. Rey-Barreiro, A. F. Villaverde, "STRIKE-GOLDD 4.0: user-friendly, efficient analysis of structural identifiability and observability," *Bioinformatics* 39(1):btac748 (2023). DAISY (Bellu et al. 2007) is the older polynomial-ODE alternative.
- *Applies?* Partially. The user's problem is **practical/experimental** non-identifiability (unexcited DC), not structural — with DC excitation the constant force *would* be recoverable. Cite these tools to *contrast*: "this is not a structural non-identifiability; the augmented model is structurally identifiable but the experiment is uninformative in the DC direction." This structural-vs-practical distinction is exactly the Raue-et-al. / Wieland-et-al. framing and is itself a clean thesis point.

**1.6 Input/experiment design criteria (D-, A-, E-optimality; dispersion function) — CANONICAL; USEFUL as the "cure" framing.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Define scalar functionals of the FIM: D-optimality maximizes det(FIM) (minimizes the volume of the parameter uncertainty ellipsoid, invariant to parameter scaling); A-optimality minimizes trace(FIM⁻¹); E-optimality minimizes the largest covariance eigenvalue (the major axis of the uncertainty ellipsoid). Use these to argue that an input with **DC content** (or a lower excitation band) would render the constant-force direction identifiable.
- *Sources.* R. Pintelon & J. Schoukens, *System Identification: A Frequency Domain Approach*, 2nd ed., Wiley-IEEE, 2012 (multisine design, CRLB, non-excited frequencies); A. De Cock, M. Gevers, J. Schoukens, "D-optimal input design for nonlinear FIR-type systems: a dispersion-based approach," *Automatica* 73 (2016) / arXiv:1703.08401 (D-optimality as a convex problem; dispersion function ν(ω)=tr[F⁻¹(χ)F(ω)]). Gevers and Bombois are the canonical names for identification-for-control experiment design.
- *Applies?* Yes, as the **remedy** section: the identifiability gap is curable by input design (add DC / low-frequency excitation) or by regularizing the mean force. E-optimality's "largest covariance eigenvalue / major axis of the uncertainty ellipsoid" corresponds directly to the sloppy DC direction.

### CATEGORY 2 — LOSS BLINDNESS: short-window / truncated-BPTT loss is blind to long-horizon drift

**2.1 Error-vs-horizon / rollout-length curves — CANONICAL; user HAS the core version; strengthen with citations.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Plot free-run (simulation) error vs rollout horizon; show the training truncation length sits in the flat, low-error region while error grows past it (the user's drift enters ~0.5 s, far beyond the 0.1 s window).
- *Canonical source.* G. I. Beintema, M. Schoukens, R. Tóth, "Deep subspace encoders for nonlinear system identification," *Automatica* 156:111210, 2023 — **Fig. 3** ("Influence of the truncation length T of the loss function on the test error") and **Fig. 4** ("The k-step NRMS error of estimated models under different truncation lengths, computed on the test data"), both confirmed verbatim. Corroborating: Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. (GNS, §3.3 below) plot rollout MSE vs time.
- *Applies?* Directly, and this is the user's own model family (SUBNET/deepSI). Cite Beintema et al. Figs. 3–4 as the canonical precedent legitimizing the existing error-vs-horizon curve.

**2.2 Truncation-length ↔ loss-smoothness / gradient behavior — CANONICAL; MISSING as an explicit figure.**
- *What it shows.* The theoretical reason a short window is used at all: the simulation-error objective's Lipschitz constant and β-smoothness can blow up exponentially with simulation length in non-contractive regions; truncation smooths the loss. This is the flip side of loss blindness — the same truncation that makes optimization tractable also removes the long-horizon signal that would penalize the DC.
- *Canonical source.* A. H. Ribeiro, K. Tiels, J. Umenberger, T. B. Schön, L. A. Aguirre, "On the smoothness of nonlinear system identification," *Automatica* 121:109158, 2020 (arXiv:1905.00820). Multiple shooting / truncation makes simulation length a design parameter and smooths the cost. Bock's multiple-shooting tradition (H. G. Bock, 1981/1983) is the origin.
- *Applies?* Yes — the mechanistic justification for why the windowed loss exists and why it is structurally blind to the free-run DC consequence. Strong citation for framing the trade-off.

**2.3 Loss-landscape slice along a specific weight direction (filter-normalized 1D/2D) — CANONICAL technique; MISSING; strongly recommended.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Take the trained weights θ*, pick the direction d corresponding to the learned mean-force parameterization (or a random filter-normalized direction), and plot loss(θ* + α d) vs α (1D) or a 2D surface. The claim: along the mean-force direction the **windowed** loss is flat/slightly-favorable, whereas a **free-run** loss along the same direction has a sharp minimum at zero mean. Plotting *both* losses on the same slice is the money figure for loss blindness.
- *Canonical source.* H. Li, Z. Xu, G. Taylor, C. Studer, T. Goldstein, "Visualizing the Loss Landscape of Neural Nets," *NeurIPS* 2018 (arXiv:1712.09913). Filter normalization d_{i,j} ← (d_{i,j}/‖d_{i,j}‖)·‖θ_{i,j}‖; their **Fig. 1** (ResNet-56 with/without skip connections) is the canonical 2D surface.
- *Applies?* Yes, and novel-but-defensible: a **windowed-loss vs free-run-loss slice along the DC direction** is an original, committee-convincing figure built on a canonical, heavily-cited method. Note: filter normalization was designed for ReLU scale-invariance; for the structured LPV+NN, normalize sensibly and describe the chosen direction explicitly.

**2.4 Train-metric vs deployment-metric divergence / per-window vs free-run scatter — CANONICAL; user PARTIALLY HAS (paired per-window RMS).**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Scatter each window's teacher-forced/short-window loss against its free-run contribution; the point cloud shows low windowed loss coexisting with high free-run error — the exposure-bias / distribution-shift visualization.
- *Canonical sources.* Exposure bias & train/inference mismatch: S. Bengio, O. Vinyals, N. Jaitly, N. Shazeer, "Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks," *NeurIPS* 2015 (arXiv:1506.03099). Distribution-shift / pushforward framing: J. Brandstetter, D. Worrall, M. Welling, "Message Passing Neural PDE Solvers," *ICLR* 2022 (arXiv:2202.03376) — the "distribution shift problem" (A♯p_k ≠ p_{k+1}) and the pushforward trick / temporal bundling. A. Lamb et al., "Professor Forcing," *NeurIPS* 2016.
- *Applies?* Yes. Important nuance to state: in classic exposure bias the fix is to expose the model to its own rollouts (scheduled sampling / pushforward). Here longer rollouts *would* penalize the DC (drift becomes visible), but Brandstetter et al. explicitly report the pushforward trick can fail to inject low-amplitude long-horizon information because gradients are not propagated through predicted inputs — a directly relevant caveat.

**2.5 Unrolled-training / optimization-horizon studies — CANONICAL; supports 2.1/2.3.**
- *Sources.* B. List, L.-W. Chen, N. Thuerey, "Learned turbulence modelling with differentiable fluid solvers: physics-based loss functions and optimisation horizons," *J. Fluid Mech.* 949:A25, 2022 (arXiv:2202.06988) — superior stability/accuracy for models trained with more unrolled steps; follow-up B. List, L.-W. Chen, K. Bali, N. ### CATEGORY 3 — ATTRIBUTION: assigning the failure to a specific component/direction

**3.1 Ablation / counterfactual simulation ("lesion study") — CANONICAL; user HAS (DC-removal counterfactual).**
- *What it shows.* Remove/zero one component (here: subtract the per-row mean of the learned force) and re-simulate; the ~2-orders-of-magnitude collapse of drift attributes the failure to the mean — the standard ablation convention.
- *Canonical precedents for residual/hybrid attribution.* A. Zeng, S. Song, J. Lee, A. Rodriguez, T. Funkhouser, "TossingBot: Learning to Throw Arbitrary Objects with Residual Physics," *RSS* 2019 / *IEEE T-RO* 36(4), 2020 — residual-on-top-of-physics decomposition and ablations isolating the residual's contribution. GNS ablations: A. Sanchez-Gonzalez, J. Godwin, T. Pfaff, R. Ying, J. Leskovec, P. Battaglia, "Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks," *ICML* 2020 — supplementary Figs. C.1–C.4 (architectural/noise ablations vs rollout error).
- *Applies?* Yes; the user's counterfactual is exactly this convention and can be cited as such.

**3.2 Per-state / per-mode error decomposition (additive identity) — CANONICAL; user HAS.**
- *What it shows.* Decompose the baseline free-run error into an encoder-initial-condition effect vs a residual, two initializations, additive identity — a standard error-budget decomposition.
- *Applies?* Yes; methodologically aligned with the GNS "acceleration error (one-step) vs rollout error (accumulated)" decomposition (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020) and the ATE-vs-RPE decomposition philosophy (3.4). No exotic citation needed.

**3.3 Paired statistical tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank) — CANONICAL; user HAS the paired stats, likely MISSING the citation.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* For paired per-window RMS of the full model vs its mean-debiased twin, report the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-parametric, paired) rather than a t-test.
- *Canonical source.* J. Demšar, "Statistical Comparisons of Classifiers over Multiple Data Sets," *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 7:1–30, 2006. Verbatim recommendation (Abstract): *"we recommend a set of simple, yet safe and robust non-parametric tests for statistical comparisons of classifiers: the Wilcoxon signed ranks test for comparison of two classifiers and the Friedman test with the corresponding post-hoc tests for comparison of more classifiers over multiple data sets."*
- *Applies?* Yes — the exact citation to legitimize the paired per-window test. Use Wilcoxon signed-rank for the two-model (full vs debiased) comparison; reserve Friedman + post-hoc (e.g., Nemenyi, with a critical-difference diagram) for any multi-model comparison.

**3.4 Drift-vs-horizon conventions from odometry / inertial navigation — CANONICAL in those fields; MISSING as citations; directly analogous.**
- *KITTI-style relative error / RPE vs subsequence length.* A. Geiger, P. Lenz, R. Urtasun, "Are we ready for autonomous driving? The KITTI vision benchmark suite," *CVPR* 2012 (relative error as a function of sub-trajectory length and speed). Z. Zhang & D. Scaramuzza, "A Tutorial on Quantitative Trajectory Evaluation for Visual(-Inertial) Odometry," *IROS* 2018, pp. 7244–7251 — defines Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) vs Relative Error (RE/RPE); verbatim: the relative error *"is less sensitive to the specific time the estimation error occurs. Geiger et al. further extended the relative error as a function of sub-trajectory length and velocity."* RE is reported as statistics over sub-trajectories of specified lengths — *exactly* the user's free-run-error-vs-horizon curve. The open-source `rpg_trajectory_evaluation` toolbox implements it.
- *Allan variance for bias identification.* IEEE Std 952-1997 (IEEE Standard Specification Format Guide and Test Procedure for Single-Axis Interferometric Fiber Optic Gyros), Annex C — the log-log σ(τ) vs τ plot whose slopes identify angle/velocity random walk (slope −½), bias instability (slope 0, the minimum of the curve), and rate ramp (slope +1). A **constant bias integrates into a ramp** — the precise analogue of the user's constant force integrating into position drift. Allan-deviation-vs-τ is the canonical figure for separating a constant bias from noise.
- *Applies?* Strongly, by analogy. The user's pathology is literally "a constant bias on a rate/velocity channel that integrates into unbounded position." Framing the free-run-error-vs-horizon curve as an RPE-vs-length plot (Zhang & Scaramuzza) and characterizing the DC force as a "bias-instability / rate-ramp" term (Allan variance, IEEE 952) provides two mature, citable vocabularies. **Drift rate per unit time** is the natural scalar metric.

### CATEGORY 4 — DEMONSTRATION TO A COMMITTEE: convincing mechanism figures

**4.1 Pole/eigenvalue map in the z-plane (unit-circle plot), before/after training — CANONICAL; user HAS the eigenvalue check, likely MISSING the plotted map.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Plot poles in the complex z-plane with the unit circle overlaid; mark the X/Y rigid-body poles at z=1 (marginally stable integrator) and the benign yaw mode inside the circle. This visually establishes the amplifier: a pole *on* the unit circle integrates a constant input into an unbounded ramp.
- *Canonical / standard?* Pole-zero maps on the unit circle are textbook-standard in discrete-time control/DSP (e.g., Oppenheim & Schafer, *Discrete-Time Signal Processing*; Åström & Wittenmark, *Computer-Controlled Systems*). No exotic citation needed; it is expected in a mechatronics thesis.
- *Applies?* Yes — the clearest single figure for *why* an identifiability nuisance becomes catastrophic drift. Recommended.

**4.2 FRF / Bode overlay of the learned component, exposing DC gain — CANONICAL in sysid; MISSING; recommended.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Compute the frequency response of the learned parallel block (or its best linear approximation) and overlay it on the physics baseline; annotate the **DC (ω→0) gain** and mark the excitation band (130–180 Hz) where data constrain the model vs the unconstrained DC region.
- *Canonical source.* Pintelon & Schoukens, *System Identification: A Frequency Domain Approach*, 2nd ed., 2012 (best linear approximation of a nonlinear block via random-phase multisines; behavior at non-excited frequencies). The BLA framework (Schoukens, Pintelon, Rolain) is the standard route to an FRF of a nonlinear/learned block.
- *Applies?* Yes. This visualizes "the input has no power at DC, so the DC gain is unconstrained" (Category 1.1) directly in the model's own transfer function — a persuasive committee figure. Caveat: the learned block is nonlinear/LPV, so present it as a BLA / operating-point FRF and say so.

**4.3 Energy / power audit of the learned force (F·v sign) and/or predicted-energy-vs-time — MIXED canonicity; MISSING; recommended with correct framing.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* (a) Energy-vs-time: plot predicted total mechanical energy of the free-run vs time and show the learned DC force does bounded work (velocity saturates at terminal F/c) — distinguishing an *identifiability* pathology from an *instability/energy-injection* pathology. (b) Power-sign audit: plot P(t)=F·v for the learned force to show it is not a runaway energy source.
- *Sources & canonicity.* The **energy-vs-time conservation plot** is a canonical diagnostic in learned-dynamics ML: S. Greydanus, M. Dzamba, J. Yosinski, "Hamiltonian Neural Networks," *NeurIPS* 2019 (arXiv:1906.01563) — **Fig. 2** (columns plotting the conserved quantity and "Total energy") and **Fig. 3** (two-body problem, "Energy" vs time column); they also define a true-vs-predicted total-energy MSE as a core metric. The **F·v power-sign / passivity–dissipativity audit** is canonical in *control theory / robotics* (Willems dissipativity; passivity via P(t)=fᵀ(t)v(t) with the passivity integral inequality), NOT in the HNN papers, which *enforce* dissipation structurally — e.g., Zhong, Dey & Chakraborty, "Dissipative SymODEN," ICLR 2020 DeepDiffEq **Workshop**, arXiv:2002.08860 (PSD dissipation matrix D=LLᵀ); Sosanya & Greydanus, "Dissipative Hamiltonian Neural Networks," 2022; S. Moradi, G. I. Beintema, N. O. Jaensson, R. Tóth, M. Schoukens, "Port-Hamiltonian neural networks with output error noise models," *Automatica* (in press, 2026), arXiv:2502.14432 — this last is directly in the user's SUBNET lineage.
- *Applies?* Yes, and this is the decisive figure for the central claim ("identifiability problem, NOT an energy/instability problem"). **Framing caveat:** cite Greydanus et al. 2019 for the energy-vs-time convention; cite dissipativity/passivity control theory (not HNN) for the F·v-sign audit; cite port-Hamiltonian NN work (Moradi et al.) as the structured way one *would* constrain energy, contrasting with the unconstrained learned force here.

**4.4 Phase portrait of the drifting mode — CANONICAL (dynamical systems); MISSING; recommended.**
- *What it shows / how to construct.* Plot velocity vs position (the (position, velocity) trajectory) for the zero-stiffness axis under the learned DC force: velocity approaches terminal F/c (bounded) while position ramps without bound. Overlay the DC-removed counterfactual (bounded) for contrast.
- *Canonical / standard?* Phase portraits are textbook-standard (Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*); no exotic citation needed.
- *Applies?* Yes — a compact, intuitive figure separating the bounded velocity mode from the unbounded position mode.

**4.5 Prediction-vs-measurement overlay with a falsifiable predicted coefficient — CANONICAL; recommended.**
- *What it shows.* Overlay the model's free-run prediction on ground truth and state a *falsifiable* predicted number: e.g., predicted drift = ½·(F_mean/M)·t² using the measured F_mean, matched against the observed position ramp; and terminal velocity = F_mean/c. The signed-error ensemble/polynomial-trend fits the user already has (offset/slope/curvature vs encoder bias) are exactly this style — tie the predicted curvature coefficient to the measured mean force.
- *Precedent.* This "predict a coefficient, then confirm it" style is the hallmark of *mechanism-diagnosis* papers (rather than benchmark-win papers): Brandstetter et al. 2022 (distribution-shift mechanism), Ribeiro et al. 2020 (smoothness mechanism), and the sloppiness papers all argue by mechanism + confirmation.
- *Applies?* Yes; the strongest rhetorical structure for a committee.

## Caveats

- **Noiseless deterministic data breaks the statistical identifiability tools.** Fisher information and the Cramér–Rao lower bound are variance statements defined relative to a noise model; with σ²=0 they degenerate (CRLB→0; information formally infinite). Use the **loss Hessian / regressor Gram matrix and its rank/null-space** as the deterministic identifiability object (the sloppiness-community usage), and reserve CRLB language for an explicitly-flagged hypothetical noisy experiment. Profile likelihood likewise becomes a **loss profile** (no likelihood without noise) — relabel accordingly. PE is technically *not necessary* for a one-off noiseless fit (Söderström & Stoica, verbatim above), so anchor the argument on the *unexcited DC direction being unconstrained* rather than on a consistency/variance claim.
- **The marginal integrator (z=1) is the amplifier, not the cause.** Emphasize in every figure that the *cause* is the uninformative experiment plus the blind loss, and the *amplifier* is the pole at z=1. Removing the mean collapses drift by ~2 orders of magnitude precisely because the pole integrates only the mean.
- **Figure-number precision.** Verified figure references: Beintema et al. 2023 Figs. 3–4 (truncation-length effect, confirmed verbatim); Li et al. 2018 Fig. 1 (ResNet-56 landscape); Transtrum et al. 2015 Fig. 1 (FIM eigenspectrum, confirmed verbatim); Greydanus et al. 2019 Figs. 2–3 (energy plots); Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020 supplementary Figs. C.1–C.4 (ablations/rollout error). For Raue et al. 2009 and Zhang & Scaramuzza 2018 the *method and figure conventions* were confirmed (with verbatim descriptive quotes) but exact figure numbers were not pinned — cite by section/method, not figure number, unless you re-open the PDFs. Ljung and Söderström & Stoica page/chapter numbers should be verified against your specific editions before final submission.
- **Venue precision.** "Dissipative SymODEN" (Zhong et al.) is an **ICLR 2020 DeepDiffEq Workshop** paper, not a main-track proceedings paper — cite as a workshop paper. Moradi et al. (port-Hamiltonian NN with OE noise) is **in press at *Automatica* (2026)**; verify the final volume/pages. "Nonlinear state-space identification using deep encoder networks" (Beintema, Tóth, Schoukens) is **L4DC/PMLR 2021**, distinct from the 2023 *Automatica* journal version ("Deep subspace encoders…") — cite the correct one for each claim.
- **The F·v passivity audit is borrowed, not native to the ML papers.** Energy-vs-time conservation plots are canonical in the HNN/learned-dynamics ML literature (Greydanus 2019); the explicit power-sign / passivity-inequality audit of a learned force is canonical in **control theory** (Willems dissipativity), so cite it there. Do not attribute an F·v-sign figure to the HNN papers.
- **Exposure-bias fixes may not transfer.** Brandstetter et al. 2022 explicitly report that the pushforward trick can fail to inject low-amplitude long-horizon information because gradients are not propagated through predicted inputs — directly relevant if you propose "just train on longer rollouts" as the cure. The more reliable remedies here are input design (DC excitation) and mean-force regularization.
