# The DC-Bias Mechanism in Neural-Augmented Dynamics Models: A Literature Synthesis

## TL;DR
- The leading hypothesis is **correct and literature-supported**: Adam's per-coordinate normalization behaves as sign/steepest descent in the ℓ∞ geometry, moving at a step size that is *independent of gradient magnitude*. A persistent, tiny, seed-consistent gradient in a loss-flat direction therefore produces persistent motion under Adam that vanishing plain-SGD steps would not — depositing a DC offset. The planned SGD-vs-Adam experiment is the right decisive test, and theory predicts SGD will not (or far more slowly) produce the DC.
- The *systematic, seed-consistent* origin of the tiny gradient in the flat direction is best explained by the **bias of the truncated-BPTT / free-run gradient estimator**: truncation systematically under-weights long-range/slow-mode sensitivities, and for a pole-at-z=1 integrator the sensitivity does not decay, so the truncated gradient carries a deterministic (non-random) directional error rather than mere variance.
- The remaining three candidate mechanisms are **contributory or explanatory of "why the first few steps" but not sufficient alone**: spectral-bias / "learn-the-mean-first" explains why a DC/low-frequency component is deposited early; implicit-bias-in-the-null-space theory explains that GD and Adam move *differently* in loss-flat directions; and hybrid-model/exposure-bias literature confirms that a constant residual on an integrating state compounds to unbounded drift, but does not by itself explain the DC's origin.

## Key Findings

**1. Adam ≈ sign descent, and sign descent moves at a magnitude-independent step (Mechanism 1 — strongly supported).** Multiple independent analyses establish that in the small-learning-rate limit with fixed β₁,β₂, both RMSProp and Adam converge to the signGD flow θ̇ = −∇f/(|∇f|+ε) ≈ −sign(∇f). This is the crux: under sign descent a coordinate with a *low-variance, consistent-sign* gradient receives a fixed ±η update regardless of how tiny the gradient is. A persistent tiny gradient in the flat direction therefore accumulates linearly into a DC, whereas SGD/GD would take a step proportional to the (near-zero) gradient and effectively stall. This is a documented, not speculative, mechanism.

**2. The truncated-BPTT gradient is systematically biased toward short-term dependencies (Mechanism 3 — strongly supported, and the best explanation for seed-consistency).** Tallec & Ollivier (2017, ARTBP) show truncated BPTT's gradient estimate is *biased* — "truncation favors short-term dependencies" — a mean/directional bias, not variance. For contractive modes the bias decays geometrically with lag (Aicher, Foti & Fox 2019), but that guarantee relies on a spectral contraction factor λ<1; for a pole at z=1 (pure integrator) λ→1 and the sensitivity does not decay, so no finite truncation window removes the bias. This gives a deterministic, data-independent, seed-consistent directional gradient — exactly the fingerprint described.

**3. Networks learn the mean/lowest-frequency component first (Mechanism 5 — supported, explains the timing).** The Frequency Principle / spectral bias literature robustly shows DNNs fit low-to-high frequency, i.e. the DC / lowest-frequency component is captured first. Direct theory shows an early "optimal constant solution" (OCS) phase in which the network first learns the average output before input structure. This explains why the DC appears in the first ~10–15 steps from a zero-initialized output.

**4. Optimizers select systematically (not randomly) in flat/degenerate directions (Mechanism 2 — supported as framing).** Implicit-bias theory shows GD in underdetermined least-squares converges to the min-norm solution, while Adam/signGD have a *different* implicit bias (max-margin in ℓ∞ / different geometry). The transferable fact: motion in a loss-flat/degenerate direction is optimizer-determined and systematic, and Adam's is genuinely different from GD's.

**5. Constant residual on an integrating state → unbounded drift (Mechanism 4 — supported as the consequence, not the cause).** Hybrid physics+ML and learned-simulator literature documents that residual/closure corrections compound over rollout; exposure bias and error accumulation are well studied. But this literature explains why a DC is *harmful* (it integrates), not why gradient descent *deposits* it.

## Details

### Setup framing and why this is a known-hard regime
The described system — a physics baseline augmented in parallel by an ANN state-correction, trained with an encoder-initialized truncated simulation-error (free-run) loss — is precisely the SUBNET / deep-subspace-encoder model-augmentation framework (Beintema, Schoukens & Tóth, "Deep subspace encoders for nonlinear system identification," *Automatica* 158:111210, 2023; arXiv:2210.14816) and its augmentation variants (Retzler, Tóth, Schoukens, Beintema, Weigand, Noël, Kollár & Swevers, "Learning-based augmentation of physics-based models: an industrial robot use case," *Data-Centric Engineering* 5:e12, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1017/dce.2024.8; Hoekstra et al., "Encoder initialisation methods in the model augmentation setting," arXiv:2602.13108; and LFR augmentation, arXiv:2404.01901). The Retzler industrial-robot model has 12 states — 6 angular positions and 6 angular velocities with ẋ = v — so the position states are pure integrators, and the ANN corrects at the acceleration level, meaning any DC in the correction is double-integrated into growing position error. This is the canonical structural setting for the phenomenon. Related work (AI-based state extension/augmentation, *Nonlinear Dynamics*, 2025, DOI:10.1007/s11071-025-11092-5) explicitly flags that the plain subspace-encoder method struggles with unstable/non-contractive open-loop systems absent a stabilizing embedding — corroborating that integrator/marginally-stable modes are a known failure mode of this model class.

### Mechanism 1 — Adam as sign descent (the amplifier)
The continuous-time result θ̇ = −∇f/(|∇f|+ε) for RMSProp/Adam in the η→0 limit with fixed momentum is derived in "A Qualitative Study of the Dynamic Behavior for Adaptive Gradient Algorithms" (arXiv:2009.06125), which shows closeness to signGD flow θ̇ = −sign(∇f). Balles & Hennig ("Dissecting Adam: The Sign, Magnitude and Variance of Stochastic Gradients," ICML 2018) established the sign/magnitude/variance decomposition and the sign-descent interpretation, valid specifically in the *low-variance* regime. Kunstner, Chen, Lavington & Schmidt ("Noise Is Not the Main Factor Behind the Gap Between SGD and Adam on Transformers, but Sign Descent Might Be," ICLR 2023, arXiv:2304.13960) present evidence that "stochasticity and heavy-tailed noise are not major factors in the performance gap between SGD and Adam"; rather, as batch size grows Adam "performs very similarly to sign descent" — important here because the DC is seed-consistent (deterministic), matching a full-batch/low-noise sign-descent picture. Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida ("On the Implicit Bias of Adam," ICML 2024, PMLR 235:5862–5906; arXiv:2309.00079) prove via backward error analysis that Adam/RMSProp's ODE correction terms "either penalize the (perturbed) one-norm of the loss gradients or, conversely, impede its reduction (the latter case being typical)" — i.e., Adam is *typically anti-regularizing* and does not damp small-gradient directions the way GD's ‖∇‖²-penalty does.

The decisive mechanistic point: under sign/normalized descent the per-step displacement in a coordinate is ≈ η·sign(gradient), independent of |gradient|. So a tiny but *sign-consistent* gradient of magnitude g≪1 produces displacement η (not ηg). Over the first k steps that is ≈ kη of accumulated DC before the (flat) loss ever pushes back. Under plain SGD the displacement is ηg per step — vanishing when g→0 in the flat direction. This is exactly the asymmetry the user hypothesizes, and it is supported.

Caveat within Mechanism 1: the ε floor matters. If the flat-direction gradient is comparable to or below ε relative to √v, the "sign" behavior degrades toward a linear (SGD-like) response; the amplification is strongest when the flat-direction gradient, though tiny in absolute terms, dominates that coordinate's own second-moment estimate (the generic case when a coordinate's gradient is small but consistent). Balles & Hennig and DP-AdamBC (arXiv:2312.14334) both note the sign interpretation holds specifically in the low-variance regime — which the seed-consistent DC gradient satisfies.

### Mechanism 3 — truncated-BPTT bias as the source of the tiny consistent gradient
This is the most likely *source* of the persistent flat-direction gradient. Tallec & Ollivier ("Unbiasing Truncated Backpropagation Through Time," 2017, arXiv:1705.08209) state plainly: "truncation favors short-term dependencies: the gradient estimate of truncated BPTT is biased, so that it does not benefit from the convergence guarantees from stochastic gradient theory," adding that "stochastic gradient descent with biased estimates, such as the one provided by truncated BPTT, can lead to divergence even in simple situations and even with large truncation lengths." Their influence-balancing example shows the truncated gradient can have the *wrong sign* relative to the true gradient when the window is too short to capture slow contributions — a directional (DC) error, not variance. Aicher, Foti & Fox ("Adaptively Truncating Backpropagation Through Time to Control Gradient Bias," UAI 2019, PMLR 115:799–808; arXiv:1905.07473) formalize a *relative bias* δ, and under the premise of "gradient bounds that decay exponentially in expectation" show "the bias in TBPTT decays geometrically," proving non-asymptotic SGD convergence rates once δ(K) is controlled. That geometric-decay premise is governed by a spectral contraction factor λ<1; for an integrator (pole z=1), λ→1, the premise fails, and the truncation bias does not vanish for any finite window.

Synthesis (flagged as inference, not a directly cited result): a ~0.1 s / ~400-step window on a marginally-stable mode yields a small, deterministic, data-independent directional gradient bias precisely along the integrator direction that the windowed loss otherwise barely penalizes. That is the seed-consistent tiny gradient; Adam's sign normalization then converts it into a persistent DC. No paper states the "DC offset on integrator modes from truncated-BPTT" claim verbatim — it is a synthesis of these two results plus the integrator structure — so it should be presented as a well-grounded inference and confirmed by the SGD-vs-Adam and truncation-length experiments.

### Mechanism 5 — early-training "learn the mean first"
The Frequency Principle / spectral bias (Xu, Zhang & Xiao 2019; Rahaman et al. 2019) robustly shows low frequencies (DC being the extreme) are fit first. Rubruck, Bauer, Saxe & Summerfield ("Early learning of the optimal constant solution in neural networks and humans," arXiv:2406.17467, 2024) prove that deep linear networks necessarily learn the OCS during early learning — "initial model responses mirror the distribution of target labels, while entirely ignoring information provided in the input," with the MSE minimiser being the average output ȳ — even from zero-initialized bias. This is a direct match to a DC appearing in the first ~10–15 steps from a zero output. This mechanism explains the *timing and sign-consistency of a DC component* but not why it lands specifically in the marginally-stable, loss-flat direction — that specificity comes from Mechanisms 1+3. Spectral bias thus best serves as the "why so early" corroborator.

### Mechanism 2 — implicit bias / behavior in flat, degenerate directions
Foundational results: GD on underdetermined least-squares → min-ℓ₂-norm solution; on separable classification → max-margin (Soudry et al. 2018). For adaptive/sign methods the implicit bias differs — max-margin in the max-norm/ℓ∞ geometry (arXiv:2502.04664, "Implicit Bias of SignGD and Adam on Multiclass Separable Data"; Gunasekar et al. 2018, "Characterizing Implicit Bias in Terms of Optimization Geometry"). The transferable insight: in a loss-flat/null direction the endpoint is *optimizer-selected and systematic*, and Adam's selection differs structurally from GD's. Additionally, Adam is known not to converge but to enter small limit cycles even on convex quadratics (Bock & Weiß, "Non-Convergence and Limit Cycles in the Adam Optimizer," arXiv:2210.02070), so in a flat direction Adam need not settle at zero DC. This supports the qualitative claim that Adam actively populates the flat direction rather than leaving it untouched.

### Mechanism 4 — the consequence: compounding to unbounded drift
Exposure bias / error accumulation is extensively documented for autoregressive and learned simulators (e.g., "Defining error accumulation in ML atmospheric simulators," arXiv:2405.14714; neural-ODE long-term instability and stabilization work, arXiv:2203.15706 "Stabilized Neural ODEs," arXiv:2602.04608 "Jacobian Regularization Stabilizes Long-Term Integration"). Hybrid physics+ML residual-learning surveys (arXiv:2107.01272, "Physics-Guided Deep Learning for Dynamical Systems") document residual-model drift. This body of work confirms the *dynamical consequence*: a constant correction on a pole-at-1 state integrates into linear position drift, harmless on the ~0.1 s window but unbounded over deployment. It does not explain the *deposition* of the DC — it is the downstream amplifier in state space, complementary to Adam's amplification in parameter space.

### Competing explanations and how to discriminate
- If the DC persists under **plain SGD at matched settings**, Mechanism 1 is *not* the amplifier and attention should shift entirely to the truncated-BPTT bias (Mechanism 3) as both source and driver. If the DC largely vanishes or shrinks by orders of magnitude under SGD, Mechanism 1 is confirmed as amplifier.
- If **increasing the truncation window / horizon** (or using ARTBP-style unbiasing, or the Aicher adaptive-truncation scheme) systematically shrinks the DC, Mechanism 3 is confirmed as the source.
- The controlled interventions already reported (zero-mean data, true-initial-state training, broadband vs narrowband excitation, normalization, encoder architecture all ruled out) are consistent with an *optimizer/estimator-geometry* origin (Mechanisms 1+3) rather than a data/identifiability origin — exactly where the remaining evidence points.

## Recommendations

1. **Run the SGD-vs-Adam test first (matched LR schedule, full-batch or large-batch to remove noise as a confound).** Prediction from theory: Adam deposits the DC in the first ~10–15 steps; SGD does not, or does so orders of magnitude slower. Threshold: if the DC magnitude under SGD is <1–5% of the Adam DC at matched training loss, treat Mechanism 1 as confirmed. Use full-batch to exploit Kunstner et al.'s finding that sign-like behavior is a full-batch phenomenon (removes the "it's just noise" alternative).
2. **Sweep truncation length and add an ARTBP / adaptive-truncation control.** If the DC scales down with longer windows or with unbiased-gradient schemes, Mechanism 3 (truncation bias on the non-decaying integrator sensitivity) is confirmed as the source. This is the single most diagnostic experiment for the *origin* (as opposed to the amplifier).
3. **Intervene directly in the flat direction.** (a) Add a tiny explicit penalty on the DC/mean of the correction rows for marginally-stable states (a "zero-mean residual" constraint), or project the ANN output onto the zero-mean subspace for those rows; (b) alternatively, replace the pure-integrator poles with a light stiffness (pole at 1−δ) during training to restore geometric sensitivity decay and test whether the DC disappears — this directly tests the λ→1 hypothesis.
4. **If Adam must be retained**, consider: increasing ε (moving Adam toward SGD-like linear response in tiny-gradient coordinates), decoupled weight decay on the correction output, or a schedule that decays LR toward zero (Adam provably needs LR→0 for convergence; a non-decaying LR sustains the sign-descent creep and hence the DC).
5. **Report the mechanism as a two-stage amplification**: truncated-BPTT bias (source of a tiny, seed-consistent, directional gradient on the non-decaying integrator mode) → Adam sign-normalization (parameter-space amplification into a DC) → open-loop integration (state-space amplification into unbounded drift). Each stage has independent literature support and an independent mitigation.

## Caveats
- **No single paper reports this exact phenomenon** (a DC offset on integrator states from truncated-BPTT + Adam). The synthesis is well-grounded in adjacent, rigorous results but the specific chain is an inference to be confirmed by the experiments above. Present it as a hypothesis with strong mechanistic backing, not an established citation.
- **The Adam-as-signGD equivalence is a small-η / fixed-momentum limit.** At practical learning rates and with bias correction, the equivalence is approximate; the amplification strength depends on where the flat-direction gradient sits relative to ε and the coordinate's second moment. This is why the SGD-vs-Adam test (not just theory) is needed to quantify the effect.
- **Spectral bias and "learn-the-mean-first" explain timing, not localization.** They predict a DC appears early but not that it lands in the marginally-stable/loss-flat direction specifically; that localization is contributed by the truncation bias + integrator structure.
- **The truncation-bias→integrator argument rests on λ→1 breaking the geometric-decay assumption** in Aicher et al.; this is a sound extrapolation from their stated assumptions but is not a theorem they prove for the marginally-stable case. The pole-perturbation experiment (Recommendation 3b) is the clean test.
- Some retrieved sources are 2025–2026 preprints (e.g., encoder-initialisation arXiv:2602.13108, various Adam-implicit-bias follow-ups); treat their specific quantitative claims as not-yet-peer-reviewed where relevant.
