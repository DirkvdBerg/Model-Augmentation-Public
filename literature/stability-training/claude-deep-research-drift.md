# Preventing Drift Along an Unexcited Direction in a Learned Residual: A Control-Theoretic Literature Review

## TL;DR
- **Your framing is essentially correct.** The DC-force drift you observe on a marginally-stable (free-integrator) mode is the neural-residual instance of the classical robust-adaptive-control parameter-drift failure mode: an estimator wandering along a data-silent (loss-neutral, unidentifiable) direction. A "for-all-weights" structural guarantee of no-drift that is *also* fully expressive and *also* preserves the marginal mode is not offered by any single published method — non-drift must be recovered on the estimator side (regularize the unexcited direction, condition the horizon), exactly as you propose.
- **No single published paper claims all four of (a) fully-expressive residual, (b) no free-run drift on a marginally-stable mode, (c) open-loop simulation-error training, and (d) physical-parameter interpretability.** The closest integrated frameworks — the Eindhoven/SZTAKI model-augmentation line (weighted-regularization PGNN, orthogonal-projection regularization, and the constraint-free contracting-LFR augmentation) — hit (a), (c), (d) squarely, but their stability tool is *contraction*, which by construction excludes the marginally-stable mode you must preserve. That is precisely the gap.
- **Recommended stack:** combine a data-informativity-weighted regularizer pulling the residual toward zero where data is silent (W-PGNN mechanism) with an orthogonal-projection/null-space penalty that acts *only* on the DC direction of the free mode, plus a horizon curriculum / multiple-shooting rollout long enough to make the drift loss-visible. This is knowledge-free, keeps full expressivity, adds no stiffness, and mirrors σ-modification/dead-zone logic without touching the model class.

## Key Findings

1. **The tension you name is real and classical.** Parameter drift along unexcited directions is a well-documented, formally-characterized failure mode in adaptive control. The fixes (σ-modification, e-modification, parameter projection, dead-zone) are all *estimator-side* modifications; none restricts the plant/model class. This is the correct lens for your problem.

2. **The unidentifiable direction is a null-space object.** Under partial/non-persistent excitation, the parameter error splits into an excited component (which converges) and an unexcited component (which is invariant / free to wander). This is proven explicitly in composite-learning adaptive control — "Composite Learning Adaptive Control under Non-Persistent Partial Excitation" (arXiv:2408.01731) shows that "under non-persistent partial excitation, the control error and the excited parameter estimation error component converge exponentially to zero, and the unexcited parameter estimation error component remains bounded." This is the same object as a Fisher-information null space and a Tikhonov/minimum-norm ill-posed-inverse direction.

3. **Simulation-error (free-run) training is known to be blind to slow drift on short windows.** Multiple-shooting / truncated-horizon / subspace-encoder (SUBNET) methods were designed to *stabilize the optimization*, not to penalize a bounded-within-window integrator drift. Horizon length is the direct lever: a window shorter than the drift timescale cannot see the drift in the loss.

4. **Marginal-mode preservation and asymptotic-stability guarantees are in direct conflict in the stability-guaranteed-network literature.** Port-Hamiltonian, contraction (REN), and Lyapunov-stable parameterizations guarantee *asymptotic* stability — they pull poles strictly inside the unit circle / into the open left half-plane, which is a fake restoring force for a free-body mode. Momentum-conserving / translation-equivariant networks are the correct structural tool for a no-stiffness free mode, but they impose a symmetry restriction that can conflict with full expressivity of an arbitrary friction residual.

5. **Epistemic-uncertainty suppression (ensembles, GP variance, MOPO pessimism) is conceptually the same operation as a Fisher-null-space penalty, but not formally identical.** All penalize model influence where data is uninformative; the GP-variance and Fisher-information objects coincide only under specific linear-Gaussian assumptions. Empirically, canonical ensemble-variance penalties best track true model error: Lu, Ball, Parker-Holder, Osborne & Roberts ("Revisiting Design Choices in Offline Model-Based RL," arXiv:2110.04135) report in their penalty-vs-true-model-error correlation study that "the ensemble penalties generally perform best."

6. **LPV self-scheduling where the scheduling variable is a learned/internal state is an active, solved-in-part problem** (deep-learning LPV identification with learned scheduling maps), but the specific pathology of a *drifting* scheduling state corrupting scheduling integrity is not directly treated; it must be handled by bounding the drift upstream.

## Details

### Q1 — Methods that prevent drift along an unexcited/unidentifiable direction

**Robust-adaptive-control drift modifications (estimator-side; no model-class restriction).** The canonical reference set is Ioannou & Sun, *Robust Adaptive Control*, and Narendra & Annaswamy, *Stable Adaptive Systems*. Gradient-based update laws drift under bounded disturbances / lack of excitation; four standard fixes:
- **σ-modification** (Ioannou): add a damping term −σθ̂ to the update law, i.e. θ̂̇ = −Γφε − σθ̂. Mechanism: leaks the estimate toward zero, providing a restoring force in parameter space along *every* direction, including the unexcited one. Does not restrict the model class; biases all parameters.
- **e-modification** (Narendra): scales the leakage by the error norm, θ̂̇ = −Γφε − σ‖e‖θ̂, so leakage vanishes as the tracking error vanishes. Same class-free property; less bias at good fit.
- **Parameter projection**: constrains θ̂ to a known convex set via a projection operator; adaptation proceeds normally inside the set and is projected back on the boundary. Requires a-priori bounds; does not restrict the model class, only the parameter domain. Widely regarded (with the dead-zone) as the most practically essential modification.
- **Dead-zone**: freezes adaptation when the error norm is below a noise-driven threshold, preventing adaptation to noise (a drift driver).

The direct analogue for your problem: the DC-force weight of the residual on the free mode is exactly a parameter that should be leaked toward zero (σ-style) or projected/dead-zoned, because the data cannot inform it.

**Null-space / composite-learning result (the precise formal object).** "Composite Learning Adaptive Control under Non-Persistent Partial Excitation" (arXiv:2408.01731) proves that without any excitation condition, "the excited parameter estimation error component θ̃₁ will converge exponentially to 0, while the unexcited component θ̃₂ will be kept unchanged," with the excited/unexcited subspaces defined as the range/null-space R[W(t)] and N[W(t)] of the accumulated regressor information matrix. This is the exact decomposition you need: your DC direction lives in N[W], and the remedy is to add a restoring term acting on the projection onto N[W]. No model-class restriction.

**Orthogonal-projection regularization for hybrid models (directly on-point).** Kon, Bruijnen, van de Wijdeven & Oomen (2022, "Physics-Guided Neural Networks for Feedforward Control: An Orthogonal Projection-Based Approach") and the state-space generalization by Györök, Hoekstra, Kon, Péni, Schoukens & Tóth ("Orthogonal projection-based regularization for efficient model augmentation," arXiv:2501.05842) penalize the neural-network output *inside the subspace spanned by the physics model* via R(φ) = ‖U₁ᵀ g_φ‖², where U₁ is an orthonormal basis of the physics-model image. Mechanism: forces the NN to learn *only* in the complementary subspace (unmodeled dynamics), preventing the NN from re-learning / corrupting the interpretable physics parameters. This is a *direction-selective* penalty and does **not** restrict the representable class (the NN can still represent any residual in the complement). It is the closest existing tool to "act only on the data-silent direction," though as published it targets the physics-image subspace, not specifically the data-null-space.

**Tikhonov / minimum-norm regularization.** Classical ill-posed-inverse theory: replace the least-squares problem by min ‖Kx−y‖² + α‖Lx‖². Via the SVD, Tikhonov applies a filter φ(sᵢ;α)=sᵢ²/(sᵢ²+α) that continuously turns off modes with small singular values (the unexcited directions), selecting the minimum-norm solution along the null-space. This is exactly your "regularize the unexcited direction" prescription in linear form; with L=I it biases the DC weight toward zero. No model-class restriction (it is a penalty, not a constraint).

**Kernel / stable-spline / RKHS priors (Pillonetto–De Nicolao).** "A new kernel-based approach for linear system identification" (Pillonetto & De Nicolao, *Automatica* 46(1):81–93, 2010) recasts identification as regularized regression in an RKHS. It models "the impulse response as the realization of a Gaussian process whose statistics ... include information not only on smoothness but also on BIBO-stability ... what we call a stable spline kernel." Mechanism: the kernel prior shrinks impulse-response components that data do not support toward a stable, decaying prior — a structured Tikhonov penalty (the TC/DC/SS kernel family). For a residual force, a stable-kernel prior would penalize a persistent (non-decaying) DC component. Restriction: the *prior* biases toward stable/decaying responses; it does not hard-restrict the class but does encode a stability preference that could fight a genuine slowly-varying residual.

**Bayesian zero-mean force priors.** In GP residual-dynamics learning (e.g., zero-mean GP prior v = q(z) ~ GP(0, k(z,z′)) for vehicle/robot residual forces), the zero-mean prior means that *far from data the posterior mean returns to zero* and the prediction reverts to the physics model — automatically suppressing a learned force where data is absent. This is the probabilistic version of the informativity-weighted penalty and is knowledge-free.

### Q2 — Stabilizing long-horizon / free-run simulation-error training without restricting the class

- **Multiple shooting** (Ribeiro & Aguirre, "Shooting Methods for Parameter Estimation of Output Error Models," IFAC 2017; Ribeiro et al., "On the smoothness of nonlinear system identification," 2019/2020; Turan & Jäschke, "Multiple Shooting for Training Neural Differential Equations on Time Series," IEEE L-CSS 2022). Splits the trajectory into short segments with continuity constraints; makes the simulation length a *design parameter*. This is exactly your "condition the horizon" lever — but note multiple shooting is designed to fix *optimization* pathologies (complex loss landscape, the "curse of length," non-contractive regions), not to make a slow bounded drift loss-visible. If your window is shorter than the drift timescale, multiple shooting alone will *not* see the drift.
- **Subspace-encoder / SUBNET** (Beintema, Schoukens & Tóth, "Deep subspace encoders for nonlinear system identification," *Automatica* vol. 156, art. 111210, 2023, arXiv:2210.14816; continuous-time version arXiv:2204.09405). The method "uses a truncated prediction loss and a subspace encoder ... under mild conditions, the proposed method is locally consistent, increases optimization stability," with an encoder that estimates each subsection's initial state. This is the training backbone the entire Eindhoven augmentation line builds on. Same caveat: the truncation that gives stability is what hides the drift.
- **Horizon curriculum / pushforward / rollout-consistency and Jacobian/spectral regularization.** Recent neural-PDE/dynamics work (e.g., "Jacobian-Adaptive Weighting for Stability," arXiv:2603.05538; "Controlling Transient Amplification Improves Long-horizon Rollouts," arXiv:2605.08856) explicitly separates *high-frequency instability* (fixed by Jacobian/spectral contraction penalties) from *low-frequency phase/drift* (which "still benefits from trajectory optimization," i.e., longer rollouts). Directly relevant conclusion: **integrator/DC drift is a low-frequency phenomenon that spectral/Jacobian regularization does not remove — only horizon extension or an explicit DC penalty does.** None of these restrict the model class.
- **Gray-box normalization.** "On the normalization of gray-box state-space neural networks" (*Automatica* 2025) addresses training-stability of exactly your additive physics+residual structure via state-trajectory normalization; relevant to conditioning but not to drift per se.

**Direct answer:** several methods stabilize free-run training, but *none of them specifically target integrator drift*; the drift is orthogonal to the instability they cure. Your instinct to lengthen/condition the horizon is the only rollout-side lever, and it must be paired with a direction-selective penalty.

### Q3 — Marginal-mode / integrator preservation (no added stiffness)

- **Port-Hamiltonian / Hamiltonian / dissipative networks** (OE-pHNN: "Port-Hamiltonian Neural Networks with Output Error Noise Models," *Automatica* 2026 / arXiv:2502.14432; "Stable Port-Hamiltonian Neural Networks," arXiv:2502.02480). These embed energy functions, skew-symmetric interconnection, and positive-semidefinite dissipation *by architecture* (no penalty needed). They can represent dissipation *without* a restoring force in principle, and pHNNs are the most natural structural home for a "damping-but-no-stiffness" mode. **Critical caveat:** the *stable* variants (sPHNN) enforce global asymptotic stability by constraining the Hamiltonian to be a convex positive-definite Lyapunov function — this *adds* an effective restoring force (a single stable equilibrium) and would violate your marginal-mode requirement. The non-stabilized pHNN preserves the marginal mode but gives up the guarantee.
- **Contraction / REN** (Revay, Wang & Manchester, "Recurrent Equilibrium Networks: Unconstrained Learning of Stable and Robust Dynamical Models," arXiv:2104.05942; Martinelli et al., "Unconstrained parametrization of dissipative and contracting neural ODEs," CDC 2023). Direct, *constraint-free* (for-all-weights) parameterizations of contracting models. This is the "for-all-weights structural guarantee" archetype — but contraction means trajectories converge to each other, which is *strictly incompatible* with a marginal (imaginary-axis / unit-circle) pole. This is the concrete embodiment of your provable tension: for-all-weights stability guarantees buy their generality by excluding the marginal mode.
- **Momentum-conserving / translation-equivariant networks** (LieConv, Finzi et al., arXiv:2002.12880; Dynami-CAL GraphNet, Nature Comms 2025 / arXiv:2501.07373; "Exact conservation laws for neural network integrators," arXiv:2209.11661). By Noether's theorem, **translation invariance ⇒ conservation of linear momentum ⇒ no net DC force on the free coordinate.** This is the *theoretically correct* structural cure for your specific pathology: a translation-equivariant residual on the free-body coordinate cannot emit a position-dependent DC force, and a free translational mode is exactly a translation symmetry. **Restriction trade-off:** enforcing translation equivariance restricts the class to symmetric residuals — which is *appropriate* if the true residual (e.g., friction depending on velocity, not absolute position) respects that symmetry, but is a genuine class restriction if the residual legitimately depends on absolute position. For a free-body translation with velocity-dependent friction, equivariance is the principled, full-expressivity-within-the-symmetry answer.

### Q4 — Epistemic-uncertainty suppression and the Fisher-null-space question

- **MOPO** (Yu et al., "MOPO: Model-based Offline Policy Optimization," arXiv:2005.13239) penalizes reward by an uncertainty estimate of the learned dynamics: penalized reward r̃ = r̂ − λ·u(s,a), where the practical uncertainty is the Frobenius norm of the max ensemble variance, U_r(s,a) = maxᵢ‖Σᵢ(s,a)‖_F. MOReL uses a hard pessimistic-MDP terminal state instead. The operational logic — *distrust the model where data is uninformative* — is identical to your requirement.
- **Ensemble disagreement / GP predictive variance.** Lu et al. ("Revisiting Design Choices in Offline Model-Based RL," arXiv:2110.04135) find canonical ensemble-variance penalties best track true dynamics error ("the ensemble penalties generally perform best"). GP predictive variance grows away from data by construction.
- **Formal identity with Fisher null-space?** *Not in general.* Fisher information I(θ)=E[∇log p ∇log pᵀ] and its null space define the *locally unidentifiable* parameter directions; GP predictive variance and Fisher information coincide only in the linear-Gaussian regime where the posterior covariance is the inverse Fisher information (I⁻¹). The "Fisher floor" diagnostic-to-intervention idea (arXiv:2510.03809) is exactly a penalty on directions where Fisher information falls below a threshold — the linear/local formalization of "regularize the unexcited direction." So: **the same object in spirit, provably identical only under linear-Gaussian assumptions.** For your linear DC direction on a marginal mode, the linear-Gaussian assumption is a reasonable local approximation, so a Fisher-null-space penalty and an ensemble-variance penalty would act on essentially the same direction.

### Q6 — DECISIVE QUESTION

**No single published method claims all of (a) fully-expressive residual, (b) no free-run drift on a marginally-stable mode, (c) open-loop simulation-error training, and (d) physical-parameter interpretability.**

The closest integrated framework is the Eindhoven/SZTAKI model-augmentation line, which nails (a), (c), (d):
- **W-PGNN** — Liu, Tóth & Schoukens, "Physics-Guided State-Space Model Augmentation Using Weighted Regularized Neural Networks," *IFAC-PapersOnLine* 58(15):295–300, 2024 (arXiv:2405.10429). Adds a weighted regularizer penalizing ‖f_θ‖² and ‖g_θ‖² of the ANN completion functions, with weight w_j given by an inverse Gaussian-kernel density estimate of proximity to training data. Verbatim (confirmed across arXiv/ScienceDirect/TU-e): *"This ensures the estimated model follows the baseline physics model functions in regions where the data has low information content, while placing greater trust in the data when a high informativity is present."* Trained with output/simulation error (Levenberg–Marquardt); physics parameters stay interpretable. **This is the single most on-point mechanism for requirement (4) — it pulls the residual toward zero exactly where data is uninformative.** But it does not explicitly address a marginally-stable mode or free-run drift (b), and its weighting is over state-input data density, not the specific DC direction of a free mode.
- **Constraint-free contracting-LFR augmentation** — Györök, Drenth, Verhoek, Péni, Schoukens & Tóth, "Data-driven augmentation of first-principles models under constraint-free well-posedness and stability guarantees," arXiv:2604.11421 (submitted to *Automatica*, 2026). Provides a *direct, constraint-free (for-all-weights) parametrization* guaranteeing well-posedness (via a convergent fixed-point iteration for the LFR feedthrough) and stability *via contraction* (Cayley-transform parameterization + Lipschitz bounding of the ANN), trainable with unconstrained optimization (Adam), preserving physical parameters, on the SUBNET simulation-error backbone. This is the archetype of a "for-all-weights structural guarantee + full expressivity + interpretability" method — **but its guarantee is contraction, which structurally excludes the marginally-stable mode you must preserve.** It therefore fails (b) in your specific sense (it would stabilize the free pole, i.e., add a fake restoring force). No explicit treatment of integrators/free rigid-body modes/drift appears in the paper.

**Where the gap is, precisely:** the literature offers, separately, (i) for-all-weights structural *stability* guarantees that kill the marginal mode (contraction/REN/sPHNN); (ii) full-expressivity data-informativity regularizers that suppress the residual off-data but make no marginal-mode or drift claim (W-PGNN, orthogonal-projection); and (iii) structural no-DC-force guarantees via symmetry that restrict the class (translation-equivariant/momentum-conserving). **No paper unifies a marginal-mode-preserving structural guarantee with a data-silent-direction penalty under open-loop simulation-error training with interpretable physics.** Your framing — that a for-all-weights structural no-drift guarantee and full expressivity are incompatible, so non-drift must be recovered empirically on the estimator side — is confirmed by this survey.

**Closest method per requirement:**
- (a) Full expressivity: W-PGNN / LFR augmentation / SUBNET (all unrestricted NN residuals).
- (b) No free-run drift on marginal mode: *no direct match*; nearest structural cure is translation-equivariant/momentum-conserving networks (Noether: no DC force), nearest estimator cure is σ-modification/parameter-projection/Fisher-floor on the DC weight.
- (c) Open-loop simulation-error training: SUBNET (Beintema et al., *Automatica* 2023) and multiple shooting (Ribeiro & Aguirre).
- (d) Physical-parameter interpretability: orthogonal-projection regularization (Kon/Györök) and W-PGNN.

### Q7 — LPV with a drifting scheduling state

Deep-learning LPV identification now jointly learns the scheduling map and the LPV-SS model from I/O data (Verhoek, Beintema, Haesaert, Schoukens & Tóth, "Deep-Learning-Based Identification of LPV Models for Nonlinear Systems," arXiv:2204.04060, with consistency guarantees under innovation noise; and "Stable-by-Design Neural Network-Based LPV State-Space Models," arXiv:2510.24757, which guarantees a Schur-stable state-transition matrix for learned internal scheduling variables). Classical embeddings (Abbas et al.; Schoukens & Tóth LFR) let the scheduling variable be a state. **However, none of these treats the case where the scheduling variable is itself a drifting/unidentifiable state whose unbounded excursion corrupts the scheduling grid.** The practical implication: because your scheduling variable *is* one of the drifting position states, bounding the drift is a *prerequisite* for scheduling integrity — you cannot self-schedule on a signal that leaves the identified scheduling envelope. This reinforces putting the drift fix upstream (on the residual/estimator), not downstream in the LPV interpolation.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Diagnose and make the drift loss-visible (do first).**
- Compute the accumulated information/regressor matrix along the free mode and confirm the DC-force direction lies in its (numerical) null space — i.e., verify empirically that the direction is loss-neutral, reproducing the composite-learning decomposition (arXiv:2408.01731). This confirms the diagnosis before you regularize.
- Extend the multiple-shooting / SUBNET rollout window (or add a small number of long "validation rollouts" to the loss) until the position drift over the window exceeds the noise floor. **Threshold that changes the plan:** if a horizon long enough to expose the drift is computationally feasible and eliminates it, you may not need an explicit penalty. If the required horizon is prohibitive (drift timescale ≫ trainable window), proceed to Stage 2.

**Stage 2 — Add a direction-selective, knowledge-free penalty (primary fix).**
- Apply a **data-informativity-weighted regularizer (W-PGNN mechanism, arXiv:2405.10429)** pulling the residual force on the free mode toward zero where data is uninformative, using an inverse-kernel-density weight. This is knowledge-free, preserves full expressivity (residual is free where data is rich), adds no stiffness, and is the published method most aligned with your requirement (4).
- Add an **orthogonal-projection / null-space penalty** (Kon/Györök mechanism, arXiv:2501.05842) that specifically penalizes the *DC component* of the residual force on the free coordinate (project the residual onto the constant/low-frequency subspace of that coordinate and penalize its norm). This is the neural analogue of σ-modification restricted to the unidentifiable direction — it leaks *only* the data-silent DC weight, not the genuine (velocity-dependent friction) residual.
- Equivalently in Bayesian form, adopt a **zero-mean prior on the residual force** with a stable/decaying (stable-spline-style) kernel, so the residual reverts to zero off-data.

**Stage 3 — Consider a structural cure only if the symmetry genuinely holds.**
- If the true residual on the free coordinate depends only on velocity (friction) and not absolute position — which is physically expected for a free-body translation — impose **translation equivariance on that coordinate's residual**. By Noether's theorem this makes a position-dependent DC force unrepresentable *by construction*, giving a for-all-weights no-drift guarantee on that mode while preserving the marginal pole. Accept this only if you are confident the residual respects the symmetry; otherwise it is a class restriction that violates requirement (2).

**Do NOT:**
- Use contraction/REN/stable-pHNN parameterizations for the free mode — they add an effective restoring force and violate marginal-mode preservation.
- Rely on Jacobian/spectral rollout regularization to fix the drift — it targets high-frequency instability, not low-frequency DC drift.
- Move to closed-loop training to hide the bias — it masks the biased model exactly as you note.

**Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:** if the free-run position over a horizon 10–100× the training window stays bounded within tolerance after Stage 2, stop. If the drift persists, escalate to Stage 3 (structural equivariance) or add a hard parameter-projection bound on the DC weight.

## Caveats
- The single most decisive source for the "closest integrated method" (constraint-free contracting-LFR augmentation, arXiv:2604.11421) is a 2026 preprint submitted to *Automatica*; some interior sections (stable-by-construction augmentation, examples) could not be fully retrieved, and its contraction-based guarantee is inferred to exclude marginal modes rather than being stated to do so for your case. Treat its marginal-mode incompatibility as strongly-supported inference, not an explicit published claim.
- The exact regularization equations for W-PGNN were partly transcribed via a secondary summary; the headline claim is verbatim-confirmed across arXiv/ScienceDirect/TU-e, but verify the precise weight formula against the primary PDF before implementation.
- The claimed formal equivalence between GP predictive variance and the Fisher-information null space holds only under linear-Gaussian assumptions; for a strongly nonlinear residual the two objects can diverge, though along your (approximately linear) DC direction they should coincide.
- "No single method claims all four" is a statement about the surveyed literature as of July 2026; the model-augmentation field (Eindhoven/SZTAKI, ERC COMPLETE project) is moving quickly, and a marginal-mode-aware variant may appear.
- Several supporting hits (neural-PDE rollout papers) are from adjacent domains (fluid/PDE surrogates); their drift/instability taxonomy transfers cleanly, but their benchmarks are not mechanical motion systems.
