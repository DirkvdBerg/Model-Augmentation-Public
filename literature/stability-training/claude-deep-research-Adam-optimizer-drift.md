# Adam's Persistent Drift Along Flat / Degenerate Directions: Name, Mechanism, and Remedies

## TL;DR
- **Yes, this is a named, characterized phenomenon.** It is the well-established consequence of Adam's **implicit bias as sign / ℓ∞-normalized steepest descent** (Balles & Hennig 2018; Kunstner et al. 2023; Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida 2024; Zhang, Zou & Cao 2024; Xie & Li 2024). Because Adam's per-coordinate second-moment normalization makes the step size ≈ learning-rate regardless of gradient magnitude, a *tiny but consistent-sign* gradient in a flat direction produces a persistent ≈η-per-step drift; plain SGD, whose step scales with the (tiny) gradient, stays ≈stationary. Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida (2024, ICML, "On the Implicit Bias of Adam," PMLR 235:5862–5906) prove Adam's implicit bias is an **"anti-regularization"**: their ODE terms "either penalize the (perturbed) one-norm of the loss gradients or, conversely, impede its reduction (the latter case being typical)" — the theoretical signature of this drift.
- **The single most reliable off-the-shelf fix is decoupled weight decay (AdamW), which adds a linear restoring force −λθ in every coordinate**, including flat ones. Xie & Li (2024, ICML, arXiv:2404.04454) prove AdamW iterates (if they converge) reach a KKT point of the loss under the constraint ‖θ‖∞ ≤ 1/λ — i.e., drift is bounded to a box of radius 1/λ. This directly stops unbounded flat-direction drift, but it does not zero the offset: it pins parameters to a weight-decay-set value, not to the SGD value. For an exact match to SGD's behavior in a *specific known* degenerate direction, **explicit null-space projection / freezing of that direction is the accepted targeted answer.**
- **There is no single canonical "toy benchmark" branded as "Adam drifts in a null direction, SGD does not, at equal loss,"** but the phenomenon is a proven corollary of the sign-descent mechanism and is documented across subfields: LLM pretraining (weight-norm/gradient-norm growth, loss spikes), grokking (Adam/AdamW-dependent norm growth), and continual/RL plasticity (drift under zero/near-zero gradients). The go-to remedies are AdamW, and increasingly **Cautious optimizers (C-AdamW/C-Lion, Liang et al. 2024)** and **Lion (Chen et al. 2023, with the ℓ∞ analysis in Chen et al. 2024)**.

## Key Findings

**1. Name & field.** The phenomenon has no single proper name but is precisely the behavior predicted by three overlapping, well-established framings: (i) **Adam ≈ sign descent** (Balles & Hennig 2018; Bernstein et al. 2018); (ii) **Adam ≈ steepest descent in the ℓ∞ norm**, hence implicit bias toward ℓ∞-max-margin / ℓ∞-bounded solutions (Zhang, Zou & Cao 2024; Xie & Li 2024); and (iii) Adam's **implicit bias as "anti-regularization"** of the gradient norm (Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida 2024). Subfields that document the concrete drift: implicit bias of adaptive methods; LLM pretraining stability (weight-norm and gradient-norm growth); grokking/delayed generalization; and continual learning / reinforcement-learning plasticity loss (parameter drift under near-zero gradients).

**2. Mechanism (what is proven vs empirical).**
- *Proven (algebraic identity):* With ε=0, Adam's update is m_t/√v_t = γ_t ⊙ sign(m_t), where γ_t = (1+(v_t−m_t²)/m_t²)^{−1/2} (Balles & Hennig 2018, "Dissecting Adam: The Sign, Magnitude and Variance of Stochastic Gradients," ICML). For a coordinate whose gradient is small but **consistent in sign** (low relative variance), γ_t → 1, so the step → ±η — a full learning-rate step regardless of how tiny the gradient is. This is the exact drift mechanism.
- *Proven (limit):* Adam reverts to signSGD as β1,β2,ε→0 (Bernstein et al. 2018).
- *Proven (ODE / backward-error analysis):* Full-batch Adam with fixed β, ρ, ε tracks a perturbed sign-GD flow θ̇ = −∇E(θ)/√(|∇E(θ)|²+ε); and Adam typically *anti-penalizes* the perturbed 1-norm ‖v‖_{1,ε}=Σ√(v_i²+ε) — "anti-regularization" (Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida 2024, ICML). This is the theoretical statement that Adam does *not* damp, and can even amplify, motion in low-gradient directions. The effect is strongest when β1<β2 and ε is small or moderate.
- *Proven (margin / constraint):* Without the ε constant, Adam converges in direction to the ℓ∞-max-margin (equivalently minimum ℓ∞-norm) solution on separable linear data (Zhang, Zou & Cao 2024, NeurIPS, "The Implicit Bias of Adam on Separable Data"); with ε>0 it eventually behaves like GD and recovers the ℓ2 solution (Wang et al. 2021/2022) — showing the drift depends on the ε regime.
- *Empirical:* The SGD–Adam gap persists in the full-batch/deterministic regime (so it is not a noise phenomenon) and is closed specifically by *coordinate-wise* sign descent, not global gradient normalization (Kunstner, Chen, Lavington & Schmidt 2023, ICLR, "Noise Is Not the Main Factor Behind the Gap Between SGD and Adam on Transformers, but Sign Descent Might Be"). Vanilla Adam takes large ≈η steps in the flat early-training loss landscape of transformers (arXiv:2412.02153).

**3. Optimizer-level remedies (ranked).**

1. **AdamW — decoupled weight decay (Loshchilov & Hutter 2017/2019).** Adds −λθ to every coordinate, a restoring force present even where the loss gradient is ~0. *Proven:* Xie & Li (2024, ICML, "Implicit Bias of AdamW: ℓ∞ Norm Constrained Optimization," arXiv:2404.04454) show AdamW, if it converges under any non-increasing LR schedule with divergent partial sum and β1≤β2<1, converges to a KKT point of min_{‖θ‖∞≤1/λ} L(θ) (convex L ⇒ constrained minimizer). Kosson, Messmer & Jaggi (2024, ICML, "Rotational Equilibrium: How Weight Decay Balances Learning Across Neural Networks," arXiv:2305.17212) show AdamW drives a nonzero steady-state weight norm V∞ ≈ ηC/(2λ), the point where "the expected magnitude and angular updates of a neuron's weight vector converge to a steady state we call rotational equilibrium." *Cost:* introduces a nonzero equilibrium offset set by λ (does NOT return the parameter to the SGD value — it pins it to a weight-decay box), requires tuning λ, and interacts with LR schedules/normalization (motivating corrected variants such as AdamC, Defazio 2025). **Verdict: the standard, best-supported off-the-shelf fix to *bound* the drift.**
2. **Cautious optimizers — C-AdamW / C-Lion (Liang, Chen, Liu & Liu 2024, "Cautious Optimizers: Improving Training with One Line of Code," arXiv:2411.16085, accepted ICLR 2026).** One-line mask that zeroes any coordinate where the proposed update and the current gradient disagree in sign, enforcing strict per-step descent. *Proven:* preserves the optimizer's Hamiltonian/Lyapunov descent structure. *Empirical:* "The cautious variants achieve better convergence and are 1.47x and 1.28x more sample efficient for AdamW and Lion respectively" on LLaMA 1B pretraining. In a truly flat direction with a consistent-sign gradient the mask does **not** fully stop drift (update and gradient agree), but it prevents momentum-driven drift where gradient and update disagree — directly relevant to RL/continual "zero-advantage" momentum drift. *Cost:* negligible.
3. **Lion / Lion-K.** Lion is from Chen et al., "Symbolic Discovery of Optimization Algorithms," NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2302.06675); its ℓ∞-constraint characterization is the separate paper Chen, Liu, Liang & Liu (2024, ICLR), "Lion Secretly Solves Constrained Optimization: As Lyapunov Predicts," which proves Lion minimizes a general loss f(x) subject to ‖x‖∞ ≤ 1/λ. Same ℓ∞-box bounding of drift as AdamW, more memory-efficient. *Cost:* still sign-based (same drift mechanism when λ=0); needs weight decay to bound drift.
4. **Explicit null-space projection / freezing the degenerate direction.** From continual learning (Orthogonal Gradient Descent, Farajtabar et al. 2020; Gradient Projection Memory, Saha et al. 2021; null-space projection, Wang et al. 2021) and gradient centralization (Yong et al. 2020, ECCV, which projects out the mean/constant direction). If the flat direction v is *known*, projecting the update off v (or freezing θ·v) removes the drift exactly and reproduces SGD-like stationarity. *Cost:* requires identifying v; the strict orthogonality constraint can limit learning in other directions. **Verdict: the accepted answer when the specific direction is known and must be held fixed.**
5. **AdaBelief (Zhuang et al. 2020) / RAdam (Liu et al. 2020) / variance-adapted methods.** AdaBelief divides by the EMA of (g−m)², so in a "large-gradient, small-curvature" region it adapts step size differently from Adam. *Caveat:* AdaBelief explicitly *intends to take a large step* in flat (near-zero-gradient) regions (its own Region-1 analysis), so it does **not** cure consistent-sign drift and may worsen it; RAdam only rectifies early-training variance. *Evidence:* empirical, not targeted at this phenomenon.
6. **Gradient centralization (Yong et al. 2020).** Projects gradients to zero-mean within each weight vector — removes drift specifically along the all-ones/constant direction; a special-case null-space projection. *Cost:* only addresses the mean direction.
7. **Gradient clipping / ε adjustments / β2 tuning / adaptive gradient clipping (e.g. AdaGC, Zhao et al. 2025).** Standard LLM-stability tools that reduce spike-driven drift but do not target the flat-direction mechanism.

**4. Canonical demonstration.** No single paper presents the exact minimal "θ·v grows linearly under Adam, flat under SGD, at equal loss" synthetic benchmark under that branding. The best-supported anchors are: Balles & Hennig (2018) for the mechanism (step→±η in consistent-sign directions); Kunstner et al. (2023) for the empirical full-batch separation that is closed by coordinate-wise sign descent; Xie & Li (2024) for the rigorous ℓ∞-drift/constraint characterization; and signSGD non-convergence counterexamples (Karimireddy et al. 2019, "Error Feedback Fixes SignSGD…," ICML; Bernstein et al. 2018) showing sign methods keep taking ±η steps even with the exact full gradient. The unbounded-linear-drift (λ=0) version is effectively folklore/textbook-level rather than attributed to one canonical toy example.

**5. Cross-field appearances.**
- *LLM pretraining:* weight-norm and gradient-norm growth under AdamW is standard; small/decoupled weight decay and corrected-decay variants (AdamC, Defazio 2025) keep norms bounded; loss spikes are mitigated with β2 tuning, gradient clipping, and ε adjustments (Chowdhery et al. 2023 PaLM; Wortsman et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2025). This is the closest to a "solved, standard issue" — with AdamW + weight decay as the go-to remedy.
- *Grokking:* delayed generalization — first reported by Power, Burda, Edwards, Babuschkin & Misra (2022, "Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets," arXiv:2201.02177), where networks show "improving generalization performance from random chance level to perfect generalization … well past the point of overfitting" — is tied to weight-norm growth under Adam/AdamW and is reported to be optimizer-dependent (originally observed with AdamW; some studies report SGD failing to grok at the same hyperparameters). Weight decay is the standard lever, though several 2024–2025 studies show grokking can occur even with rising weight norm, so the norm account is contested.
- *Continual learning / RL plasticity:* "optimizer-induced drift" under near-zero or zero gradients (momentum + second-moment normalization keep updating even when the batch gradient is ~0), addressed by null-space/orthogonal projection, dormant-neuron resets (ReDo, Sokar et al. 2023), L2-init / regenerative regularization (Kumar et al. 2023), and optimizer-state handling.

## Details

**The precise mechanism, restated.** Adam's per-coordinate update is Δθ_i = −η · m_i/(√v_i+ε). In a flat direction with a small but sign-consistent gradient g_i, both m_i and √v_i scale with |g_i|, so the ratio m_i/√v_i → sign(g_i) and Δθ_i → −η·sign(g_i): a magnitude-independent, same-sign step every iteration. Summed over T steps, θ·v grows ≈ −ηT·sign(g·v) — linear drift to a nonzero offset. SGD's step is Δθ_i = −η·g_i, which vanishes with g_i, so θ·v stays ≈ stationary. Both reach the same loss because motion is along a (near-)flat direction. This is not zero-mean noise being integrated; it is a deterministic bias, exactly what Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida (2024) formalize as anti-regularization / impeded reduction of the (perturbed) 1-norm of the gradient.

**Why AdamW bounds it but does not zero it.** With decoupled weight decay the flat-direction recursion becomes θ_i ← (1−λη)θ_i − η·sign(g_i). The fixed point is θ_i* ≈ −sign(g_i)/λ, i.e., the drift saturates at magnitude 1/λ rather than growing without bound (Xie & Li 2024; consistent with Kosson et al. 2024's ηC/(2λ) rotational-equilibrium norm). So AdamW converts unbounded linear drift into a bounded offset whose size is controlled by λ — helpful for stability, but the offset is nonzero and generally different from SGD's (≈0) value. If a downstream integrator amplifies any offset, AdamW reduces but does not eliminate the effect; you must either (a) increase λ (at a generalization/loss cost), or (b) remove the direction explicitly.

**The Xie & Li (2024) guarantee, precisely.** Theorem 1.1: for continuously differentiable L, β1≤β2<1, and non-increasing LR {η_t} with Σ η_t = ∞, if AdamW's iterates converge to x∞ then x∞ is a KKT point of min_{‖x‖∞<1/λ} L(x); if L is convex, x∞ is the constrained minimizer. The proof routes through a connection between normalized steepest descent with weight decay and Frank-Wolfe on the ℓ∞ ball. Notably the authors observe that "AdamW cannot be written in the form of Lion-K for any convex function K," so this is a distinct result from the Lion Frank–Wolfe analysis. This is *proven* but conditional on convergence and on β1≤β2 (they give a counterexample when β1>β2).

## Recommendations

1. **First, adopt AdamW with a deliberately chosen weight decay λ** (if not already). This is the standard, theoretically grounded fix that bounds flat-direction drift to a box of radius 1/λ (Xie & Li 2024). *Benchmark to check:* monitor θ·v (projection of parameters on the flat direction) and the weight norm over training; if drift saturates instead of growing linearly, weight decay is working. *Threshold to change approach:* if the saturated offset ~1/λ is still large enough that the downstream integrator amplifies it unacceptably, and raising λ hurts training loss, move to step 3.
2. **If the flat/degenerate direction is known and must stay fixed, project the Adam update off that direction (or freeze θ·v).** This is the accepted targeted remedy from continual learning (orthogonal/null-space projection; gradient centralization for the mean direction). It reproduces SGD-like stationarity in that direction exactly, at essentially zero training cost, and is more surgical than global weight decay. **This is the recommended answer for your configuration** (a specific flat output direction amplified downstream): targeted regularization/projection of the specific direction is the correct fix, not a generic optimizer swap.
3. **If you want a drop-in optimizer change rather than a projection, try Cautious AdamW (C-AdamW, Liang et al. 2024) or Lion with weight decay.** C-AdamW is a one-line change that stops momentum-driven drift where update and gradient disagree; Lion gives the same ℓ∞-box bounding as AdamW with less memory. Neither fully removes consistent-sign drift without weight decay, so pair with λ.
4. **Do NOT rely on AdaBelief/RAdam to fix this** — AdaBelief is explicitly designed to take *large* steps in flat regions and can worsen the drift; RAdam only addresses early-training variance.
5. **As a diagnostic**, reduce or increase Adam's ε and sweep β2: the drift is strongest in the small-ε / β1<β2 regime (Cattaneo et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024). If increasing ε toward the gradient scale reduces the drift, that confirms the sign-descent mechanism is the cause; equivalently, if replacing Adam with plain SGD (or normalized GD) at equal loss removes the offset, the ℓ∞/coordinate-wise sign mechanism (not noise) is responsible, per Kunstner et al. (2023).

## Caveats
- **No exact-configuration paper.** I did not find a peer-reviewed source presenting the precise setup you describe — same net/data/loss/LR, Adam grows a constant offset in a flat *output* direction while SGD does not, at equal loss, with a downstream integrator amplifying it — as a named, solved problem. Your phenomenon is a correct and direct corollary of proven results, but the specific "flat *output*-direction drift amplified by an integrator" instance appears uncovered; treat it as an application of the sign-descent/ℓ∞ theory rather than a documented benchmark.
- **Proven vs empirical boundary.** *Proven:* the sign-descent identity (Balles & Hennig 2018), the anti-regularization ODE (Cattaneo, Klusowski & Shigida 2024), the ℓ∞-margin/constraint results (Zhang, Zou & Cao 2024; Xie & Li 2024), the rotational-equilibrium steady state (Kosson et al. 2024), Lion's ℓ∞ constraint (Chen et al. 2024), and signSGD non-convergence (Karimireddy et al. 2019). *Empirical:* the full-batch SGD–Adam separation (Kunstner et al. 2023), grokking's optimizer-dependence, and the practical LLM-stability remedies.
- **AdamW does not equal SGD in flat directions.** It bounds but does not zero the offset; the residual is ~1/λ-sized. Only explicit projection/freezing reproduces SGD's stationarity exactly.
- **Conditionality of the AdamW theorem.** Xie & Li (2024) is conditional on convergence and on β1≤β2; it does not by itself guarantee AdamW converges.
- **Some cited items are recent preprints** (2025–2026 arXiv on corrected decoupled weight decay, mini-batch-noise implicit bias, and masked optimizers); treat their quantitative claims as not-yet-peer-reviewed. The core references (Balles & Hennig 2018; Kunstner et al. 2023; Cattaneo et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024; Xie & Li 2024; Kosson et al. 2024; Liang et al. 2024) are peer-reviewed (ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR).
