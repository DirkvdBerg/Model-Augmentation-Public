# Optimizer Choice and Implicit Bias for Physics-Augmented Neural State-Space Models: Eliminating Adam-Induced DC Drift in Marginally-Stable Modes

## TL;DR
- **The root cause is correctly diagnosed and is a known phenomenon**: Adam behaves as sign/ℓ∞ steepest descent (Balles & Hennig 2018 isolate "the sign aspect as the problematic one"; Kunstner et al. 2023 find "the behavior of Adam with large batches is similar to sign descent with momentum"; Zhang et al. 2024 on ℓ∞-max-margin), so it converts a tiny, seed-consistent, TBPTT-biased gradient in the loss-flat integrator-velocity direction (Tallec & Ollivier 2017: "truncation favors short-term dependencies... the gradient estimate of truncated BPTT is biased") into a persistent DC walk that integrates into unbounded position drift. The best first move is **not** to abandon Adam wholesale but to **remove the flat-direction degree of freedom directly**: enforce a zero-mean (DC-free) constraint on the integrator-velocity correction rows, which surgically kills the pathology while leaving Adam's conditioning benefit intact.
- **Do not switch to plain global-learning-rate SGD as your primary fix.** On badly-scaled, block-heterogeneous recurrent/SSM objectives (exactly your mixed positions/velocities/latents case), the peer-reviewed evidence is that SGD systematically underfits or converges far slower than Adam (Wilson et al. 2017; Kunstner et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024, who show "SGD can perform on par with Adam on problems without block heterogeneity, but performs worse than Adam when the heterogeneity exists"). Your 2000× smaller DC under SGD is real but is a byproduct of SGD taking a vanishing step in a flat direction — you would likely pay for it in fit quality and conditioning.
- **Recommended stack (ranked):** (1) architectural zero-mean/DC-free constraint on the integrator correction + AdamW; (2) add a small explicit DC/offset penalty on the integrator-velocity output as a soft backstop; (3) reduce TBPTT bias at its source (longer/re-randomized windows, better encoder warm-up, or ARTBP-style reweighting); (4) consider cautious-Adam (C-AdamW, Liang et al. 2024) as a low-cost drop-in; (5) treat gradient centralization and generic pole/stability regularization as diagnostics or last resorts, **not** first-line — naive spectral regularization is actively dangerous because it will bias your true z=1 poles inward. Validate every candidate on **held-out free-run NRMSE/BFR and long-horizon rollout drift**, never on matched short-window training loss.

## Key Findings

1. **Adam ≈ sign descent, and that is exactly why it produces the DC walk.** Balles & Hennig (2018, ICML, PMLR 80:404–413; arXiv:1705.07774) formally decompose Adam into (i) the *sign* of the stochastic gradient and (ii) a variance-adaptation magnitude, stating their "analysis also extends recent results on adverse effects of ADAM on generalization, isolating the sign aspect as the problematic one." Kunstner, Chen, Lavington & Schmidt (2023, ICLR; arXiv:2304.13960) show that in the full-batch / large-batch regime — which your single-seed, seed-consistent-gradient probe approximates — "the behavior of Adam with large batches is similar to sign descent with momentum." Because sign descent takes a step of magnitude ≈ learning rate largely independent of gradient magnitude, a tiny but consistent gradient component in a flat direction produces a steady, non-vanishing parameter walk. This is a mechanistic match to your observation.

2. **Adam's implicit bias is toward an ℓ∞ / max-margin-type solution, not the minimum-norm solution SGD prefers.** Zhang et al. (2024) prove that Adam without the stability constant ε converges in direction to the ℓ∞-max-margin solution in separable linear classification (contrast: Soudry et al. 2018 show GD → ℓ2-max-margin); Xie & Li (2024, arXiv:2404.04454) show AdamW implicitly solves an ℓ∞-norm-constrained problem connected to Frank–Wolfe. The practical upshot for you: Adam has no built-in pressure toward the small-norm / zero-offset solution in a degenerate subspace, whereas SGD/GD does. This is the theoretical basis for both (a) why Adam creeps and (b) why targeted, explicit regularization of that one subspace is the right remedy.

3. **TBPTT bias does not vanish for marginally-stable modes — this is your gradient source.** Tallec & Ollivier (2017, "Unbiasing Truncated Backpropagation Through Time," arXiv:1705.08209) state that "truncation favors short-term dependencies: the gradient estimate of truncated BPTT is biased, so that it does not benefit from the convergence guarantees from stochastic gradient theory," and demonstrate a synthetic "influence balancing" system where fixed-truncation BPTT "displays unreliable performance, and in worst case scenarios, divergence." For a pole at z=1 the state's sensitivity to past inputs decays as λ^k with λ→1, i.e. it does *not* decay within a ~400-step window, so the truncated gradient omits a non-decaying tail and is persistently biased — precisely a small, seed-consistent, non-zero-mean gradient on the integrator rows. (That the bias specifically fails to decay as λ→1 is a reasoned extrapolation from the λ^k sensitivity structure; Tallec & Ollivier prove the general bias and its pathology on a slow-mode synthetic task but do not state the λ→1 limit verbatim.)

4. **SGD's tiny DC is a symptom, not a solution.** SGD's magnitude-proportional step means it takes a vanishing step along a near-zero-gradient flat direction. Multiple lines of work (Poggio et al., CBMM Memo 067; the "Degeneracies are sticky for SGD" analysis; SGD-diffusion studies such as Feng & Tu, PNAS 2021 on the inverse variance–flatness relation) show SGD noise covariance shrinks along degenerate/zero-curvature Hessian directions, so SGD is nearly stationary there — hence your 2000× smaller offset. But the same body of work (Wilson et al. 2017; Kunstner et al. 2023) shows SGD pays for this with worse optimization on ill-conditioned/heterogeneous objectives.

5. **On badly-scaled sequence/SSM objectives, SGD generally underfits relative to Adam.** Wilson et al. (2017, NeurIPS, "The Marginal Value of Adaptive Gradient Methods") is often cited for "adaptive methods generalize worse," but that result is for well-conditioned vision classification; the mechanism that makes Adam win — block-heterogeneous curvature — is precisely present in your problem. Zhang et al. (2024, "Why Transformers Need Adam: A Hessian Perspective," NeurIPS 2024; arXiv:2402.16788) show that "SGD can perform on par with Adam on problems without block heterogeneity, but performs worse than Adam when the heterogeneity exists," because "SGD fails because it applies one single learning rate for all blocks, which cannot handle the heterogeneity among blocks"; they induce block heterogeneity artificially and make SGD fail even without attention. Your state vector mixing positions, velocities, and latent states on very different scales, plus a physics baseline plus an ANN correction, is a textbook block-heterogeneous objective. Community SSM/sysid practice reflects this: S4/S4D/S5 (Gu et al.), the SUBNET deep-subspace-encoder line (Beintema, Tóth, Schoukens 2021 L4DC, 2023 Automatica), and dynoNet (Forgione & Piga 2021, Int. J. Adapt. Control Signal Process.) all train with Adam/AdamW by default.

6. **Structured SSMs succeed by *parameterizing* stable/near-unit dynamics, not by penalizing them post hoc.** S4 (Gu et al. 2021) and its diagonal successors S4D/S5 keep eigenvalues in the stable region by construction (continuous-time real parts ≤ 0), initialized via HiPPO; Recurrent Equilibrium Networks (Revay, Wang, Manchester 2021, IEEE TAC; arXiv:2104.05942) give "built-in" contraction/stability guarantees via a direct unconstrained parameterization "by a vector in R^N." The transferable lesson: handle the marginal mode *structurally* (make the integrator an exact, protected z=1 pole and constrain what the ANN is allowed to add to it) rather than nudging poles with a generic penalty that cannot distinguish a true integrator from an instability.

## Details: Candidate-by-Candidate Assessment

For each candidate I give the expected effect on the **DC offset** and on **fit/conditioning**, and label evidence as **[peer-reviewed]**, **[reasoned extrapolation]**, or **[unknown/untested]** for your specific setting.

### A. Zero-mean / DC-free output constraint on the integrator-velocity correction (TOP RECOMMENDATION)
- **Mechanism:** Reparameterize the ANN output rows that feed the pure-integrator velocity states so their long-run/temporal mean is structurally zero — e.g., subtract a running/batch mean of that output channel, route the integrator-velocity correction through a mean-removing layer, or forbid a bias/DC term on those specific rows. The degenerate direction is then simply *not in the model's image*, so no optimizer can walk along it.
- **Effect on DC:** Eliminates it by construction (removes the degree of freedom entirely). **[reasoned extrapolation — strong].** This is the same principle as Gradient Centralization (Yong et al. 2020, ECCV; arXiv:2004.01461), which the authors describe as "a projected gradient descent method with a constrained loss function" enforcing zero mean; here you apply the constraint to the physically-meaningful output rather than to all gradients.
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Minimal downside — a true velocity correction on a frictionless integrator axis *should* be zero-mean over any physical trajectory (a nonzero mean is literally the drift artifact). You retain full Adam adaptivity everywhere else. **[reasoned extrapolation].**
- **Caveat:** If a genuinely nonzero mean velocity correction is physically required on that axis (e.g., an uncompensated constant force), a hard zero-mean constraint would underfit; use the soft penalty (B) instead or in combination. **[unknown/untested for your rig].**

### B. Soft DC/offset penalty on the integrator-velocity output + Adam (STRONG BACKSTOP)
- **Mechanism:** Add λ_DC · (mean_k w_ANN,velrow(x[k],u[k]))² to the loss, penalizing the batch/temporal mean of the offending output rows.
- **Effect on DC:** Suppresses it continuously; tunable. Because it creates real curvature in the previously-flat direction, it gives Adam's sign-step something to balance against, converting the random walk into a damped equilibrium. **[reasoned extrapolation].**
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Negligible if λ_DC is small and the penalty targets only the mean (not the dynamics). Safer than a hard constraint because it degrades gracefully. **[reasoned extrapolation].**
- **Relation to literature:** This is the "add explicit regularization in the exact degenerate subspace" strategy that the implicit-bias literature implies is necessary when the optimizer has no implicit pressure there (Wilson et al. 2017; Zhang et al. 2024).

### C. Reduce TBPTT bias at the source (longer windows / ARTBP / better encoder warm-up) (TREAT-THE-CAUSE)
- **Mechanism:** The gradient that Adam amplifies is itself an artifact of truncation. Options: (i) longer truncation windows so the λ^k tail is better captured; (ii) Anticipated Reweighted Truncated Backpropagation (ARTBP, Tallec & Ollivier 2017), which uses random truncation lengths with compensation factors to make the gradient *unbiased*; (iii) a longer encoder/washout prefix (as used in SUBNET, Beintema et al. 2023) so the integrator state is well-initialized.
- **Effect on DC:** Longer windows shrink the bias but cannot fully remove it for λ=1 within any finite window; ARTBP removes the bias in expectation and is the principled fix. **[peer-reviewed for the bias-removal claim; reasoned extrapolation that it removes *your* DC].**
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Longer windows increase compute and can worsen the non-convexity of simulation-error minimization (the very problem SUBNET's multiple-shooting split was designed to avoid, Beintema et al. 2021); ARTBP adds gradient variance. So this is complementary to A/B, not a standalone replacement. **[peer-reviewed context].**
- **Unknown/untested:** ARTBP has, to my knowledge, not been evaluated on physics-augmented neural state-space identification with integrator modes. **[unknown/untested].**

### D. Cautious Adam (C-AdamW) / cautious optimizers (LOW-COST DROP-IN)
- **Mechanism:** Liang, Chen, Liu & Liu (2024, "Cautious Optimizers: Improving Training with One Line of Code," arXiv:2411.16085) mask any update coordinate where the proposed update u_t and the current gradient g_t have opposite signs: mask φ = 𝟙(u_t ⊙ g_t > 0), with a rescaling to preserve average step size. The one-line change is `m = (u * g > 0).to(g.dtype); p.add_(u * m/(m.mean()+eps), alpha=-lr)`. The formal rule zeroes "the update... for coordinates on which the sign of u_t and g_t are inconsistent."
- **Effect on DC:** Plausibly *reduces* it. In a flat direction driven by momentum, the instantaneous gradient sign fluctuates around zero while the momentum-driven update keeps a consistent sign; on steps where they disagree the update is masked, damping the coherent walk. **[reasoned extrapolation].** Important caveat the authors themselves state: cautious optimizers are designed *not* to get stuck — "it does not get stuck at non-stationary points... because in typical momentum methods, the update direction u_t continues to accumulate the gradients and will eventually be updated to have a positive inner product with g_t." The paper does **not** analyze the g≈0 flat-direction/marginal-stability case, so using C-AdamW as a DC fix is an extrapolation, not a published result. **[unknown/untested for this purpose].**
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Preserves Adam's per-coordinate adaptivity and is proven to "preserve Adam's Hamiltonian function" and "not break the convergence guarantee under the Lyapunov analysis," with reported gains where C-AdamW and C-Lion are "1.47x and 1.28x more sample efficient for AdamW and Lion respectively" on LLaMA-1B (and MAE) pretraining. Downside risk is essentially zero (one line, no new hyperparameters). **[peer-reviewed for the training-speed claims; the DC benefit is extrapolation].**

### E. AdamW (decoupled weight decay) (HELPFUL, INSUFFICIENT ALONE)
- **Mechanism:** Loshchilov & Hutter (2019, ICLR, "Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization," arXiv:1711.05101) decouple weight decay from the adaptive step; weight decay then adds curvature (an −ε·λ·w drift) in *all* directions, including flat ones. Note L2 regularization and weight decay are *not* equivalent for adaptive methods — this is the paper's central point.
- **Effect on DC:** Adds a restoring force toward zero in the flat direction, so it *does* damp the walk to a small equilibrium — but only if the offending offset is realized through a decayed weight; if the DC is produced by a bias term or a combination that weight decay does not directly shrink, it may be weak. Weight decay competes against, rather than removes, the bias. **[reasoned extrapolation].**
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Generally positive/neutral and standard practice for SSMs (S4 is trained with AdamW). Use AdamW as your baseline optimizer under all other fixes. **[peer-reviewed as good practice].**

### F. Gradient Centralization (DIAGNOSTIC / NARROW USE)
- **Mechanism:** Yong et al. (2020, ECCV) project gradients to zero-mean per weight vector.
- **Effect on DC:** GC centralizes the *gradient* across a weight vector's fan-in, which is not the same axis as your *output* DC on specific state rows; it is not targeted at your degenerate direction and may or may not touch it. **[unknown/untested].** Prefer output-space mean removal (A), which is directly on the pathological axis.
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Usually mildly positive; low risk but low specificity here. **[peer-reviewed generally, off-target for this problem].**

### G. Plain / full-batch SGD (NOT RECOMMENDED AS PRIMARY)
- **Effect on DC:** Strongly suppresses it (your observed 2000×) because SGD's step vanishes with the gradient in the flat direction and SGD noise shrinks along degenerate directions ("Degeneracies are sticky for SGD"). **[peer-reviewed mechanism].**
- **Effect on fit/conditioning:** Likely worse fit and much slower convergence on your block-heterogeneous, multi-scale objective (Wilson et al. 2017; Kunstner et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024 Hessian perspective, whose explicit finding is that a single global learning rate "cannot handle the heterogeneity among blocks"). A single global learning rate cannot serve positions, velocities, and latent states with very different curvature simultaneously. **[peer-reviewed for the general claim; the size of the accuracy gap on *your* system is untested].**
- **Verdict:** Useful as a *scientific control* to confirm the mechanism (which you have effectively done), not as the deployed optimizer. A hybrid (Adam→SGD switch, SWATS-style, Keskar & Socher 2017) is a possible compromise but adds tuning and is unproven for sysid.

### H. Pole / spectral / stability regularization (USE WITH CAUTION — CAN BIAS PHYSICS)
- **Mechanism:** Penalize eigenvalues/spectral radius outside the unit disk (many variants; e.g., ReLU(|λ_j|−1+margin) penalties).
- **Effect on DC:** Indirect at best; a spectral-radius penalty does not specifically remove a DC output offset.
- **Critical caveat:** Your integrator poles are *genuinely* at z=1. Naive stability regularization pushes poles strictly inside the unit circle, which would **corrupt the physics** (turn a true integrator into a leaky one), introducing a systematic low-frequency modeling error — arguably worse than the drift you are fixing. The structured-SSM lesson (Gu et al.; Revay et al.) is to *parameterize* the marginal mode as protected and constrain only the ANN's deviation from it, not to apply a blanket eigenvalue penalty. **[reasoned extrapolation grounded in peer-reviewed parameterization practice].**
- **Verdict:** Do not use generic spectral regularization on the axis that must stay at z=1. If you want a stability guarantee elsewhere, prefer a *structural* parameterization (S4D-style stable region, or REN-style contraction) applied only to the learned/latent part.

## Recommendations (staged, with decision thresholds)

**Stage 0 — Instrument first.** Define the metrics you will decide on, all on held-out data and unseen excitation, following standard sysid practice (Beintema et al. 2023, which reports test simulation NRMS down to 0.0987% on Wiener–Hammerstein via five-seed confirmation; dynoNet benchmarks — Silverbox, Wiener–Hammerstein, cascaded tanks, EMPS — report simulation RMSE/NRMSE and fit index):
- **Free-run (simulation) NRMSE / Best-Fit-Rate (BFR = 100·(1−NRMSE))** on held-out operating conditions.
- **Long-horizon drift metric:** integrated position error / slope of position error vs. time over a rollout ≥10× the training window (this is the quantity that exposes DC).
- **DC probe:** temporal mean of the integrator-velocity correction output, per seed.
- Report **≥5 seeds** (SUBNET-style five-seed confirmation) because the pathology is seed-consistent but small.

**Stage 1 — Structural fix + AdamW (do this first).** Implement the **zero-mean/DC-free constraint on the integrator-velocity correction rows (A)** on top of **AdamW (E)**. Keep everything else identical. **Success threshold:** long-horizon drift slope drops to the SGD-probe level (≈2000× reduction) *and* held-out free-run NRMSE is within noise of (or better than) the Adam baseline. If met, stop here — you have removed the pathology without sacrificing conditioning.

**Stage 2 — Add the soft DC penalty (B)** if the hard constraint underfits (i.e., held-out NRMSE degrades), or if a small physical mean correction is genuinely needed. Sweep λ_DC over ~3 decades; choose the smallest λ_DC that keeps drift below your threshold without raising held-out NRMSE. **Switch-away trigger:** if no λ_DC simultaneously controls drift and preserves fit, the DC is not a pure nuisance — revisit the physics baseline/encoder.

**Stage 3 — Attack the TBPTT bias (C)** in parallel as the principled cause-fix: lengthen the encoder washout and/or trial ARTBP or longer/re-randomized windows. **Benchmark:** does the *raw* per-seed mean gradient on the integrator rows shrink (bias reduction) independent of the constraint? If yes, you can relax λ_DC.

**Stage 4 — Cheap optimizer hedges.** Try **C-AdamW (D)** as a one-line drop-in; keep it if it reduces drift at equal held-out NRMSE (expected upside, near-zero risk). Deprioritize gradient centralization (F) and avoid generic pole/spectral regularization (H) on the z=1 axis.

**What would change these recommendations:**
- If held-out NRMSE under Adam-family optimizers is *already* poor (not just drifting), the problem is conditioning/architecture, not implicit bias — then invest in structured parameterization (S4D/REN-style) and possibly second-order/quasi-Newton methods (L-BFGS is standard in classical sysid) rather than DC fixes.
- If the DC persists even with the output DC removed, the drift is entering through the *physics baseline state coupling* or the encoder's initial-state estimate, not the ANN output — re-diagnose before adding more penalties.
- If you must have a certified-stable model for control, move to a structural stability parameterization (RENs, Revay et al. 2021), which removes the need for penalty-based pole control entirely.

## Caveats and Honest Unknowns
- **The strongest claims here are mechanistic, not turnkey-validated on your exact system.** The Adam-as-sign-descent (Balles & Hennig 2018; Kunstner et al. 2023) and Adam-→-ℓ∞ (Zhang et al. 2024, separable classification) results are peer-reviewed but proven in classification settings; their application to your integrator-drift phenomenon is a well-motivated extrapolation, not a published identity.
- **"TBPTT bias does not decay as λ→1" is a reasoned extrapolation.** Tallec & Ollivier (2017) prove the general biasedness and show pathological behavior on a slow-mode synthetic task; the specific λ→1 non-decay follows from the λ^k sensitivity structure but is not stated verbatim.
- **Cautious optimizers as a DC remedy is untested.** Liang et al. (2024) demonstrate speedups (1.47×/1.28× sample efficiency for C-AdamW/C-Lion on LLaMA-1B), not flat-direction-drift suppression; their own text notes the design goal is to *avoid* getting stuck, and they do not analyze the g≈0 regime.
- **The SGD-vs-Adam accuracy gap size for physics-augmented neural SSMs with integrators is not directly measured in the literature I found.** The block-heterogeneity argument (Zhang et al. 2024) makes underfitting the expected outcome, but you should confirm it with your Stage-0 metrics rather than assume it.
- **No paper I found treats exactly your configuration** (LPV physics baseline + parallel ANN correction + encoder + TBPTT + pure-integrator axes). The closest bodies of work are SUBNET (Beintema/Tóth/Schoukens), dynoNet/neural-SSM (Forgione/Piga), structured SSMs (Gu et al.), and stable recurrent parameterizations (Revay/Manchester). Recommendations synthesize across these; the integration is mine, not a cited result.
