Table~\ref{tab:res_structure} compares the final grey box model with the baseline at
the true parameters and with the black box (Aspect~1).
For the data with multisine, the final model lowers the error on $Y$ by more 
than an order of magnitude and halves the error on $X_1$ and $X_2$.
For the data without multisine both models achieve similar results.
Fig.~\ref{fig:res_time_trace} shows this on record I1, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:res_frf} shows that the final model is able to capture the resonance
of the system, which is not modeled in the baseline.

with plain training of the added states and with zero and eight added states.}

\subsection{Interpretability}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Combination errors, full model, physical part and overlaps, $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ against $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ from the detuned start (Table~\ref{tab:res_obc})
\item Correct start and the models without added states in the appendix
\item Uniqueness from the two starts (?: row requested, revnote at the end)
\end{itemize}
\fi
Table~\ref{tab:res_obc} compares $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ with
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$, both from the detuned start (Aspect~2).
Both models have deviated further from the true combinations, than their intial values.
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ close to double that of $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$.
The combinations describing the damping deviate the most, 
while the mass combinations stay close to their true values.
The final model with the orthogonality constraint and the unconstrained model
perform equally well. 
\kw{OBC keeps the network out of the parameter directions: its network overlap is 
much lower than that of $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$.
The missing dynamics are not orthogonal to these directions, their overlap is 0.59.
From the correct start, OBC also ends closer to the true values (Appendix~\ref{app:choices}).
Without added states, $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ ends closer instead.}

\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Final model, baseline and black box per test record, each E record beside its I record (Fig.~\ref{fig:res_generalisation})
\item Multisine twins of the records without multisine

The error per test record are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:res_generalisation}, 
for extrapolation (E) and interpolation (I) (Aspect~3).
Without multisine the model performs as well or slightly above the baseline.
The black box has 74 to 193\,\% on the records without multisine, and it is
below the final model only on I2 $X_2$.
On the \kw{multisine twins}, the final model stays below 10\,\% on $Y$, while the
baseline and the black box are above 47\,\%.
