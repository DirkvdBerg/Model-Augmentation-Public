The dynamics depend on $\theta$ only through the entries of $M_0$, $M_1$, $M_2$, $C$
and $K$ in \eqref{eq:eom} and \eqref{eq:Msplit}. Omitting zeros and symmetric
duplicates, these are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
  M_0 &: m_1{+}m_2{+}m_b{+}m_h,\quad \tfrac{L_b}{2}(m_1{-}m_2),\\
      &\quad J_b{+}J_h{+}\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(m_1{+}m_2){+}m_h d^2,\\
      &\quad -m_h d,\quad m_h,\\
  M_1,\,M_2 &: -m_h,\;\; m_h,\\
  C &: c_{g1}{+}c_{g2},\quad \tfrac{L_b}{2}(c_{g1}{-}c_{g2}),\\
    &\quad c_{b1}{+}c_{b2}{+}\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(c_{g1}{+}c_{g2}),\quad c_y,\\
  K &: k_{b1}{+}k_{b2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Every entry is a function of $\varphi$ in \eqref{eq:phi}. Conversely, $\varphi$ is
recovered entry by entry:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
  m_h &= M_{0,33}, & d &= -M_{0,23}/m_h,\\
  m_\Sigma &= M_{0,11}-m_h,
    &m_\Delta &= 2M_{0,12}/L_b,\\
  J_{\mathrm{eff}} &= M_{0,22}-m_h d^2,\\
  c_{g1},c_{g2}&:\ C_{11},C_{12},
    &c_y&=C_{33},\\
  c_{b,\Sigma}&=C_{22}-\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(c_{g1}+c_{g2}),
    &k_{b,\Sigma}&=K_{22}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The map from $\varphi$ to the matrices is therefore injective. Twelve entries yield
ten independent quantities because $M_{0,33}$, $M_{1,12}$ and $M_{2,22}$ all equal
$\pm m_h$.

The matrices are fixed by input-output data. Since only the yaw coordinate has
stiffness, every $q=[X_0\;0\;Y_0]^\top$ with $\dot q=0$ and $\uact=0$ is an
equilibrium. Linearizing \eqref{eq:eom} there, the term
$(\partial M/\partial Y)\ddot q$ vanishes, so the small-signal model is
$M(Y_0)\delta\ddot q + C\delta\dot q + K\delta q = P\delta\uact$ with transfer matrix
$H(s)=P^\top Z(s)^{-1}P$, $Z(s)=M(Y_0)s^2+Cs+K$. As $P$ is invertible,
$Z(s)=P\,H(s)^{-1}P^\top$, whose polynomial coefficients give $M(Y_0)$, $C$ and $K$.
Three distinct $Y_0$ then give $M_0$, $M_1$ and $M_2$ from the quadratic
\eqref{eq:Msplit}.

The four directions of $\theta$ that leave every entry unchanged are
$\delta k_{b1}=-\delta k_{b2}$, $\delta c_{b1}=-\delta c_{b2}$,
$\delta J_b=-\delta J_h$, and
$(\delta m_1,\delta m_2,\delta m_b,\delta J_b)=t\,(-2/L_b^2,\,-2/L_b^2,\,4/L_b^2,\,1)$.
Accordingly, the Jacobian of the twelve entries with respect to $\theta$ has rank ten
for every positive $\theta$, which was confirmed symbolically.

Positivity of the reduced coordinates alone does not guarantee that they correspond
to a positive-definite inertia matrix. With
$a=L_bm_\Delta/2$, elimination of the $m_h$ block gives
\begin{equation*}
  \min_Y\frac{\det M(Y)}{m_h}
  =(m_\Sigma+m_h)\left(J_{\mathrm{eff}}-\frac{a^2}{m_\Sigma}\right).
\end{equation*}
Hence, for positive $m_h$, $m_\Sigma$, and $J_{\mathrm{eff}}$,
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)\succ0\ \text{for all }Y\in\R
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  m_\Sigma J_{\mathrm{eff}}>\frac{L_b^2}{4}m_\Delta^2.
  \label{eq:admissible}
\end{equation}
For the physical parameterization, the difference between the two sides is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
  m_\Sigma J_{\mathrm{eff}}-\frac{L_b^2}{4}m_\Delta^2
  ={}&m_\Sigma(J_b+J_h)\\
  &+\frac{L_b^2}{4}\left[4m_1m_2+m_b(m_1+m_2)\right],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
which is strictly positive for positive component masses and yaw inertias.

For completeness, let $R=M_0^{-1}$. The LTI matrices in \eqref{eq:lfr} are
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
A&=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\-RK&-RC\end{bmatrix}, &
B_w&=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\-RM_1&-RM_2\end{bmatrix},\\
B_u&=\begin{bmatrix}0\\RP\end{bmatrix}, &
C_z&=\begin{bmatrix}-RK&-RC\\0&0\end{bmatrix},\\
D_{zw}&=\begin{bmatrix}-RM_1&-RM_2\\I&0\end{bmatrix}, &
D_{zu}&=\begin{bmatrix}RP\\0\end{bmatrix},\\
C_y&=\begin{bmatrix}P^\top&0\end{bmatrix}.&&
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Taking the Schur complement of the lower-right identity block gives
\begin{equation}
 \det\!\left(I-D_{zw}\Dsch(Y)\right)
 =\det\!\left(M_0^{-1}M(Y)\right)
 =\frac{\det M(Y)}{\det M_0},
 \label{eq:wellposed_identity}
\end{equation}
which proves the well-posedness equivalence used in Section~\ref{sec:lfr}.
