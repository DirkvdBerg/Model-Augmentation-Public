If the effects that the baseline omits are dynamic, its six states cannot
represent them.
Refitting $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ adds no states.
Neither does a static augmentation, which corrects the state update of the
baseline without extending its state~\cite[Sec.~2]{hoekstra2025lfr}.
The augmentation therefore needs states of its own.

We use the LFR-based augmentation structure of Hoekstra et al., in which an
interconnection matrix connects a baseline block and a learning
block~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{hoekstra2025lfr}, here a fixed
one~\cite[Sec.~3.2]{hoekstra2025lfr}: the dynamic-parallel choice below
specifies every entry of the interconnection, so nothing in it is left to
learn.
\todo{Candidate reason for the fixed interconnection (own reasoning, no source or decision entry yet); confirm or replace.}
Since $Y$ is a state, the self-scheduled LFR of Section~\ref{sec:lfr} is a
nonlinear map of the state and the input, and it enters this structure as the
baseline block.
The augmented model is thus an LFR whose baseline block is itself an LFR
in~$Y$.
The learning block is a neural network, as in Hoekstra et al., not an LPV-LFR
with a learned scheduling map as in the augmentation of
Drenth~\cite[Eq.~(5.2)]{drenth2025thesis}.

Of the interconnections, we choose the dynamic-parallel
one~\cite[Table~1]{hoekstra2025lfr}, because the orthogonal-by-construction
parametrisation that Section~\ref{sec:obc} extends is defined for this
additive structure~\cite{gyorok2026obc}.
It also keeps the baseline an explicit term of the state update.
The output map is not augmented, because it is the exact kinematic relation
\eqref{eq:Ptransform}.
Kessels likewise leaves an accurate output map
unaugmented~\cite[Remark~5.3]{kessels2025ai}.

The structure is a discrete-time state update, in which the learned
correction is added to the baseline transition~\cite[Sec.~2, Table~1]{hoekstra2025lfr}.
The baseline therefore enters through its transition over one sample period
$T_s$, one fourth-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) step of \eqref{eq:eom} with the
input held over the step:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &F(\tilde x,u_{\mathrm{act}})=\begin{bmatrix}\dot q\\
  M(Y)^{-1}\bigl(Pu_{\mathrm{act}}-C\dot q-Kq\bigr)\end{bmatrix},\\
 &k_1=F(\tilde x_k,u_{\mathrm{act},k}),\quad
  k_2=F\bigl(\tilde x_k+\tfrac{T_s}{2}k_1,u_{\mathrm{act},k}\bigr),\\
 &k_3=F\bigl(\tilde x_k+\tfrac{T_s}{2}k_2,u_{\mathrm{act},k}\bigr),\quad
  k_4=F\bigl(\tilde x_k+T_sk_3,u_{\mathrm{act},k}\bigr),\\
 &f_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\tilde x_k,u_{\mathrm{act},k})
  =\tilde x_k+\tfrac{T_s}{6}\bigl(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_rk4}
\end{equation}
where $M$, $C$ and $K$ depend on $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ through
\eqref{eq:phi}, and $M(Y)^{-1}$ is the closed loop of the LFR of
Section~\ref{sec:lfr}.
Each stage evaluates $M(Y)$ at the $Y$ of its own state, so
$f_{\mathrm{base}}$ remains self-scheduled.
In physical units, the augmented model is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \tilde x_{k+1}
 &=f_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\tilde x_k,u_{\mathrm{act},k})
   +f_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x_k,u_{\mathrm{act},k}),\\
 \bar x_{k+1}
 &=g_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x_k,u_{\mathrm{act},k}),\\
 \hat y_k&=P^\top q_k,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_transition}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde x_k=\col(q_k,\dot q_k)\in\R^6$ is the physical state of
\eqref{eq:lfr_G}, $\bar x_k\in\R^{n_{\bar x}}$ the added state,
$\hat x_k=\col(\tilde x_k,\bar x_k)$, $u_{\mathrm{act},k}\in\R^3$ the actuator
forces, $\hat y_k\in\R^3$ the predicted actuator positions, whose recorded
counterpart $q_{\mathrm{act},k}$ we write $y_k$,
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}\in\R^{10}$ the combinations \eqref{eq:phi},
and $\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}\in\R^{n_{\mathrm{aug}}}$ the network parameters.
The baseline transition reads only the physical state, whereas
$f_{\mathrm{aug}}$ and $g_{\mathrm{aug}}$ read the full state $\hat x_k$: the
learned part sees the added states and, through $\tilde x_k$, the payload
position $Y$.
No learned function feeds the baseline block or itself within a step, so the
interconnection graph is acyclic~\cite[Thm.~1]{hoekstra2025lfr} and, with
the well-posedness of the baseline LFR (Section~\ref{sec:lfr}), the augmented
model is well posed for every $Y$.

The network and the identification act on centred signals of unit variance,
so that network inputs in metres, radians and newtons, and output channels of
different amplitude, enter on a comparable scale.
\todo{Candidate reason for the normalisation (own reasoning; D-055 and D-119 cover the mechanics, not the reason); confirm or replace with a source.}
Every statistic is taken from the training records, the state statistics from
the positions $q=P^{-\top}y$ and their first differences divided by $T_s$:
\begin{equation}
 \tilde x^{\mathrm n}=T_x(\tilde x-\mu_x),\quad
 u^{\mathrm n}=T_u(u_{\mathrm{act}}-\mu_u),\quad
 y^{\mathrm n}=T_y(y-\mu_y),
 \label{eq:aug_norm}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_x\in\R^6$, $\mu_u,\mu_y\in\R^3$ are the channel means,
$T_\bullet=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\bullet)^{-1}$ with $\sigma_\bullet$ the
channel standard deviations, and $\bar x$ is not scaled, so
$\hat x^{\mathrm n}=\col(\tilde x^{\mathrm n},\bar x)$.
In these coordinates the baseline transition is
$f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n})
=T_x\bigl(f_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},T_x^{-1}\tilde x^{\mathrm n}+\mu_x,
T_u^{-1}u^{\mathrm n}+\mu_u)-\mu_x\bigr)$: it maps back to physical units,
takes the RK4 step and normalises the result.
A correction only scales, $f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}=T_xf_{\mathrm{aug}}$,
because it is a difference of states, and
$g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}=g_{\mathrm{aug}}$.
Since the position part of $\mu_x$ is $P^{-\top}\mu_y$, the output map stays
linear,
$\hat y^{\mathrm n}_k=C^{\mathrm n}\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_k$ with
$C^{\mathrm n}=T_yP^\top[I_3\;\,0]\,T_x^{-1}$.

One multilayer perceptron $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$ with tanh hidden layers and a
linear output layer supplies both learned functions,
$\col(f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}},g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}})
=\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x^{\mathrm n}_k,u^{\mathrm n}_k)$:
it reads the $6+n_{\bar x}$ states and the three inputs, its first six
outputs are the corrections of the physical rows and its last $n_{\bar x}$
outputs the next added state (Fig.~\ref{fig:aug_structure}).
A feedforward network suffices because the baseline is augmented additively,
whereas a series structure needs a residual network to reproduce the baseline
at initialisation~\cite[Sec.~4.3]{hoekstra2025lfr}.
The corrections act on every physical row of the baseline transition, as in
the dynamic-parallel structure~\cite[Table~1]{hoekstra2025lfr}.
The learned component thus also acts on the free axes $X$ and $Y$, which have
no stiffness in \eqref{eq:damping_stiffness_matrices}, so a persistent
correction on these rows accumulates in position.

With $n_{\bar x}=0$, $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$ corrects the physical rows only,

Because $f_{\mathrm{aug}}$ acts on the same rows as $f_{\mathrm{base}}$, it
can also learn changes that a different $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ would
produce, so the split between baseline and network is not
unique~\cite[Sec.~5.2]{hoekstra2026lfr}.
In the experiments of Hoekstra et al., approximately initialised physical
parameters stay close to their initial values, while the network learns
dynamics the baseline could represent~\cite[Sec.~6.3]{hoekstra2026lfr}.
With a non-unique $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$, the baseline can lose its physical
meaning~\cite[Sec.~3]{gyorok2026obc}.
Here, the estimated combinations are interpretable if they keep that meaning,
which requires a unique estimate.
Section~\ref{sec:obc} extends the method so that this estimate is unique.

Only the positions of the state at the start $\tau$ of a training window are
measured.
Following Beintema et al., an encoder estimates this state from the input and
output history~\cite{beintema2023subnet}.
Simulating short windows from encoded states keeps the cost of a gradient step
independent of the record length and stabilises the
optimisation~\cite[Secs.~2, 3]{beintema2023subnet}.
Unlike the criterion of Hoekstra et al., whose encoder reads the samples up to
$\tau-1$~\cite[Eq.~(22e)]{hoekstra2026lfr}, the history includes sample
$\tau$, as the reconstructability map used below
requires~\cite[Eqs.~(11) to (17)]{hoekstra2026encoder}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \hat x^{\mathrm n}_{\tau|\tau}
 &=\psi(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_\tau),\\
 \chi_\tau&=\col\bigl(y^{\mathrm n}_{\tau-n},\dots,y^{\mathrm n}_\tau,\,
   u^{\mathrm n}_{\tau-n},\dots,u^{\mathrm n}_\tau\bigr),
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:aug_encoder}
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the encoder history, $\chi_\tau\in\R^{6(n+1)}$ the recorded
window and $\theta_{\mathrm{enc}}\in\R^{n_{\mathrm{enc}}}$ the encoder
parameters.
The encoder thus estimates all $6+n_{\bar x}$ states, the measured positions
included.

\eqref{eq:aug_encoder}, which also uses the current sample.}

The encoder is a linear map with a nonlinear
correction~\cite[Eq.~(8)]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
Its added-state rows $W^a$ have no baseline counterpart.
The linear part acts on the scaled but uncentred window, for which the map
\eqref{eq:aug_wb} below is built:
\begin{equation}
 \psi(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_\tau)
 =\begin{bmatrix}W^b\\ W^a\end{bmatrix}(\chi_\tau+\chi_0)
  -\begin{bmatrix}T_x\mu_x\\ 0\end{bmatrix}
  +\tilde\psi(\chi_\tau),
 \label{eq:aug_enc_lin}
\end{equation}
where $W^b\in\R^{6\times6(n+1)}$, $W^a\in\R^{n_{\bar x}\times6(n+1)}$,
$\chi_0$ restores the means that \eqref{eq:aug_norm} removes, $\tilde\psi$ is
a multilayer perceptron, and $\theta_{\mathrm{enc}}$ collects $W^b$, $W^a$
and the parameters of $\tilde\psi$.
We write $\psi_a$ for the last $n_{\bar x}$ rows of $\psi$ and
$\theta^a_{\mathrm{enc}}$ for $W^a$ with the added-state rows of the output
layer of $\tilde\psi$.

Check for a published (IFAC) version before submission.}

The physical rows start from the baseline, through the model-based
initialisation of Hoekstra et al.~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
Its map needs a linear model, so as in~\cite[Eqs.~(30) to (32)]{hoekstra2026encoder}
we linearise the baseline at an equilibrium.
We take rest at $Y=0$, which is an equilibrium for zero input because $K$ in
\eqref{eq:damping_stiffness_matrices} vanishes on $X$ and $Y$, and the
position at which the controller of the records is designed
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
Frozen at $Y=0$, \eqref{eq:eom} is linear, so the linearisation is the
baseline itself at that position and approximates it only as $Y$ moves.
Discretising by zero-order hold over $T_s$ and scaling as \eqref{eq:aug_norm}
without the means gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &A_d=T_xe^{A_cT_s}T_x^{-1},\quad
  B_d=T_x\!\int_0^{T_s}\!e^{A_cs}\,\mathrm ds\,B_cT_u^{-1},\quad
  C_d=T_yC_cT_x^{-1},\\
 &A_c=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_3\\-M_0^{-1}K&-M_0^{-1}C\end{bmatrix},\quad
  B_c=\begin{bmatrix}0\\M_0^{-1}P\end{bmatrix},\quad
  C_c=\begin{bmatrix}P^\top&0\end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_lin}
\end{equation}
with $M_0=M(0)$ from \eqref{eq:Msplit}, evaluated at the nominal parameters.
Stacking the outputs and inputs of the window as $Y_\tau$ and $U_\tau$, the
output and input parts of $\chi_\tau+\chi_0$ with the oldest sample first,
gives $Y_\tau=O_n\tilde x_{\tau-n}+\Gamma_nU_\tau$ and
$\tilde x_\tau=A_d^{\,n}\tilde x_{\tau-n}+r_nU_\tau$.
Eliminating $\tilde x_{\tau-n}$ gives the reconstructability
map~\cite[Eqs.~(15) to (17)]{hoekstra2026encoder}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \tilde x_\tau&=A_d^{\,n}O_n^{+}\bigl(Y_\tau-\Gamma_nU_\tau\bigr)+r_nU_\tau,\\
 W^b&=\begin{bmatrix}
  A_d^{\,n}O_n^{+} & \;r_n-A_d^{\,n}O_n^{+}\Gamma_n
 \end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:aug_wb}
\end{equation}
where $O_n=\col(C_d,C_dA_d,\dots,C_dA_d^{\,n})\in\R^{3(n+1)\times6}$,
$\Gamma_n$ is block lower triangular with block $(i,j)$ equal to
$C_dA_d^{\,i-j-1}B_d$ for $i>j$ and zero otherwise,
$r_n=[A_d^{\,n-1}B_d\;\cdots\;B_d\;\,0]$, and ${}^{+}$ is the Moore-Penrose
pseudoinverse.
The paper assumes a stable baseline~\cite[Sec.~2]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
Ours is marginally stable, but the measured positions make $(A_d,C_d)$
observable, so $O_n$ has full column rank.
The map is derived without noise; with white Gaussian measurement noise it is
the maximum-likelihood estimate of the state~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
The paper also gives a version for process noise, which needs a known noise
gain~\cite[Eqs.~(18) to (29)]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
The noise in our records enters at the position measurement
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}) and no noise gain is known, so we use
\eqref{eq:aug_wb}.
Our measurement noise is coloured, so the map is a starting value for $W^b$,
not an optimal estimator.
It leaves co-estimation of the baseline parameters out of
scope~\cite[footnote~1]{hoekstra2026encoder}, whereas here $W^b$ is trained
jointly with them.

Hoekstra et al.\ instead fit the baseline encoder to states simulated with the
baseline~\cite[Eq.~(30)]{hoekstra2026lfr}, the data-based alternative
of~\cite[Sec.~3.2]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
On the gantry, that simulation is driven by the recorded input and drifts on
the free axes $X$ and $Y$ (Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}), so it would fit $W^b$
to wrong states.
The map \eqref{eq:aug_wb} needs no simulation and negligible
computation~\cite[Sec.~4.4]{hoekstra2026encoder}.
It gives better starting values and faster convergence than a random encoder,
at comparable final accuracy~\cite[Sec.~4.4]{hoekstra2026encoder}.

\subsection{Closed-loop simulation}\label{sec:aug_loop}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item The records are measured under feedback and the model must predict the machine under feedback, so each window is simulated in the loop, not driven by the recorded input (2026 Eq.~(22))
\item Kessels (Eqs.~(5.12), (5.13); Remark~5.6, noisy records); for linear systems the indirect approach (Forssell and Ljung); free $X$, $Y$ violate the stability condition behind SUBNET consistency (Beintema Cond.~1), the loop can satisfy it; motivation, not a guarantee
\item Both loops displayed; subtracting the machine's controller equations gives our residual form, exact under a compatible controller, signals and initial state (check in Setup)
\item One normalised closed-loop step displayed; the model's output error re-enters its input through the controller
\item $x^{c}_\tau=0$ needs no controller initialisation and matches the encoder; Kessels' direct form needs the machine's controller state (Remark~5.4)
\item No plant feedthrough fixes the step order: no algebraic loop, no added delay; error before $\tau$ is not carried into the window
\end{itemize}
\fi

The records are measured under feedback, and the model must predict the
machine under feedback: its response to a reference, a feedforward and a
known controller.
We therefore simulate each training window in closed loop with that
controller, rather than driving the model with the recorded input as
in~\cite[Eq.~(22)]{hoekstra2026lfr}.
Kessels trains augmented models in this way~\cite[Eqs.~(5.12), (5.13)]{kessels2025ai}
and reports that identifying the open-loop model from his closed-loop data
failed, which he attributes to noise-induced
bias~\cite[Remark~5.6]{kessels2025ai}; our records are noisy as well
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
For linear systems, fitting the closed loop from the reference with a known
controller is the indirect approach to closed-loop identification, in which
an output-error criterion remains consistent because the reference is
uncorrelated with the noise~\cite{forssell1999revisited}.
The free axes give a second reason.
$X$ and $Y$ have no stiffness, so the open-loop gantry is marginally stable:
a difference in initial velocity leaves a permanent position offset.
The consistency argument of the identification
method~\cite[Sec.~5.5]{hoekstra2026lfr} rests on that of SUBNET, which assumes
an incrementally exponentially output stable data-generating
system~\cite[Cond.~1]{beintema2023subnet}; the open-loop gantry violates this
condition, whereas the loop with the stabilising controller can satisfy it.
For our nonlinear model, both arguments motivate the closed loop but do not
prove consistency.

The controller of each record is known, linear and time invariant, and maps
the position error in actuator coordinates to actuator forces.
The machine and the model each close this controller around their own
output, with controller states $x^{\mathrm{fb}}$ and $\hat x^{\mathrm{fb}}$,
and both loops are driven by the reference $r$ and feedforward
$u^{\mathrm{ff}}$ of the record (Fig.~\ref{fig:aug_closed_loop}):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 x^{\mathrm{fb}}_{k+1}&=A_{\mathrm{fb}}x^{\mathrm{fb}}_k+B_{\mathrm{fb}}(r_k-y_k),\\
 u_{\mathrm{act},k}&=C_{\mathrm{fb}}x^{\mathrm{fb}}_k+D_{\mathrm{fb}}(r_k-y_k)+u^{\mathrm{ff}}_k,\\
 \hat x^{\mathrm{fb}}_{k+1}&=A_{\mathrm{fb}}\hat x^{\mathrm{fb}}_k+B_{\mathrm{fb}}(r_k-\hat y_k),\\
 \hat u_{\mathrm{act},k}&=C_{\mathrm{fb}}\hat x^{\mathrm{fb}}_k+D_{\mathrm{fb}}(r_k-\hat y_k)+u^{\mathrm{ff}}_k,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_two_loops}
\end{equation}
where $(A_{\mathrm{fb}},B_{\mathrm{fb}},C_{\mathrm{fb}},D_{\mathrm{fb}})$ are
the state-space matrices of the controller discretised over $T_s$, $r_k$ and
$u^{\mathrm{ff}}_k$ are in actuator coordinates, and $\hat u_{\mathrm{act},k}$
is the input the model receives in place of the recorded $u_{\mathrm{act},k}$.
Subtracting the machine's lines from the model's, with
$x^{c}=\hat x^{\mathrm{fb}}-x^{\mathrm{fb}}$, removes $r$ and
$u^{\mathrm{ff}}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 x^{c}_{k+1}&=A_{\mathrm{fb}}x^{c}_k+B_{\mathrm{fb}}(y_k-\hat y_k),\qquad x^{c}_\tau=0,\\
 \hat u_{\mathrm{act},k}&=u_{\mathrm{act},k}+C_{\mathrm{fb}}x^{c}_k+D_{\mathrm{fb}}(y_k-\hat y_k).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_residual_loop}
\end{equation}
The model input is thus the recorded input plus the controller's response to
the output error of the model.
This form equals the model loop of \eqref{eq:aug_two_loops} when the recorded
input was produced by this controller from the same output samples and
initial state.
The difference of the controller states needs no estimate: setting it to zero
at the window start places the model's controller in the machine's controller
state, as the encoder places the model state on the recorded trajectory.
Kessels' direct form instead needs the machine's controller state at every
window start, read from the machine or reconstructed from the reference, the
output and the controller~\cite[Remark~5.4]{kessels2025ai}.

The augmented model has no feedthrough, so each sample is evaluated in a fixed
order: the output from the physical state, then the input, then the model and
controller states advance.
The controller feedthrough $D_{\mathrm{fb}}$ therefore closes no algebraic
loop, and the loop adds no delay.
In the coordinates of \eqref{eq:aug_norm}, where the output means cancel in
the error, one step of a window started at $\tau$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}&=C^{\mathrm n}\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau},\qquad
 \varepsilon_{k|\tau}=T_y^{-1}\bigl(y^{\mathrm n}_k-\hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}\bigr),\\
 \hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}&=u^{\mathrm n}_k
  +T_u\bigl(C_{\mathrm{fb}}x^{c}_k+D_{\mathrm{fb}}\varepsilon_{k|\tau}\bigr),\\
 x^{c}_{k+1}&=A_{\mathrm{fb}}x^{c}_k+B_{\mathrm{fb}}\varepsilon_{k|\tau},\qquad x^{c}_\tau=0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_cl_step}
\end{equation}
after which $\hat x^{\mathrm n}_{k+1|\tau}$ follows from
\eqref{eq:aug_transition} with input $\hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}$, starting
from the encoder state \eqref{eq:aug_encoder}.
Here $\varepsilon_{k|\tau}=y_k-\hat y_{k|\tau}$ is the output error in
metres.
The output error of the model thus re-enters its own input through the
controller, so a model error is partly corrected within the window, as on the
machine.
Model error made before $\tau$ is not carried into the window, whereas the
free-run validation of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} applies
\eqref{eq:aug_cl_step} over whole records and carries it.

\subsection{Identification problem}\label{sec:aug_closed_loop}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ is estimated jointly with network and encoder, as in 2026 Eq.~(22); Kessels fixes the first-principles parameters (Eq.~(5.12))
\item Training coordinates $\xi$, zero at the start; nine positive combinations logarithmic, the signed $m_\Delta$ through \eqref{eq:mdelta_map}, so every iterate satisfies \eqref{eq:lfr_admissibility} without clamp or penalty (D-229)
\item One display: cost over $\vartheta=(\xi,\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\theta_{\mathrm{enc}})$ with the model, encoder and loop constraints; every window sample scored; controller fixed; a closed-loop fit can hide plant error (Section~VI)
\item Adam with exact backpropagation through the window, then an unbounded L-BFGS polish ($\xi$ admissible for every value); best validation free-run checkpoint; polish kept only if it improves validation and no meter regresses
\item Initialisation display: $\xi=0$, zero output layer, encoder start; reproduces the baseline (2026 Eq.~(29)); $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ named
\end{itemize}

We estimate $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ jointly with the network and the encoder,
as Hoekstra et al.\ do~\cite[Eq.~(22)]{hoekstra2026lfr}, whereas Kessels
leaves the first-principles parameters fixed~\cite[Eq.~(5.12)]{kessels2025ai}.
Every iterate must satisfy \eqref{eq:lfr_admissibility}, so that $M(Y)\succ0$
and the LFR stays well posed without a clamp or a penalty.
We therefore train in coordinates $\xi\in\R^{10}$ that are zero at the start
$\theta_{\mathrm{base},0}$.
The nine positive combinations are logarithmic,
$\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}=\theta_{\mathrm{base},0,j}\,e^{\xi_j}$, which keeps
them positive.
The signed $m_\Delta$ is bounded by \eqref{eq:lfr_admissibility} itself:
\begin{equation}
  m_\Delta=\rho\,\frac{2}{L_b}\sqrt{m_\Sigma J_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\tanh(\eta_0+s\,\xi_\Delta),
  \label{eq:mdelta_map}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_\Delta$ is the component of $\xi$ for $m_\Delta$, $\rho\in(0,1)$ a
margin, and $\eta_0$ and $s$ reproduce $m_{\Delta,0}$ and its sensitivity at
$\xi=0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:lfr-wellposed}).
Every $\xi$ thus gives $M(Y)\succ0$ for all $Y$, which guarantees positive
definite inertia but not positive raw masses.

Over the window starts $\mathcal T$ and the window length $n_f$, the trained
parameters $\vartheta=(\xi,\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\theta_{\mathrm{enc}})$
minimise the normalised closed-loop output error, with the controller fixed.
As in~\cite[Eq.~(22)]{hoekstra2026lfr}, every sample of a window is scored.
We adapt that criterion in two respects: the model is simulated in the loop
of Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}, and the encoder reads sample $\tau$.
Because the controller acts on the output error, a good closed-loop fit can
mask plant error, so Section~\ref{sec:results} also tests the model under a
changed controller.
The identification problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &\min_{\vartheta}\;V(\vartheta)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal T|\,n_f\,n_y}
 \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T}\sum_{k=\tau}^{\tau+n_f-1}
 \norm{y^{\mathrm n}_{k}-\hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}}_2^2\quad\text{s.t.}\\
 &\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_{k+1|\tau}
  =f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}(\xi),
   \tilde x^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau},\hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}\bigr)
  +f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},
   \hat x^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau},\hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}\bigr),\\
 &\bar x_{k+1|\tau}=g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},
   \hat x^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau},\hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}\bigr),\\
 &\hat x^{\mathrm n}_{\tau|\tau}=\psi(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_\tau),\quad
  \hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau},\,\hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}\text{ from \eqref{eq:aug_cl_step}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aug_loss}
\end{equation}
for $\tau\le k<\tau+n_f$, where $n_y=3$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}(\xi)$ is
the coordinate map above.
The controller matrices are fixed, but they are part of the computation:
since $\hat u^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}$ depends on $\hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}$,
the gradient of $V$ also passes through the controller recursion.

We minimise $V$ with Adam on minibatches of windows, with the gradient
obtained by automatic differentiation through the whole window, so no part of
the window is truncated from the gradient.
We then polish with L-BFGS on a fixed set of windows.
Drenth et al.\ also follow Adam with L-BFGS, in its bounded form
L-BFGS-B~\cite[Sec.~5]{drenth2025lpvlfr}.
Our polish needs no bounds, because \eqref{eq:mdelta_map} and the logarithmic
coordinates give an admissible baseline for every $\xi$.
During Adam, the parameters with the lowest validation error are kept, the
closed-loop free-run error over whole validation records of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}, each simulated from one encoder start with $x^{c}=0$.
The polish is kept only if it lowers this error and no monitored quantity
regresses.
The encoder history $n$, the window length $n_f$, the added-state dimension
$n_{\bar x}$, the width and depth of $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$ and the optimiser
settings are given with their basis in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.
\todo{Fix before the final run (\texttt{FINAL-RUN-FIXES.md} item~2): the polish acceptance meters include \texttt{combo\_err}, the error of the ten combinations against the true parameters, and possibly \texttt{param\_loss}, the drift from the start. The final runs accept on the validation error and non-oracle meters only.}

Training starts from the baseline, as Hoekstra et al.\ require of the
initialisation~\cite[Eq.~(29)]{hoekstra2026lfr}.
Every trained block starts as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &\xi=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\theta_{\mathrm{base}}=\theta_{\mathrm{base},0},\\
 &W_L=0,\;b_L=0\;\Longrightarrow\;f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}=0,\;g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}=0,\\
 &W^b\text{ from \eqref{eq:aug_wb}},\quad W^a\sim\mathcal U\bigl(-a,a\bigr),\quad
  \tilde W_L=0,\;\tilde b_L=0,
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:aug_init}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{\mathrm{base},0}$ are the starting combinations
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}), $W_L\in\R^{(6+n_{\bar x})\times n_h}$ and
$b_L\in\R^{6+n_{\bar x}}$ the weight and bias of the output layer of
$\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$ with $n_h$ its hidden width, $\tilde W_L$ and
$\tilde b_L$ those of $\tilde\psi$, and $a=\sqrt{6/(n_{\bar x}+6(n+1))}$ the
Xavier bound, as in~\cite[Eq.~(31)]{hoekstra2026lfr}.
The hidden layers of $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$ and $\tilde\psi$ keep the default
random initialisation of the software.
From the same physical initial state, the augmented model then reproduces the
baseline.

We call $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ the augmented model \eqref{eq:aug_transition}
identified by \eqref{eq:aug_loss} from the start \eqref{eq:aug_init}.
Its input is the actuator force $\uact$ and its output the actuator positions

\subsection{Added-state initialisation}\label{sec:aug_ps2}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Problem: from the start \eqref{eq:aug_init} the added states are zero after one step and $V$ gives $W^a$ and the added-state output rows zero gradient; $V$ can fall through $f_{\mathrm{aug}}$ alone (PS2-METHOD Sec.~2.2; Section~VI tests it)
\item PS is this work's, adapted from two-stage regression (Hefny Sec.~2) and its use before backpropagation through time (Downey Sec.~4.2)
\item S1: residual target from sample $\tau$, as $V$ scores it; decoder plus $\psi_a$; trains $\theta_{\mathrm p}$, $\theta^a_{\mathrm{enc}}$ only, own optimiser, after every Adam update
\item Unexplained fraction on calibration records decides the S1 end and the fallback
\item S2: once, one-step regression of $g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$ on frozen encoder pairs with the recorded input, which equals the loop input when the encoder reproduces $y_k$
\item S3: decoder discarded, \eqref{eq:aug_loss} continues unchanged; deployed model, criterion and selection unchanged; added states latent; $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ named
\end{itemize}

The start \eqref{eq:aug_init} leaves the added states without a role.
They are zero after the first step, and the output depends on them only
through the zero output layer, so the gradient of $V$ with respect to $W^a$
and to the added-state rows of $W_L$ is zero at the start.
Minimising $V$ can then lower the error through $f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$
alone, with static corrections or real lags, and leave the omitted dynamics
unrepresented.
Section~\ref{sec:results} tests whether it does.
We therefore propose predictive state (PS) initialisation, which gives the added states a predictive role from
data before the network reads them and then hands over to \eqref{eq:aug_loss}.
It adapts the two-stage regression of predictive-state
learning~\cite[Sec.~2]{hefny2015supervised} and its use as an initialisation
before backpropagation through time~\cite[Sec.~4.2]{downey2017psrnn}.
Unlike~\cite{hefny2015supervised}, the first stage predicts the residual of
the model being trained rather than features of future observations, and the
second stage regresses the nonlinear transition of the deployed model rather
than a linear operator.

During the first updates of \eqref{eq:aug_loss}, one S1 update with its own
Adam optimiser follows every update of $V$.
S1 draws windows from the training records except a calibration subset of
records.
Its target is the current model's closed-loop residual over the $M=n+1$
samples from the window start, and a temporary decoder reads only
$\bar x_\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 e_\tau&=\frac{1}{\sigma_e}\col\bigl(y^{\mathrm n}_{k}-\hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}\bigr)_{k=\tau}^{\tau+M-1},\\
 &\min_{\theta_{\mathrm p},\,\theta^a_{\mathrm{enc}}}\;
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal B|\,M\,n_y}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal B}
 \norm{d\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm p},\psi_a(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_\tau)\bigr)-e_\tau}_2^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ps2_s1}
\end{equation}
where $\hat y^{\mathrm n}_{k|\tau}$ is the output of the loop of
Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop} at the current $\vartheta$, held fixed in the
gradient, $\mathcal B$ a minibatch of window starts, $\sigma_e$ the RMS of the
residuals over $\mathcal B$, and $d$ a decoder with parameters
$\theta_{\mathrm p}$ that maps the $n_{\bar x}$ added states to the $Mn_y$
residual samples, with one tanh layer of the width of $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$
and a zero output layer.
Only $\theta_{\mathrm p}$ and $\theta^a_{\mathrm{enc}}$ receive a gradient,
so the rest of $\vartheta$ is left to $V$.
Sample $\tau$ is both the last sample of the encoder window and the first of
the target, so S1 predicts exactly the residual sequence that $V$ scores.

Our records are noisy and the windows share sample $\tau$, so the noise in $y_\tau$ enters both sides of \eqref{eq:ps2_s1}. The final runs start the target at $\tau+1$; the text and \eqref{eq:ps2_s1} then change accordingly.}

Whether $\bar x_\tau$ predicts the residual is measured on a fixed set
$\mathcal C$ of windows from the calibration records, by the fraction the
decoder leaves unexplained,
\begin{equation}
 \nu=\frac{\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal C}\norm{d\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm p},\psi_a(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_\tau)\bigr)-e_\tau}_2^2}
 {\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal C}\norm{e_\tau}_2^2}.
 \label{eq:ps2_nu}
\end{equation}
S1 ends when $\nu$ stops falling, or after a maximum number of updates.
If $\nu$ then exceeds a threshold, or already does at a fixed early update,
the residual is not predictable from $\bar x_\tau$: S2 is skipped and
training continues with the S1 changes kept.
Otherwise, stage S2 fits the added-state transition so that it propagates
these states.
Once, at the end of S1, we encode window pairs that start at samples $k_i$
and $k_i+1$ anywhere in the fit records, freeze them as the set
$\mathcal P$, and fit $g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$ by one-step regression
with a fresh optimiser, keeping the best parameters on pairs from the
calibration records:
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}}\;
 \frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}\norm{g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},
   \psi(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_{k_i}),u^{\mathrm n}_{k_i}\bigr)
   -\psi_a(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_{k_i+1})}_2^2}
 {\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\bar x}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}\bigl(\psi_{a,j}(\theta_{\mathrm{enc}},\chi_{k_i+1})-\bar\psi_{a,j}\bigr)^2},
 \label{eq:ps2_s2}
\end{equation}
minimised on minibatches of $\mathcal P$, where $\theta_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is
held at its value at the end of S1 and $\bar\psi_{a,j}$ is the mean of the
$j$th target.
The input $u^{\mathrm n}_{k_i}$ is the recorded one: the force that moved the
machine from $k_i$ to $k_i+1$, from whose response the target is encoded.
At a window start of the loop \eqref{eq:aug_residual_loop}, $x^c=0$, so the
model input there is
\begin{equation*}
 \hat u_{\mathrm{act},k_i}=u_{\mathrm{act},k_i}+D_{\mathrm{fb}}\bigl(y_{k_i}-\hat y_{k_i}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
which equals the recorded input when the encoder reproduces $y_{k_i}$.
The loss reaches $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$ only through its added-state rows, so
S2 leaves the physical rows of $W_L$ unchanged, while the shared hidden layers
move.

In stage S3 the decoder is discarded and the minimisation of
\eqref{eq:aug_loss} continues from the modified parameters, followed by the
polish and selection of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop}.
PS adds nothing to the deployed model, the criterion or the selection rule.
The added states remain latent, since PS assigns them no physical
coordinates.
An invertible transformation of $\bar x$, absorbed by $\phi_{\mathrm{aug}}$,
leaves the input-output behaviour unchanged.
We call $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ the model \eqref{eq:aug_transition}
identified by \eqref{eq:aug_loss} with this opening, from the same start as
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$.
