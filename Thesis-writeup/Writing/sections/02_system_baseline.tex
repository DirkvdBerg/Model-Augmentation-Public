\section{Dual-Drive Gantry System and Physics Baseline}

\subsection{System and first-principles baseline}
The physics baseline used in this work is based on the lumped-parameter model of the 
dual-drive gantry developed by García-Herreros et al. \cite{garcia2013model}.
The gantry has two actuators that carry a cross-arm and a 
third actuator that moves the payload along the arm (Fig.~\ref{fig:gantry_lfr}(a)).
Dynamics not represented by the baseline are treated as model discrepancy and addressed by the augmentation in Section~\ref{sec:augmentation}. 
In figure~\ref{fig:gantry_lfr}(a) the physics baseline components are depicted in black; 
the simulated ground-truth system additionally includes the light-gray absorber.
Define the actuator forces
$\uact=[F_{X1}\;F_{X2}\;F_Y]^\top$, the measured positions
$\qact=[X_1\;X_2\;Y]^\top$, and the generalized coordinates
$q=[X\;\Theta\;Y]^\top$. Let \(L_b\) denote the cross-arm length between the two flexible joints. 
The coordinate and force transformations are
\begin{equation}
  \qact=P^\top q,
  \qquad
  u=P\uact,
  \qquad
  P=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & 1 & 0\\
    L_b/2 & -L_b/2 & 0\\
    0 & 0 & 1
  \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:Ptransform}
\end{equation}
where $u$ denotes the generalized force. These coordinates avoid
a closed kinematic-chain description and expose the position-dependent load
distribution \cite{garcia2013model}.


With the kinematics linearized about \(\Theta=0\) and the Coriolis and centripetal terms neglected, 
the baseline equations of motion are
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)\ddot{q}(t) + C\dot{q}(t) + K q(t) = P\uact(t),
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where $C$ and $K$ are constant, whereas the inertia matrix depends
quadratically on the payload position:
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)=M_0+YM_1+Y^2M_2.
  \label{eq:Msplit}
\end{equation}
The payload position changes both the translational--yaw coupling and the yaw
inertia, which makes $Y$ the scheduling variable. Only $\Theta$ has a restoring
stiffness; $X$ and $Y$ are rigid-body coordinates. Unlike the final two-coordinate
simplification in \cite{garcia2013model}, this model retains $Y$ and the symmetric
$\Theta$--$Y$ inertia coupling of the supplied reference implementation.

The fourteen parameters in $\theta$ comprise four masses, two rotary inertias,
five viscous damping coefficients, two joint stiffnesses, and the payload offset
$d$; $L_b$ is fixed by the coordinate transformation. Their nominal values come
from the ETEL system reported in \cite[Table~I]{garcia2013model}, not from an
identification of the present Telica machine. They remain fixed except in the
joint-estimation experiments. Values and units are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:params}.

The baseline deliberately excludes the effects to be learned by the augmentation.
The simulated plant adds a payload-mounted absorber; the friction benchmark also
adds Coulomb friction. These synthetic discrepancies are distinct from the
supporting-structure vibration, detent forces, and stroke-dependent friction
reported for the experimental system in \cite{garcia2013model}.

\subsection{Self-scheduled LPV-LFR representation}\label{sec:lfr}
The measured state $Y$ schedules the inertia matrix, making the model quasi-LPV
\cite{toth2010modeling}. The known map $p=Y$ provides self-scheduling. Because
$M(Y)^{-1}$ depends rationally on $Y$, this dependence is represented by an LTI
part $G$ interconnected with a scheduling block \cite{drenth2025thesis}.

Collecting the net generalized force acting on the inertia,
$\fnet = P\uact-C\dot{q}-Kq$, and substituting \eqref{eq:Msplit}
into \eqref{eq:eom} gives
\begin{equation}
  M_0\,\ddot{q} + M_1 w_1 + M_2 w_2 = \fnet,
  \qquad
  w_1 = Y z_1, \quad w_2 = Y z_2,
  \label{eq:latent}
\end{equation}
with the loop signals $z_1=\ddot{q}$ and $z_2=w_1$. Stacking
$z=[z_1^\top\;z_2^\top]^\top$ and $w=[w_1^\top\;w_2^\top]^\top$, the scheduling
variable acts only through
\begin{equation}
  w = \Dsch(Y)\,z, \qquad \Dsch(Y) = Y I_6 ,
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
while all remaining relations are independent of $Y$. The direct six-channel
realization is retained because its two three-dimensional channels correspond
directly to the linear and quadratic terms in \eqref{eq:Msplit}. The LTI part is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ z \\ \qact \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
      A   & B_w    & B_u \\
      C_z & D_{zw} & D_{zu} \\
      C_y & 0      & 0
  \end{bmatrix}}_{G}
  \begin{bmatrix} x \\ w \\ \uact \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:lfr}
\end{equation}
where $x=[q^\top\;\dot{q}^\top]^\top$, $n_x=6$, $n_z=n_w=6$, and
$n_u=n_y=3$. Here $B_u$ includes the force transformation $P$, and
$C_y=[P^\top\;0]$. Closing $w=\Dsch(Y)z$ and substituting
\eqref{eq:delta} into \eqref{eq:latent} returns
$(M_0+YM_1+Y^2M_2)\ddot{q}=\fnet$, so the realization reproduces \eqref{eq:eom}
exactly.

The interconnection is well posed when $I-D_{zw}\Dsch(Y)$ is nonsingular for
every admissible $Y$ \cite{drenth2025thesis}. Appendix~\ref{app:params} shows that
this is equivalent to nonsingularity of $M(Y)$. Positive component masses and
inertias give $M(Y)\succ0$ for all $Y\in\R$, so the nominal interconnection is
well posed.

The continuous-time form preserves the polynomial dependence in
\eqref{eq:Msplit}; exact discretization would instead introduce a matrix-exponential
dependence on $Y$. A fixed-step RK4 scheme evaluates $Y$ at its intermediate
stages and supplies the sampled transition used for training and evaluation. Its
step size and number of substeps are specified in Section~\ref{sec:setup}; the
equivalent rational inverse is evaluated in Horner form.

The fourteen raw physical parameters enter the model only through
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \varphi &=[k_{b,\Sigma},\ c_{g1},\ c_{g2},\ c_y,\ c_{b,\Sigma},\ m_h,\
                    m_\Sigma,\ m_\Delta,\ J_{\mathrm{eff}},\ d]^\top,\\
  k_{b,\Sigma} &=k_{b1}+k_{b2},\qquad
  c_{b,\Sigma}=c_{b1}+c_{b2},\\
  m_\Sigma &=m_1+m_2+m_b,\qquad m_\Delta=m_1-m_2,\\
  J_{\mathrm{eff}} &=J_b+J_h+\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(m_1+m_2).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
The raw-to-model Jacobian has rank ten, so four raw parameter directions do not
affect the dynamics. Parameter recovery is therefore assessed using $\varphi$;
individual raw splits are not interpreted without an explicit gauge choice. The
mapping, null directions, and admissibility condition are derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:params}. Structural identifiability does not guarantee that a
finite dataset excites all ten directions; practical recoverability is assessed
in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.
