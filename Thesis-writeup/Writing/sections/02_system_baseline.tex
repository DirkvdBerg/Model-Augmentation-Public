The system is a dual-drive H-type gantry stage: two parallel linear actuators
carry a cross-arm through flexible joints, and a third actuator moves the payload
along that arm (Fig.~1) \cite{garcia2013model}. The measured actuator coordinates
are $\qact(t)=[X_1\;X_2\;Y]^\top$, while the equations of motion are written in the
generalized coordinates $q(t)=[X\;\Theta\;Y]^\top$, collecting cross-arm
translation, yaw and payload position. Both sets describe the same configuration
and are related by the constant transformation
\begin{equation}
  \qact = P^\top q,
  \qquad
  P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ L_b/2 & -L_b/2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:Ptransform}
\end{equation}
so that the actuator forces $\uact$ act as $P\uact$ on the generalized coordinates
and the measured output is $y=\qact=P^\top q$. This choice of coordinates avoids
closed kinematic chains and exposes the coupling associated with the non-uniform
load distribution between the actuators \cite{garcia2013model}.

The Euler-Lagrange equations of the lumped-parameter model give
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)\ddot{q}(t) + C\dot{q}(t) + K q(t) = P\uact(t),
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with the parameter values inherited from the reference implementation of the
machine. The payload position enters the inertia matrix without derivatives, and
does so quadratically,
\begin{equation}
  M(Y) = M_0 + Y M_1 + Y^2 M_2 ,
  \label{eq:Msplit}
\end{equation}
where $M_1$ holds only the off-diagonal entries $-m_h$ that couple translation and
yaw, and $M_2$ the single entry $m_h$ on the yaw diagonal; the full matrices are
listed in the appendix. Damping and stiffness are constant. Only the yaw
coordinate carries a restoring stiffness $k_{b1}+k_{b2}$, while the translational
coordinates carry none, so the baseline contains rigid-body integrators in which a
small velocity error accumulates into a position drift.

For positive masses and rotary inertias, $M(Y)\succ 0$ for every $Y\in\R$, which
the appendix proves with Sylvester's criterion. The inertia matrix is therefore
invertible over the whole operating range, as the realization of
Section~\ref{sec:lfr} requires.

Several effects are deliberately outside the baseline. Garc\'ia-Herreros et al.
neglect the vibration of the cross-arm on the elastic support formed by the plates
that attach it to the actuators, the detent forces of the linear motors, and the
variation of the friction parameters along the stroke; they separately report a
37.7 Hz resonance of the supporting structure, which their experiments
suppress by input shaping rather than model \cite{garcia2013model}. The simulation
study of this paper introduces its own absorber mode on the payload (shaded in
Fig.~1) and, in the friction benchmark, dry friction in the data-generating model
only; neither is represented in the baseline (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

The payload stroke of the machine is $\pm 0.40$ m. The records used here
cover $Y\in[-0.30,0.30]~\mathrm{m}$, which is the range this study represents.

The payload position is a state of the model and not an external signal. Since
$Y$ is a system state rather than an exogenous signal, the resulting formulation
is quasi-LPV \cite{toth2010modeling}; the model is self-scheduled, with the known
scheduling map $p=Y$, whereas \cite{drenth2025lpvlfr} estimates that map jointly
with the model. Because $M(Y)^{-1}$ is rational in $Y$, no affine state-space
realization reproduces the dependence exactly, which is what an LFR provides.

Collecting the net generalized force acting on the inertia,
$\fnet = P\uact - C\dot{q} - Kq$, and substituting the split \eqref{eq:Msplit}
into \eqref{eq:eom} gives
\begin{equation}
  M_0\,\ddot{q} + M_1 w_1 + M_2 w_2 = \fnet,
  \qquad
  w_1 = Y z_1, \quad w_2 = Y z_2,
  \label{eq:latent}
\end{equation}
with the loop signals $z_1=\ddot{q}$ and $z_2=w_1$. Stacking
$z=[z_1^\top\;z_2^\top]^\top$ and $w=[w_1^\top\;w_2^\top]^\top$, the scheduling
variable acts only through
\begin{equation}
  w = \Dsch(Y)\,z, \qquad \Dsch(Y) = Y I_6 ,
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
and every remaining relation is parameter independent. The baseline is therefore
the lower linear fractional transformation $\mathcal{F}_\ell(G,\Dsch(Y))$ of the
constant realization
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ z \\ y \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
      A   & B_w    & B_u \\
      C_z & D_{zw} & D_{zu} \\
      C_y & 0      & 0
  \end{bmatrix}}_{G}
  \begin{bmatrix} x \\ w \\ \uact \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:lfr}
\end{equation}
shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:lfr}, with state $x=[q^\top\;\dot{q}^\top]^\top$ and dimensions $n_x=6$,
$n_z=n_w=6$ and $n_u=n_y=3$. All blocks are constant and built from $M_0^{-1}$,
$M_1$, $M_2$, $C$, $K$ and $P$; they are listed in the appendix. Substituting
\eqref{eq:delta} back into \eqref{eq:latent} returns
$(M_0+YM_1+Y^2M_2)\ddot{q}=\fnet$, so the realization reproduces \eqref{eq:eom}
exactly rather than approximating it.

An LFR is well posed when $I-D_{zw}\Dsch(p)$ is non-singular for every admissible
scheduling value \cite{drenth2025lpvlfr}. For the realization \eqref{eq:lfr}, and
with $M_0$ invertible, $I-D_{zw}\Dsch(Y)$ is non-singular if and only if $M(Y)$
is. Positive definiteness of $M(Y)$ then makes the loop well posed over the entire
payload range, not only at the operating points visited by the data.

The loop is resolved in closed form rather than by a linear solve at each
evaluation: with $N_i$ the coefficients of the adjugate of $M(Y)$ and $d(Y)=\det
M(Y)$,
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)^{-1} = \frac{N_0 + Y N_1 + Y^2 N_2}{d(Y)},
  \label{eq:rational}
\end{equation}
evaluated in Horner form. The continuous-time vector field is retained and
integrated with a fixed-step RK4 scheme, which supplies the sampled transition
used for training and evaluation; its step size is given in
Section~\ref{sec:setup}. Holding $Y$ fixed reduces \eqref{eq:lfr} to a local LTI
model \cite{toth2010modeling}, which provides the frozen-model comparator if the
matched evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:results} is retained.
