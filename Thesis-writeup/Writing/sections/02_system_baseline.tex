\section{Dual-Drive Gantry System and Physics Baseline}

\subsection{System and first-principles baseline}
The physics baseline used in this work is based on the lumped-parameter model of the
dual-drive gantry developed by Garc\'ia-Herreros et al.~\cite{garcia2013model}.
The gantry has two actuators that carry a cross-arm and a 
third actuator that moves the payload along the arm (Fig.~\ref{fig:gantry_system}).

Define the actuator forces
$\uact=[F_{X1}\;F_{X2}\;F_Y]^\top$, the measured positions
$\qact=[X_1\;X_2\;Y]^\top$, and the generalized coordinates
$q=[X\;\Theta\;Y]^\top$. Let \(L_b\) denote the cross-arm length between the two flexible joints. 
The coordinate and force transformations are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  q &= P^{-\top}\qact,
  \qquad
  u = P\uact,\\
  P &=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & 1 & 0\\
    L_b/2 & -L_b/2 & 0\\
    0 & 0 & 1
  \end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Ptransform}
\end{equation}
where $u$ denotes the generalized force. 
The generalized coordinates are defined as $X=(X_1+X_2)/2$ and $\Theta=(X_1-X_2)/L_b$, while $Y$ is
unchanged. These coordinates avoid
a closed kinematic-chain description and make the coupling caused by the
nonuniform load distribution explicit \cite{garcia2013model}.

Following Garc\'ia-Herreros et al. \cite{garcia2013model}, the baseline uses \(\cos\Theta\approx1\) 
and \(\sin\Theta\approx0\) and assumes that the Coriolis and centripetal force term is 
negligible compared with the inertia, damping, and stiffness forces. 
% The simulated ground-truth model uses the same simplifications. 
Under these assumptions, the baseline equations of motion are
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)\ddot{q}(t) + C\dot{q}(t) + K q(t) = P\uact(t),
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with
{\footnotesize
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{3pt}
\begin{equation}
M(Y)=
\begin{bmatrix}
m_1+m_2+m_b+m_h & \tfrac{L_b}{2}(m_1-m_2)-m_hY & 0\\
\tfrac{L_b}{2}(m_1-m_2)-m_hY &
\substack{J_b+J_h+\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(m_1+m_2)\\{}+m_h(d^2+Y^2)} & -m_hd\\
0 & -m_hd & m_h
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:mass_matrix}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C&=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_{g1}+c_{g2} & \tfrac{L_b}{2}(c_{g1}-c_{g2}) & 0\\
\tfrac{L_b}{2}(c_{g1}-c_{g2}) &
c_{b1}+c_{b2}+\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(c_{g1}+c_{g2}) & 0\\
0 & 0 & c_y
\end{bmatrix},\\[1ex]
K&=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&0&0\\
0&k_{b1}+k_{b2}&0\\
0&0&0
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:damping_stiffness_matrices}
\end{equation}
}
The masses, flexible-joint stiffness and damping coefficients, and geometric
dimensions are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:gantry_system}. Here, $J_b$ and $J_h$
denote the yaw inertias, while $c_{g1}$, $c_{g2}$, and $c_y$ denote the actuator
damping coefficients. The nominal values are taken from
\cite[Table~I]{garcia2013model} and listed in Appendix~\ref{app:params} \todo{Not sure if to add this, depends on real data or not; they were not identified on the present Telica machine.}
To prevent the definition of \(q\) from being parameter-dependent, 
the cross-arm length \(L_b\) is fixed at the nominal value listed in Appendix~\ref{app:params} 
rather than treated as a free model parameter.

\subsection{Self-scheduled LPV-LFR representation}\label{sec:lfr}
The position dependence of \eqref{eq:mass_matrix} can be written as
\begin{equation}
  M(Y)=M_0+YM_1+Y^2M_2,
  \label{eq:Msplit}
\end{equation}
where $M_0$, $M_1$, and $M_2$ are constant. The measured payload position $Y$
is used as the scheduling variable. Because $Y$ is also a model state, the
representation is self-scheduled, or quasi-LPV \cite{toth2010modeling}.

Define the net generalized force as
\begin{equation}
  \fnet=P\uact-C\dot q-Kq.
  \label{eq:fnet}
\end{equation}
Solving \eqref{eq:eom} for $\ddot q$ introduces the rational dependence
$M(Y)^{-1}$. Rather than forming this inverse explicitly, the LFR is
constructed from the mass equation by introducing
\begin{equation}
  w_1=Y\ddot q, \qquad w_2=Yw_1=Y^2\ddot q.
  \label{eq:internal_signals}
\end{equation}
The resulting $Y$-independent relation is
\begin{equation}
  M_0\ddot q+M_1w_1+M_2w_2=\fnet.
  \label{eq:constant_mass_relation}
\end{equation}
Both products in \eqref{eq:internal_signals} apply the same scalar $Y$ to a
three-dimensional signal. Defining
\begin{equation}
  z=\begin{bmatrix}\ddot q\\w_1\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  w=\begin{bmatrix}w_1\\w_2\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
their stacked relation is
\begin{equation}
  w=
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
    YI_3&0\\
    0&YI_3
  \end{bmatrix}}_{\Dsch(Y)=YI_6}z.
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
Thus, all dependence on $Y$ is contained in the scheduling block
$\Dsch(Y)$ \cite{drenth2025thesis}. With
$x=[q^\top\;\dot q^\top]^\top$, the remaining relations define the constant
block
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x} \\ z \\ \qact \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}
      A   & B_w    & B_u \\
      C_z & D_{zw} & D_{zu} \\
      C_y & 0      & 0
  \end{bmatrix}}_{G}
  \begin{bmatrix} x \\ w \\ \uact \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:lfr}
\end{equation}
The state is expressed in generalized coordinates, whereas the input $\uact$
and output $\qact$ remain in actuator coordinates to match the applied forces
and measured positions. The transformations in \eqref{eq:Ptransform} are
included in $G$, whose matrices are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:params}.

Closing the loop in \eqref{eq:constant_mass_relation} gives
\begin{equation}
  M_0\ddot q+YM_1\ddot q+Y^2M_2\ddot q=\fnet,
\end{equation}
which, by \eqref{eq:Msplit} and \eqref{eq:fnet}, is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:eom}.

  by $\Dsch(Y)=YI_6$, where $Y$ is a component of the state.}

The internal loop is well posed if and only if $M(Y)$ is nonsingular. For
positive component masses and yaw inertias, $M(Y)$ is positive definite for
every $Y\in\R$; hence, the nominal interconnection is globally well posed. The
proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:params}. The continuous-time model is
integrated using the fixed-step RK4 scheme specified in
Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

The fourteen raw physical parameters enter the model only through
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \varphi &=[k_{b,\Sigma},\ c_{g1},\ c_{g2},\ c_y,\ c_{b,\Sigma},\ m_h,\
                    m_\Sigma,\ m_\Delta,\ J_{\mathrm{eff}},\ d]^\top,\\
  k_{b,\Sigma} &=k_{b1}+k_{b2},\qquad
  c_{b,\Sigma}=c_{b1}+c_{b2},\\
  m_\Sigma &=m_1+m_2+m_b,\qquad m_\Delta=m_1-m_2,\\
  J_{\mathrm{eff}} &=J_b+J_h+\tfrac{L_b^2}{4}(m_1+m_2).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
The raw-to-model Jacobian has rank ten, so four raw parameter directions do not
affect the dynamics. Parameter recovery is therefore assessed using $\varphi$;
individual raw splits are not interpreted without an explicit gauge choice. The
mapping, null directions, and admissibility condition are derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:params}. Structural identifiability does not guarantee that a
finite dataset excites all ten directions; practical recoverability is assessed
in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.
