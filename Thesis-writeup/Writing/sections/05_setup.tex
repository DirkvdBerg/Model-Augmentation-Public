The system used to obtain the data is simulated. 
When referring to the system in this section, we refer to this simulated model. 
The data consist of simulated records, with motion profiles, 
limits and noise level taken from an industrial gantry whose data is confidential.
Frequencies are therefore given relative to the controller bandwidth
$f_b$, positions relative to the training range, and velocities and
accelerations relative to the machine limits.

\subsection{Simulated system}\label{sec:setup_system}
% Sources: D-204, D-224 (truth), D-188 (absorber), D-223, D-226 (friction gain), D-221 (K1),
% D-238 (noise); tdg_config.m, gtd_build_plant.m; Code/Setup rail_velocity.py, band_edge.py, snr.py.
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item The data is simulated: the system is the baseline plus rail friction and a payload absorber, both absent from the baseline
\item The friction subtracts from the actuator forces; a smoothed Coulomb law, Coulomb-like on the excited records
\item The absorber adds one coordinate, coupled through the inertia; matrices written out as in Section~II
\item Parameter values with their basis; both absorber features lie above the crossover of the loop
\item One known controller $K_1$, the same in every record; the injected force is the only feedforward
\item Noise matched to the machine; noise-free twin of every record; SNR (?: confidentiality)
\end{itemize}
\fi
The system consists of the baseline model \eqref{eq:eom}, with two additions.
The first is Coulomb friction at the actuators $X_1$, $X_2$ and $Y$, as
modelled by Garc\'ia-Herreros et al.~\cite{garcia2013model}.
We deliberately left it out of the baseline, to add nonlinear unmodelled
dynamics to the system. The friction works as a force against each actuator 
force, this is modeled as a term entering \eqref{eq:eom} through the input.
For the second addition, we split the payload mass $m_h$ into
$m_a$ and $m_{h,r}=m_h-m_a$, with $m_a$ connected to $m_{h,r}$
by a spring and damper in the $Y$ direction
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gantry_absorber}).
This adds a vibration mode which the 
baseline model is not able to represent with its states.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{figures/gantry_system_with_absorber}}
  \caption{\todo{Simulated gantry with a flexibly mounted part $m_a$ of the
  payload mass $m_h$; the rest of the payload is $m_{h,r}=m_h-m_a$. The
  light-gray elements $(m_a,k_a,c_a,q_a)$ are absent from the baseline.}}
  \label{fig:gantry_absorber}
\end{figure}
With the friction acting against each actuator force on the input, 
\eqref{eq:eom} becomes
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:setup_truth}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 M(Y)\ddot q+C\dot q+Kq&=P\bigl(\uact-F_{\mathrm{fr}}(P^\top\dot q)\bigr),\\
 F_{\mathrm{fr},i}(v_i)&=c_{c,i}\tanh\bigl(v_i/v_{\mathrm{fr}}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:setup_fric}
\end{equation}
where $P^\top\dot q$ gives the actuator velocities
for $i\in\{X_1,X_2,Y\}$, and $v_{\mathrm{fr}}$ sets
the velocity scale of the smoothing.
For $\lvert v_i\rvert\gg v_{\mathrm{fr}}$, the friction
approaches the Coulomb law
$F_{\mathrm{fr},i}=c_{c,i}\operatorname{sign}(v_i)$
of~\cite{garcia2013model}.
$v_{\mathrm{fr}}=\SetupFricVzero$~mm/s was chosen by simulating
the loop with values between 0.1 and 10~mm/s.
\kw{Higher values turn the friction into viscous damping under the multisine,
and lower values keep the standstill servo error above the noise level.}
\\
The added mass adds the displacement $q_a$ of $m_a$ relative to
$m_{h,r}$ along $Y$.
With $q_{\mathrm e}=\begin{bmatrix}q^\top&q_a\end{bmatrix}^\top$, the
system is
\begin{equation}
 M_{\mathrm e}(Y,q_a)\,\ddot q_{\mathrm e}+C_{\mathrm e}\dot q_{\mathrm e}+K_{\mathrm e}q_{\mathrm e}
 =\begin{bmatrix}P\bigl(\uact-F_{\mathrm{fr}}(P^\top\dot q)\bigr)\\0\end{bmatrix},
 \label{eq:setup_abs}
\end{equation}
\end{subequations}
with
{\footnotesize
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{3pt}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
 M_{\mathrm e}(Y,q_a)=\begin{bmatrix}M(Y)+M_a(Y,\ell)&m_a\begin{bmatrix}0&-d&1\end{bmatrix}^\top\\m_a\begin{bmatrix}0&-d&1\end{bmatrix}&m_a\end{bmatrix},\\
 M_a(Y,\ell)=m_a\ell\begin{bmatrix}0&-1&0\\-1&2Y+\ell&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\\
 C_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{diag}(C,c_a),\quad
 K_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{diag}(K,k_a),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}}
where $M(Y)$ is \eqref{eq:mass_matrix} with $m_h$ the total payload mass.
$M_a(Y,\ell)$ corrects it for $m_a$ being at $Y+\ell$ instead of $Y$,
where $\ell=L_0+q_a$ and $L_0$ is the offset of the added mass along $Y$.
$k_a=m_a(2\pi f_a)^2$ and
$c_a=2\zeta_a\sqrt{k_am_a}$, with natural frequency $f_a$
and damping ratio $\zeta_a$.
The added mass changes the inertia through $M_a$,
which also includes its coupling to $\Theta$ and $Y$.
We simulate the system with fixed-step RK4 at
$f_{\mathrm s}=20$~kHz.
All parameter values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:nominal_parameters}.
The friction levels $c_c$ are taken from Garc\'ia-Herreros et
al.~\cite[Table~I]{garcia2013model}.
The added mass creates an anti-resonance
at $f_a$ and a resonance at $f_a\sqrt{1+m_a/m_{h,r}}\approx2.12\,f_b$,
in the transfer function from $F_Y$ to $Y$.
Both are designed to be above the crossover of the loop with $K_1$
(Section~\ref{sec:setup_data}).
As a result, the feedback does not suppress them, and we can identify
them from the data.
All data is generated in closed loop with the controller $K_1$ of
Appendix~\ref{app:setup_system}, which is the known controller of
Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}.
The only feedforward, $u^{\mathrm{ff}}$ in \eqref{eq:aug_two_loops}, is the
injected excitation force.
Independent coloured noise is added to the position measurements
of each axis, $y=\qact+v$.
The simulated servo error matches at standstill the noise level
and spectral shape of the industrial gantry, but its resonances are left
out (Appendix~\ref{app:setup_system}).
The servo-error signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) ranges from
\SetupSnrMinX{} to \SetupSnrMaxX~dB on the $X$ actuators
and \SetupSnrMinY{} to \SetupSnrMaxY~dB on $Y$, depending on the motion of the record.
The data is also generated without measurement noise, with output $y^\circ$.

\ifdraft

\item The motion profiles excite up to the crossover above the noise; the multisine band starts at the highest crossover, ends at 90\,\% of the model difference and holds the absorber features of the simulated system (simulation only)

The data is divided into five types of motion profiles and each record is 12~s
(Appendix~\ref{app:setup_records}).
The motion profiles excite the low frequencies up till the loop crossover, 
for at least \SetupGapSnrMinMotion~dB above the noise.
A multisine force is added to each of the generalized channels $X$,
$\Theta$, $Y$, except in records with an operational motion profile.
The force is applied to the actuators through $P^{-1}$.
The multisine band starts at the highest loop crossover 
$\fpeval{round(\SetupCrossMax/100,2)}\,f_b$, to prevent feedback suppression.
The band ends at $2.97\,f_b$, which keeps 90\,\% of the squared
frequency-response error of the baseline above the crossover. This was 
measured on the system without noise.
The band contains the anti-resonance and the
resonance of the added mass (Fig.~\ref{fig:setup_band}), 
for all the positions used in training.
This was determined from the frequency response of the known system model at
each training position.
\\
We use 18 records for training, 6 for validation and 11 for testing
(Table~\ref{tab:setup_records} in Appendix~\ref{app:setup_records}).
\revnote{11? and the multinse doubles?}
Training and validation include all five motion types, with
$\lvert Y\rvert\le Y_{\mathrm{tr}}$, up to \SetupTrainAcc\,\% of the
acceleration limit and \SetupTrainVel\,\% of the velocity limit.
The validation records are used to select the best-performing checkpoints
and the hyperparameters of the black box.
We separate the test records into interpolation and extrapolation.
Interpolation stays within the training range, while each
extrapolation record exceeds one of the limits on $Y$,
acceleration or velocity.
The results for all three sets are reported, but performance is evaluated on
the test set.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:setup_metrics}
% Sources: D-256 (NRMS_e), THESIS-RESULTS Sec. 2; D-259 (m_Delta scale, revnotes); Section IV
% eq:obc_eps, eq:obc_recovery, eq:obc_overlap; Code/Evaluation eval_overlap.py.
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Every model is simulated over whole test records: grey-box models in closed loop with $K_1$, the black box in free run
\item Primary metric: the simulation error relative to the servo error, $\mathrm{NRMS}_e$, per stage axis; beside it the RMS in metres and the noise floor
\item Parameter error per combination against the true values; $m_\Delta$ scaled by the mean actuator mass; simulation only
\item The two predictions of Section~\ref{sec:obc}: the network stays out of the parameter directions, and the remaining parameter error is the first-order bias
\end{itemize}
\fi
Each grey-box model is simulated in closed loop with $K_1$
(Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}) over each whole test record, from the encoder
estimate at the record start.
The black box is simulated in free run over the same records.

The reference is given, so a model has to predict the servo error $y-r$.
The primary metric is therefore the RMS of the simulation error per stage
axis $j$ relative to the standard deviation of the servo error.
Beside it, the RMS in metres and the noise floor of the record are
reported:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{RMS}_j&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{k=n}^{N-1}(\hat y_{j,k}-y_{j,k})^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\\
 \mathrm{NRMS}_{e,j}&=\frac{\mathrm{RMS}_j}{\sigma_{e,j}},\\
 \mathrm{RMS}^{\mathrm{fl}}_j&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{k=n}^{N-1}(y_{j,k}-y^{\circ}_{j,k})^2\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:setup_rms}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the record length, $n$ the encoder window, and
$\sigma_{e,j}$ is the standard deviation of the servo error $y_j-r_j$ over
the same samples.
Since $\hat y-y=(\hat y-r)-(y-r)$, $\mathrm{NRMS}_{e,j}$ is the NRMS
of~\cite[Eq.~(43)]{beintema2023subnet} with the servo error as output.
A value of 100\,\% means no better than $\hat y=r$.
The floor is the error of an exact noise-free prediction.
\revnote{The current evaluation reports the RMS in metres per record and
axis; $\mathrm{NRMS}_{e,j}$ is computed from these outside the evaluation
code.}
\revnote{Start index of \eqref{eq:setup_rms}: the window $\chi_\tau$ holds
the $n+1$ samples up to $\tau-1$ (\eqref{eq:aug_encoder}), so the first
window covers samples $0$ to $n$ and the first prediction is at $k=n+1$.
Following~\cite[Eq.~(43)]{beintema2023subnet}, which leaves out the outputs
that fed the encoder, the sums run from $k=n+1$ with $1/(N-n-1)$; the code
of the final runs scores from that sample. $\sigma_{e,j}$ is the population
standard deviation over the same samples (D-262).}

The combinations are compared with their true values
$\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base}}$ by
$\delta_j=\lvert\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}-\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}\rvert/\lvert\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}\rvert$.
For $m_\Delta=m_1-m_2$, the denominator is the mean actuator mass
$(m_1+m_2)/2$ instead.
Its true value of $-0.5$~kg is close to zero, while it acts in $M(Y)$
beside the actuator masses.
This uses the true parameters and is available only in simulation.

Section~\ref{sec:obc} predicts two properties of
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$.
Both are measured on the one-step tuples of I1 to I6 at $\bar x=0$.
The first is that the network stays out of the parameter directions.
It is measured by the overlap $\rho$ of \eqref{eq:obc_overlap}, with $\Phi$
at the trained coordinates, for $F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$ of
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ and for $\tilde F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$ of
\eqref{eq:obc_corr} for $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$.
The second is that the remaining parameter error is the first-order bias of
\eqref{eq:obc_recovery}.
Its first line, with $\Xi$ of \eqref{eq:obc_eps} and $\Phi$ at
$\beta^\star$, predicts the $\delta_j$ of the trained
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$.
The truth overlap $\rho(\Xi)$ measures how far the missing dynamics lie in
the parameter directions.
\revnote{The predicted first-order bias is not yet computed for the current
runs.}
\revnote{Final runs (D-268, Dirk 2026-10-16): the overlaps and the predicted
first-order bias are computed on three tuple stacks, training, validation and
test (I1 to I6), so the sentence ``Both are measured on the one-step tuples of
I1 to I6'' changes. The training stack, where $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ is
formed, gives the prediction of \eqref{eq:obc_recovery}; validation and test
show how it carries over. On the training stack the network overlap of
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ is zero by construction, so that overlap is
informative on validation and test. The prediction holds for the one-step
least-squares fit at $\bar x=0$, while training minimises the closed-loop loss
with active added states (Section~\ref{sec:obc}); the agreement criterion
between predicted and observed $\delta_j$ is to be stated before the results.}
