\subsection{Data-generating system}\label{sec:setup_system}
\ifdraft

  \caption{Simulated gantry with a payload-mounted absorber. The light-gray

The data are simulated, so the dynamics the baseline misses are known.
The data-generating system extends the baseline \eqref{eq:eom} with a
vibration absorber on the payload and with Coulomb friction on the three
rails (Fig.~\ref{fig:gantry_absorber}).
Both are absent from the baseline, so the augmentation has to capture them.
With $q_{\mathrm e}=\col(q,\delta_a)$, where $\delta_a$ is the displacement
of the absorber mass relative to the head along $Y$, the system is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &M_{\mathrm e}(Y,\delta_a)\,\ddot q_{\mathrm e}+C_{\mathrm e}\dot q_{\mathrm e}+K_{\mathrm e}q_{\mathrm e}
 =\begin{bmatrix}P\bigl(\uact-F_{\mathrm{fr}}(P^\top\dot q)\bigr)\\0\end{bmatrix},\\
 &M_{\mathrm e}(Y,\delta_a)=\begin{bmatrix}M(Y)+m_aE(Y,\ell)&m_ae\\m_ae^\top&m_a\end{bmatrix},\\
 &E(Y,\ell)=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\ell&0\\-\ell&\ell(2Y+\ell)&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\\
 &F_{\mathrm{fr},i}(v_i)=c_{c,i}\tanh(g\,v_i),\qquad i\in\{X_1,X_2,Y\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:setup_truth}
\end{equation}
where $M(Y)$ is \eqref{eq:mass_matrix} with $m_h$ the total head mass, of
which $m_a$ moves relative to the head, $\ell=L_0+\delta_a$ with $L_0$ the
offset of the absorber along $Y$, $e=\col(0,-d,1)$,
$C_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{diag}(C,c_a)$,
$K_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{diag}(K,k_a)$, and $P^\top\dot q$ are the rail
velocities.
The baseline parameters take the values of Appendix~\ref{app:params}.
The friction levels $c_c=(16.80,18.35,11.60)$~N are those identified by
Garc\'ia-Herreros et al.~\cite{garcia2013model}.
\todo{Missing: the table or section of \cite{garcia2013model} that gives $c_c$ (citation-log check). Candidate: the identification of Sec.~2.2 (D-204 gate 1 recovers these intercepts).}
The absorber has mass $m_a=0.5\,m_h$ at $L_0=0.10$~m, natural frequency
$f_a=150$~Hz and damping ratio $\zeta_a=0.03$, so
$k_a=m_a(2\pi f_a)^2$ and $c_a=2\zeta_a\sqrt{k_am_a}$.
Seen from the head, $f_a$ is an anti-resonance.
The coupled mode lies at $f_a\sqrt{1+m_a/(m_h-m_a)}\approx212$~Hz.
\todo{Missing: reason for $m_a=0.5\,m_h$, $f_a=150$~Hz and $\zeta_a=0.03$. Candidate: $m_a$ large enough that the mode carries a learnable share of the baseline error (D-170, D-188); $\zeta_a$ the damping of jointed metal structures (generator comment, source to verify).}

For $\lvert v_i\rvert\gg1/g$, the friction equals the Coulomb law
$c_{c,i}\operatorname{sign}(v_i)$ of~\cite{garcia2013model}.
With $g=1000$~s/m it is Coulomb-like at the rail velocities of the excited
records.
The axes still settle at standstill without a limit cycle.
The system is integrated by fixed-step RK4 at $f_{\mathrm s}=20$~kHz.

The records are closed loop, with one controller per stage axis $j$,
designed on the rigid baseline at $Y=0$:
\begin{equation}
 K_{1,j}(s)=\kappa_j\,\frac{s+\omega_b/6}{s}\,
 \frac{s+\omega_b/3}{s+3\omega_b}\,\frac{10\,\omega_b}{s+10\,\omega_b},
 \label{eq:setup_K1}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_b=2\pi\cdot100$~rad/s is the bandwidth and $\kappa_j$ sets the
open-loop gain of axis $j$ to one at $\omega_b$, for the diagonal entry of
the rigid baseline from stage force to stage position.
$K_1$ is discretised by the Tustin rule at $f_{\mathrm s}$.
It is the same in every record and is the known controller of
Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}.
The feedforward $u^{\mathrm{ff}}$ of \eqref{eq:aug_two_loops} is the
injected excitation force only (Section~\ref{sec:setup_data}), so $K_1$
alone makes the stages follow the reference.

The position measurement carries noise, $y=\qact+v$.
The components of $v$ are independent, with one-sided spectra
\begin{equation}
 \phi_j(f)=g_j^2\,\lvert H_j(f)\rvert^2+\sigma_j^2,
 \label{eq:setup_noise}
\end{equation}
where $H_j$ is a second-order Butterworth low-pass with cutoff $f_{c,j}$,
$g_j$ its level and $\sigma_j$ a white floor.
The parameters give a standstill servo error with a level and spectral
shape representative of the industrial machine.
Every record also exists without noise ($v=0$), denoted $y^{\circ}$.
Over the training records, the signal-to-noise ratio of the servo error,
$\mathrm{SNR}_j=20\log_{10}\bigl(\mathrm{RMS}(y^{\circ}_j-r_j)/\mathrm{RMS}(y_j-y^{\circ}_j)\bigr)$,
lies between \SetupSnrMinX{} and \SetupSnrMaxX~dB on the $X$ rails and
between \SetupSnrMinY{} and \SetupSnrMaxY~dB on $Y$.

\subsection{Records}\label{sec:setup_data}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Motion profiles excite the low frequencies, an injected multisine the absorber band
\item Multisine 106 to 297~Hz on a 0.5~Hz grid, random phases, lowest crest factor of 30; no record needed scaling to stay within the machine limits (MANIFEST scale 1.00; D-219)
\item Lower edge at the highest $K_1$ crossover over axes and training positions (band\_edge.py); placement from the simulated frequency response; simulation only, stated once for crossover, placement and loop memory (D-219)
\item Five motion types; 12~s records at 20~kHz, noisy and noise-free twin
\item To 4~kHz: phase-margin loss (rate.py), block mean of $u$, zero-phase low-pass on $y-r$ (filter\_leak.py), 5~ms trimmed (D-087, D-166, D-232)
\item Window 0.1~s against the closed-loop memory (D-220, loop\_memory.py); controller re-discretised at $T_s$, median force change (controller\_rate.py), details to the appendix (?)
\item Split 18 / 6 / 12; test records by layer: interpolation (I1, I2), operational motion (I3 to I6), range extrapolation (E1 to E4, E6) (D-221, README validation story)
\end{itemize}
\fi
The records excite the low frequencies with motion profiles and the band of
the absorber with an injected multisine force.
In each channel $c\in\{X,\Theta,Y\}$ of the generalised force, the
multisine is
\begin{equation}
 f_c(t)=A_c\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lvert\mathcal L\rvert}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal L}
 \cos(2\pi l\,\Delta f\,t+\varphi_{c,l}),
 \label{eq:setup_ms}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta f=0.5$~Hz, $\mathcal L=\{212,\dots,594\}$ spans 106 to
297~Hz, and the phases $\varphi_{c,l}$ are drawn uniformly per record.
Of 30 draws, the one with the lowest crest factor is kept.
The force enters at the actuators as $P^{-1}\col(f_X,f_\Theta,f_Y)$.
The rms amplitudes $A=(240~\mathrm N,87~\mathrm{N\,m},180~\mathrm N)$ keep
every record within the force and stroke limits of the machine without
scaling.
\todo{Missing: reason for the 0.5~Hz grid. Candidate: D-219, more power per line than the full 1/12~Hz grid of the 12~s record while enough lines per resonance width remain (Geerardyn et al.\ 2013, no cite key yet).}

The lower edge of the band is the highest crossover of the loop with $K_1$
over the axes and the training positions, \SetupCrossMax~Hz (lowest
\SetupCrossMin~Hz).
Below the crossover, the feedback suppresses an injected force.
The band is placed with the frequency response of \eqref{eq:setup_truth}
under $K_1$ at the five training positions, so that the anti-resonance, the
coupled mode and the closed-loop peak lie inside it.
The crossover, this placement and the loop memory below use the simulated
system without friction, so they are available only in simulation.
On a machine, a measured frequency response plays this role.

Each record lasts 12~s at $f_{\mathrm s}$.
Five motion types are used: standstill at a fixed $Y$, slow sweeps of $Y$,
$X$-$Y$ Lissajous paths, point-to-point moves with S-curve profiles, and
sine-shaped moves representative of machine operation.
The sine-shaped moves carry no multisine.

The records are reduced to the model rate of 4~kHz, $T_s=0.25$~ms.
Against 20~kHz, the loop with $K_1$ re-discretised at $T_s$ loses at most
\SetupPmLossFour$^\circ$ of phase margin at 4~kHz and \SetupPmLossTwo$^\circ$
at 2~kHz.
4~kHz is the lowest of these rates within a loss of \SetupPmGate$^\circ$.
With the argument counting samples at $f_{\mathrm s}$,
\begin{equation}
 u_k=\frac15\sum_{i=0}^{4}\uact(5k+i),\qquad
 y_k=r(5k)+\bigl(\mathcal B\ast(y-r)\bigr)(5k),
 \label{eq:setup_decim}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal B$ is a zero-phase eighth-order Butterworth low-pass at
1.6~kHz.
The block mean is the exact input of a zero-order-hold model at $T_s$.
The filter acts on the servo error only, so the reference passes unchanged.
It lowers the noise above the Nyquist frequency of $T_s$ from
$\SetupAliasRaw$ to $\SetupAliasFilt$~m RMS in the training records.
5~ms is removed at both record ends, where the filter has its transient.

Training windows last $n_fT_s=0.1$~s, longer than the memory of the closed
loop.
At the training positions, the slowest closed-loop time constant is
\SetupTauMax~ms.
The response of the output to a force error stays below 1\,\% of its peak
after \SetupDecay~ms.
The training loop re-discretises $K_1$ at $T_s$
(Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}).
On the training records, this changes the feedback force by a median of
\SetupKmismatchMed\,\% RMS.
\todo{Missing: the cause of this change and whether it affects the results. Candidate: the half-sample offset between the block-mean force and the controller output at $T_s$ (not verified); the maximum (\SetupKmismatchMax\,\%), the records without multisine (at most \SetupKmismatchMotion\,\%) and the 20~kHz check ($\SetupKcheck$) go to the appendix implementation checks; the effect to Section~\ref{sec:results} or the Discussion.}

The 36 records are split into 18 for training, 6 for validation and 12 for
testing.
Training covers $\lvert Y\rvert\le\SetupTrainY$~m, accelerations up to
\SetupTrainAcc\,\% and velocities up to \SetupTrainVel\,\% of the machine
maximum.
The validation records use new positions and new random draws.
They only select the checkpoints and the size of the black box.
Every reported number is computed on the test records, which test three
properties separately.
Interpolation (I1, I2) repeats a training motion with new multisine phases
and setpoints, and holds a standstill at a new position.
Operational motion (I3 to I6) uses moves without multisine: sine-shaped
routes with $X$ and $Y$ together, alternating $X$-only and $Y$-only moves,
an S-curve route and long strokes.
Extrapolation (E1 to E4, E6) leaves the training range in one property
against an I record: the position (E1, E2, up to
$\lvert Y\rvert=\SetupEY$~m), the acceleration (E3, \SetupEAcc\,\%), the
velocity (E4, \SetupEVel\,\%) and a move with the kinematics of the machine
(E6).

The models that compare structures start at the true combinations, so each
comparison changes one thing.
The comparison of OBC with the unconstrained model, and the final model,
start from combinations detuned by 10\,\%,
$\theta^0_{\mathrm{base},j}=(1+0.1\,s_j)\,\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}$.

The black box $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{BB}}$ is a SUBNET
model~\cite{beintema2023subnet} of the closed loop, from the reference and

\begin{equation}
 x_{k+1}=f_\theta(x_k,w_k),\qquad \hat e_k=h_\theta(x_k),\qquad

 \label{eq:setup_bb}
\end{equation}

It predicts the same output as the grey-box models.
Its state count $n_x$, network widths and learning rate are selected on the
validation records from 11 configurations, with the records, rate, window
and encoder window of the grey-box models.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:setup_metrics}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Primary: simulation error relative to the servo error, $\mathrm{NRMS}_e$, per record and stage axis; beside it the RMS in m and the record's noise floor (D-256, THESIS-RESULTS Sec. 2)
\item Why the servo error: the reference is given, so $y-r$ is what the model must predict; $y$ itself is dominated by the motion (D-256)
\item Combination error against the true values, $m_\Delta$ by the mean actuator mass; simulation only
\item Overlaps $\rho$ of \eqref{eq:obc_overlap} for the network and for the true discrepancy (moved from Section~IV-C)
\item Single-seed rule for differences (?: seed spread)
\end{itemize}
\fi
Each grey-box model is simulated in closed loop with $K_1$
(Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}) over each whole test record, from the encoder
estimate at the record start.
The black box is simulated in free run over the same records.
The reference is given, so the part of the output a model has to predict is
the servo error $y-r$.
The primary metric is therefore the RMS of the simulation error per stage
axis $j$ relative to the RMS of the servo error, with the RMS in metres and
the noise floor of the record beside it:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{RMS}_j&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{k=n}^{N-1}(\hat y_{j,k}-y_{j,k})^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\\
 \mathrm{NRMS}_{e,j}&=\frac{\mathrm{RMS}_j}{\mathrm{RMS}(y_j-r_j)},\\
 \mathrm{RMS}^{\mathrm{fl}}_j&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{k=n}^{N-1}(y_{j,k}-y^{\circ}_{j,k})^2\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:setup_rms}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the record length, $n$ the encoder window, and
$\mathrm{RMS}(y_j-r_j)$ is taken over the same samples.
The floor is the error of a model that predicts the noise-free output
exactly.
An NRMS normalised by $y$ itself would be dominated by the motion, which is
orders of magnitude larger than the servo error.
\todo{Missing: citations for NRMS and BFR as the usual normalised measures. Candidate: Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ 2025 (test error in NRMS\,\%, Fig.~3) and Drenth et al.\ (BFR, Eq.~(22)); verify their definitions in the PDFs; Hoekstra's papers report the absolute RMSE.}

The combinations are compared with their true values $\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base}}$ by
$\epsilon_j=\lvert\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}-\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}\rvert/\lvert\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}\rvert$.
The small, signed $m_\Delta$ is normalised by the mean actuator mass
instead.
This uses the true parameters and is available only in simulation.

The overlaps of Section~\ref{sec:obc} are $\rho$ of \eqref{eq:obc_overlap}
on the one-step tuples of I1 to I6 at $\bar x=0$.
The network overlap $\rho(\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}})$ uses
$\boldsymbol\Phi$ at the trained coordinates and, for
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$, the network after the correction
\eqref{eq:obc_corr}.
The truth overlap $\rho(\varepsilon)$ uses the discrepancy
\eqref{eq:obc_eps}, with $\boldsymbol\Phi$ at $\beta^\star$.

A difference between two models counts only if it exceeds twice the spread
over seeds of an earlier campaign with the same settings.
