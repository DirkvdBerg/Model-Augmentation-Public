\begin{wip}
The parameter pair $(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})$
minimising the criterion of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} is non-unique:
different pairs give the same fit~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{gyorok2026obc}.




The split between baseline and network in the augmented model of
Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} is not unique, so the estimate of
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ can lose its physical
meaning~\cite[Sec.~3.2]{gyorok2026obc}.
Our goal is an estimate that is unique under stated conditions.
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ construct such an estimate for input-output baselines that
are linear in their parameters.
They name state-space baselines without full-state measurement as future
research~\cite[Sec.~6]{gyorok2026obc}.
We extend their construction to our baseline, a state-space model that is
nonlinear in its parameters.
Only its positions are measured.
It is identified in closed loop.
Section~\ref{sec:obc_map} constructs an orthogonal correction of the physical
rows and gives the condition under which its estimate equals the true
combinations.
Section~\ref{sec:obc_ident} states the identification problem of the corrected
model.

\end{wip}

\begin{wip}

The non-unique split lets the network negate the baseline, Kessels' term for
learned terms that partially cancel the first-principles
terms~\cite[Sec.~5.3.1.3]{kessels2025ai}.
Let $\beta'\in\R^{10}$ be other values of the training coordinates $\beta$ of
\eqref{eq:mdelta_map}.
The network term
$f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}-(\partial f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}/\partial\beta)(\beta'-\beta)$
with $\beta'$ then gives, to first order in $\beta'-\beta$, the same one-step map as
$f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$ with $\beta$.
On the gantry, each column of $\partial f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}/\partial\beta$
is the RK4 propagation, scaled by $T_x$, of the acceleration change
$-M(Y)^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\partial M}{\partial\beta_j}\ddot q+\tfrac{\partial C}{\partial\beta_j}\dot q+\tfrac{\partial K}{\partial\beta_j}q\bigr)$
of \eqref{eq:eom} at fixed state and input.
A negating term therefore acts as an added mass, damping or stiffness force
whose effect on the state depends on $Y$.

The research plan proposed the orthogonality penalty of Gy\"or\"ok et al.,
whose weight trades orthogonality against fit~\cite[Eq.~(13), Remark~1]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We instead adopt their later construction, which makes the network term
orthogonal to the baseline without a weight to
tune~\cite[Secs.~3.2, 5.2]{gyorok2026obc}.
It needs a baseline linear in its parameters~\cite[Eq.~(2)]{gyorok2026obc}.
Our transition is rational in $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ through $M(Y)^{-1}$, so
it has no linear-in-parameters form.

We therefore linearise the normalised transition in its parameters, as
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ do for a nonlinear baseline~\cite[Sec.~4, Eq.~(15)]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We differentiate with respect to $\beta$ rather than the fourteen raw
parameters, whose four lost directions (Section~\ref{sec:params}) would give
the Jacobian four null directions.
The map \eqref{eq:mdelta_map} has a nonsingular Jacobian for
$m_{\Delta,0}\neq0$, so the Jacobian in $\beta$ spans the sensitivity directions
of the ten combinations $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$.
Their extended regressor adds an offset column $\Gamma$, which adds the
direction of the transition $f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}$ itself to the
protected range~\cite[Eqs.~(17), (18)]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We omit it, for two reasons.
To first order, the estimate of $\beta$ depends on the data only through
their projection onto the range of the stacked Jacobian (below
\eqref{eq:obc_eps}).
The offset would therefore constrain directions that do not move the estimate.
A parameter derivative also does not change when the origin of the normalised
state moves, whereas the offset does, so the protected directions would depend
on the centring $\mu_x$ of \eqref{eq:aug_norm}.
The sensitivity is
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n})
 =\left.\frac{\partial f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta),\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n}\bigr)}
   {\partial\beta}\right|_{\beta=\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}},
\label{eq:obc_sens}
\end{equation}

\item Choice: $\Phi$ is evaluated on fixed one-step tuples $i\in\mathcal I$ from the training data: measured positions, finite difference to get the velocities, added states $\bar x=0$
\item The network's physical rows are fitted by the stacked sensitivity in least squares; the remainder is orthogonal to its range:
\end{outline}

We evaluate $\Phi$ on a fixed reference set, which Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ allow
for the construction of the coefficient~\cite[Sec.~3.2]{gyorok2026obc}.
Only the positions of the baseline state are measured.
Each tuple therefore holds the positions $q_i=P^{-\top}q_{\mathrm{act},i}$ of a
training sample, velocities from a fourth-order central difference of $q$
within the record, $\bar x_i=0$ and the recorded input.
The tuples cover every sample at which this difference exists, both at the
sample and at the next sample.
\revnote{The current results also use the last such sample of each record,
which has no successor. The final runs will use the tuples as described here.}
Each tuple is one step at the recorded input, so the controller does not enter.
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ simulate the baseline when the states are not
measured~\cite[Sec.~3]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We build the tuples from the data instead, as their later construction
evaluates its regressor at the measured data~\cite[Eq.~(5)]{gyorok2026obc}.
The tuples then follow the trajectories reconstructed from the measured
positions of the system, where the omitted dynamics act, rather than those of
the baseline at $\theta^0_{\mathrm{base}}$.
The same reconstruction gives the next states that define the discrepancy
\eqref{eq:obc_eps} below.
The tuples are built once, before training, and depend neither on the trained
parameters nor on the encoder.

Adapting the coefficient of Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ to these
tuples~\cite[Eqs.~(8), (11), Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}, we fit the network's
physical rows by the stacked sensitivity in the least-squares sense.
As they assume of their regressor~\cite[Assumption~1]{gyorok2026obc}, the
stack has full column rank:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})
 &=\col\bigl(\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i,u^{\mathrm n}_i)\bigr)_{i\in\mathcal I},\\
 \boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})
 &=\col\bigl(f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\col(\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i,0),u^{\mathrm n}_i)\bigr)_{i\in\mathcal I},\\
 \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})
 &=\boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})^{+}\,\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_coef}
\end{equation}
so that the corrected stack is orthogonal to the sensitivity:
\begin{equation*}
 \boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})^{\top}\bigl(\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})
 -\boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})\,\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})\bigr)=0.
\end{equation*}

\item Choice: the correction is subtracted from the physical rows at every step; the added-state rows are not corrected:
\end{outline}
\begin{wip}
where $\mathcal I$ is the set of tuples, $N=|\mathcal I|$,
$\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i\in\R^6$ and $u^{\mathrm n}_i\in\R^3$ are the state and

$\boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})\in\R^{6N\times10}$,
$\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})\in\R^{6N}$,

As in~\cite[Eqs.~(9), (10), (13)]{gyorok2026obc}, the coefficient is a function of
the network parameters, and every prediction subtracts the sensitivity times
the coefficient.
We subtract it from the physical rows at every step of the augmented model, at
the state and input of that step.
The added-state rows are not corrected, because the baseline has no
added-state update to be orthogonal to.
The correction is

\begin{wip}
where
$f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{obc}}:\R^{n_{\mathrm{aug}}}\times\R^6\times\R^3\to\R^6$.
Differentiating $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})$ gives the

We set $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$ to the current $\beta$ at each epoch boundary,
rebuild $\boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})$ there and hold it for the epoch.
No gradient therefore reaches $\beta$ through $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$.
Each epoch thus minimises a fixed objective, while the linearisation point
follows the estimate between epochs.
For a nonlinear baseline, Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ update this point at every
evaluation of the objective or fix it at the nominal
parameters~\cite[Sec.~4]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
Our interval adapts the per-epoch recomputation they allow for the state
estimates in their linear case~\cite[Remark~2]{gyorok2025l4dc}.

Orthogonality on the tuples makes the estimate of $\beta$ unique to first
order, but not yet equal to the true combinations.
That depends on what the baseline misses in the data.
Let $\beta^\star\in\R^{10}$ be the coordinates of the true combinations.
On the tuples, the true one-step discrepancy is
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon
 =\boldsymbol{\tilde x}^{\mathrm n}_{+}-\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta^\star),
\label{eq:obc_eps}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
where
$\boldsymbol{\tilde x}^{\mathrm n}_{+}=\col(\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_{i+1})_{i\in\mathcal I}$
holds the next state of each tuple,
$\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta)=\col\bigl(f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta),\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i,u^{\mathrm n}_i)\bigr)_{i\in\mathcal I}$
and $\varepsilon\in\R^{6N}$.
Since the tuple velocities are differenced positions, $\varepsilon$ also holds
the error of this reconstruction.
We write $\boldsymbol\Phi$ for $\boldsymbol\Phi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}})$.
To first order, the one-step error of the corrected model on the tuples is
then minimised by $\beta=\beta^\star+\boldsymbol\Phi^{+}\varepsilon$ for every
$\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$.
This minimiser equals $\beta^\star$ if and only if
$\boldsymbol\Phi^\top\varepsilon=0$.
We adapt this from the recovery theorem of Gy\"or\"ok et al., which in
addition assumes that the model reproduces the noise-free system dynamics on
the training data
exactly~\cite[Cond.~4, Assumption~6, Thm.~7, Eq.~(21)]{gyorok2026obc}.
Without the correction, the minimiser is
$\beta^\star+\boldsymbol\Phi^{+}(\varepsilon-\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}})$,
which moves with the network.
The condition $\boldsymbol\Phi^\top\varepsilon=0$ depends on the excitation.
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ note that an input can be designed to meet it when the
structure of the omitted terms is known~\cite[Remark~5]{gyorok2026obc}.
The result concerns the one-step error on the tuples at $\bar x=0$, whereas
\eqref{eq:obc_problem} minimises a multistep closed-loop output error.
It is therefore a surrogate, not a statement on the trained model.

\end{wip}

After the best Adam checkpoint is restored, the basis is rebuilt at its $\beta$.
The polish then changes every entry of $\vartheta$ with this basis, under the
acceptance rule of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop}.
The polish is compared with the validation error of the model with the rebuilt
basis.
\revnote{The current results compare the polish with the validation error
measured before the basis is rebuilt. The final runs will measure it after the
rebuild, as described here.}
\revnote{The current results also reject the polish when the error of the ten
combinations against the true parameters of the simulated system rises by more
than 0.1\,\%. The final runs will accept the polish on the validation error
alone, as described in Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop}.}

We call $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ the model $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ with
the correction \eqref{eq:obc_corr}, identified by \eqref{eq:obc_problem}.
Its input is the actuator force $\uact$ and its output the actuator positions
$\qact$.
It starts as $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ does, with the first basis built at
$\beta=0$.
Section~\ref{sec:results} compares $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ with
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ on the ten combinations and on accuracy.
