\kw{Joint estimation} fits the \kw{physical parameters} $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ and \kw{ANN
weights} $\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$ together. The \kw{ANN} writes
$f_{\mathrm{aug}}$ into the six \kw{physical rows} and $g_{\mathrm{aug}}$ into the

to realise the same \kw{one-step map} \cite[Ex.~2]{gyorok2026obc},
\cite[Ex.~1]{gyorok2025l4dc}. The \kw{physical parameter directions} are the columns
of the \kw{transition sensitivity} \cite[Eq.~(15)]{gyorok2025l4dc}
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde x,u)
 =\left.\frac{\partial f_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\tilde x,u)}
   {\partial\theta_{\mathrm{base}}}\right|_{\theta_{\mathrm{base}}=\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}}}
   \in\R^{6\times10},
\label{eq:obc_sens}
\end{equation}
at the \kw{expansion point} $\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$. A \kw{learned write} can cancel
a \kw{physical displacement} $\delta\theta$ to first order:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &f_{\mathrm{base}}(\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+\delta\theta,\tilde x,u)
 +\bigl[f_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x,u)
 -\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde x,u)\,\delta\theta\bigr]\\
 &\quad\approx f_{\mathrm{base}}(\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\tilde x,u)
 +f_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x,u).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_negation}
\end{equation}
The data cannot attribute this direction to either component. We call this
\kw{negation} and aim for a \kw{split} whose \kw{physical estimate} is not selected by the
\kw{initial parameter pair}.

For the gantry, differentiating \eqref{eq:eom} with respect to its $j$-th
\kw{identifiable combination} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\ddot q}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}}
 =-M(Y)^{-1}\Bigl[\frac{\partial M(Y)}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}}\,\ddot q
 +\frac{\partial C}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}}\,\dot q
 +\frac{\partial K}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}}\,q\Bigr].
\label{eq:obc_gantry_sens}
\end{equation}
The \kw{RK4 integrator} propagates this \kw{acceleration sensitivity} into the \kw{normalised
one-step transition}. The \kw{mass combinations} act through $\ddot q$, the \kw{damping
combinations} through $\dot q$, and the \kw{stiffness combination} through $q$.
Hence, a \kw{negating write} acts like an added mass, damping, or stiffness force.
The factor $M(Y)^{-1}$ makes these directions depend on the \kw{payload position}.

The fourteen \kw{raw parameters} contain four \kw{structural null directions}, whereas
the ten \kw{combinations} $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:params}
remove that ambiguity. \kw{OBC} therefore protects the ten \kw{combination directions}.
Recovery of their true values requires the additional condition in
Section~\ref{sec:obc_scope}.

\subsection{Orthogonal-by-Construction for the Gantry}\label{sec:obc_map}
For an \kw{input-output baseline} linear in its parameters, Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ fit
the stacked \kw{ANN output} to the \kw{baseline regressor} and subtract that fit:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}&=(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}\Phi^\top F^{\mathrm{ANN}}_{\theta_a},\\
 \widetilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}_{\theta_a}&=F^{\mathrm{ANN}}_{\theta_a}-\Phi\,\theta_{\mathrm{aux}},\\
 0&=\Phi^\top\widetilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}_{\theta_a},\\
 \hat y_k&=\phi(x_k)\theta_b+f^{\mathrm{ANN}}_{\theta_a}(x_k)-\phi(x_k)\,\theta_{\mathrm{aux}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_gyorok}
\end{equation}
\cite[Eqs.~(8), (10), (11), (13)]{gyorok2026obc}.
The \kw{corrected stack} is orthogonal to the \kw{baseline range} without the \kw{penalty
weight} required by \cite{gyorok2025l4dc}.

We apply \eqref{eq:obc_gyorok} to the \kw{physical rows} of the \kw{normalised RK4 state
transition}. Its \kw{regressor} is \eqref{eq:obc_sens}, because
$f_{\mathrm{base}}$ is rational in $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ through
$M(Y)^{-1}$ and has no exact \kw{linear-in-parameters form}.
Unlike \cite[Eq.~(18)]{gyorok2025l4dc}, the \kw{affine offset} of this expansion is
not protected, because it multiplies no \kw{estimated parameter}.

Only \kw{positions} are measured, while an \kw{open-loop baseline reconstruction} drifts
on the \kw{free axes} $X$ and $Y$. We therefore build fixed \kw{reference tuples} from
every valid \kw{training sample}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 q_i&=P^{-\top}y_i,\qquad
 \dot q_i=\frac{-q_{i+2}+8q_{i+1}-8q_{i-1}+q_{i-2}}{12\,T_s},\\
 \tilde x_i&=\col(q_i,\dot q_i),\qquad \bar x_i=0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_tuples}
\end{equation}
where $u_i=P\,u_{\mathrm{act},i}$ and $i=1,\dots,N$. The implementation stores the recorded
\kw{actuator force} $u_{\mathrm{act},i}$ and applies $P$ inside the \kw{physical block}. The \kw{stencil}
does not cross \kw{record boundaries}. Each \kw{tuple} uses its measured \kw{scheduling value}
$Y_i$, and the \kw{recorded input} already contains the \kw{feedback action}. The \kw{tuples}
are normalised before the following \kw{stacks} are evaluated. A fixed \kw{auxiliary
evaluation set} is permitted by \cite{gyorok2026obc}.

On these \kw{tuples}, the construction \eqref{eq:obc_gyorok} becomes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde X,U)
 &=\col\bigl(\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde x_1,u_1),\dots,
   \Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde x_N,u_N)\bigr)\in\R^{6N\times10},\\
 F_{\mathrm{aug}}
 &=\col\bigl(f_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x_1,u_1),\dots,
   f_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x_N,u_N)\bigr),\\
 \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}&=\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde X,U)^{+}F_{\mathrm{aug}},\\
 0&=\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde X,U)^\top
   \bigl(F_{\mathrm{aug}}-\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde X,U)\,\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}\bigr),\\
 0&=\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde X,U)^\top\,
   \frac{\partial\bigl(F_{\mathrm{aug}}-\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde X,U)\,
   \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}\bigr)}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_ours}
\end{equation}
with $\hat x_i=\col(\tilde x_i,\bar x_i)$.
The \kw{protected range} contains every first-order change of the stacked \kw{physical
transition} generated by $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$. \kw{Full column rank} is required
and monitored at every rebuild; the thesis-data \kw{rank} is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. The fourth line of \eqref{eq:obc_ours} is
\cite[Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}. Differentiating that \kw{stacked identity} gives the
last line because the \kw{basis} is fixed with respect to
$\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$.

Fitting the \kw{coefficient} on the \kw{rollout} itself would make the \kw{correction} depend
on later \kw{rollout states}. We instead compute $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ once per
\kw{objective} from the fixed \kw{tuples} and differentiate through this solve. The
\kw{sensitivity} and $\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ are detached within an \kw{epoch} and
rebuilt between \kw{epochs} at the current $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ (D-192).
The \kw{corrected augmented model} is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \begin{bmatrix}\tilde x_{k+1}\\ \bar x_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}
 &=\begin{bmatrix}f_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\tilde x_k,u_k)\\ 0\end{bmatrix}\\
 &\quad+\begin{bmatrix}f_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x_k,u_k)
   -\Phi_{\bar\theta}(\tilde x_k,u_k)\,\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}\\
   g_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\hat x_k,u_k)\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_model}
\end{equation}
At \kw{rollout point} $(\tilde x_k,u_k)$, the \kw{correction} uses the frozen \kw{expansion
point} and the current \kw{objective}'s \kw{coefficient}, as in
\cite[Eq.~(13)]{gyorok2026obc}. A \kw{hand-written forward sensitivity} propagates
the required \kw{directional derivative} through \kw{RK4} (appendix). The zero \kw{baseline
rows} leave $g_{\mathrm{aug}}$ uncorrected.

\subsection{Scope and Condition for True Parameter Recovery}\label{sec:obc_scope}
The \kw{guarantee} is local in $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ and holds for the \kw{one-step
stack} over the chosen \kw{tuples}. It uses the \kw{Euclidean inner product}
$\langle a,b\rangle=a^\top b$ in \kw{normalised state coordinates}, so the \kw{state
normalisation} defines its \kw{metric}. It is aggregate rather than pointwise and
does not establish \kw{global uniqueness}. The \kw{tuples} lie on $\bar x=0$, so the
\kw{coefficient} does not see directions that act only through nonzero \kw{added states}.
The \kw{added-state rows} are never corrected, and $g_{\mathrm{aug}}$ can affect the
\kw{physical rows} one step later through $f_{\mathrm{aug}}$. The \kw{rollout correction}
depends only on $(\tilde x_k,u_k)$, and there is no \kw{stacked guarantee} away from
the $\bar x=0$ \kw{slice}. Consequently, the construction does not imply
\kw{trajectory-output orthogonality}. The \kw{consistency} and \kw{zero-covariance results}
of \cite[Thms.~16, 18]{gyorok2026obc} do not transfer.

\kw{Algebraic non-overlap} does not imply the \kw{true split}.
Let $\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}}$ be the \kw{true parameters} and $\Delta^*$ the \kw{true
one-step discrepancy} on the \kw{tuples}, in the same \kw{normalised coordinates},
\begin{equation}
 \Delta^*=\col\bigl(\tilde x_{i+1}-f_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}},
 \tilde x_i,u_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^{N},
\label{eq:obc_delta}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde x_{i+1}$ is the \kw{recorded successor}, built with the same \kw{stencil}.
Suppose the data are noise-free, $f_{\mathrm{base}}$ is affine in
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ near $\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}}$, the \kw{stack} $\Phi$ of
\eqref{eq:obc_ours} is evaluated at $\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}}$, and the
parameters minimise the \kw{one-step least-squares error} on the \kw{tuples}.
The \kw{corrected learned write} then lies in the \kw{orthogonal complement} of
$\ran\Phi$, and the \kw{parameter error} is
\begin{equation}
 \hat\theta_{\mathrm{base}}-\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}}=\Phi^{+}\Delta^*,
 \qquad
 \hat\theta_{\mathrm{base}}=\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}}
 \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
 \Phi^\top\Delta^*=0,
\label{eq:obc_bias}
\end{equation}
which is the state-level counterpart of \cite[Cond.~4, Eq.~(21)]{gyorok2026obc}.
The \kw{training objective} is a \kw{closed-loop multistep output error}, so
\eqref{eq:obc_bias} is a \kw{one-step surrogate}, not the \kw{bias} of the \kw{trained model}.
For the gantry equations, no nonzero \kw{missing generalized force} is orthogonal to
the \kw{sensitivity} at a single \kw{tuple}. The contributions must therefore cancel
over the \kw{reference stack}. Such \kw{aggregate cancellation} requires \kw{designed
excitation} \cite[Remark~5]{gyorok2026obc}.

Two \kw{overlap measures} follow from the same \kw{inner product}.
For a \kw{stacked vector} $v$ over the \kw{tuples}, with $\Phi$ the \kw{stack} at
$\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ and
$F_{\mathrm{base}}=\col\bigl(f_{\mathrm{base}}(\bar\theta_{\mathrm{base}},
\tilde x_i,u_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^{N}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \cos\angle\bigl(v,\ran\Phi\bigr)
 &=\frac{\norm{\Phi\Phi^{+}v}}{\norm{v}},\\
 \cos\angle\bigl(v,F_{\mathrm{base}}\bigr)
 &=\frac{|\langle F_{\mathrm{base}},v\rangle|}
   {\norm{F_{\mathrm{base}}}\,\norm{v}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_overlap}
\end{equation}
Applied to $\Delta^*$, the first \kw{measure} is zero exactly when the \kw{recovery
condition} holds.
It needs $\theta^*_{\mathrm{base}}$, so it is available in \kw{simulation} only.
Applied to the \kw{corrected learned write} on held-out \kw{tuples}, the first \kw{measure}
shows how far the \kw{guarantee} extends beyond the \kw{reference set}.
The second measures the \kw{overlap} with the \kw{baseline output} that the construction
leaves unprotected.

only where the condition holds, whether the \kw{true parameters} are recovered.
