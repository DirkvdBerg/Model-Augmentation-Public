\section{Interpretability by Orthogonal Construction}\label{sec:obc}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Goal from Section~III: an estimate of $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ that is unique under stated conditions; names IV-A and IV-B (style profile)
\item Contribution: the extension Gy\"or\"ok 2026 Sec.~6 leaves open: a state-space baseline without full-state measurement, here nonlinear in its parameters and identified in closed loop (this work)
\end{itemize}
\fi

\begin{wip}
The parameter pair $(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})$
minimising the loss function of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} can be non-unique:
different pairs can give the same fit~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{gyorok2026obc}.
Meaning that the rigid-body dynamics that the baseline parameters 
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ are able to explain from the data, can instead be represented by 
a neural network correction.
To prevent the neural network from correcting what $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ 
can represent we introduce orthogonality by construction~\cite{gyorok2026obc}.
Orthogonality here is defined as the neural network correction to the
physical states having no component in the directions in which a change of
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ is able to change the next state predicted by the baseline.
This gives us a unique estimate of $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ up to first order,
but it does not guarantee recovering the true baseline parameters.
\\
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ have constructed this for input-output baseline models that
are linear in their parameters~\cite{gyorok2026obc}.
We extend this to state-space models nonlinear in their parameters,
specifically for our gantry system.
\end{wip}
\subsection{Orthogonal-by-construction parametrisation}\label{sec:obc_construction}
\ifdraft
\begin{itemize}
\item Gy\"or\"ok's construction as adopted: baseline linear in its parameters, regressor at measured input-output data (past outputs, past and current inputs), stacked over the training data; lower case per sample, upper case stacked (2026 Eqs.~(1), (2), (5))
\item $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ is the least-squares fit of the network output by the regressor; its fitted part is removed and the remainder is orthogonal to $\Phi$ on the training data (2026 Eqs.~(8) to (11), Lemma~3)
\item $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ exists for full column rank of $\Phi$: identifiable baseline, weakly persistently exciting input (2026 Eqs.~(6), (7), Assumption~1)
\item Data-driven: orthogonality holds on the data used, the projection depends on their distribution; any fixed set may be used, the analysis uses the whole training set (2026 Sec.~3.2, text after Eqs.~(12), (13))
\item Uniqueness of $\hat\theta_{\mathrm b}$ shown under Assumption~1 and exact reproduction of the noise-free dynamics on the same training data (2026 Sec.~4.2, Assumption~6)
\end{itemize}
\fi
\begin{outline}
\item Problem: the split between baseline and network is not unique; the network can cancel the baseline (negation)
\item Choice: the orthogonal construction of Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ 
\item Choice: the baseline is not linear in its parameters, so we use its derivative instead: the sensitivity of the one-step map to the ten trained coordinates $\beta$, taken at the current point $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$:   % CHANGED
\end{outline}
\begin{wip}
For a baseline linear in its parameters,
$\hat y_k=\varphi(x_k)\theta_{\mathrm b}$,
changing $\theta_{\mathrm b}$ changes the model output $\hat y_k$
by a linear combination of the columns of the
regressor $\varphi(x_k)$~\cite[Eqs.~(2), (39)]{gyorok2026obc}.
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ subtract from the neural network output
its least-squares fit by the stacked
regressor~\cite[Eqs.~(8), (9), (11), Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \hat Y&=\Phi\theta_{\mathrm b}+\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}},\\
 \tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}&=F^{\mathrm{ANN}}-\Phi\theta_{\mathrm{aux}},\\
 \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}&=\bigl(\Phi^\top\Phi\bigr)^{-1}\Phi^\top F^{\mathrm{ANN}},\\
 0&=\Phi^\top\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_gyorok}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
where $x_k$ is the vector of past measured outputs and past and current
inputs~\cite[Eq.~(1)]{gyorok2026obc}, $\hat Y$ stacks the
predicted outputs over the training data, $\Phi$ is the stacked
regressor, $F^{\mathrm{ANN}}$ stacks the parallel network
outputs~\cite[Eq.~(5)]{gyorok2026obc} and $\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}$ is the
projected network output~\cite[Eqs.~(9), (10)]{gyorok2026obc}.
\\
The projected network output $\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}$ is
orthogonal to the regressor~\cite[Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}.
Calculating $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ requires
$(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}$, which exists only if $\Phi$ has
full column rank~\cite[Assumption~1]{gyorok2026obc}.
Therefore different values of $\theta_{\mathrm b}$ are unable 
to give the same stacked prediction on the training data. 
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ call an input with this property persistently
exciting~\cite[Eq.~(6), Sec.~4.3]{gyorok2026obc}.
This also requires an identifiable baseline, where different
values of $\theta_{\mathrm b}$ give different functions
$\varphi(\cdot)\theta_{\mathrm b}$.
\end{wip}

\ifdraft

\begin{wip}
Our baseline differs from theirs in three ways.
It is a state-space model that is nonlinear in its parameters, only its
positions are measured, and it is simulated in closed loop together with added
states.
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ treat state-space baselines in their regularisation
paper, where the state transition is stacked over the data and a nonlinear
transition is linearised in its parameters, with its parameter Jacobian as
the regressor~\cite[Secs.~3, 4, Eqs.~(9), (15)]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We use these concepts in the construction of \eqref{eq:obc_gyorok}.

First, our transition is rational in $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ through
$M(Y)^{-1}$.
As the regressor of the physical rows, we therefore take the Jacobian of the
normalised transition at a linearisation point, as Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ do in
their linearisation of a nonlinear baseline~\cite[Sec.~4, Eq.~(15)]{gyorok2025l4dc}:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
 \varphi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n})
 =\left.\frac{\partial f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta),\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n}\bigr)}
   {\partial\beta}\right|_{\beta=\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}},
\label{eq:obc_sens}
\end{equation}
\begin{outline}
\item Choice: only the range of $\Phi$ is protected, without the offset column $\Gamma$ of Gy\"or\"ok et al.
\item Choice: $\Phi$ is evaluated on fixed one-step tuples $i\in\mathcal I$ from the training data: measured positions, finite difference to get the velocities, added states $\bar x=0$
\item The network's physical rows are fitted by the stacked sensitivity in least squares; the remainder is orthogonal to its range:
\end{outline}
\revnote{Orthogonality here means orthogonality to the stacked parameter
sensitivity $\Phi$ of the baseline on the reference tuples: the construction
of Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ (2026, Lemma~3), with their regressor taken as the
parameter Jacobian. To first order, for the one-step error on these tuples,
this is exactly the condition under which the estimate of
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ does not depend on the network, which is how
Section~I defines interpretability. It is not shown for the nonlinear
closed-loop training optimum. It prevents the network from representing what a
change of mass, damping or stiffness could explain, in this first-order sense.
It does not prevent the network from cancelling part of the baseline response
itself (negation as in Kessels). The offset column $\Gamma$ of Gy\"or\"ok et
al.\ (2025, Eq.~(18)) would add at most one protected direction,
$(I-\Pi)F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}$, and remove the component of any network
field along it. To first order it does not change the parameter estimate.
Adding $\Gamma$ would therefore not avoid negation in general. We leave it out;
the current runs are also without $\Gamma$.}
\begin{wip}
where $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}\in\R^{10}$ is the linearisation point and
$\varphi:\R^{10}\times\R^6\times\R^3\to\R^{6\times10}$.
On the gantry, each column of $\varphi$ is the RK4 propagation, scaled by $T_x$,
of the acceleration change
$-M(Y)^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac{\partial M}{\partial\beta_j}\ddot q+\tfrac{\partial C}{\partial\beta_j}\dot q+\tfrac{\partial K}{\partial\beta_j}q\bigr)$
of \eqref{eq:eom} at fixed state and input.
Each column is thus the next-state sensitivity generated by a mass, damping or
stiffness force perturbation, whose effect on the state depends on $Y$.
Their linearised regressor also has an offset column
$\Gamma$~\cite[Eqs.~(17), (18)]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We leave it out, because to first order it does not enter the estimate of
$\beta$ (below \eqref{eq:obc_recovery}) and would only constrain the network.

The coefficient needs full column rank of the stacked
regressor~\cite[Assumption~1]{gyorok2026obc}.
With the fourteen raw parameters, the Jacobian has four structural null
directions, the combinations that the data cannot separate
(Section~\ref{sec:params}).
We therefore differentiate with respect to $\beta$, the coordinates of the ten
identifiable combinations.
The map \eqref{eq:mdelta_map} has a nonsingular Jacobian for
$m_{\Delta,0}\neq0$, so the change of coordinates preserves the rank of the
Jacobian in the ten combinations.
Full column rank of the stack is then an assumption on the excitation, as
in~\cite[Eq.~(6)]{gyorok2026obc}, and on the linearisation point.
It is checked each time the stack is built.

Second, only the positions of the baseline state are measured.
Where states are not measured, Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ simulate the
baseline~\cite[Sec.~3]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
We instead reconstruct the states from the data, as the construction of
\eqref{eq:obc_gyorok} evaluates its regressor at the measured
data~\cite[Eq.~(5)]{gyorok2026obc}:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \tilde x_i&=\col(q_i,\dot q_i),\qquad q_i=P^{-\top}y_i,\\
 \dot q_i&=\frac{-q_{i+2}+8q_{i+1}-8q_{i-1}+q_{i-2}}{12\,T_s},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_tuples}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
where $y_i$ is the measured actuator position of sample $i$, and each tuple
also holds $\bar x_i=0$ and the recorded actuator force $u_i$, all mapped to
the coordinates of \eqref{eq:aug_norm} as $\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i$ and
$u^{\mathrm n}_i$.
The tuples cover every sample at which this difference exists, both at the
sample and at the next sample.
\revnote{The current results also use the last such sample of each record,
which has no successor. The final runs will use the tuples as described here.}
Each tuple is one step at the recorded input, so the controller does not enter.
The tuples follow the trajectories reconstructed from the measured positions
of the system, rather than those of the baseline at
$\theta^0_{\mathrm{base}}$.
They are built once, before training, and depend neither on the trained
parameters nor on the encoder.
On the stacked tuples, the coefficient of \eqref{eq:obc_gyorok} becomes
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi
 &=\col\bigl(\varphi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i,u^{\mathrm n}_i)\bigr)_{i\in\mathcal I},\\
 F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})
 &=\col\bigl(f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\col(\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i,0),u^{\mathrm n}_i)\bigr)_{i\in\mathcal I},\\
 \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})
 &=\bigl(\Phi^\top\Phi\bigr)^{-1}\Phi^\top F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_coef}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
where $\mathcal I$ is the set of tuples, $N=|\mathcal I|$,
$\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i\in\R^6$ and $u^{\mathrm n}_i\in\R^3$ are the state and
input of tuple $i$, $\Phi\in\R^{6N\times10}$ is evaluated at
$\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$,
$F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})\in\R^{6N}$
and $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}\in\R^{10}$.
On the stacked reference tuples, the orthogonality defined at the start of
this section reads, for the projected network output,
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \tilde F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})
 &=F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})-\Phi\,\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}),\\
 0&=\Phi^{\top}\tilde F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_orth}
\end{equation}
\begin{outline}
\item Choice: the correction is subtracted from the physical rows at every step; the added-state rows are not corrected:
\end{outline}
\begin{wip}
which \eqref{eq:obc_coef} satisfies for every $\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$, as in~\cite[Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}.
As there, it holds for the stack on the data used, here the reference tuples at
$\bar x=0$, and not for each tuple separately.
We measure how close a stacked field $d\in\R^{6N}$ is to orthogonal by the
normalised inner product, with the Euclidean inner product on the stacked
tuples in the coordinates of \eqref{eq:aug_norm}:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \cos\angle(a,b)&=\frac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\norm{a}_2\norm{b}_2},\\
 c_j(d)&=\cos\angle(\Phi_j,d),\\
 \rho(d)&=\max_{v\in\ran\Phi\setminus\{0\}}\cos\angle(v,d)
 =\frac{\norm{\Pi d}_2}{\norm{d}_2},\\
 \Pi&=\Phi\bigl(\Phi^\top\Phi\bigr)^{-1}\Phi^\top,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_overlap}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
where $a,b,d\in\R^{6N}$ are nonzero, $\Phi_j$ is column $j$ of $\Phi$,
$j=1,\dots,10$, and $\Pi$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\ran\Phi$, so that
$\Pi F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}=\Phi\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ is the part the
correction removes.
$c_j(d)$ is the signed overlap of $d$ with one parameter direction.
$\rho(d)$ is the largest normalised inner product of $d$ with a nonzero vector
in $\ran\Phi$.
It is zero if and only if every $c_j(d)$ is zero, that is, if $d$ is
orthogonal in the sense of \eqref{eq:obc_orth}.

Third, our model is a state update simulated in closed loop.
As in their prediction~\cite[Eqs.~(9), (13)]{gyorok2026obc}, we subtract the
regressor times the coefficient, at every step and at the state and input of
that step.
Only the physical rows are corrected, because the baseline has no added-state
update:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{obc}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n})
 &=\varphi(\beta_{\mathrm{lin}},\tilde x^{\mathrm n},u^{\mathrm n})\,
  \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}),\\
 \tilde x^{\mathrm n}_{k+1}
 &=f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}+f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}-f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{obc}},\\
 \bar x_{k+1}&=g^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_corr}
\end{equation}
\begin{outline}
\item Choice: the gradient of the training cost $V$ of \eqref{eq:aug_loss} to the network parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$ includes the path through $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})=\boldsymbol\Phi^{+}\boldsymbol f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}(\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})$; with $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ held fixed, each step would give the network the derivative of its uncorrected output   % CHANGED
\item Choice: $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$ is set to the current $\beta$ at each epoch and held fixed within it
\end{outline}
\begin{wip}
where
$f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{obc}}:\R^{n_{\mathrm{aug}}}\times\R^6\times\R^3\to\R^6$,
and the second line takes the arguments of \eqref{eq:aug_loss}, with
$f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{obc}}$ at $(\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_k,\hat u^{\mathrm n}_k)$.
As in~\cite[Eqs.~(9), (10)]{gyorok2026obc}, the coefficient is a function of
the network parameters and is differentiated.
This gives the network, at each state and input, the derivative of the
corrected term.
A detached coefficient would give that of the uncorrected term.

\end{wip}
\begin{outline}
\item The identification problem of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} gets the correction in the physical-row update; trained parameters and cost are unchanged:
\end{outline}
\begin{outline}
\item $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ is solved again at every evaluation of $V$; $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$ is refreshed per epoch
\item The PS stages, optimiser and selection rule are reused; $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ is $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ with the correction
\end{outline}
\begin{wip}
We call $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ the model $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ with
the correction \eqref{eq:obc_corr}.
Its input is the actuator force $\uact$ and its output the actuator positions
$\qact$.
It is identified by \eqref{eq:aug_loss} with this physical-row update.
The trained parameters
$\vartheta=(\beta,\theta_{\mathrm{aug}},\theta_{\mathrm{enc}})$ and the cost
$V$ are unchanged.
$\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ is solved by \eqref{eq:obc_coef} from the current
$\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$ at every evaluation of $V$.
We set $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$ to the current $\beta$ at each epoch boundary,
rebuild $\Phi$ there and hold it for the epoch.
No gradient therefore reaches $\beta$ through $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$.
Each epoch thus minimises a fixed objective, while the linearisation point
follows the estimate between epochs.
For a nonlinear baseline, Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ update this point at every
evaluation of the objective or fix it at the nominal
parameters~\cite[Sec.~4]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
Our interval adapts the per-epoch recomputation they allow for the state
estimates~\cite[Sec.~3, Remark~2]{gyorok2025l4dc}.
After the best checkpoint is restored, the basis is rebuilt at its $\beta$,
and the polish of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} keeps this basis.
The polish is compared with the validation error of the model with the rebuilt
basis.
\revnote{The current results compare the polish with the validation error
measured before the basis is rebuilt. The final runs will measure it after the
rebuild, as described here.}
\revnote{The current results also reject the polish when the error of the ten
combinations against the true parameters of the simulated system rises by more
than 0.1\,\%. The final runs will accept the polish on the validation error
alone, as described in Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop}.}
Section~\ref{sec:results} compares $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ with
$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{PS}}$ on the ten combinations and on accuracy.

Orthogonality on the tuples makes the estimate of $\beta$ unique to first
order, but not yet equal to the true combinations.
That depends on what the baseline misses in the data.
Let $\beta^\star\in\R^{10}$ be the coordinates of the true combinations.
On the tuples, the true one-step discrepancy is
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
 \Xi
 =\tilde X^{\mathrm n}_{+}-F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta^\star),
\label{eq:obc_eps}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
where
$\tilde X^{\mathrm n}_{+}=\col(\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_{i+1})_{i\in\mathcal I}$
holds the next state of each tuple,
$F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta)=\col\bigl(f^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{base}}(\theta_{\mathrm{base}}(\beta),\tilde x^{\mathrm n}_i,u^{\mathrm n}_i)\bigr)_{i\in\mathcal I}$
and $\Xi\in\R^{6N}$.
$\Xi$ plays the role of the unmodelled terms of Gy\"or\"ok
et al.~\cite[Eqs.~(14), (15)]{gyorok2026obc}.
Since the tuple velocities are differenced positions, $\Xi$ also holds the
error of this reconstruction and the measurement noise.
With \eqref{eq:obc_orth}, the one-step error on the tuples is minimised over
$\beta$, to first order, by the first line, and without the correction by the
second line:
\end{wip}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \beta&=\beta^\star+\bigl(\Phi^\top\Phi\bigr)^{-1}\Phi^\top\Xi,\\
 \beta&=\beta^\star+\bigl(\Phi^\top\Phi\bigr)^{-1}\Phi^\top\bigl(\Xi-F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:obc_recovery}
\end{equation}
\begin{wip}
With the correction, the minimiser is the same for every
$\theta_{\mathrm{aug}}$.
Without it, the minimiser depends on the network through
$\Phi^\top F^{\mathrm n}_{\mathrm{aug}}$.
The first line equals $\beta^\star$ if and only if $\Phi^\top\Xi=0$.
We adapt this from the recovery theorem of Gy\"or\"ok et al., which in
addition assumes that the model reproduces the noise-free system dynamics on
the training data
exactly~\cite[Cond.~4, Assumption~6, Thm.~7, Eq.~(21)]{gyorok2026obc}.
The correction thus prevents the network from representing what a change of
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ could explain, in this first-order sense.
It does not prevent a network term that cancels part of the baseline response
itself, negation in the sense of Kessels~\cite[Sec.~5.3.1.3]{kessels2025ai}.
The condition $\Phi^\top\Xi=0$ depends on the excitation.
Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ note that an input can be designed to make the regressor
orthogonal to suspected unmodelled terms when their structure is
known~\cite[Remark~5]{gyorok2026obc}.
Such a design does not address the reconstruction error and noise in $\Xi$.
The result concerns the one-step error on the tuples at $\bar x=0$, whereas
\eqref{eq:aug_loss} minimises a multistep closed-loop output error.
It is therefore a surrogate, not a statement on the trained model.

\end{wip}
