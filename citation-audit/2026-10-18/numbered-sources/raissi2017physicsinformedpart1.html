00020 [PDF p.1]                                          Abstract


00021 [PDF p.1]                                              We introduce physics informed neural networks – neural networks that


00022 [PDF p.1]                                          are trained to solve supervised learning tasks while respecting any given law


00023 [PDF p.1]                                          of physics described by general nonlinear partial differential equations. In


00024 [PDF p.1]                                          this two part treatise, we present our developments in the context of solving


00025 [PDF p.1]                                          two main classes of problems: data-driven solution and data-driven discovery


00026 [PDF p.1]                                          of partial differential equations. Depending on the nature and arrangement


00027 [PDF p.1]                                          of the available data, we devise two distinct classes of algorithms, namely


00028 [PDF p.1]                                          continuous time and discrete time models. The resulting neural networks


00029 [PDF p.1]                                          form a new class of data-efficient universal function approximators that nat-


00030 [PDF p.1]                                          urally encode any underlying physical laws as prior information. In this first


00031 [PDF p.1]                                          part, we demonstrate how these networks can be used to infer solutions to


00032 [PDF p.1]                                          partial differential equations, and obtain physics-informed surrogate models


00033 [PDF p.1]                                          that are fully differentiable with respect to all input coordinates and free


00034 [PDF p.1]                                          parameters.

00047 [PDF p.2] However, more often than not, in the course of analyzing complex physical,


00048 [PDF p.2] biological or engineering systems, the cost of data acquisition is prohibitive,


00049 [PDF p.2] and we are inevitably faced with the challenge of drawing conclusions and


00050 [PDF p.2] making decisions under partial information. In this small data regime, the


00051 [PDF p.2] vast majority of state-of-the art machine learning techniques (e.g., deep/-


00052 [PDF p.2] convolutional/recurrent neural networks) are lacking robustness and fail to


00053 [PDF p.2] provide any guarantees of convergence.


00054 [PDF p.2] 


00055 [PDF p.2]     At first sight, the task of training a deep learning algorithm to accurately


00056 [PDF p.2] identify a nonlinear map from a few – potentially very high-dimensional –


00057 [PDF p.2] input and output data pairs seems at best naive. Coming to our rescue, for


00058 [PDF p.2] many cases pertaining to the modeling of physical and biological systems,


00059 [PDF p.2] there a exist a vast amount of prior knowledge that is currently not being


00060 [PDF p.2] utilized in modern machine learning practice. Let it be the principled physical


00061 [PDF p.2] laws that govern the time-dependent dynamics of a system, or some empirical


00062 [PDF p.2] validated rules or other domain expertise, this prior information can act as


00063 [PDF p.2] a regularization agent that constrains the space of admissible solutions to a


00064 [PDF p.2] manageable size (for e.g., in incompressible fluid dynamics problems by dis-


00065 [PDF p.2] carding any non realistic flow solutions that violate the conservation of mass


00066 [PDF p.2] principle). In return, encoding such structured information into a learning


00067 [PDF p.2] algorithm results in amplifying the information content of the data that the


00068 [PDF p.2] algorithm sees, enabling it to quickly steer itself towards the right solution


00069 [PDF p.2] and generalize well even when only a few training examples are available.


00070 [PDF p.2] 


00071 [PDF p.2]     The first glimpses of promise for exploiting structured prior information


00072 [PDF p.2] to construct data-efficient and physics-informed learning machines have al-


00073 [PDF p.2] ready been showcased in the recent studies of [5, 6, 7]. There, the authors


00074 [PDF p.2] employed Gaussian process regression [8] to devise functional representations


00075 [PDF p.2] that are tailored to a given linear operator, and were able to accurately infer


00076 [PDF p.2] solutions and provide uncertainty estimates for several prototype problems


00077 [PDF p.2] in mathematical physics. Extensions to nonlinear problems were proposed


00078 [PDF p.2] in subsequent studies by Raissi et. al. [9, 10] in the context of both in-


00079 [PDF p.2] ference and systems identification. Despite the flexibility and mathematical


00080 [PDF p.2] elegance of Gaussian processes in encoding prior information, the treatment


00081 [PDF p.2] of nonlinear problems introduces two important limitations. First, in [9, 10]


00082 [PDF p.2] the authors had to locally linearize any nonlinear terms in time, thus limit-


00083 [PDF p.2] ing the applicability of the proposed methods to discrete-time domains and


00084 [PDF p.2] compromising the accuracy of their predictions in strongly nonlinear regimes.

00091 [PDF p.3] lems [11].

00093 [PDF p.3] 1.1. Problem setup and summary of contributions


00094 [PDF p.3]     In this work we take a different approach by employing deep neural net-


00095 [PDF p.3] works and leverage their well known capability as universal function ap-


00096 [PDF p.3] proximators [12]. In this setting, we can directly tackle nonlinear problems


00097 [PDF p.3] without the need for committing to any prior assumptions, linearization, or


00098 [PDF p.3] local time-stepping. We exploit recent developments in automatic differen-


00099 [PDF p.3] tiation [13] – one of the most useful but perhaps underused techniques in


00100 [PDF p.3] scientific computing – to differentiate neural networks with respect to their


00101 [PDF p.3] input coordinates and model parameters to obtain physics informed neural


00102 [PDF p.3] networks. Such neural networks are constrained to respect any symmetry,


00103 [PDF p.3] invariance, or conservation principles originating from the physical laws that


00104 [PDF p.3] govern the observed data, as modeled by general time-dependent and non-


00105 [PDF p.3] linear partial differential equations. This simple yet powerful construction


00106 [PDF p.3] allows us to tackle a wide range of problems in computational science and


00107 [PDF p.3] introduces a potentially disruptive technology leading to the development


00108 [PDF p.3] of new data-efficient and physics-informed learning machines, new classes of


00109 [PDF p.3] numerical solvers for partial differential equations, as well as new data-driven


00110 [PDF p.3] approaches for model inversion and systems identification.


00111 [PDF p.3]

00115 [PDF p.3] presented in the context of two main problem classes: data-driven solution


00116 [PDF p.3] and data-driven discovery of partial differential equations. To this end, let
