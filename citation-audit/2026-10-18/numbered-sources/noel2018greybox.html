00027 [PDF p.1]                                                     The present paper deals with the identification of nonlinear mechanical vibrations. A grey-box, or semi-


00028 [PDF p.1]                                                     physical, nonlinear state-space representation is introduced, expressing the nonlinear basis functions


00029 [PDF p.1]                                                     using a limited number of measured output variables. This representation assumes that the observed


00030 [PDF p.1]                                                     nonlinearities are localised in physical space, which is a generic case in mechanics. A two-step identification

00033 [PDF p.1]                                                     the behaviour of a single-input, single-output (SISO) nonlinear mechanical system and to a single-input,


00034 [PDF p.1]                                                     multiple-output (SIMO) geometrically nonlinear beam structure.

00223 [PDF p.5] 


00224 [PDF p.5]                   The simplified structure of the grey-box state-space model proposed in Eqs. (6) lends itself to a


00225 [PDF p.5]                   two-step identification procedure, whereas four steps including two nonlinear optimisation searches


00226 [PDF p.5]                   are needed in classical black-box state-space identification [Paduart et al. (2010)]. First, initial esti-


00227 [PDF p.5]                   mates of the A, B, C and D matrices are obtained using nonlinear subspace identification. Second,


00228 [PDF p.5]                   the quality of the initial subspace parameter estimates is improved using nonlinear optimisation.


00229 [PDF p.5]                   The complete procedure is carried out in the frequency domain, opening the possibility to apply


00230 [PDF p.5]                   user-defined weighting functions in specific frequency intervals. We also opt for a discrete-time


00231 [PDF p.5]                   formulation to ensure a proper conditioning of the identification algorithm and to permit effective


00232 [PDF p.5]                   model-based time simulations.

00573 [PDF p.11]                   in Ref. [Marchesiello & Garibaldi (2008)], the generated nonlinear coefficients are frequency-


00574 [PDF p.11]                   dependent and possess a spurious imaginary part. They are obtained as the ratios of elements


00575 [PDF p.11]                   of the transfer function matrix associated with the model in Eqs. (7). This matrix is formed in


00576 [PDF p.11]                   continuous time, assuming zero-order-hold equivalence between the discrete- and continuous-time


00577 [PDF p.11]                   domains.
