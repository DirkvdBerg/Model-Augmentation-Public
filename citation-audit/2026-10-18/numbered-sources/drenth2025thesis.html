00033 [PDF p.2] Abstract


00034 [PDF p.2] This thesis addresses the surrogate modelling of nonlinear systems by Linear Parameter Varying (LPV)


00035 [PDF p.2] models with Linear Fractional Representation (LFR) and rational scheduling dependency. A novel


00036 [PDF p.2] parameterization is developed to constrain LPV-LFR models to be well-posed. The parameterization


00037 [PDF p.2] is used for gradient-based joint estimation of LPV models and scheduling maps under complex noise


00038 [PDF p.2] scenarios. Furthermore, the reliability of the estimation approach is improved by the introduction of


00039 [PDF p.2] an initialization and bootstrapping approached based on the best linear approximation and the use of


00040 [PDF p.2] unconstrained multiple shooting. These approaches are validated by Monte Carlo studies on datasets


00041 [PDF p.2] from two different non-linear models. Finally, a model augmentation framework is introduced to allow the


00042 [PDF p.2] incorporation of prior system knowledge into the identification process. Together, the findings in this


00043 [PDF p.2] thesis provide a reliable and computationally efficient method for the joint-estimation of parsimonious


00044 [PDF p.2] LPV models with rational scheduling dependency through the use of a novel well-posed parameterization.

00104 [PDF p.4] 1 | Introduction


00105 [PDF p.4] As the demands on performance of systems in engineering grow, the ubiquitous Linear Time-Invariant


00106 [PDF p.4] (LTI) framework shows its limits. High performant, model-based controllers rely, as the name implies, on


00107 [PDF p.4] accurate models of systems to be controlled. Modelling the inherently nonlinear (NL) behaviour present


00108 [PDF p.4] in many physical systems, via LTI methods, results in inaccurate models. When the aim is to push


00109 [PDF p.4] performance to its limits, these inaccurate models can result in limitations on the achievable performance


00110 [PDF p.4] under control. These limitations are becoming more apparent as demands on performance of controlled


00111 [PDF p.4] systems increase, requiring more accurate models.


00112 [PDF p.4] 


00113 [PDF p.4] As an alternative to the LTI framework we consider the Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) framework.


00114 [PDF p.4] LPV models retain the linear structure of LTI models, but with the notable difference that its parameters


00115 [PDF p.4] can now vary as a function of a scheduling variable. Through the variation of the scheduling variable,


00116 [PDF p.4] LPV models are capable of representing Time-Varying (TV) as well as NL behaviour, with a simple


00117 [PDF p.4] linear structure. This structure provides a good trade-off between the simple, but restricted modelling


00118 [PDF p.4] capabilities of the LTI framework and more complex NL or TV methods. Additionally, this linear structure


00119 [PDF p.4] enables the application of sophisticated controller synthesis tools within the LPV framework. (see, e.g.,


00120 [PDF p.4] [1]). Notably, similar to the LTI case, these controllers can be synthesized with performance guarantees.


00121 [PDF p.4] The LPV framework also enjoys the support of various software toolboxes, implementing modeling and


00122 [PDF p.4] control tools ([2], [3]).


00123 [PDF p.4] 


00124 [PDF p.4] The subject of LPV data-driven modelling, i.e., system identification is still an actively researched topic.


00125 [PDF p.4] Currently, the majority of LPV identification research has focused on methods with (partial) prior


00126 [PDF p.4] knowledge of the scheduling. Considered are internally scheduled models, where direct measurement


00127 [PDF p.4] of the scheduling variable (see Figure 1.1a) is available and externally scheduled models, where the


00128 [PDF p.4] scheduling map, which maps signals to the scheduling variable, is known and its inputs are measured.


00129 [PDF p.4] (see Figure 1.1b). Within the setting with known scheduling, a wide range of estimation methods exists,


00130 [PDF p.4] often extensions of LTI identification methods. We provide an overview of some notable methods. Using


00131 [PDF p.4] Input-Output (IO) structures, PEM methods similar to the LTI case have been developed for LPV-IO


00132 [PDF p.4] models, including representation of various noise models (AutoRegressive model with eXogenous input


00133 [PDF p.4] (ARX) to Box-Jenkins (BJ)) [4]. As controller synthesis methods typically rely on LPV State-Space (SS)


00134 [PDF p.4] models, signficant research effort has been put into the estimation of LPV-SS models under (partially)


00135 [PDF p.4] known scheduling. Like in the IO case, PEM methods for LPV-SS models have been researched, including


00136 [PDF p.4] identification of plant and noise models [5]. Additionally, significant research on the topic of Subspace


00137 [PDF p.4] Identification for LPV-SS models has been performed ([6], [7]).

00184 [PDF p.5] The above mentioned black-box methods have been shown to provide accurate results in modeling of NL


00185 [PDF p.5] systems, however prior system knowledge, for example based on physical laws through First Principle


00186 [PDF p.5] Modeling (FPM), is left unused. On the other hand, purely FPM based or mechanistic models often perform


00187 [PDF p.5] worse than well-identified black-box models, for example due to unmodelled dynamics. Instead, model


00188 [PDF p.5] augmentation methods for NL systems (sometimes also called hybrid modeling methods in literature) have


00189 [PDF p.5] been proposed [13]. In this framework, mechanistic models are augmented with black-box terms, typically


00190 [PDF p.5] neural networks, to estimate unmodelled behaviour. Often, parameters of the mechanistic part of the


00191 [PDF p.5] model are updated during identification. This provides a number of key benefits: (i) Better initialization


00192 [PDF p.5] as prior knowledge does not have to be learned by black box models; (ii) Improved extrapolation to


00193 [PDF p.5] unseen data as NL black-box models often struggle generalizing to input-state combinations that have


00194 [PDF p.5] not been incorporated in training data, (partially) mechanistic models can improve this behaviour; (iii)


00195 [PDF p.5] Better interpretability of models as, after identification, the mechanistic part of the model retains the


00196 [PDF p.5] predetermined, physics inspired relations. As a result, the updated model parameters can retain physical


00197 [PDF p.5] meaning. To the best of the authors knowledge, there is no prior work found in the literature regarding


00198 [PDF p.5] integration of model augmentation methods into an LPV framework, despite its potential benefits over


00199 [PDF p.5] general NL methods.

00202 [PDF p.5] LPV models are capable of representing NL behaviour through the concept of LPV embedding. In this


00203 [PDF p.5] process, an LPV model capable of representing all solutions of the original NL system is found. To achieve


00204 [PDF p.5] this, a latent variable p (the scheduling variable) is introduced. The NL model (Figure 1.3a) can be


00205 [PDF p.5] reformulated by defining a functional dependency of the plant on p and characterizing p such that in


00206 [PDF p.5] combination with the functional dependency, the reformulation is equivalent to the original NL system

00223 [PDF p.6] to the same values as achievable in the connected scenario, the behaviour of the NL representation is

00226 [PDF p.6] the NL system and the LPV system is typically referred to as overbounding or conservativeness. When the


00227 [PDF p.6] LPV embedding is subsequently used in controller synthesis, the additional behaviour must be addressed,


00228 [PDF p.6] possibly leading to conservativeness in the controller. A major challenge in modelling of LPV systems is


00229 [PDF p.6] finding models that minimize overbounding. At the same time, the total amount of scheduling variables


00230 [PDF p.6] should be kept low, to reduce computational challenges in controller synthesis and keep the amount of


00231 [PDF p.6] model parameters low.


00232 [PDF p.6] 


00233 [PDF p.6] As mentioned, in practice, LPV controller synthesis methods often rely on LPV-SS models, specifically


00234 [PDF p.6] with an affine dependency on the scheduling variables. However, in case the underlying dependency is NL


00235 [PDF p.6] rather than affine, this approach can lead to an increase in the required amount of scheduling variables,


00236 [PDF p.6] resulting in a significant growth in model parameters. Additionally, the resulting models can lead to


00237 [PDF p.6] unnecessary conservatism during controller synthesis. A possible solution to this problem is the use of


00238 [PDF p.6] LPV models with a Linear Fractional Representation (LFR) model structure. This model structure admits


00239 [PDF p.6] rational dependency structures which can reduce the effects of overbounding, yet is still suited for LPV


00240 [PDF p.6] controller synthesis (see e.g., [14] for a comparison). However, so far, little research on the identification of


00241 [PDF p.6] LPV-LFR models has been performed. In the first paper on LPV-LFR identification, [15], an instrumental


00242 [PDF p.6] variable approach to perform parameter estimation of a SISO LPV-LFR models with a single scheduling


00243 [PDF p.6] variable is used. In [16] and [17], LPV-LFR models with OE noise model are considered, models are


00244 [PDF p.6] optimized using nonlinear optimization to minimize a prediction error cost function. In [18] SISO LPV


00245 [PDF p.6] identification with rational dependency on a single scheduling variable is considered. This paper shows


00246 [PDF p.6] that under certain conditions, the scheduling trajectory can be manipulated to identify a model using


00247 [PDF p.6] convex optimization. In [19] a kernelized canonical correlation analysis approach is used to first estimate


00248 [PDF p.6] a state sequence based on input, output and scheduling data. A nonlinear coordinate descent method is


00249 [PDF p.6] then used to minimize a least squares cost of the state and output prediction error. Unfortunately, all


00250 [PDF p.6] of these methods fail to address a major challenge in LPV-LFR identification: ensuring well-posedness


00251 [PDF p.6] of the models during identification. When LPV-LFR models admit rational scheduling dependencies,


00252 [PDF p.6] identification can result in an ill-posed model in terms of algebraic loops without solutions. By assuming


00253 [PDF p.6] affine dependency in LPV-LFR models the problem of ill-posedness can be avoided, however these types


00254 [PDF p.6] of models are equivalent to affine LPV-SS models, preventing potential benefits in the reduction of


00255 [PDF p.6] overbounding of rational LPV-LFR models. In current research, the well-posedness condition is typically


00256 [PDF p.6] (and naively) assumed to be met, however outside of toy examples this makes identification of rational


00257 [PDF p.6] LPV-LFR models infeasible. To the authors knowledge, no prior method that guarantees well-posedness


00258 [PDF p.6] of LPV-LFR models with rational scheduling dependency exists.

00260 [PDF p.6] 1.2 | Research questions


00261 [PDF p.6] Based on the overview given in the previous section, three challenges in the field of LPV system


00262 [PDF p.6] identification have been presented. First, as applicability for controller synthesis hinges on parsimonious


00263 [PDF p.6] models, identifying LPV-LFR with rational scheduling dependency is of great value. However, no reliable


00264 [PDF p.6] method for ensuring well-posedness in LPV-LFR models with rational dependency currently exists. To


00265 [PDF p.6] resolve this, the first key goal of the research will be to answer the following research question:


00266 [PDF p.6] 


00267 [PDF p.6]    ■ How can the well-posedness, during estimation, of LPV-LFR models with rational scheduling depen-


00268 [PDF p.6]       dency be guaranteed?


00269 [PDF p.6] 


00270 [PDF p.6] Secondly, to enable computationally efficient joint-estimation we aim to extend the gradient-based


00271 [PDF p.6] optimization methods in [12] to LPV-LFR models. Currently however, this method can result in


00272 [PDF p.6] inaccurate surrogate models, typically relying on selecting the best from a set of models with different


00273 [PDF p.6] initialization. Without proven reliability, in terms of converging to accurate models, the usefulness of the


00274 [PDF p.6] proposed identification method is extremely limited. Therefore, there is a strong need to improve the


00275 [PDF p.6] reliability of the methodology. To achieve this we aim to answer the following research question:


00276 [PDF p.6] 


00277 [PDF p.6]    ■ How can reliable convergence to accurate surrogate models be achieved using gradient-based algorithms


00278 [PDF p.6]       for efficient estimation of self-scheduled LPV-LFR models?

00406 [PDF p.9] 2.1.1 | The trade-off between affine and rational dependency models


00407 [PDF p.9] In Section 1 we stated that the use of rational dependency LPV-LFR models can reduce overbounding


00408 [PDF p.9] compared to affine dependency models, and subsequently reduce conservatism in controllers. It is important


00409 [PDF p.9] to understand, that in the LPV embedding process, specifically the last step, (see Figure 1.3c) knowledge


00410 [PDF p.9] of the scheduling map, except for its range is discarded. Specifically, any relationship or inherent coupling


00411 [PDF p.9] [22] between scheduling variables is discarded. For example, if multiple scheduling variables are dependent


00412 [PDF p.9] on the same state, treating the scheduling variables independently can lead to combinations of scheduling


00413 [PDF p.9] variables that could not occur in the original NL system. By extending the LPV model structure to allow


00414 [PDF p.9] rational dependency structures, rather than capturing inherent couplings in the scheduling map, of which

00416 [PDF p.9] Crucially, this coupling is not ignored during controller synthesis. Besides reducing the overbounding,


00417 [PDF p.9] moving complexity from the scheduling map to the dependency structure can also reduce the required


00418 [PDF p.9] number of scheduling variables, further improving usability in controller synthesis.

00802 [PDF p.15] To solve this optimization problem, we leverage the optimization approach described in [11] based on


00803 [PDF p.15] JAX [27]. In this gradient-based method, local optimization is performed in two steps. First, a fixed


00804 [PDF p.15] number of iterations is taken using the Adam algorithm [28], followed by a number of iterations using

00810 [PDF p.15] well as self-scheduled LPV-SS models [12], particularly when compared with methods using only Adam.

00814 [PDF p.15] (2.21). This is supported by the flexibility of the AD-based optimization approach, as changes to the cost


00815 [PDF p.15] function or model structure do not require manual recalculation of Jacobians required.

00818 [PDF p.15] timestep, this operation can be numerically and computationally challenging. JAX solves the linear


00819 [PDF p.15] system at each time step, through the use of an LU-decomposition approach. Notably, for additional


00820 [PDF p.15] computational efficiency JAX implements efficient methods to evaluate the gradient using AD over the


00821 [PDF p.15] linear solve operations. Through use of the implicit function theorem, the gradients of linear solves are


00822 [PDF p.15] defined directly at the solution, ignoring unnecessary factorization operations. This approach enables


00823 [PDF p.15] evaluation of LPV-LFR models with rational dependency without increasing evaluation time excessivey.

01005 [PDF p.19] 3 | Multiple-Shooting-Based Identification


01006 [PDF p.19] In this chapter we discuss and experimentally analyze various types of shooting methods in joint-estimation


01007 [PDF p.19] of LPV-LFR models and scheduling maps. First, an overview of existing research on shooting methods


01008 [PDF p.19] and their properties is given. Second, the extensions required to incorporate Unconstrained Multiple


01009 [PDF p.19] Shooting into the identification method proposed in Section 2 are explained. Finally, effectiveness of the


01010 [PDF p.19] unconstrained multiple shooting method in the joint-estimation of LPV-LFR models and scheduling maps


01011 [PDF p.19] is investigated by means of Monte-Carlo analysis on two identification examples.


01012 [PDF p.19] 


01013 [PDF p.19] 3.1 | Introduction


01014 [PDF p.19] In prediction error based system identification, calculation of the cost function requires evaluation of


01015 [PDF p.19] the model and dataset. In cases where (partially) simulated models are used, this requires the forward


01016 [PDF p.19] simulation over the entire length the training record. For example, to evaluate the PEM-cost of LPV-LFR


01017 [PDF p.19] models as proposed in this work, simulation of the state-trajectory is always required, as direct measure-


01018 [PDF p.19] ment of states is assumed unavailable.


01019 [PDF p.19] 


01020 [PDF p.19] Simulating the entire trajectory from start to finish is typically called Single Shooting. In modern system


01021 [PDF p.19] identification the use of single shooting presents two challenges. (i) As shown in [34], for increasing


01022 [PDF p.19] lengths of the training records the cost function becomes more irregular, particularly for unstable or


01023 [PDF p.19] slowly contracting models. As a result, the performance of gradient-based optimization methods is


01024 [PDF p.19] severely negatively affected. (ii) The recurrent evaluation of the parameterized model is not amenable to


01025 [PDF p.19] parallelization, resulting in under utilization of the capabilities of modern hardware.


01026 [PDF p.19] 


01027 [PDF p.19] Various alternative shooting methods, aimed at overcoming the shortcomings of single shooting, exist. In


01028 [PDF p.19] [35] Incremental Single Shooting (ISS) is proposed. In this method, the model is trained on an initially


01029 [PDF p.19] short, increasingly longer subset of the full training record, aiding in stability of the optimization. In


01030 [PDF p.19] Multiple Shooting (MS), originally used for parameter identification in [36], the full training record is


01031 [PDF p.19] instead split into multiple sub records for which identification is performed jointly. By splitting the


01032 [PDF p.19] training data into shorter sub records, large errors in simulation are prevented from building up. However,


01033 [PDF p.19] for accurate simulation, the initial states of each subrecord must now also be estimated. In [36] and [37]


01034 [PDF p.19] each timestep is treated as a different subrecord and the initial state of each subrecord is made equal


01035 [PDF p.19] to the final state of the previous subrecord. Alternatively, in [34], longer subrecords are used. Instead


01036 [PDF p.19] of constraining the first state of each subrecord to be equal to the final state of the previous subrecord,


01037 [PDF p.19] [38] uses soft constraints. This is achieved by introducing ℓ2 -regularization on the difference of final state


01038 [PDF p.19] of the previous subrecord and the initial state of the current subrecord. In [39] it is shown that even


01039 [PDF p.19] if no constraints or regularization on initial and final states of the subrecords is used, unconstrained


01040 [PDF p.19] multiple shooting (UMS) still provides benefits in optimization, particularly for systems that are (locally)


01041 [PDF p.19] non-contractive or when initial model estimates are non-contractive. [40] adapts MS to (i) use overlapping


01042 [PDF p.19] subrecords to increase data-efficiency and (ii) to solve the initial state estimation problem by jointly


01043 [PDF p.19] training a NN-based state-encoder.

01391 [PDF p.26] 4 | Bootstrapping approaches for the identification of LPV models


01392 [PDF p.26] In this chapter, we introduce an initialization and bootstrapping procedure for LPV-LFR models with


01393 [PDF p.26] affine and rational dependency based on the Best Linear Approximation. First, we give an overview of


01394 [PDF p.26] existing initialization and bootstrapping procedures common in LPV and NL system identification. Next,


01395 [PDF p.26] we propose an LPV-LFR specific initialization and bootstrapping procedure, partially adapting the work


01396 [PDF p.26] in [42]. Finally, we investigate the performance of the new initialization and bootstrapping procedure in


01397 [PDF p.26] comparison to less sophisticated random initialization methods. This investigation is performed by means


01398 [PDF p.26] of Monte Carlo analysis on two identification examples.


01399 [PDF p.26] 


01400 [PDF p.26] 


01401 [PDF p.26] 4.1 | Introduction


01402 [PDF p.26] As discussed in the previous chapter, the non-convex nature of the identification problem (2.29) can prove


01403 [PDF p.26] challenging for gradient-based optimization methods. This often leads to sub-optimal results as algorithms


01404 [PDF p.26] converge to local minima in the cost function. Because of this behaviour, particularly for complex model


01405 [PDF p.26] structures, the parameter initialization approach plays a significant role in results achieved by optimization


01406 [PDF p.26] algorithms.


01407 [PDF p.26] 


01408 [PDF p.26] To aid the initialization process, in system identification, partial prior knowledge is often incorporated in


01409 [PDF p.26] what is called a bootstrapping procedure, where a part of the optimization parameters is initialized with


01410 [PDF p.26] previously found values. For example, in the identification of LTI SS models, it is common to first initialize


01411 [PDF p.26] a SS-OE model based on subspace identification, optimize for simulation error and then bootstrap the


01412 [PDF p.26] SS-OE model into a combined plant and process model with BJ structure which is subsequently optimized


01413 [PDF p.26] for prediction error minimization.


01414 [PDF p.26] 


01415 [PDF p.26] In the identification of externally scheduled LPV models, initialization and bootstrapping approaches are


01416 [PDF p.26] common. Already in [16], [17], a bootstrapping approach is used in which a rational LPV-LFR model is


01417 [PDF p.26] bootstrapped from an affine LPV-LFR model which in turn is bootstrapped from an LTI model obtained


01418 [PDF p.26] using subspace identification. Despite the increase in model complexity, initialization and bootstrapping


01419 [PDF p.26] procedures for joint-estimation of self-scheduled LPV models and scheduling maps follow the same simple


01420 [PDF p.26] procedure. Both [20] and [9] bootstrap from LTI models and use random initialization for additional


01421 [PDF p.26] plant and scheduling map parameters. In [10], a random initialization without bootstrapping is employed,


01422 [PDF p.26] instead the method relies on its subspace encoder to ensure stability of the optimization process.


01423 [PDF p.26] 


01424 [PDF p.26] For the identification of more general (without LPV structure) nonlinear models, initialization approaches


01425 [PDF p.26] based on the Best Linear Approximation (BLA) framework [43] are used for a variety of model structures


01426 [PDF p.26] such as polynomial nonlinear state space models [44] and nonlinear-LFR models, [45], [42] of which the


01427 [PDF p.26] latter model structure is of particular importance, as it is closely related to the LPV-LFR model structure.


01428 [PDF p.26] In [42] the BLA is used to initialize the LTI parameters of an NL-LFR, moreover two important steps are


01429 [PDF p.26] taken. First, after a BLA is obtained, the model simulated and a similarity transform is performed to


01430 [PDF p.26] guarantee the sampled standard deviation of the state trajectory is 1 with mean 0. This ensures that


01431 [PDF p.26] all inputs to the neural network, used to model nonlinearities, have standard deviation 1. Which, when


01432 [PDF p.26] paired with random uniform initialization of the neural network is recognized to reduce problems with


01433 [PDF p.26] exploding and vanishing gradients in optimization. Secondly, the NL part of the NL-LFR is initialized


01434 [PDF p.26] with random or 0 values, such that the initial NL-LFR trajectory matches the BLA initialization. This


01435 [PDF p.26] initialization and scaling strategy is shown to be beneficial on two benchmarks.

01754 [PDF p.32] 5.1 | Introduction


01755 [PDF p.32] As discussed in Section 1, no methods for the incorporation of prior knowledge, in terms of FPM, into


01756 [PDF p.32] the identification process of LPV models exists in literature. In the field of chemical engineering, model


01757 [PDF p.32] augmentation or hybrid modelling, by combining FPM with NN was introduced by [13], an extensive


01758 [PDF p.32] overview including common model structures can be found in [46]. In the fields of identification and


01759 [PDF p.32] control, the incorporation of information from FPM is still an active field of research. Common static


01760 [PDF p.32] augmentation structures include parallel augmentations [47] and series augmentations [48]. Dynamic


01761 [PDF p.32] parallel augmentations, in which the augmentations include the extension of the state vector, are used


01762 [PDF p.32] in [49] on an industrial example. Dynamic augmentations with more complex augmentation structures


01763 [PDF p.32] are used on an industrial example in [50]. In [51], a unifying interconnection approach, using LFRs to


01764 [PDF p.32] represent all commonly used types of interconnections, has been proposed. It should be noted that this


01765 [PDF p.32] approach is designed for NL-LFR models, making it not directly suitable for use with LPV-LFR models.

02219 [PDF p.39] 6 | Conclusions & Recommendations


02220 [PDF p.39] In this thesis, a novel method for guaranteeing well-posedness of LPV-LFR models has been presented.


02221 [PDF p.39] We have shown that by using a novel parameterization method, this technique is effectively incorporated


02222 [PDF p.39] in system identification methods for surrogate modelling of nonlinear systems. This approach, and the


02223 [PDF p.39] results obtained based on the method contribute to the development of modern computationally efficient


02224 [PDF p.39] nonlinear system identification methods. This chapter summarizes the most important results obtained in


02225 [PDF p.39] answering the research questions posed in Section 1 and gives recommendations for future research on


02226 [PDF p.39] this subject.

02228 [PDF p.39] 6.1 | Conclusions


02229 [PDF p.39] In the start of this thesis, three research questions were posed with the aim of improving joint-estimation


02230 [PDF p.39] methods for identification of LPV models and scheduling maps. The first question asked how well-posedness


02231 [PDF p.39] of LPV models with LFR structure and rational scheduling dependency can be guaranteed in identification.


02232 [PDF p.39] The results of Section 2 show how, by specific parameterization, LPV-LFR models can be constrained


02233 [PDF p.39] to always be well-posed. Subsequently, this section showed how the well-posed parameterization is


02234 [PDF p.39] incorporated into a system identification approach for joint-estimation of well-posed LPV-LFR models


02235 [PDF p.39] and scheduling maps, capable of accurately modelling nonlinear models under a wide range of complex


02236 [PDF p.39] noise scenarios. Moreover, this identification approach has been shown to be computationally efficient


02237 [PDF p.39] when compared to alternative deep-learning based methods.


02238 [PDF p.39] 


02239 [PDF p.39] The second research question asked how reliable convergence to accurate surrogate models using the


02240 [PDF p.39] above-mentioned methods can be achieved. Section 3 partially answered this question by showing that,


02241 [PDF p.39] incorporating multiple shooting in the identification process, improves reliability in two Monte Carlo


02242 [PDF p.39] studies. Furthermore, Section 4 introduced a novel initialization approach for LPV-LFR models based on


02243 [PDF p.39] BLA initializations, which improves the reliability of convergence to accurate surrogate models in two


02244 [PDF p.39] Monte Carlo studies.


02245 [PDF p.39] 


02246 [PDF p.39] Finally, the third research question asked how prior knowledge of systems can be incorporated into the


02247 [PDF p.39] identification process. In Section 5 this question was answered by the introduction of a model augmentation


02248 [PDF p.39] approach for joint-estimation of LPV-LFR models and scheduling maps. This approach is shown to be


02249 [PDF p.39] effective in two simulation examples, illustrating how the versatility of the LFR model structure can be


02250 [PDF p.39] used to incorporate prior system knowledge.

02253 [PDF p.39] 6.2 | Recommendations


02254 [PDF p.39] In answering the research questions posed in this thesis, some questions have been left open and potential


02255 [PDF p.39] avenues of future research have been found. Below we detail the most important recommendations.


02256 [PDF p.39] 


02257 [PDF p.39] First, in Section 2, we argue that LPV-LFR models with rational scheduling dependency can, in some cases,


02258 [PDF p.39] reduce overbounding/conservatism over models with affine scheduling dependency. However, currently no


02259 [PDF p.39] investigation has been performed to compare the conservatism of identified LPV-LFR models with affine


02260 [PDF p.39] and rational scheduling dependency using the well-posed parameterization. In the future, we recommend


02261 [PDF p.39] further investigation of this, by using these models in control or finding methods to quantify the amount


02262 [PDF p.39] of overbounding/conservatism.


02263 [PDF p.39] 


02264 [PDF p.39] Secondly, in Section 3, we have explored only a small subset of what is possible with multiple shooting


02265 [PDF p.39] methods. As previously discussed, methods based on SUBNET encoders, such as [10], have shown great


02266 [PDF p.39] performance on a wide range of NL system identification examples. Based on this, we strongly recommend


02267 [PDF p.39] to incorporate SUBNET encoders into the identification process described in this thesis, to evaluate its


02268 [PDF p.39] effects on performance and reliability.


02269 [PDF p.39] 


02270 [PDF p.39] Thirdly, the BLA-based initialization and bootstrapping approach proposed in Section 4 has been shown


02271 [PDF p.39] to significantly improve the reliability at which accurate models can be identified. However, a clear


02272 [PDF p.39] understanding of why this method, particularly the initialization procedure, works so well is still lacking.
