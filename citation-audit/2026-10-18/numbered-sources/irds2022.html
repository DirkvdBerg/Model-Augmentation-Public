00287 [PDF p.9] FOREWORD


00288 [PDF p.9] The IEEE International Roadmap for Devices and Systems (IRDS) is the continuation and extension of the National Technology


00289 [PDF p.9] Roadmap for Semiconductors/International Technology Roadmap for Semiconductors (NTRS/ITRS) with a record of


00290 [PDF p.9] uninterrupted publications spanning from 1992 to 2015. The IRDS has followed several of the successful methodologies


00291 [PDF p.9] demonstrated by the NTRS/ITRS with additional expansion to system integration, data centers and Internet communications


00292 [PDF p.9] requirements as well as quantum computing and quantum communications. The IRDS has been previously published in 2016,


00293 [PDF p.9] 2017, 2018, 2020 and 2021.


00294 [PDF p.9] Historically the draft of all the chapters of the ITRS used to be collected in the month of October. Afterwards, the Executive


00295 [PDF p.9] Summary was quickly assembled and delivered to the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) Board at their mid-November


00296 [PDF p.9] annual meeting.


00297 [PDF p.9] In the subsequent months major results and forecasts for the following year were delivered in key conferences like the


00298 [PDF p.9] International Electron Devices Meeting (IEDM) (mid-December) and International Solid-State Circuits Conference (ISSCC)


00299 [PDF p.9] (late-February). In addition, multiple company announcements were made in January outlining their vision for the year that had


00300 [PDF p.9] just started. Historically, the publication of ITRS was timed consistently with the SIA board meeting. With the transition to IEEE


00301 [PDF p.9] in May 2016 this constraint no longer existed, and the publication schedule evolved towards release of the IRDS at the beginning


00302 [PDF p.9] of 2Q of the following year. As a result, all the information that previously was included as part of the roadmap update and


00303 [PDF p.9] scheduled to be published the following year has now become available for the IRDS publication in real time. As a result, the


00304 [PDF p.9] 2019 IRDS was renamed the 2020 IRDS.


00305 [PDF p.9] Additionally, historically the NTRS/ITRS and IRDS have predicted specific problems to occur early on, allowing enough time


00306 [PDF p.9] for research and supplier organizations to develop possible solutions. For instance, in 1998 the transformation of the planar


00307 [PDF p.9] silicon gate CMOS to strained silicon, high-κ/metal-gate and FinFET was clearly articulated, and these key milestones were


00308 [PDF p.9] accomplished in 2003, 2007 and 2011, respectively. Starting with the 2020 IRDS a section dedicated to Industry Highlights and


00309 [PDF p.9] IRDS messages was introduced to better show the relations between the two organizations.

01372 [PDF p.44] mechanics is typically relevant only when describing behavior at the atomic level, it can apply to the behavior of superconducting


01373 [PDF p.44] circuits at extremely low temperatures, typically below 0.1 K.

01377 [PDF p.44] organizing them effectively into scalable architectures has, to date, proved to be a formidable but not impossible engineering

02783 [PDF p.75] 5.7.     THE 3 ERAS OF SCALING


02784 [PDF p.75] The foundations of the IC industry were laid out with the invention of the self-aligned silicon gate planar process in the late


02785 [PDF p.75] 1960’s. Moore’s predictions of the doubling of the number of transistors per die on an annual and then bi-annual pace formulated


02786 [PDF p.75] in 1965 and in 1975, respectively, in conjunction with Dennard’s scaling guidelines led to the growth of the semiconductor


02787 [PDF p.75] industry until the beginning of the last decade.


02788 [PDF p.75] Geometrical scaling characterized the 70s, 80s and 90s. This was the first generation of transistor scaling. The National


02789 [PDF p.75] Technology Roadmap for Semiconductors (NTRS) was initiated in the U.S. with a workshop held in 1991 and subsequent


02790 [PDF p.75] publications occurred in 1992, 1994, and 1997, respectively. The electronics industry was primarily “bottom up” driven during


02791 [PDF p.75] this period since any new technology generation provided transistors operating with continuously better performance that could


02792 [PDF p.75] power new memory and processors that easily fitted in the existing system architecture. System integrators could barely keep up


02793 [PDF p.75] with the rate at which new memory and new processors products were introduced since the industry changed in the 90’s from a


02794 [PDF p.75] 3-4-year cycle to a 2-year technology cycle. However, major upcoming material and structural limitations were identified by the


02795 [PDF p.75] NTRS between 1994 and 1997. These problems were so fundamental that it was deemed necessary to engage the whole


02796 [PDF p.75] international semiconductor community to successfully identifying possible solutions in a timely fashion. A proposal to extend


02797 [PDF p.75] the NTRS to European, Japanese, Korean, and Taiwanese technical communities was presented in April 1998 to the World


02798 [PDF p.75] Semiconductor Council (WSC). The proposal was accepted and the ITRS was formed. The international research community


02799 [PDF p.75] met in San Francisco on July 1998 and at that meeting the research activities necessary to completely restructure the MOS


02800 [PDF p.75] transistor and the necessary methodology were approved and launched worldwide (Figure ES48).
