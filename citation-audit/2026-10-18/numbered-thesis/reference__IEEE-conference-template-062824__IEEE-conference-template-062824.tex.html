0075 \subsection{Maintaining the Integrity of the Specifications}


0076 


0077 The IEEEtran class file is used to format your paper and style the text. All margins, 


0078 column widths, line spaces, and text fonts are prescribed; please do not 


0079 alter them. You may note peculiarities. For example, the head margin


0080 measures proportionately more than is customary. This measurement 


0081 and others are deliberate, using specifications that anticipate your paper 


0082 as one part of the entire proceedings, and not as an independent document. 


0083 Please do not revise any of the current designations.

0085 \section{Prepare Your Paper Before Styling}


0086 Before you begin to format your paper, first write and save the content as a 


0087 separate text file. Complete all content and organizational editing before 


0088 formatting. Please note sections \ref{AA} to \ref{FAT} below for more information on 


0089 proofreading, spelling and grammar.

0091 Keep your text and graphic files separate until after the text has been 


0092 formatted and styled. Do not number text heads---{\LaTeX} will do that 


0093 for you.

0095 \subsection{Abbreviations and Acronyms}\label{AA}


0096 Define abbreviations and acronyms the first time they are used in the text, 


0097 even after they have been defined in the abstract. Abbreviations such as 


0098 IEEE, SI, MKS, CGS, ac, dc, and rms do not have to be defined. Do not use 


0099 abbreviations in the title or heads unless they are unavoidable.

0101 \subsection{Units}


0102 \begin{itemize}


0103 \item Use either SI (MKS) or CGS as primary units. (SI units are encouraged.) English units may be used as secondary units (in parentheses). An exception would be the use of English units as identifiers in trade, such as ``3.5-inch disk drive''.


0104 \item Avoid combining SI and CGS units, such as current in amperes and magnetic field in oersteds. This often leads to confusion because equations do not balance dimensionally. If you must use mixed units, clearly state the units for each quantity that you use in an equation.


0105 \item Do not mix complete spellings and abbreviations of units: ``Wb/m\textsuperscript{2}'' or ``webers per square meter'', not ``webers/m\textsuperscript{2}''. Spell out units when they appear in text: ``. . . a few henries'', not ``. . . a few H''.


0106 \item Use a zero before decimal points: ``0.25'', not ``.25''. Use ``cm\textsuperscript{3}'', not ``cc''.)


0107 \end{itemize}

0109 \subsection{Equations}


0110 Number equations consecutively. To make your 


0111 equations more compact, you may use the solidus (~/~), the exp function, or 


0112 appropriate exponents. Italicize Roman symbols for quantities and variables, 


0113 but not Greek symbols. Use a long dash rather than a hyphen for a minus 


0114 sign. Punctuate equations with commas or periods when they are part of a 


0115 sentence, as in:


0116 \begin{equation}


0117 a+b=\gamma\label{eq}


0118 \end{equation}


0119 


0120 Be sure that the 


0121 symbols in your equation have been defined before or immediately following 


0122 the equation. Use ``\eqref{eq}'', not ``Eq.~\eqref{eq}'' or ``equation \eqref{eq}'', except at 


0123 the beginning of a sentence: ``Equation \eqref{eq} is . . .''

0125 \subsection{\LaTeX-Specific Advice}


0126 


0127 Please use ``soft'' (e.g., \verb|\eqref{Eq}|) cross references instead


0128 of ``hard'' references (e.g., \verb|(1)|). That will make it possible


0129 to combine sections, add equations, or change the order of figures or


0130 citations without having to go through the file line by line.


0131 


0132 Please don't use the \verb|{eqnarray}| equation environment. Use


0133 \verb|{align}| or \verb|{IEEEeqnarray}| instead. The \verb|{eqnarray}|


0134 environment leaves unsightly spaces around relation symbols.


0135 


0136 Please note that the \verb|{subequations}| environment in {\LaTeX}


0137 will increment the main equation counter even when there are no


0138 equation numbers displayed. If you forget that, you might write an


0139 article in which the equation numbers skip from (17) to (20), causing


0140 the copy editors to wonder if you've discovered a new method of


0141 counting.


0142 


0143 {\BibTeX} does not work by magic. It doesn't get the bibliographic


0144 data from thin air but from .bib files. If you use {\BibTeX} to produce a


0145 bibliography you must send the .bib files. 


0146 


0147 {\LaTeX} can't read your mind. If you assign the same label to a


0148 subsubsection and a table, you might find that Table I has been cross


0149 referenced as Table IV-B3. 


0150 


0151 {\LaTeX} does not have precognitive abilities. If you put a


0152 \verb|\label| command before the command that updates the counter it's


0153 supposed to be using, the label will pick up the last counter to be


0154 cross referenced instead. In particular, a \verb|\label| command


0155 should not go before the caption of a figure or a table.


0156 


0157 Do not use \verb|\nonumber| inside the \verb|{array}| environment. It


0158 will not stop equation numbers inside \verb|{array}| (there won't be


0159 any anyway) and it might stop a wanted equation number in the


0160 surrounding equation.

0162 \subsection{Some Common Mistakes}\label{SCM}


0163 \begin{itemize}


0164 \item The word ``data'' is plural, not singular.


0165 \item The subscript for the permeability of vacuum $\mu_{0}$, and other common scientific constants, is zero with subscript formatting, not a lowercase letter ``o''.


0166 \item In American English, commas, semicolons, periods, question and exclamation marks are located within quotation marks only when a complete thought or name is cited, such as a title or full quotation. When quotation marks are used, instead of a bold or italic typeface, to highlight a word or phrase, punctuation should appear outside of the quotation marks. A parenthetical phrase or statement at the end of a sentence is punctuated outside of the closing parenthesis (like this). (A parenthetical sentence is punctuated within the parentheses.)


0167 \item A graph within a graph is an ``inset'', not an ``insert''. The word alternatively is preferred to the word ``alternately'' (unless you really mean something that alternates).


0168 \item Do not use the word ``essentially'' to mean ``approximately'' or ``effectively''.


0169 \item In your paper title, if the words ``that uses'' can accurately replace the word ``using'', capitalize the ``u''; if not, keep using lower-cased.


0170 \item Be aware of the different meanings of the homophones ``affect'' and ``effect'', ``complement'' and ``compliment'', ``discreet'' and ``discrete'', ``principal'' and ``principle''.


0171 \item Do not confuse ``imply'' and ``infer''.


0172 \item The prefix ``non'' is not a word; it should be joined to the word it modifies, usually without a hyphen.


0173 \item There is no period after the ``et'' in the Latin abbreviation ``et al.''.


0174 \item The abbreviation ``i.e.'' means ``that is'', and the abbreviation ``e.g.'' means ``for example''.


0175 \end{itemize}

0178 \subsection{Authors and Affiliations}\label{AAA}


0179 \textbf{The class file is designed for, but not limited to, six authors.} A 


0180 minimum of one author is required for all conference articles. Author names 


0181 should be listed starting from left to right and then moving down to the 


0182 next line. This is the author sequence that will be used in future citations 


0183 and by indexing services. Names should not be listed in columns nor group by 


0184 affiliation. Please keep your affiliations as succinct as possible (for 


0185 example, do not differentiate among departments of the same organization).

0187 \subsection{Identify the Headings}\label{ITH}


0188 Headings, or heads, are organizational devices that guide the reader through 


0189 your paper. There are two types: component heads and text heads.


0190 


0191 Component heads identify the different components of your paper and are not 


0192 topically subordinate to each other. Examples include Acknowledgments and 


0193 References and, for these, the correct style to use is ``Heading 5''. Use 


0194 ``figure caption'' for your Figure captions, and ``table head'' for your 


0195 table title. Run-in heads, such as ``Abstract'', will require you to apply a 


0196 style (in this case, italic) in addition to the style provided by the drop 


0197 down menu to differentiate the head from the text.


0198 


0199 Text heads organize the topics on a relational, hierarchical basis. For 


0200 example, the paper title is the primary text head because all subsequent 


0201 material relates and elaborates on this one topic. If there are two or more 


0202 sub-topics, the next level head (uppercase Roman numerals) should be used 


0203 and, conversely, if there are not at least two sub-topics, then no subheads 


0204 should be introduced.

0206 \subsection{Figures and Tables}\label{FAT}


0207 \paragraph{Positioning Figures and Tables} Place figures and tables at the top and 


0208 bottom of columns. Avoid placing them in the middle of columns. Large 


0209 figures and tables may span across both columns. Figure captions should be 


0210 below the figures; table heads should appear above the tables. Insert 


0211 figures and tables after they are cited in the text. Use the abbreviation 


0212 ``Fig.~\ref{fig}'', even at the beginning of a sentence.

0237 Figure Labels: Use 8 point Times New Roman for Figure labels. Use words 


0238 rather than symbols or abbreviations when writing Figure axis labels to 


0239 avoid confusing the reader. As an example, write the quantity 


0240 ``Magnetization'', or ``Magnetization, M'', not just ``M''. If including 


0241 units in the label, present them within parentheses. Do not label axes only 


0242 with units. In the example, write ``Magnetization (A/m)'' or ``Magnetization 


0243 \{A[m(1)]\}'', not just ``A/m''. Do not label axes with a ratio of 


0244 quantities and units. For example, write ``Temperature (K)'', not 


0245 ``Temperature/K''.

0247 \section*{Acknowledgment}


0248 


0249 The preferred spelling of the word ``acknowledgment'' in America is without 


0250 an ``e'' after the ``g''. Avoid the stilted expression ``one of us (R. B. 


0251 G.) thanks $\ldots$''. Instead, try ``R. B. G. thanks$\ldots$''. Put sponsor 


0252 acknowledgments in the unnumbered footnote on the first page.

0254 \section*{References}


0255 


0256 Please number citations consecutively within brackets \cite{b1}. The 


0257 sentence punctuation follows the bracket \cite{b2}. Refer simply to the reference 


0258 number, as in \cite{b3}---do not use ``Ref. \cite{b3}'' or ``reference \cite{b3}'' except at 


0259 the beginning of a sentence: ``Reference \cite{b3} was the first $\ldots$''


0260 


0261 Number footnotes separately in superscripts. Place the actual footnote at 


0262 the bottom of the column in which it was cited. Do not put footnotes in the 


0263 abstract or reference list. Use letters for table footnotes.


0264 


0265 Unless there are six authors or more give all authors' names; do not use 


0266 ``et al.''. Papers that have not been published, even if they have been 


0267 submitted for publication, should be cited as ``unpublished'' \cite{b4}. Papers 


0268 that have been accepted for publication should be cited as ``in press'' \cite{b5}. 


0269 Capitalize only the first word in a paper title, except for proper nouns and 


0270 element symbols.


0271 


0272 For papers published in translation journals, please give the English 


0273 citation first, followed by the original foreign-language citation \cite{b6}.
