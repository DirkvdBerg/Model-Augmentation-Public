0054 \subsection{Simulated system}\label{sec:setup_system}


0055 % Sources: D-204, D-224 (truth), D-188 (absorber), D-223, D-226 (friction gain), D-221 (K1),


0056 % D-238 (noise); tdg_config.m, gtd_build_plant.m; Code/Setup rail_velocity.py, band_edge.py, snr.py.


0057 \ifdraft


0058 \begin{itemize}


0059 \item The data is simulated: the system is the baseline plus rail friction and a payload absorber, both absent from the baseline


0060 \item The friction subtracts from the actuator forces; a smoothed Coulomb law, Coulomb-like on the excited records


0061 \item The absorber adds one coordinate, coupled through the inertia; matrices written out as in Section~II


0062 \item Parameter values with their basis; both absorber features lie above the crossover of the loop


0063 \item One known controller $K_1$, the same in every record; the injected force is the only feedforward


0064 \item Noise matched to the machine; noise-free twin of every record; SNR (?: confidentiality)


0065 \end{itemize}


0066 \fi


0067 The system consists of the baseline model \eqref{eq:eom}, with two additions.


0068 The first is Coulomb friction at the actuators $X_1$, $X_2$ and $Y$, as


0069 modelled by Garc\'ia-Herreros et al.~\cite{garcia2013model}.


0070 We deliberately left it out of the baseline, to add nonlinear unmodelled


0071 dynamics to the system. The friction works as a force against each actuator 


0072 force, this is modeled as a term entering \eqref{eq:eom} through the input.


0073 For the second addition, we split the payload mass $m_h$ into


0074 $m_a$ and $m_{h,r}=m_h-m_a$, with $m_a$ connected to $m_{h,r}$


0075 by a spring and damper in the $Y$ direction


0076 (Fig.~\ref{fig:gantry_absorber}).


0077 This adds a vibration mode which the 


0078 baseline model is not able to represent with its states.


0079 \begin{figure}[t]


0080   \centering


0081   \resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{figures/gantry_system_with_absorber}}


0082   \caption{\todo{Simulated gantry with a flexibly mounted part $m_a$ of the


0083   payload mass $m_h$; the rest of the payload is $m_{h,r}=m_h-m_a$. The


0084   light-gray elements $(m_a,k_a,c_a,q_a)$ are absent from the baseline.}}


0085   \label{fig:gantry_absorber}


0086 \end{figure}


0087 With the friction acting against each actuator force on the input, 


0088 \eqref{eq:eom} becomes


0089 \begin{subequations}\label{eq:setup_truth}


0090 \begin{equation}


0091 \begin{aligned}


0092  M(Y)\ddot q+C\dot q+Kq&=P\bigl(\uact-F_{\mathrm{fr}}(P^\top\dot q)\bigr),\\


0093  F_{\mathrm{fr},i}(v_i)&=c_{c,i}\tanh\bigl(v_i/v_{\mathrm{fr}}\bigr),


0094 \end{aligned}


0095  \label{eq:setup_fric}


0096 \end{equation}


0097 where $P^\top\dot q$ gives the actuator velocities


0098 for $i\in\{X_1,X_2,Y\}$, and $v_{\mathrm{fr}}$ sets


0099 the velocity scale of the smoothing.


0100 For $\lvert v_i\rvert\gg v_{\mathrm{fr}}$, the friction


0101 approaches the Coulomb law


0102 $F_{\mathrm{fr},i}=c_{c,i}\operatorname{sign}(v_i)$


0103 of~\cite{garcia2013model}.


0104 $v_{\mathrm{fr}}=\SetupFricVzero$~mm/s was chosen by simulating


0105 the loop with values between 0.1 and 10~mm/s.


0106 \kw{Higher values turn the friction into viscous damping under the multisine,


0107 and lower values keep the standstill servo error above the noise level.}


0108 \\


0109 The added mass adds the displacement $q_a$ of $m_a$ relative to


0110 $m_{h,r}$ along $Y$.


0111 With $q_{\mathrm e}=\begin{bmatrix}q^\top&q_a\end{bmatrix}^\top$, the


0112 system is


0113 \begin{equation}


0114  M_{\mathrm e}(Y,q_a)\,\ddot q_{\mathrm e}+C_{\mathrm e}\dot q_{\mathrm e}+K_{\mathrm e}q_{\mathrm e}


0115  =\begin{bmatrix}P\bigl(\uact-F_{\mathrm{fr}}(P^\top\dot q)\bigr)\\0\end{bmatrix},


0116  \label{eq:setup_abs}


0117 \end{equation}


0118 \end{subequations}


0119 with


0120 {\footnotesize


0121 \setlength{\arraycolsep}{3pt}


0122 \begin{equation*}


0123 \begin{gathered}


0124  M_{\mathrm e}(Y,q_a)=\begin{bmatrix}M(Y)+M_a(Y,\ell)&m_a\begin{bmatrix}0&-d&1\end{bmatrix}^\top\\m_a\begin{bmatrix}0&-d&1\end{bmatrix}&m_a\end{bmatrix},\\


0125  M_a(Y,\ell)=m_a\ell\begin{bmatrix}0&-1&0\\-1&2Y+\ell&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\\


0126  C_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{diag}(C,c_a),\quad


0127  K_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{diag}(K,k_a),


0128 \end{gathered}


0129 \end{equation*}}


0130 where $M(Y)$ is \eqref{eq:mass_matrix} with $m_h$ the total payload mass.


0131 $M_a(Y,\ell)$ corrects it for $m_a$ being at $Y+\ell$ instead of $Y$,


0132 where $\ell=L_0+q_a$ and $L_0$ is the offset of the added mass along $Y$.


0133 $k_a=m_a(2\pi f_a)^2$ and


0134 $c_a=2\zeta_a\sqrt{k_am_a}$, with natural frequency $f_a$


0135 and damping ratio $\zeta_a$.


0136 The added mass changes the inertia through $M_a$,


0137 which also includes its coupling to $\Theta$ and $Y$.


0138 We simulate the system with fixed-step RK4 at


0139 $f_{\mathrm s}=20$~kHz.


0140 All parameter values are listed in Table~\ref{tab:nominal_parameters}.


0141 The friction levels $c_c$ are taken from Garc\'ia-Herreros et


0142 al.~\cite[Table~I]{garcia2013model}.


0143 The added mass creates an anti-resonance


0144 at $f_a$ and a resonance at $f_a\sqrt{1+m_a/m_{h,r}}\approx2.12\,f_b$,


0145 in the transfer function from $F_Y$ to $Y$.


0146 Both are designed to be above the crossover of the loop with $K_1$


0147 (Section~\ref{sec:setup_data}).


0148 As a result, the feedback does not suppress them, and we can identify


0149 them from the data.


0150 All data is generated in closed loop with the controller $K_1$ of


0151 Appendix~\ref{app:setup_system}, which is the known controller of


0152 Section~\ref{sec:aug_loop}.


0153 The only feedforward, $u^{\mathrm{ff}}$ in \eqref{eq:aug_two_loops}, is the


0154 injected excitation force.


0155 Independent coloured noise is added to the position measurements


0156 of each axis, $y=\qact+v$.


0157 The simulated servo error matches at standstill the noise level


0158 and spectral shape of the industrial gantry, but its resonances are left


0159 out (Appendix~\ref{app:setup_system}).


0160 The servo-error signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) ranges from


0161 \SetupSnrMinX{} to \SetupSnrMaxX~dB on the $X$ actuators


0162 and \SetupSnrMinY{} to \SetupSnrMaxY~dB on $Y$, depending on the motion of the record.


0163 The data is also generated without measurement noise, with output $y^\circ$.

0181 The data is divided into five types of motion profiles and each record is 12~s


0182 (Appendix~\ref{app:setup_records}).


0183 The motion profiles excite the low frequencies up till the loop crossover, 


0184 for at least \SetupGapSnrMinMotion~dB above the noise.


0185 A multisine force is added to each of the generalized channels $X$,


0186 $\Theta$, $Y$, except in records with an operational motion profile.


0187 The force is applied to the actuators through $P^{-1}$.


0188 The multisine band starts at the highest loop crossover 


0189 $\fpeval{round(\SetupCrossMax/100,2)}\,f_b$, to prevent feedback suppression.


0190 The band ends at $2.97\,f_b$, which keeps 90\,\% of the squared


0191 frequency-response error of the baseline above the crossover. This was 


0192 measured on the system without noise.


0193 The band contains the anti-resonance and the


0194 resonance of the added mass (Fig.~\ref{fig:setup_band}), 


0195 for all the positions used in training.


0196 This was determined from the frequency response of the known system model at


0197 each training position.

0293 \subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:setup_metrics}


0294 % Sources: D-256 (NRMS_e), THESIS-RESULTS Sec. 2; D-259 (m_Delta scale, revnotes); Section IV


0295 % eq:obc_eps, eq:obc_recovery, eq:obc_overlap; Code/Evaluation eval_overlap.py.


0296 \ifdraft


0297 \begin{itemize}


0298 \item Every model is simulated over whole test records: grey-box models in closed loop with $K_1$, the black box in free run


0299 \item Primary metric: the simulation error relative to the servo error, $\mathrm{NRMS}_e$, per stage axis; beside it the RMS in metres and the noise floor


0300 \item Parameter error per combination against the true values; $m_\Delta$ scaled by the mean actuator mass; simulation only


0301 \item The two predictions of Section~\ref{sec:obc}: the network stays out of the parameter directions, and the remaining parameter error is the first-order bias


0302 \end{itemize}


0303 \fi


0304 All models are evaluated based on its output simulated over the full records. 


0305 Since the reference is known, we compare the predicted servo error


0306 with the recorded servo error $y-r$.


0307 The primary metric is the NRMS of~\cite[Eq.~(43)]{beintema2023subnet},


0308 adapted by using the servo error as output.


0309 We also report the RMS in metres and the noise floor:


0310 \begin{equation}


0311 \begin{aligned}


0312  \mathrm{RMS}_j&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N-n-1}\sum_{k=n+1}^{N-1}(\hat y_{j,k}-y_{j,k})^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\\


0313  \mathrm{NRMS}_{e,j}&=\frac{\mathrm{RMS}_j}{\sigma_{e,j}},\\


0314  \mathrm{RMS}^{\mathrm{fl}}_j&=\Bigl(\frac{1}{N-n-1}\sum_{k=n+1}^{N-1}(y_{j,k}-y^{\circ}_{j,k})^2\Bigr)^{1/2},


0315 \end{aligned}


0316 \label{eq:setup_rms}


0317 \end{equation}


0318 where $j$ is the stage axis, $N$ the record length, $n+1$ the encoder


0319 window length and $\sigma_{e,j}$ the population standard deviation of the servo error


0320 $y_j-r_j$ over the same samples.


0321 An NRMS of 100\,\% means the model performs no better than $\hat y=r$.


0322 The noise floor is the RMS difference between the measured and noise-free


0323 outputs.


0324 \\


0325 The parameter error of combination $j$ is


0326 \begin{equation}


0327 \begin{aligned}


0328  \delta_j&=\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{base},j}-\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}}{s_j},\\


0329  s_j&=\begin{cases}


0330   (m_1+m_2)/2, & \text{for } m_\Delta,\\


0331   \lvert\theta^\star_{\mathrm{base},j}\rvert, & \text{otherwise},


0332  \end{cases}


0333 \end{aligned}


0334  \label{eq:setup_delta}


0335 \end{equation}


0336 where the true value of $m_\Delta=m_1-m_2$ is close to zero,


0337 so we scale its error by the mean actuator mass $(m_1+m_2)/2$ instead.


0338 \\

0340 $\rho$ of \eqref{eq:obc_overlap} is computed at $\bar x=0$ on the one-step


0341 tuples of the training, validation and test records (test: I1 to I6), for


0342 the network of $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$, the corrected network of


0343 $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ and the missing dynamics $\Xi$.


0344 On the training tuples the corrected overlap is zero by construction.
