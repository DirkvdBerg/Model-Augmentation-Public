0034 \subsection{Augmentation structure}


0035 \ifdraft


0036 \begin{itemize}


0037 \item The gain with multisine is the absorber mode, carried by the network and the added states (Fig.~\ref{fig:res_frf}, Table~\ref{tab:res_obc}, Table~\ref{tab:app_structure})


0038 \item Added states and PS are both needed; shown on validation with one seed (Table~\ref{tab:app_structure})


0039 \item Without multisine the final model is slightly above the baseline; candidates only (Table~\ref{tab:res_structure}) (?)


0040 \end{itemize}


0041 \fi


0042 The gain on the records with multisine comes from the absorber mode.


0043 The final model has a resonance at 2.11 to 2.13\,$f_b$ over $Y$, against


0044 2.12\,$f_b$ of the system (Fig.~\ref{fig:res_frf}).


0045 Its damping ratio is 0.036 to 0.080 over $Y$, against 0.043, so the mode


0046 frequency is learned more accurately than its damping.


0047 With the learned block switched off, the $Y$ error of the trained model


0048 returns to about 42\,\% (Table~\ref{tab:res_obc}), so the network and the


0049 added states carry the mode, not the refitted parameters.


0050 \\


0051 Both the added states and their PS start are needed to learn the mode.


0052 On the validation records with multisine, the $Y$ error is 6.4\,\% with PS,


0053 78.1\,\% with plain training and 86.5\,\% without added states


0054 (Table~\ref{tab:app_structure}).


0055 Eight added states give the same error as two.


0056 This rests on one seed per model, so a seed that lets plain training find


0057 the mode is not excluded.


0058 The missing dynamics hold one mode of known order, so the result shows that


0059 two added states suffice for this mode, not how $n_{\bar x}$ should be


0060 chosen when the order is unknown.


0061 \\


0062 Without multisine, the final model is 0.2 to 0.4 points above the baseline


0063 at the true parameters (Table~\ref{tab:res_structure}).


0064 At this level the baseline misses only the friction and the part of the


0065 absorber that the motion excites.


0066 A candidate explanation is that the learned correction, fitted mainly where


0067 the multisine excites the absorber, adds small errors where the baseline is


0068 already close to its limit.


0069 The detuned start is not the cause: on the validation records, the model


0070 trained from the true parameters is also above the baseline on $Y$


0071 (Table~\ref{tab:app_structure}).


0072 The candidate is not tested.


0073 The error spectrum of both models on these records would show whether the


0074 difference lies in the absorber band or below the crossover.

0077 \subsection{Interpretability}


0078 \ifdraft


0079 \begin{itemize}


0080 \item OBC halves the drift of U but does not recover the truth; expected from the truth overlap and \eqref{eq:obc_recovery} (Table~\ref{tab:res_obc})


0081 \item The dampers move most; the friction projects onto the damping sensitivities by construction (D-204 argument, not measured)


0082 \item OBC holds to first order at $\bar x=0$ for the one-step fit only; added states are not protected


0083 \item Uniqueness only partly shown: the two starts still differ (Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_full}) (?: row requested)


0084 \item Without added states the order reverses; candidate only (Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_na0})


0085 \item Root cause analysis compares unique estimates only while the missing dynamics do not change


0086 \end{itemize}


0087 \fi


0088 OBC reduces the parameter drift, but it does not recover the true


0089 combinations.


0090 From the detuned start, the combination error grows from 30.0\,\% to


0091 47.7\,\% with OBC and to 83.1\,\% without (Table~\ref{tab:res_obc}).


0092 From the correct start, OBC ends at 35.4\,\% and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ at


0093 58.3\,\% (Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_full}).


0094 This drift is expected from Section~\ref{sec:obc_ident}.


0095 The truth overlap of 0.59 shows that $\Phi^\top\Xi\neq0$ on the tuples, so


0096 the estimate of \eqref{eq:obc_recovery} is biased even with the projection.


0097 OBC costs no accuracy, since the full models and their physical parts differ


0098 by at most 0.9 points between the two arms.


0099 \todo{Missing: the numbers of $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ (R1, R2) are preliminary (update 19500 of 33400). Candidate: update 47.7, 35.4, 0.9 and the two-start differences after the final rerun (basis: handoff 2026-10-18, Sec.~3).}


0100 \\


0101 The damping combinations $c_{g1}$, $c_{g2}$ and $c_y$ move most, by 23 to


0102 30 points with OBC and 43 to 46 points without, while the mass combinations


0103 stay within 3.4\,\% of their true values.


0104 A candidate cause is the rail friction of \eqref{eq:setup_fric}.


0105 The sensitivity to $c_{g1}$ is proportional to $\dot X_1$, and the friction


0106 force has the sign of $\dot X_1$.


0107 Their inner product over a moving record is therefore nonzero, so the


0108 friction lies partly in the damping directions and biases the dampers


0109 through $\Phi^\top\Xi$.


0110 This argument is ours and is not measured on the final data.


0111 The truth overlap of $\Xi$ split into its friction and absorber parts would


0112 test it.


0113 \\


0114 The guarantee of OBC is also narrower than the training it is applied to.


0115 The projection holds to first order around $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$, for the


0116 one-step fit on the reference slice $\bar x=0$


0117 (Section~\ref{sec:obc_ident}).


0118 With PS the added states are active, so the part of $f_{\mathrm{aug}}$ that


0119 depends on $\bar x$ is not orthogonal to the parameter directions, and the


0120 closed-loop criterion \eqref{eq:aug_loss} is not the one-step fit.


0121 The two starts measure how far uniqueness holds in practice.


0122 Their final estimates differ by 25.1\,\% with OBC and 36.4\,\% without, from


0123 Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_full}.


0124 OBC brings the two estimates closer, but they are not equal, so uniqueness is


0125 only partly shown.


0126 \todo{Missing: the two-start difference is computed by hand from Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_full}, not shown in a float. Candidate: the ``difference between starts'' row requested at the end of Section~\ref{sec:results}, computed by \texttt{res\_obc\_combinations.py} after the final rerun.}


0127 \\


0128 Without added states the order reverses: $\mathcal M_{\mathrm U}$ ends at


0129 48.8\,\% and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{OBC}}$ at 68.4\,\%


0130 (Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_na0}).


0131 A candidate explanation is that the network must then represent the absorber


0132 statically, and a large part of it lies in the parameter directions (truth


0133 overlap 0.59).


0134 The projection removes that part from the network, so the parameters absorb


0135 it.


0136 This is not tested.


0137 The result shows that OBC does not bring the estimate closer to the truth in


0138 general.


0139 Its guarantee is a unique split, and only the pair from two starts tests that.


0140 \todo{Missing: a possible inconsistency in Table~\ref{tab:app_obc_na0}: its full-model $Y$ row (41.8, 42.7) equals the physical-part $Y$ row of Table~\ref{tab:res_obc}. Candidate: check \texttt{res\_obc\_na0} in the evaluation code before the paragraph above relies on that table.}


0141 \\


0142 Root cause analysis (Section~\ref{sec:intro}) compares unique estimates


0143 over time.


0144 By \eqref{eq:obc_recovery}, the estimate contains the bias


0145 $(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}\Phi^\top\Xi$, which depends on the missing dynamics.


0146 A change of the estimate therefore indicates a changed physical parameter


0147 only while the missing dynamics and the excitation stay the same.


0148 This is our reasoning and is not tested.

0150 \subsection{Generalisation}


0151 \ifdraft


0152 \begin{itemize}


0153 \item The final model keeps its accuracy on each E record; each E record leaves the training range in one direction only (Fig.~\ref{fig:res_generalisation}, Fig.~\ref{fig:setup_coverage})


0154 \item The black box does not fit the records without multisine, training records included; candidates, with the budget evidence (Table~\ref{tab:app_bb_search})


0155 \item The comparison does not test the black box at its best; a black box of the plant inside the known loop would


0156 \end{itemize}


0157 \fi


0158 The final model keeps its accuracy outside the training range.


0159 Each extrapolation record is within 2.0 points of its interpolation record


0160 (Fig.~\ref{fig:res_generalisation}).


0161 On the multisine twins it stays below 10\,\% on $Y$, against 48 to 87\,\% for


0162 the baseline.


0163 Each extrapolation record leaves the training range in one direction only


0164 ($Y$, acceleration or velocity; Fig.~\ref{fig:setup_coverage}), so a


0165 combined extrapolation is not tested.


0166 The physical part extrapolates by construction through $M(Y)$, while the


0167 network has only seen the training range.


0168 \\


0169 The black box does not fit the records without multisine, including the


0170 training records, where its error is 72 to 106\,\%.


0171 It therefore fails to fit these records rather than overfitting them.


0172 It is below the final model only on $X_2$ of I2.


0173 It models the whole closed loop from the reference and the injected force,


0174 and must learn the rigid-body and controller dynamics from servo errors of


0175 order $10^{-5}$~m.


0176 Its selected configuration reached its best validation error at update 78000


0177 of 80000 (Table~\ref{tab:app_bb_search}), so its training had not converged


0178 within the budget.


0179 Both are candidate causes and neither is tested.


0180 A longer budget would test the second.


0181 A black box of the plant inside the known loop would separate the first from


0182 the comparison of the model classes.


0183 The comparison therefore shows that this black box, at this budget, is less


0184 accurate than the final model, not that every black box would be.

0186 \subsection{Limits and departures from the research plan}


0187 \ifdraft


0188 \begin{itemize}


0189 \item Simulation only, one absorber plus friction, one seed, the same known controller in data and model


0190 \item Closed-loop training and evaluation can hide plant error behind the controller; no changed-controller test


0191 \item Departures: LPV vs LTI aspect removed, controller transfer (E5) dropped, real Telica data out, settling dropped (D-241)


0192 \end{itemize}


0193 \fi


0194 Three limits bound every result above.


0195 The data are simulated, and the missing dynamics are one absorber mode and


0196 rail friction, both chosen for this study.


0197 Every model is trained once with one seed (Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}), so


0198 differences of a few points between models are not resolved.


0199 The controller in the model is the controller that generated the data.


0200 \\


0201 This last limit matters because training and evaluation are both closed


0202 loop.


0203 The controller corrects part of the output error, so a plant error at low


0204 frequencies can stay hidden in the servo error.


0205 An evaluation under a changed controller would expose it, and it is the use


0206 that the Introduction motivates.


0207 \\


0208 The results depart from the research plan in four points.


0209 The comparison of the LPV baseline with an LTI baseline is no longer an


0210 aspect, because the LPV form follows from the equations of motion


0211 (Section~\ref{sec:lfr}); it justifies the baseline in


0212 Appendix~\ref{app:choices} (Fig.~\ref{fig:res_lpv_lti}).


0213 The planned test with a changed controller is not in the results.


0214 The planned evaluation on measured data from the industrial gantry is not


0215 part of the results either.


0216 Settling prediction is replaced by prediction over whole records, which the


0217 metric of Section~\ref{sec:setup_metrics} evaluates.

0220 \subsection{Conclusion}


0221 \ifdraft


0222 \begin{itemize}


0223 \item Aspect 1: parallel augmentation with two added states and PS learns the missing mode from closed-loop data (Table~\ref{tab:res_structure})


0224 \item Aspect 2: OBC halves the drift at no accuracy cost, gives a partly unique split, does not recover the truth


0225 \item Aspect 3: the final model keeps its accuracy at extrapolation; better than this black box


0226 \item Research question, central limit, one recommendation


0227 \end{itemize}


0228 \fi


0229 A parallel augmentation with added states, initialised by PS and trained in


0230 closed loop with the known controller, learns the missing absorber mode from


0231 closed-loop data (Aspect~1).


0232 With multisine it lowers the $Y$ error from 116.6\,\% to 6.2\,\%, and


0233 without multisine it stays at the level of the baseline.


0234 OBC halves the drift of the physical combinations at no cost in accuracy


0235 (Aspect~2).


0236 It makes the estimates from two starts closer but not equal, and it does


0237 not recover the true values, because the missing dynamics are not orthogonal


0238 to the parameter directions.


0239 The augmented model keeps its accuracy at held-out positions, velocities and


0240 accelerations, and is more accurate than the black box on every record


0241 except one axis of one record (Aspect~3).


0242 The answer to the research question is therefore a parallel augmentation


0243 with added states, a PS start and the orthogonal construction: it predicts


0244 the missing dynamics, and its parameters stay closer to a unique estimate


0245 than without the construction.


0246 The central limit is that uniqueness holds only to first order at


0247 $\bar x=0$, and has been shown in simulation with one seed.


0248 The next step is to evaluate the final model under a changed controller,


0249 the use that motivates it.
