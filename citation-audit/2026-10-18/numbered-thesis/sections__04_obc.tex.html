0083 \begin{wip}


0084 The parameter pair $(\theta_{\mathrm{base}},\theta_{\mathrm{aug}})$


0085 minimising the loss function of Section~\ref{sec:aug_closed_loop} can be non-unique:


0086 different pairs can give the same fit~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{gyorok2026obc}.


0087 Meaning that the rigid-body dynamics that the baseline parameters 


0088 $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ are able to explain from the data, can instead be represented by 


0089 a neural network correction.


0090 To prevent the neural network from correcting what $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ 


0091 can represent we introduce orthogonality by construction~\cite{gyorok2026obc}.


0092 Orthogonality here is defined as the neural network correction to the


0093 physical states having no component in the directions in which a change of


0094 $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ is able to change the next state predicted by the baseline.


0095 This gives us a unique estimate of $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ up to first order,


0096 but it does not guarantee recovering the true baseline parameters.


0097 \\


0098 Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ have constructed this for input-output baseline models that


0099 are linear in their parameters~\cite{gyorok2026obc}.


0100 We extend this to state-space models nonlinear in their parameters,


0101 specifically for our gantry system.


0102 \end{wip}


0103 \subsection{Orthogonal-by-construction parametrisation}\label{sec:obc_construction}


0104 \ifdraft


0105 \begin{itemize}


0106 \item Gy\"or\"ok's construction as adopted: baseline linear in its parameters, regressor at measured input-output data (past outputs, past and current inputs), stacked over the training data; lower case per sample, upper case stacked (2026 Eqs.~(1), (2), (5))


0107 \item $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ is the least-squares fit of the network output by the regressor; its fitted part is removed and the remainder is orthogonal to $\Phi$ on the training data (2026 Eqs.~(8) to (11), Lemma~3)


0108 \item $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ exists for full column rank of $\Phi$: identifiable baseline, weakly persistently exciting input (2026 Eqs.~(6), (7), Assumption~1)


0109 \item Data-driven: orthogonality holds on the data used, the projection depends on their distribution; any fixed set may be used, the analysis uses the whole training set (2026 Sec.~3.2, text after Eqs.~(12), (13))


0110 \item Uniqueness of $\hat\theta_{\mathrm b}$ shown under Assumption~1 and exact reproduction of the noise-free dynamics on the same training data (2026 Sec.~4.2, Assumption~6)


0111 \end{itemize}


0112 \fi


0113 \begin{wip}


0114 For a baseline linear in its parameters,


0115 $\hat y_k=\varphi(x_k)\theta_{\mathrm b}$,


0116 changing $\theta_{\mathrm b}$ changes the model output $\hat y_k$


0117 by a linear combination of the columns of the


0118 regressor $\varphi(x_k)$~\cite[Eqs.~(2), (39)]{gyorok2026obc}.


0119 Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ subtract from the neural network output


0120 its least-squares fit by the stacked


0121 regressor~\cite[Eqs.~(8), (9), (11), Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}:


0122 \end{wip}


0123 \begin{equation}


0124 \begin{aligned}


0125  \hat Y&=\Phi\theta_{\mathrm b}+\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}},\\


0126  \tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}&=F^{\mathrm{ANN}}-\Phi\theta_{\mathrm{aux}},\\


0127  \theta_{\mathrm{aux}}&=\bigl(\Phi^\top\Phi\bigr)^{-1}\Phi^\top F^{\mathrm{ANN}},\\


0128  0&=\Phi^\top\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}},


0129 \end{aligned}


0130 \label{eq:obc_gyorok}


0131 \end{equation}


0132 \begin{wip}


0133 where $x_k$ is the vector of past measured outputs and past and current


0134 inputs~\cite[Eq.~(1)]{gyorok2026obc}, $\hat Y$ stacks the


0135 predicted outputs over the training data, $\Phi$ is the stacked


0136 regressor, $F^{\mathrm{ANN}}$ stacks the parallel network


0137 outputs~\cite[Eq.~(5)]{gyorok2026obc} and $\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}$ is the


0138 projected network output~\cite[Eqs.~(9), (10)]{gyorok2026obc}.


0139 \\


0140 The projected network output $\tilde F^{\mathrm{ANN}}$ is


0141 orthogonal to the regressor~\cite[Lemma~3]{gyorok2026obc}.


0142 Calculating $\theta_{\mathrm{aux}}$ requires


0143 $(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}$, which exists only if $\Phi$ has


0144 full column rank~\cite[Assumption~1]{gyorok2026obc}.


0145 Therefore different values of $\theta_{\mathrm b}$ are unable 


0146 to give the same stacked prediction on the training data. 


0147 Gy\"or\"ok et al.\ call an input with this property persistently


0148 exciting~\cite[Eq.~(6), Sec.~4.3]{gyorok2026obc}.


0149 This also requires an identifiable baseline, where different


0150 values of $\theta_{\mathrm b}$ give different functions


0151 $\varphi(\cdot)\theta_{\mathrm b}$.


0152 \end{wip}


0153 \subsection{Extension to the gantry}\label{sec:obc_map}


0154 \ifdraft


0155 \begin{itemize}


0156 \item Three differences, each changing one element of \eqref{eq:obc_gyorok}: regressor, data, prediction (this work)


0157 \item Regressor: Jacobian of the normalised transition in $\beta$ at $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$, since the baseline is nonlinear through $M(Y)^{-1}$; $\beta$ because only the ten combinations are identifiable (adapted from Gy\"or\"ok 2025 Sec.~4, Eq.~(15); 2026 Assumption~1; Section~II-C; D-190, D-229); offset column $\Gamma$ included (D-257)


0158 \item Data: reference tuples reconstructed from the measured positions, regressor at the data as in 2026 Eq.~(5) rather than at a simulated baseline (2025 Sec.~3); each with a successor; built once before training (D-252, D-254)


0159 \item Projection on the stacked tuples, with the orthogonality of the section opening as its last line; holds for the stack at $\bar x=0$ (adapted from 2026 Eqs.~(8) to (11), Lemma~3)


0160 \item Prediction: the correction at every step of the closed-loop state update, physical rows only; coefficient differentiated (2026 Eqs.~(9), (10), (13); added states this work)


0161 \end{itemize}

0163 \begin{wip}


0164 Our baseline violates three requirements of the projection in


0165 \eqref{eq:obc_gyorok}.


0166 Its state update is nonlinear in the parameters, because


0167 the next state update depends on $M(Y)^{-1}$ in \eqref{eq:aug_ct}, 


0168 violating the linear requirement for the regressor.


0169 Instead of the regressor the Jacobian of the baseline state update is taken 


0170 with respect to $\beta$,


0171 as in the orthogonal regularisation method of


0172 Gy\"or\"ok et al.~\cite[Sec.~4, Eq.~(15)]{gyorok2025l4dc}.


0173 Like the regressor, the stacked Jacobian must have full column rank.


0174 This requires identifiable parameters, which is why we use $\beta$.


0175 The Jacobian is taken at a linearisation point $\beta_{\mathrm{lin}}$ as


0176 \end{wip}


0177 \begin{equation}

0181 \label{eq:obc_sens}


0182 \end{equation}

0184 $\varphi:\R^{10}\times\R^6\times\R^3\to\R^{6\times10}$.

0186 $\beta$~\cite[Eq.~(16)]{gyorok2025l4dc}:}
