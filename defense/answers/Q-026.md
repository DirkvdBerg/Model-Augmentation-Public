# Q-026 Physical absorber instead of a network (considered, Shakib)
**Position.** The method is for missing dynamics whose structure is unknown. In simulation the absorber is one extra mass (E-101) and friction was left out on purpose as nonlinear unmodelled dynamics (E-100); the network has to find both without being told their form.
**Mechanism.** Augmentation needs no model structure for the missing part; a physical extension needs the type, location and order of the dynamics first.
**Careful.** "To capture friction" is an aim, not a result: the thesis says the augmentation might not capture the friction (E-067) and the dampers may absorb part of it (E-066).
**What else was possible.** Extend the baseline with the mass, spring and damper and estimate them: better, if you know the mode is there.
**My view (opinion).** Both, in sequence: augmentation as a detector (the final model learns one mode at 212 Hz, E-127), then move what it finds into the physics, and keep the network for what has no physical form.
**30 s.** "On the machine I do not know which dynamics are missing. The augmentation finds them from data without a structure; in simulation it learned the absorber mode. Once found, I would put a known mode into the physical model."
**If pushed.** "If you know it is one absorber, the physical extension is better, and I agree my simulation is that case."
**Chain: "why would a network capture Coulomb friction on real data?" (2026-11-02).** Do not claim it will. Representation is possible: the network sees the physical state, velocities included (E-091). Shown is not: the model stays far above the noise floor and one listed reason is that the augmentation might not capture the friction (E-067); dampers may absorb part of it because both oppose the velocity (E-066). That is an identifiability problem: viscous and Coulomb terms look alike unless the data have low speeds and velocity reversals. Test it: friction-only dataset, excitation with reversals, plot the learned force against velocity (a step at v = 0 means friction). On real data I would add an explicit friction block and let the network take the rest (Q-021).
