# Q-028 Network overlap threshold (claim, Schoukens)
**Definition.** rho(Z) = ||Pi Z|| / ||Z||: the largest normalised inner product of Z with a vector in ran Phi, zero iff Phi^T Z = 0 (E-118, E-119). Phi spans the directions in which a change of the parameters changes the one-step baseline prediction (E-003).
**Two overlaps.** Network: Z = network output on the physical states over the tuples. Truth: Z = Xi, true next state minus baseline at the true parameters, i.e. unmodelled terms, reconstruction error and noise (E-120).
**Numbers.** OBC: 4.9e-15 on training (zero by construction, E-126), 0.24 on interpolation test (E-124); unconstrained 0.84 on test (E-125); truth 0.59 on test (E-124).
**What they mean.** 0.00 on training is a check that the projection works, not evidence. 0.24 on test: the projection is fitted on the stacked training tuples (E-093), so on new data the network partly re-enters the parameter directions, still far below 0.84. Low network overlap removes non-uniqueness (the network cannot take what beta explains); the truth overlap 0.59 means the missing dynamics themselves point partly along the parameter directions, and beta absorbs that part: bias (E-002), whatever the network overlap.
**Limit.** No data-derived threshold for "low enough" in the thesis (honest limitation). The truth overlap needs the true parameters, so it cannot be computed on real data.
**30 s.** "Overlap is the cosine between a signal and the parameter directions. The network's is zero on training by construction and 0.24 on test, against 0.84 without OBC. But the missing dynamics have 0.59, and that part goes into the parameters as bias. I have no data-derived threshold."
