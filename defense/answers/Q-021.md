# Q-021 Lur'e structure with an explicit friction block (discussion, Shakib)
**Position.** The simulated friction is Coulomb friction at the three actuators (E-064), a static smoothed Coulomb law of actuator velocity (E-065), left out of the baseline on purpose as nonlinear unmodelled dynamics for the augmentation to capture (`05_setup.tex:90`).
**Mechanism.** A static nonlinearity in feedback around a linear block is a block-oriented feedback structure; Schoukens and Tiels motivate feedback structures by nonlinear feedback in the system (E-075, E-076).
**What else was possible.** An explicit static friction block with a few parameters in the baseline.
**My view (opinion).** With known friction physics an explicit block is the better grey-box choice. The results point the same way: the dampers may absorb part of the friction (E-066), and it is unclear whether the augmentation captures it (E-067).
**30 s.** "Friction was left out on purpose as unknown nonlinear dynamics. If it is known, an explicit Lur'e-type block is better, and my results hint at that: damper estimates may absorb the friction."
**If pushed.** "On a real machine I would add a friction block first and let the network take the rest."
