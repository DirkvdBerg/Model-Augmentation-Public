# Handoff: what the 2026-07-25 session learned

**Read `docs/ISSUE-OVERVIEW-FOR-INDEPENDENT-REVIEW.md` first** if you are diagnosing from scratch. This
file is narrower: what THIS session measured, what it got wrong, and what is unfinished. Treat every
claim here as scrutinizable.

## 1. The single most important thing

**The session spent most of its budget scoring the wrong metric.** The campaign folder
(`scripts/gantry/drift-diagnostics/`) is built around **R4 free-run drift**, so that is what D1 to D7
measured. The user's actual complaint is **R2**: *"the ANN not learning, and it only becomes worse than
the encoder initialization"*, i.e. best checkpoint = epoch 0. Those are different failures and may have
different causes. The user also confirmed the drift is real, but **on the MSD data**, whereas D1 to D8
ran on the perfect-match null where there is nothing to learn and where the project's own I7 says the
testbed cannot discriminate.

Lesson logged: `tasks/lessons.md` -> `score-the-metric-the-user-is-actually-failing-on`.

## 3. What I now believe, and how strongly

* **High confidence:** the drift mechanism in the null is understood (D1, D2, D6). In particular D6
  reframes the whole thing: "which component carries the drift" is the wrong question, the imbalance
  between two large opposing components is the answer, and that is why single-component fixes such as
  the rank-1 prox helped Y and hurt X.
* **Medium:** Adam's normalised step damages a near-optimal init (D8, with SGD as a clean control). The
  mechanism is real; whether it explains the user's failure is not established.
* **Low, and this is the honest position:** that any of it explains the MSD failure. D9's one clean arm
  points at a **horizon** story (train objective improves, free run explodes) rather than at the
  optimiser, and its SGD control is missing.

## 4. What I got wrong, so you do not repeat it

1. **Scored R4 for a whole campaign when the complaint was R2.** See §1.
2. **Claimed Adam "cannot resolve a quantity smaller than its own step".** False. Measured realised step
   in the tail is `0.005` to `0.013 x lr` over 9 runs. This also **voids Leg 2** of
   `docs/drift-literature-sweep-2026-07-25.md`, which dismissed "Adam is not converged yet" on that same
   false premise.
3. **Repeated a wrong claim about Bock and Weiss** from the earlier sweep (that they derive without bias
   correction at `eps = 0`). Bias correction is that paper's contribution. Their bifurcation inequality
   also puts this rig on the **stable** side by 7x, so the "8.9x prefactor gap" never existed.
4. **Reported RMS shares for a telescoping decomposition** in D6's first run, producing a meaningless
   "6471% additivity residual". Use projections onto the total; they sum to 1 by algebra.
5. **Launched a literature sweep on a generic optimisation question** (where does Adam park) whose answer
   would not have changed anything we build, and then **launched a second one on a different workstream**
   (projection/joint estimation) that the user had not asked for. Both stopped.
6. **Two implementation bugs in D9** worth knowing: `loss_kwargs stride=1` instead of the intended
   stride (10x cost), and probing after `fit()` returns, which is useless because deepSI **restores the
   best-validation checkpoint** at the end and here that is the init. Use
   `gantry_dynamic.training._install_nf_val_probe` to measure during training.

All six are in `tasks/lessons.md`.

## 5. Two things the project believed that are now falsified

Beyond the list in `docs/ISSUE-OVERVIEW-FOR-INDEPENDENT-REVIEW.md` §5, these came from the literature
agent and are worth carrying:

* `arXiv:2006.06650` does **not** support "projection induces a compensating stochastic bias"
  (`thread-AB` item A8). Full-text grep: the word "bias" does not appear in its body. The predicted
  failure mode of the proximal fix has no source.
* `tasks/lessons.md` claimed multiple shooting "was tried and failed (Optuna 69399)". Wrong twice: that
  run was lr-bug confounded, and it was an `nf` sweep under **single** shooting with no continuity term.
  Not yet corrected in the file.

## 6. The most useful new lead

`docs/multiple-shooting-sweep-2026-07-25.md`: **our pipeline already is multiple shooting** (the SUBNET
encoder is one of the three known ways to handle shooting-node states). What we omit is the
**continuity/defect term between consecutive segments**. Ribeiro et al. (Automatica 2020) Thm 2 / Cor 3:
with continuity enforced, the segmented problem is exactly equivalent to the full-record
simulation-error problem *regardless of segment length*.

**My own critique of it, unresolved:** the equivalence needs hard equality constraints on the node
states, our encoder fixes those states from data, and our windows overlap (stride 100, nf 400) so
"consecutive segments" do not even exist in the bank. Whether a local defect term prices a constant
force any better than the current loss needs deriving before anyone writes code.

Also from that sweep: weak-constraint 4D-Var sets the defect weight from a model-error covariance
`Q^-1` rather than a swept knob, and data assimilation has a name for our structural case, the
**unstable-neutral subspace**, a vocabulary this project has never searched.
