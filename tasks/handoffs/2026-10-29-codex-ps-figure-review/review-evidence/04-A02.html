02The core visual idea is that every past window becomes one point in the x̄ plane: S1 sorts the points by the phase of the coming error, and S2 is a step of one sample along the ring.
S1 eτ χτ ψa x̄1 x̄2 init p
minθp, θaenc Στ ‖p(ψa(χτ)) − eτ‖²
eτ = [yτ − ŷτ|τ, …, yτ+H−1 − ŷτ+H−1|τ],  x̄τ|τ = ψa(χτ)
S2 χτ χτ+1 x̄τ|τ ψa(χτ+1) gaug(x̂τ|τ, uτ) 2π·212/4000
minθaug Στ ‖gaug(x̂τ|τ, uτ) − ψa(χτ+1)‖²

S1 builds
  1. Inset: the slide-13 gap, labelled eτ.
  2. Empty x̄ plane; untrained encoder puts all windows in a grey blob at the origin ("init").
  3. One window χτ with ψa to one point (highlight).
  4. All windows as black points on a ring; next to four of them, the future error they precede (each a phase-shifted wave).
  5. Dashed p from the highlighted point draws its wave over the true one; the S1 minimisation appears.
S2 builds
  1. Ring of encoded points, light grey; consecutive τ joined by a thin trail.
  2. Two past windows, one sample apart (grey χτ, highlight χτ+1).
  3. Their points: filled x̄τ|τ, open target ψa(χτ+1) one step further; angle wedge labelled 2π·212/4000.
  4. Arrow gaug from the filled point lands near the open target (highlight); S2 minimisation appears.
Answers best: why x̄ must carry the dynamics (windows with different coming error must land at different points) and what stepping looks like. Weakest: the clean ring is idealised; real encoded states need a measured scatter.
